                                      Portland Public Schools
                          SIA Annual Report Responses - Year 3 (2022-2023)

1. What changes in behavior, actions, policies, or practices have you observed related to SIA implementation
during the 2022-23 school year? How do you see these changes contributing to the goals and outcomes in
your SIA plan? (500 words or less)

The changes contributing to the goals in the SIA plan represent sustained investment and focus on high-leverage
strategies, culturally responsive and sustaining practices, and a commitment to educational equity. In 2018, PPS
launched a collaborative effort to develop a vision, a "North Star" to guide the transformation of PPS into one of
the premier systems in the country over the next decade. Since that time, PPS began implementing Forward
Together, the 2021–2025 Strategic Plan for Racial Equity, Inclusion, and Excellence. The changes we are seeing
represent a sustained effort to implement the vision, system shifts, and strategies outlined in those guiding
documents. SIA was a funding source used to deepen and accelerate that work, especially for our focal student
groups.

SIA invested in professional learning, site-based staff, and responsive resources to implement the PPS
Instructional Framework, Multi-Tiered Systems of Supports (MTSS), Master Arts Education Plan, school
counseling core curriculum, and the RESJ Partnership Strategy. These frameworks and strategies have been
central to improving the quality and coherence of student learning across our schools.

PPS remains committed to continuous improvement to meet our students’ differentiated needs, which is
reflected in the positive student achievement trends. Despite national declines in reading and math
performance, PPS students are displaying growth. Notably, overall math achievement has improved across all
grade levels in 2023 compared to 2022 and exceeds statewide averages. The achievements in PPS are nearing
or, in some cases, surpassing pre-pandemic levels.

PPS also recognizes there is still a lot of work to do. When data are disaggregated by race and ethnicity,
performance gaps between student groups are pronounced. We must accelerate growth for all students,
especially focal student groups.

2. What barriers or challenges to SIA implementation have you experienced that are helpful for your
community and/or state leaders to be aware of? What adjustments, if any, did you make to your SIA plan as a
result of these challenges? (500 words or less)

Long-term improvement to achieve transformational change in PreK-12 education is complex and has many
inherent challenges. It requires doing the right work in every classroom for every student every day.

The past three years have had profound challenges which have been documented in previous SIA reports. PPS
has led this transformational change through rapidly fluctuating budgets, inflation, staffing, the pandemic, and
socio-political events. We’ve adjusted and refined our approaches to support the well-being of our staff and
families while sustaining deeper investments in high-quality instruction and comprehensive wrap-around
supports.

Navigating and managing SIA as a discrete funding source, which represents less than 2% of the total district
budget, has added administrative challenges to the process. While the SIA grant’s allowable uses are relatively
flexible, we have spent an extremely disproportionate amount of time managing compliance requirements that
have been exacting in their oversight while also changing each year. The requirements do not always reflect the
size and complexity of PPS, which has necessitated significant clarifications when implementing them. There has
been an especially disproportionate amount of time spent clarifying and managing charter school requirements.

3. SIA implementation includes ongoing engagement with all students, focal students, families, staff, and
community partners. How have relationships with or between those groups changed and/or been maintained
throughout this academic year? Consider the Community Engagement Toolkit and where your efforts might
land on the Levels of Community Engagement spectrum as you complete your response. (500 words or less)

As described in previous SIA plans and reports in addition to the Integrated Grant plan submitted in March 2023,
PPS has ongoing community engagement that continuously informs decisions.

The levels of engagement range from 1-Informed to 4-Collaborate, and where possible 5-Defer.

Whether developing our vision, shaping our strategic plan, or checking in with our communities, Portland Public
Schools is deeply committed to meaningful, authentic, and ongoing community engagement. We leverage
community-based partners, advisory bodies, and existing networks. Many of these are rooted in underserved
student populations, and we make a point of approaching these students and their families first. Our work is
guided by communications and engagement plans, and we’re constantly reflecting on our practices to
incorporate improvements.

Specifically, SIA funded deeper investments in:
   ● BIPOC student-led affinity opportunities and events at middle schools and high schools
   ● Specially designed training and supports for Spanish-speaking families with students experiencing
        disability
   ● Collaboration and relationship building with early childhood families transitioning into Kindergarten
   ● Contracts to engage racial equity social justice (RESJ) partners as collaborators and direct service
        providers
   ● Forming the PPS Arts Education Advisory Committee to establish and sustain equitable access to high-
        quality arts education for all students

4. As you think about what guided your choices and prioritization efforts in this year of SIA implementation,
what stands out? How will what you’ve learned this year impact future SIA implementation efforts? (500
words or less)

PPS is making a long-term commitment to transformational change. We are still early in the process three years
in, but we are encouraged by positive performance trends noted previously in this report.

In guiding choices and priorities, we always start with our Why. It is centered on Educational Equity – raising the
achievement of all students while (1) eliminating the gaps between the lowest and highest performing students
and (2) eliminating the racial predictability and disproportionality of which student groups occupy the highest
and lowest achievement categories.

We are approaching our work with the Instructional Framework as our What and High-Quality Instructional
Materials and Teacher Professional Learning as our How. They are all three connected and critical for our
Roadmap to Educational Equity to be successful.

An example of an SIA funding shift informed by recent learning is more deeply investing in site-based
instructional coaches for the 2023-2024 school year. These coaches will focus on providing professional learning
and embedded support for teachers, facilitating the full implementation of the Instructional Framework. This
adjustment aims to reduce the need for tiered instruction.

PPS will continue to leverage SIA as a funding source to bolster the strategic priorities for sustaining robust
Multi-tiered Systems of Support, critical wrap-around services, and well-rounded educational experiences
through arts and career pathways that are guided by new learning and the implementation of our Strategic Plan.
We will sustain our commitment to continuous improvement to achieve educational equity.
