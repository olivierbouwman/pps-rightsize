BACKGROUND

In 2019 the PPS Board of Directors authorized an enrollment and program balancing initiative
for schools in the southeastern region of our district. In a follow-up 2020 scope of work the
Board laid out three outcome goals:
    ● Optimize Use of Facilities
    ● Support Middle School Redesign
    ● Minimize Program Co-Locations

A diverse stakeholder group of parents, school administrators, teachers and community
partners, were assembled as the Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC) and began meeting
virtually in fall 2020 to review enrollment and program challenges and opportunities. Their work
was aided by FLO Analytics, who provided facilitation, data analysis and modeling, and
community engagement support. The work was impacted by the constraints of the virtual
meeting environment, complexities due to the ongoing, pandemic-related and school closures,
and the fast-approaching 2021 opening date for newly rebuilt Kellogg Middle School.

Data analysis from FLO Analytics showed that, while some schools in the region were
experiencing overcrowding, the most likely enrollment-based challenges facing Black,
Indigenous and other Students of Color living in Southeast were lack of access to robust
programming. This was due in large part to the number of K-8 neighborhood schools that
remained in SE, which did not provide the same degree of programming as comprehensive
middle schools.

After twelve coalition meetings, numerous focus groups and a virtual open house attended by
more than 800 community members, the SEGC delivered a recommendation in December 2020
for the Kellogg MS feeder pattern, impacting six K-8 schools from K-8 to K-5 structures. The
PPS Board approved a modified version of the recommendation in February 2021. The Board
also charge the coalition to continue on to Phase II of the project, and focus on:
     ● Attendance area and special program assignments for Harrison Park Middle School
     ● Relocating K-5 students and programs currently served at Harrison Park
     ● Increasing enrollment at Lane Middle School

The Board narrowed the scope for SEGC Phase II, so fewer schools were included in the
coalition. FLO Analytics returned, and BRIC Architecture was brought on to provide virtual
meeting facilitation and engagement support. The coalition held 15 virtual meetings over ten

PHASE II IMPLEMENTATION

Due to the length of the SEGC process, implementation of Phase II changes was deferred to
the start of the 2023-24 school year. Planning for changes to twenty schools began in Spring of
2022 and involved seventeen central departments. Implementation highlights include:

Engagement - Principals held community building events to ensure families and staff are aware
of upcoming changes that may impact them individually or their school community. These
community building events included, but were not limited to parent and family nights focused on
community and culture of the school, administrator and student leader visits, and
family/community listening sessions.

Child Care and SUN Services - Access to child care and engaging after school activities was a
primary concern for many parents throughout the SEGC process. New partnership agreements
were forged for schools undergoing significant changes, and both SUN and childcare providers
are at Bridger Creative Science, Lent and Marysville. Childcare is available at Clark, and SUN
is open at Harrison Park. Ongoing work includes arranging for transportation from Marysville
after the SUN program ends.

Transportation - Numerous route additions and changes were made, and all routes operated
as planned on day one. Guaranteed transitional bus service was established for students in
boundary change areas who want to stay at their 22/23 schools, all Lent DLI students living in
the 22/23 Bridger attendance area and Harrison Park DLI students living in the Woodstock
attendance boundary. Transitional services are temporary exceptions to transportation
guidelines that will last 5 years for elementary - 2 years for Middle school - 8 years for K-8..

As a result of the SEGC changes, two vehicles were added to the fleet, at an annual cost of
$220,000.

Safe Routes to Schools - PPS Safe Routes to Schools is a collaborative program between
PPS, PBOT, and METRO promoting active transportation getting students safely to and from

as Lane, Whitman, Woodmere and Vestal. Enrollment rose, as hoped, at most of those schools.
Full impact of the boundary changes will not be felt until 2025 for middle schools and 2028 for
elementary schools.

OUTCOME GOALS: INITIAL ASSESSMENT

SEGC Phase I and Phase II impacts will emerge over time through analysis of school climate
surveys, student outcomes and other important data not yet available. However, there are
some clear, albeit initial, outcomes.

#1 Optimize Use of Facilities - Since the launch of SEGC, overcrowding is significantly
reduced across Southeast schools. While pandemic-related factors and housing availability
have contributed to enrollment declines in the region and district-wide, it is also clear that SEGC
changes reduced crowding at Bridger, Hosford, Mt. Tabor and other schools.

Small schools, such as Lane, Whitman, Woodmere and Vestal were already a focus for concern
before pandemic-era enrollment losses. Boundary changes are showing early signs of helping
to off-set recent enrollment losses and should create more predictable and robust enrollment
over time, which supports better school staffing.

#2 Support Middle School Redesign - With two new middle schools, all Southeast students
now have access to comprehensive middle schools. This includes students who live in the
Bridger Creative Science boundary, who have guaranteed transfer to Harrison Park MS if they
do not wish to continue in their K-8.

#3 Minimize Program Co-Locations - Program co-location changes played a dominant role in
SEGC Phase II. As a result of the coalition’s work, there are fewer dual language immersion
co-locations in Southeast. While co-location still exists at Atkinson (Spanish DLI), Kelly
(Russian DLI) and Woodstock (Chinese DLI), there are no longer any single strand English
(neighborhood) programs co-located with immersion programs.
