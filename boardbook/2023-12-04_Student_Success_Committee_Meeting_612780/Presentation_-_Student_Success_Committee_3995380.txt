Graduation Requirements



                      PORTLAND PUBLIC SCHOOLS
Senate Bill 3
In spring 2023, SB 3 established Oregon’s newest diploma
requirements: Beginning with the Class of 2027, Oregon
students must complete a 0.5 credit in Personal Financial
Education and a 0.5 credit in Higher Education and Career
Path Skills as part of their 24 required credits.




                                                            3
Career Path Skills
Higher Education and Career Path Skills mean providing instruction on:
Applying for jobs; Applying for admission to a post-secondary
institution of education; Applying for post-secondary learning and job
training opportunities; Developing career-related skills; and Seeking
assistance, including accessing community resources and acting as a
self-advocate for mental, physical and financial well-being.




                                                                    4
Personal Financial Education
Personal Financial Education means instruction that provides guidance
on: Credit scores; Investments, asset building and debt; Strategies for
creating a budget; Taxes, including accessing tax credits, understanding
tax cycles, being familiar with state and federal tax forms and being
familiar with federal, state, regional and local taxes; and Building
financial well-being, including evaluating the impact of behavioral
economics and the psychology of money.

ODE Timeline
2023-24: ODE content panels develop standards; ODE and TSPC engage in
rulemaking to determine licensure requirements.
2024-25: ODE develops resources to support implementation, including
instructional materials evaluation tools.
2025-26: Most districts and public charter schools implement new course
requirements.
2026-27: Students are required to have completed a 0.5 credit in Personal
Financial Education and a 0.5 credit in Higher Education and Career Path Skills

Draft, Draft, Draft PPS Timeline
2023-2024: Conduct course audits to identify courses that meet the new grad
requirements.
2024-2025: Create new courses as needed.
2025-2026: Students may begin enrolling in required courses if they haven’t met
the requirements.
2026-2027: Students continue enrolling in required courses as needed.

Equitable Grading Practices



                         PORTLAND PUBLIC SCHOOLS
Key Shifts to Equitable Grading
Shifts to Key Practices on the Equitable Grading Practices Continuum
●   Mathematically sound scales
●   Performance standards
●   Rubrics
●   Mitigate Non-academic factors
●   Eliminate Bias-resistant practices
●   Provide multiple opportunities to demonstrate proficiency
●   Normalize feedback and include opportunities for student self-assessment
●   Use a standards-based or competency-based grading model
●   Calculate grades in ways that reflect a student's final proficiency
