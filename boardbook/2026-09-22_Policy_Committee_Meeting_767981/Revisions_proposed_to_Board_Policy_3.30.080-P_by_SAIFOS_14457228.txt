​Under Glossary of Definitions, add the following definitions:​

​Airflows:​​Volumes of outdoor air, total air, and air​​recirculated through MERV-rated filters per​
 ​unit time delivered to each room via the HVAC system measured via test, adjust, balance​
  ​methods, and volumes of clean air per unit time delivered by in-room air filtration. Typically in​
   ​cubic feet per minute, which can also be converted to air changes per hour or per-person rates.​

​Effective air changes per hour:​​A measure of how many​​times in one hour ventilation and​
 ​filtration systems deliver a volume of air equal to the size of the room. Effective air changes per​
  ​hour include outdoor air from ventilation, recirculated air filtered through MERV-rated filters in​
   ​HVAC systems, and in-room filtered air.​

​Electronic air cleaning technologies:​​Devices or features​​that use electricity, electric fields,​
 ​UV light, or other additive technologies to produce ions, charged airborne particles, activated​
  ​metal catalysts, or other reactive compounds. Examples include bipolar ionization, corona​
   ​discharge, electrostatic precipitators, ionizers, needle point discharge, ozone, photocatalytic​
    ​oxidation, plasma, and reactive oxygen species. These technologies can unintentionally alter​
     ​room chemistry and produce harmful contaminants, such as ultrafine particles, formaldehyde,​
      ​and other volatile organic compounds.​

​In-room filtration:​​The process of moving air through​​mechanical fibrous filters in air purifiers​
 l​ocated inside classrooms and other occupied school spaces in order to remove airborne​
  ​particles and aerosols. Air purifiers that can provide in-room filtration have a measurable clean​
   ​air delivery rate (the volume of clean air the device supplies per unit time) and include portable​
    ​floor models, wall-mounted filters, table-top units, and furniture- or ceiling-integrated units.​
     ​These devices may operate via electronic / digital control panels needing active management​
      ​(daily turning on by staff or students) or with mechanical switch-based designs that can be​
       ​paired with external timers or motion sensors to enable automatic operation.​

​PM​2.5​:​​Fine particulate matter in air; a mix of inhalable​​solid particles and liquid droplets with​
 ​diameters of 2.5 micrometers or smaller. Sources of PM2.5 include vehicle exhaust, wildfires,​
  ​wood burning for winter home heating, dust, mold, combustion from school boilers and heating​
   ​equipment, cooking operations in school kitchens, and secondary formation from indoor​
    ​chemical reactions.​

​Urban heat islands:​​Areas within cities that lack​​adequate vegetation and become significantly​
 ​hotter due to concrete and asphalt absorbing, retaining, and re-releasing heat.​

​(See also:​​EPA EnviroAtlas Urban Heat Islands and​​Vulnerable Communities in Portland, OR​)​

​Ventilation:​​The process of supplying and removing​​air within a building to maintain indoor air​
 ​quality; includes both outdoor air brought in through mechanical or natural ventilation systems​
  ​and indoor air recirculated through MERV 13 or higher-rated filters to remove respiratory​
   ​aerosols, particulates, and other filterable air pollutants.​

​Revisions in Context of Full Policy​

​In response to the human-caused climate crisis currently underway and the direct harm being​
 ​done to our District, society, and planet, Portland Public Schools (PPS) is committing to​
  i​mmediately mobilize resources for climate action. To this end, the District commits to reducing​
   ​greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and minimizing other negative environmental impacts;​
    i​mproving our school communities’ health and wellness; and building a culture of learning,​
     ​responsibility, and sustainability centered on our values of racial equity and climate justice.​

​Climate change disproportionately impacts the vulnerable members of our community.​
 ​Implementation of this policy will prioritize serving people with disabilities, communities of color,​
  ​and other vulnerable populations.​

​The District aims to educate and empower students as leaders in the just transition to a​
 ​sustainable city and restorative society. Our goal is for all students to be environmentally literate,​
  ​and to understand the connections between their daily actions and the natural world​, as well as​
   ​the larger social, economic, and infrastructural systems that drive environmental change,​
    ​climate disruption, and public health. The District supports students’ environmental literacy​​by​
     ​using community-based learning and civic action as a context for meeting academic goals​
      ​described in the​​Graduate Portrait​.​

​The District shall prioritize investments that achieve the greatest emissions reduction, while​
 i​ntegrating climate action into our curriculum and developing a culture of environmental​
  ​stewardship and climate justice throughout our organization. Other efforts which have a lower​
   i​mpact on emissions and require major changes in infrastructure can be implemented over the​
    l​onger term.​

​Our two overarching objectives are:​

        ​Emissions Reduction:​​PPS will reduce its greenhouse​​gas emissions by 50 percent by​
         ​2030, using the 2018-2019 school year baseline, and reach net zero emissions by 2040.​

        ​For the sake of emissions tracking, PPS will use a Scope 1 and Scope 2 analysis, which​
         i​ncludes all emissions associated with the following: district-operated buses and fleet​
          ​vehicles, and building energy use from both natural gas and electricity. In addition to​
           ​Scope 1 and Scope 2, PPS will also track emissions associated with waste disposal​
            ​(food waste, recycling, and landfill-bound garbage), which is defined as part of a Scope​
             ​3 analysis.​

        ​Engagement, Resilience, and Wellness:​​The District​​will take steps to prepare schools​
         ​for the effects of climate change, and will ensure staff and students learn about and​
          ​engage in climate solutions, climate resiliency, and climate justice practices.​

                           ​CLIMATE CRISIS RESPONSE COMMITTEE​

​To monitor effective implementation, transparency, and tracking of progress, PPS will establish a​
 ​committee of no more than nine persons that is made up of a majority of members who identify​
  ​as people of color, and two members who are current PPS students. The Board shall approve​

                                       ​Glossary of Definitions​

​Scope 1 Emissions:​​direct greenhouse (GHG) emissions​​that occur from sources that are​
 ​controlled or owned by an organization (e.g., emissions associated with fuel combustion in​
  ​boilers, furnaces, water heaters, vehicles).​

​Scope 2 Emissions:​​indirect GHG emissions associated​​with the purchase of electricity, steam,​
 ​heat, or cooling. Although scope 2 emissions physically occur at the facility where they are​
  ​generated, they are accounted for in an organization’s GHG inventory because they are a result​
   ​of the organization’s energy use.​


                                                                                                       ​9​
​Scope 3 Emissions:​​the result of activities from assets​​not owned or controlled by the reporting​
 ​organization, but that the organization indirectly impacts in its value chain. Scope 3 emissions​
  i​nclude all sources not within an organization’s scope 1 and 2 boundary. The scope 3 emissions​
   ​for one organization are the scope 1 and 2 emissions of another organization. Scope 3 emission​
    ​sources include emissions both upstream and downstream of the organization’s activities.​

​(Scope emissions defined by​​EPA Center for Corporate​​Climate Leadership Home​)​

​Airflows:​​Volumes of outdoor air, total air, and air​​recirculated through MERV-rated filters per​
 ​unit time delivered to each room via the HVAC system measured via test, adjust, balance​
  ​methods, and volumes of clean air per unit time delivered by in-room air filtration. Typically in​
   ​cubic feet per minute, which can also be converted to air changes per hour or per-person rates.​

​Effective air changes per hour:​​A measure of how many​​times in one hour ventilation and​
 ​filtration systems deliver a volume of air equal to the size of the room. Effective air changes per​
  ​hour include outdoor air from ventilation, recirculated air filtered through MERV-rated filters in​
   ​HVAC systems, and in-room filtered air.​

​Electronic air cleaning technologies:​​Devices or features​​that use electricity, electric fields,​
 ​UV light, or other additive technologies to produce ions, charged airborne particles, activated​
  ​metal catalysts, or other reactive compounds. Examples include bipolar ionization, corona​
   ​discharge, electrostatic precipitators, ionizers, needle point discharge, ozone, photocatalytic​
    ​oxidation, plasma, and reactive oxygen species. These technologies can unintentionally alter​
     ​room chemistry and produce harmful contaminants, such as ultrafine particles, formaldehyde,​
      ​and other volatile organic compounds.​

​Feasible:​​capable of being done or carried out. Feasibility​​will include the following factors:​
 ​economics/available funding and appropriate trade-offs (i.e. the district will not spend general​
  ​funds on energy efficiency if it is in lieu of incremental staffing in schools).​

​Frontline Communities:​​Communities that experience​​continuing injustice—including people of​
 ​color, immigrants, people with lower incomes, those in rural areas, and indigenous people—and​
  ​face a legacy of systemic, largely racialized, inequity that influences their living and working​
   ​places, the quality of their air and water, and their economic opportunities.​

​In-room filtration:​​The process of moving air through​​mechanical fibrous filters in air purifiers​
 l​ocated inside classrooms and other occupied school spaces in order to remove airborne​
  ​particles and aerosols. Air purifiers that can provide in-room filtration have a measurable clean​
   ​air delivery rate (the volume of clean air the device supplies per unit time) and include portable​

     ​These devices may operate via electronic / digital control panels needing active management​
