BACKGROUND

Jason Lee Elementary, located at 2222 NE 92nd Avenue, sits at the bottom of Rocky Butte, in
the furthest northeast corner of Portland Public Schools, and is home to nearly 300 students in
grades Kindergarten to 5th Grade, plus over 30 additional preschool students who are part of
the Head Start program housed at Lee.

We are a racially diverse school, with 72 percent students of color, students with special needs
and other historically underserved students. Our students are 19 percent Latino, 13 percent
Black, 11 percent Asian, 11 percent multiracial. We are a Title 1 school as well as a Community
Eligibility Provision school, which means all of our students qualify for free breakfast and lunch
each school day. Over 23 percent of our students receive special education services.

While many schools in our district have lost enrollment, we have grown by more than 20 percent
since the largest drop following the pandemic. We have a vibrant and involved parent
community headed by an active PTA and we host a myriad of community engagement events
throughout the school year to celebrate our cultural diversity including Black Brilliance, Lunar
New Year and Festival de la Familia.

Team Lee strives to be responsive to our students’ needs, particularly to their need to have their
identities honored at school. We host a student GSA group and a diverse Student Leadership
group, and we’ve spent the past year honing our instruction around Social and Emotional
Learning through the adoption of the Caring Schools Community curriculum. Among other
things, this adoption has ensured that all students have access to a class morning circle every
day, where they can develop peer and student-teacher relationships as well as learn skills to
help with their social and emotional development.

This past fall we took a strong step to advance this work by aligning with the Anti-Defamation
League’s No Place for Hate program, which will ensure that our students have leadership
opportunities to create a school where everyone can belong, no matter their race, language or
other identity markers.

It is at this time in the history of our school, and in the wake of recent social justice social
movements, that our community wants to reflect on the values we hold, and what is represented
by what we call ourselves.

The petition to change the name of our school came to our school last spring accompanied by
78 signatures, including those of many parents, staff, students and community members. These
signatures were gathered at a number of events in the spring.
I believe the request is a timely one and it speaks to our school’s need to honor who we are
today. A number of members of our community have done a bit of research on the history of
Jason Lee, for whom a number of schools are named in the Northwest, including a couple in
Washington state.

According to the Oregon Encyclopedia, Jason Lee was the first Protestant Christian Missionary
to the Pacific Northwest and “exemplified the effort to convert northwestern Indians to
Christianity and assimilate them into Western culture. Lee played an important role in promoting
white settlement in the Willamette Valley.”

Records show that in 1842 Lee opened the Oregon Institute to educate white children. That
institute eventually became Willamette University. Willamette University has done some work in
recent years to repair relationships with local Native American tribes and acknowledge some of
the harm done by its founder in the past.

A plaque that currently sits in a corner of the principal’s office was placed on the school at its
founding in 1953. It states that Jason Lee was “a pioneer who practiced what he preached, he
helped to bring statehood to the Oregon territory and Christian education to all its children, both
red and white.”

We believe that it’s time to establish a new identity for our school, one that carries a name more
closely associated with who we are as a school community today.

At this point in our history, our school community is learning not only of the ways that people of
color in the past have been marginalized, but also the way they continue to be. Our own
campus has been the site of incidents of hate speech among students, and it’s clear that as a
school we need to send a consistent message to our students about our values as a community,
the way in which we honor each other’s identities and celebrate what makes each of us unique,
no matter our race, gender, linguistic or cultural background.

ANALYSIS OF SITUATION

We believe we need a process that engages our students in determining our collective school
values, as well as a school name, and can uplift those values and inspire our students, staff and
families to be the best versions of ourselves. As our community is racially diverse, we want the
process to ensure that students and families from all backgrounds are part of the process, so
that all voices are heard and all perspectives honored.
We believe that the process of identifying our values and choosing a new school name will in
and of itself be generative of a new school community identity, where our students - particularly
our Black and Brown students, experience a sense of belonging. Our new school name and
identity will more closely align with our district’s RESJ values and goals, as well as our district’s
vision and mission.


FISCAL IMPACT

The cost estimate to remove and replace the signage on the building and the readerboard, and
to remove and repaint a wall mural, is $32,000. Board Policy 2.20.010-P does not require a
school community to propose a method of covering the expense of a name change when they
can show the change is being made to address discrimination.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT

Along with students, staff, families, as well as community members, will be invited to a number
of sessions to engage in the process of outlining community values, and then the characteristics
of the name that deserves the honor of naming our school after.
We want our students then through classroom projects during their content rotation block to
spend some time researching names of people or concepts that highlight these values and
characteristics. Students would then host presentations before a committee of students, staff
and community members, who can help to shape the final proposed name that we would bring
before the School Board for approval.


TIMELINE FOR IMPLEMENTATION / EVALUATION

We would love to begin the process of outlining community values this Spring, perhaps
continuing with student suggestions and presentations through the fall semester. After a
selection process, we would hope to bring the name proposal in the Winter of 2025.


BOARD OPTIONS

According to Board Policy 2.20.010-P Naming School District Property, requests for renaming of
District properties may be presented by individuals, by petition, by chosen committees, or by
other representative groups to the School Board. The petition needs to demonstrate diverse
school community support or indicate the name change is being requested in accordance with
the policy. The School Board will take one of these actions:

        • Direct the Superintendent to conduct a renaming process
        • Decline the request
        • Defer action on the request to a later date


CONNECTION TO BOARD GOALS

We believe that in the process of outlining community values, our students will more clearly
understand the importance we place on honoring the diversity and multiplicity of their identities.
Our goal is ultimately to select a name that more accurately represents for our students their
value in our community, specifically our historically underserved students. We know that
students need to experience a strong sense of belonging on their path to academic success.
Our goal is for all our students to reach academic success in every grade level, and we work
toward this through multiple strategies and supports.
Renaming our school is an important part of the process of our students experiencing a strong
sense of belonging so they can succeed academically.
