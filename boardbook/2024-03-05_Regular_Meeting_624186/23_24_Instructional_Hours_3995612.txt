Subject:       Instructional Hours Make Up


Current Context
Given our current context with the disruptions brought forward via the PAT strike followed by a
loss of time due to the ice storm in January, we would like to present some potential options to
make up for lost hours.

Currently, as it sits, the second column from the right shows where we stand without the addition
of 14 hours via the waiver from ODE. This does not include the individual school closures that
have happened throughout the year like the late start for westside schools (Lincoln, West
Sylvan, Ainsworth, Skyline and Forest Park) on January 10, 2024.

The far right column shows the impact on instructional time if we request the emergency waiver
(14 hours).




Summary:
Currently, we are over the division 22 instructional time requirement for all levels with the
exception of kindergartners and seniors. When we include the 14 hours via the ODE waiver we
are within 10 hours of meeting the requirement for both grade levels.

Options
High School Options

   ●   Given the strike AND the ice storm, the board approves the 14 hours waiver from ODE
       for all levels.
   ●   Add May 31st to be an instructional day for seniors (6.13 hours for rotating A/B schools
       or 5.86 hours for fixed 8 schools) *We have made this change already.
   ●   Make the SAT day a full instructional day for seniors (2 hours) *We have made this
       change already.

Summary: Applying these three changes would get us over the division 22 requirements for
seniors with the exception of Jefferson and McDaniel because they operate on a “fixed 8”
schedule. They are not on a rotating A/B block schedule. We would then apply for a division 22
exception which we do every year for some schools who don’t meet time based on a myriad of
things (weather, power, etc).

Elementary School Options

   ●   For Elementary School (Because it’s difficult to differentiate just for kinders) the board
       could approve converting 15 minutes of recess to instruction (16.25 hrs). We will provide
       guidance to teachers to allow brain and movement breaks when they feel it is necessary.

Summary: If we start this at the beginning of the 4th quarter we will have made up more than
the time needed. With the 14 hour waiver, we only need to make up 2 hours for elementary. We
also will only need to do this for one month if we decide that.

If we don’t add the 14 hours per ODE:
High Schools (Seniors)
    ● Add May 31st to be an instructional day for seniors (6.13 hours for rotating A/B schools
       or 5.86 hours for fixed 8 schools) *We have made this change already.
    ● Make the SAT day a full instructional day for seniors (2 hours) *We have made this
       change already.

Additional Options:
   ● Convert Memorial Day to a school day and add three days at the end of the year OR
   ● Add four days to the calendar at the end of the year (June 17-18, 20-21st) OR
   ● Add 15 minutes to the high school day so as not to interfere with after school athletics
       and activities.

Cons:
Seniors last day would be June 6th. We would need to move graduations back a week. We
would eat the cost of Providence Park ($100,000), graduations would need to be at school sites.
And families are already planning on the original dates. This is a huge logistical problem.

We would have significant attendance issues on Memorial Day and if we added these days at
the end of the year both for students and teachers similar to winter break. Memorial Day
conversion would require negotiations with all of our labor partners as it is non-work day.

We would need to work out logistics with transportation knowing that there would be an added
cost and logistical challenges if we add 15 minutes on the front end to the high school schedule.
