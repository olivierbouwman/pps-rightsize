To:            School Board Policy Committee

From:          Don Wolff, Chief Technology Officer
               Judy Brennan, Director, Enrollment and Transfer


Subject:       Requested Revisions to Policy 4.10.051-P



This memo outlines minor policy language changes to the Student Enrollment and Transfers
policy, 4.10.051-P. The changes are designed to leverage new technical ability due to a change
in software vendors and enable several transfer system improvements aligned with
recommendations submitted to the PPS Audit Committee in April 2023. In order to impact the
2024-25 middle and high school transfer process, Board approval is needed by mid-January
2024. The proposed minor adjustments do not limit the Board’s ability to propose additional,
substantive policy changes for implementation in a later school year.

BACKGROUND
School choice has existed in PPS for more than a century. Approximately 30% of PPS students
attend schools other than their assigned neighborhood schools, many enrolled in specialized
programs such as Benson Polytechnic High School, DaVinci Arts Middle School or Dual
Language Immersion (DLI), which is offered at 27 schools and in five languages. Most students
enter such schools and programs through an annual lottery, and PPS uses software to
determine the results using factors described in Student Enrollment and Transfers policy.

The district also allows hardship transfers when there is evidence of an extraordinary situation
that poses imminent health, safety or serious educational concerns for the student. The chart
below shows the volume of hardship petitions and lottery applications received for the past two

General transfer procedures are described in policy, 4.10.051-P, while more detailed transfer
steps are explained in the Student Transfer Administrative Directive, 4.10.054-AD. The Board
Policy Committee has discussed the policy in recent years, but major changes have not been
made since 2014, when neighborhood-based schools and programs were moved out of the
annual lottery and into a petition-only process, and an income eligibility preference for
low-income students was added to most lottery-based schools and programs.

The hardship petition process was the subject of a 2022 internal audit that resulted in five
recommendations, including strengthening the “internal control structure to ensure Hardship
Petition Transfer requests are approved or denied in alignment with the school district’s policy
and administrative directive.” The internal auditor noted that some hardship petition transfers
had been approved because space remained in the requested focus option or dual language
immersion program, which was not the same as evidence of a hardship.

PROPOSED POLICY CHANGES AND RATIONALE
In July 2023, PPS shifted to a new Synergy transfer software which will allow parents easier
access to transfer applications and streamline enrollment changes due to lottery and petition
decisions. Testing of new lottery functionality revealed an unexpected opportunity to shift all
applications for lottery-based schools and programs to a single application structure, preserving
the hardship petition exclusively for transfer to neighborhood-based schools and programs. The
change will help fulfill the internal audit implementation plan, ensure consistency in
implementation of Board-approved factors such as income eligibility and regional preferences
for Benson HS, while also streamlining waitlist management and improving transparency.

After the lottery application period closes, the “open enrollment” application will use the exact
same priorities and set-up as the lottery application, with the addition of one field for families to
describe any special circumstances that may have prevented them from applying during the
lottery cycle or indicate an exceptional need for placement at the requested school. Special
circumstances will be reviewed by ETC staff, and input may be gathered from school

administrators to determine whether the student qualifies for the additional priority. The special
circumstances priority falls below Board-approved preferences (co-enrolled sibling, income
eligibility and regional preference), but can result in a student having a higher placement on a
waiting list than applicants without the priority. Lottery and open enrollment applications will be
viewable by school staff on a single waitlist, with priority eligibility clearly displayed for improved
transparency.

The proposed changes will shift approximately 700 lottery-based school and program
applications from the hardship petition into the lottery/open enrollment application. The technical
set-up is underway, but policy language needs to be aligned, as well. There are three lines in
4.10.051-P, that link applications to focus options with the petition transfer. If those lines can be
revised by mid-January 2024, the new technical system can be used for all 2024-25 transfer
results.

There may be other areas of the Student Enrollment and Transfers policy that could be
improved, as well. However, due to upcoming transfer cycle, we request that additional
modifications be considered at a later date.

FISCAL IMPACT
There is no identified fiscal impact associated with this change. The number of transfers into
lottery-based schools is not expected to change because of the new policy language and
process. The expected impact is improved efficiency and transparency, particularly for
school-based staff and families.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT
The software and workflow changes have been vetted with lottery-based school administrators
and counselors. They provided positive feedback about the potential improvements.

NEXT STEPS
2024-25 middle and high school transfer applications are open through December 22, 2023.
The proposed policy language changes are needed by mid-January, in order to use new
software functionality to process all transfers into lottery-based schools and programs.
