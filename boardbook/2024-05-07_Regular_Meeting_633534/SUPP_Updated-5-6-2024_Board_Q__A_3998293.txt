    A-Student impact:
    Students will continue to receive the same required minutes of arts instruction they are
    receiving in 2023-24. The current model is 45 minutes once per week per art form (music
    and visual arts) for the entire academic year. Most of our schools provide access to two art
    forms so the total average weekly number is 90 minutes instruction. No K-5 schools will
    experience a reduction in the minutes of instruction.

    Educator impact:
    The current funding formula creates staffing inconsistencies across the system. In 2023-24,
    some schools received FTE allocations over the number of required minutes of instruction
    and other schools staffed more proportional to the required minutes. The new funding
    formula in 24-25 is shifting to more accurately staff the needs of the arts equitably across
    all schools. Schools will be funded in 0.2 FTE increments based on homerooms instead of
    0.5 and 1.0 increments based on total enrollment.

    Using a more exact formula means that some educators will be staffed across two buildings
    and in rare cases three. In most scenarios, educators will be at one school three days per
    week and another two days per week. This will result in educators engaging in two school
    communities and cultures and an increased student caseload. Pairing educators to schools

Provide a high-level rundown of changes in how equity allocations were done in the K-8
schools. What was the decision-making process?

   Equity is allocated to grades K-8 and high schools as separate buckets. With the addition of
   the teacher academic interventionists in grades K-8, Equity was reduced and reallocated in
   K-8 to support the addition of this new targeted investment that many schools were
   previously self-funding with Equity. We are seeing promising results with student growth
   based on the Teacher Academic Interventionists.

   The increase in the Direct Certification threshold was done to concentrate the Equity in the
   schools with the greatest needs, rather than dilute it across many schools. Reallocating a
   portion of the Equity staffing formula was a way to be more strategic with targeted
   investments for the students with the greatest need to support in reading and math.

What efforts have been put in place to build and retain workforce diversity?

   As identified during the 4/24/2024 Superintendent’s proposed budget presentation, in a time
   of budget cuts and layoffs, PPS works to mitigate the impact of layoffs on our workforce
   diversity by implementing seniority “exceptions” available in our collective bargaining
   agreements to reduce the number of staff of color displaced from their current assignments
   or subject to layoff. PPS will continue to partner with a number of universities on “grow-your-
   own” programs that provide career paths and reduce barriers to underrepresented talent.
   This includes our Paras to Professionals and Coaches to Classroom programs with Warner
   Pacific University, both of which create career paths to licensed educator positions for
   current diverse employees. PPS also has a long-standing teacher pipeline program, Rise to
   Teach, with Portland Community College, Portland State University, and partner school
   districts to encourage and reduce barriers of access to aspiring teachers of color. The
   Professional Learning & Leadership team leads the PPS cross-functional work funded by
   the Wallace Foundation on our racial equity centered pipeline initiative focused on principal
   supports and career pathways.

      as MAP achievement, attendance, behavior and discipline, among others.

What are the criteria for staffing custodians?
      Schools are grouped into four categories for custodial staffing purposes, based primarily
      on size and building usage (e.g., recurring CUB (community use of buildings) permits),
      and staffing allocations are adjusted as needed throughout the year. We have a baseline
      staffing model for Elementary (A & B), Middle (C) and High Schools (D). These baseline
      numbers are driven by the school's size (square footage wise) and also are adjusted
      with school enrollment (e.g., Alameda is technically a 'B' based on square footage, but
      due to the large student enrollment), they are categorized as a 'C'). Each Sr. Facilities
      Operations Manager (FOM) has the authority to adjust the staffing based on size,
      enrollment, etc. Typically, the allocation is as follows: A & B have 2 to 2.5 FTE, C have
      3 to 3.5 and D have 6-8, all depending on enrollment, building usage and size. These
      FTE include the Head Custodian, which works by themself during the day and the
      evening custodian that may have a co-worker, depending on building size.

       The current recommendations include reducing five full-time custodial vacancies. These
       positions will come from high schools which are typically used to provide backup support
       at lower grade schools when needed. This will leave the Custodial Services team with
       approximately 350 custodians who provide near 24/7 support across all of our school
       buildings.

What is the reason behind the reductions in Early Learning/Head Start?
       Unlike any other school or department in Portland Public Schools, our preschool
       program is 100% grant-funded – and 90% of those funds pay for staff salaries. Like
       other schools and departments, our staff salaries will increase next year in line with new
       labor contracts while our funding will remain flat. Additionally, our grants have enrollment
       requirements to retain our funding. We have not yet reached full enrollment, but need to
       do so to sustain our funding.

       We need to cut $2.5 million from our roughly $20 million budget – approximately a
       12.5% cut. This reduction will come from approximately 20 FTE and consolidation of
       programs.
           ● We will continue to operate 51 preschool classrooms across the district.
           ● We will continue to staff classrooms with one full-time teacher, one full-time
              educational assistant, and one part-time educational assistant.
           ● We will continue to provide the wrap-around services required by our current
              grantors, but make minor reductions in all program areas.
           ● We will reorganize our Early Learners department to streamline staffing and
              budgets.
       In September, we will relocate four Head Start classrooms out of the Applegate building,
       where enrollment has been persistently low. All four classes will move to other PK sites.
       We will work directly with returning Applegate families around this relocation.

Why keep a 5% reserve?
      Board policy calls for maintaining a reserve fund of at least 5% of General Fund
      revenues to avoid financial instability and to insulate expenditure requirements from
      typical fluctuations in revenues and expenditures. Maintaining adequate reserves is
      sound budgetary practice and provides an important layer of financial stability in light of
      uncertainty and unpredictability in revenues and expenditures.

       As stated in the Board policy, reserves may be used at the discretion of the Board to
       address one-time emergencies and unanticipated expenditure requirements or to offset
       unanticipated revenue fluctuations occurring within a fiscal year. In the event that
       reserves are expended, there will be a plan to replenish the expended funds within three
       fiscal years.
