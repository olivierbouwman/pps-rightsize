Tommy Cardwell
Ainsworth

Hello,

I wanted to comment on the proposal to end school based fundraising. I think the idea of ending it is
shortsighted and will hurt PPS overall, at a time when more and not less funding is needed.

I believe in economic equality and will always vote for bonds/etc to increase school funding district wide,
but this is a general voter and legislative problem. I give to my child’s school because they go there, I give
to the district in taxes. Ending school fundraising as proposed may contribute to equality, but by bringing
all schools down (although, those schools that can raise funds more significantly of course). Without the
drive to give to your school foundation, these donations will dry up, or if capped at $20,000, that is where
it will stop. The schools that have traditionally been lucky enough to benefit from this will be hit
significantly, but the district will also not benefit from the 33% they would have traditionally received.

The schools that do have these foundation also received less funding per child from the district, which I
assume has been based on needs and scores. It would make sense that these schools have been
historically propped up by the foundations they have. Does the district have plans to counter this? Taking
the donations away and keeping funding where it is seems cruel and shortsighted.

Another worry is that families that live in neighborhoods that have these schools and that can afford it will
transfer to private schools, decreasing enrollment, and with that the federal funding attached to
enrollment.

Please work to bring all schools up, and not enact rules whose sole purpose from my view is to bring
them down.

Thank you, Tommy

Wesley Grout
Beach ES

PPS funding needs to be reformed. I grew up in Portland and attended Ainsworth, West Sylvan and
Lincoln. My 7 year old daughter currently attends Beach Elementary. The fact that wealthy communities
can buy extra staff is nuts. While schools with wealthy foundations might receive fewer dollars per student
than Beach, I would love to know how many of Beach's dollars are going to cover the basic needs of
many of our students. While it is ridiculous that we cannot provide ALL children with the support and
services they need, the people who have more need to share. Maybe schools with wealthier communities
will not have as many services, but they will likely still have more than the kids at schools like Beach.
