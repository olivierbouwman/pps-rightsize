Tommy Cardwell

Hello, I wanted to comment on the proposal to end school based fundraising. I think the idea of
ending it is shortsighted and will hurt PPS overall, at a time when more and not less funding is
needed. I believe in economic equality and will always vote for bonds/etc to increase school
funding district wide, but this is a general voter and legislative problem. I give to my child’s school
because they go there, I give to the district in taxes. Ending school fundraising as proposed may
contribute to equality, but by bringing all schools down (although, those schools that can raise
funds more significantly of course). Without the drive to give to your school foundation, these
donations will dry up, or if capped at $20,000, that is where it will stop. The schools that have
traditionally been lucky enough to benefit from this will be hit significantly, but the district will also
not benefit from the 33% they would have traditionally received. The schools that do have these
foundation also received less funding per child from the district, which I assume has been based
on needs and scores. It would make sense that these schools have been historically propped up
by the foundations they have. Does the district have plans to counter this? Taking the donations
away and keeping funding where it is seems cruel and shortsighted. Another worry is that families
that live in neighborhoods that have these schools and that can afford it will transfer to private
schools, decreasing enrollment, and with that the federal funding attached to enrollment. Please
work to bring all schools up, and not enact rules whose sole purpose from my view is to bring
them down. Thank you, Tommy
