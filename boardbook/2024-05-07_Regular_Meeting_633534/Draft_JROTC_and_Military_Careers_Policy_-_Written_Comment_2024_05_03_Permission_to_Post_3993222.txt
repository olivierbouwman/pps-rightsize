JROTC is not a career training program, it is a military recruiting tool. JROTC will only further
cut into funds needed for teaching staff and student supports and will prey on vulnerable
students and students who do not see any future ahead of them besides joining the military. To
consider implementing JROTC while cutting critical supports for special ed, reducing teaching
positions, and facing multiple lawsuits involving allegations of sexual assault and racial violence
against students at PPS is unconscionable. If the school board has any integrity left, they must
listen to the people they represent and vote no on JROTC at PPS.


5/3/24
Marilyn Schirk
PPS

I have always been uncomfortable with the idea of military recruitment on public school campuses.
Although service in the military can appear to offer some advantages to students, it is most likely to
appeal to students of color and those with lower academic achievement. I believe that these students
would be better served with improved academic programs and options. That is what public schools
should be about. I believe that military service could be easily sought out by students who are determined
to enter the service, but making it such an attractive option to impressionable students without any other
side of the story being presented, can lead students into a life of suffering and regret dealing with the
unspoken reality of engaging in combat that is the true work of the military. Let's keep our students
reaching for the best they can be. We must also consider the fact that service in the American military can
involve students in cooperating with political objectives that they little understand or would not choose to
support if educated about. Although hard to admit, military objectives of the United States have often
involved direct suppression of indigenous aspirations for justice and betterment of life (e. g., Vietnam,
Iraq, etc.).
