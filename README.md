# pps-rightsize

Research archive and analysis of **Portland Public Schools' rightsizing process**: the district's plan to close and consolidate schools, change grade configurations and redraw boundaries. It also covers **the seven board members who will decide it**.

As of **October 7, 2026**. Everything here comes from public sources: board meeting videos and materials, district documents, state campaign finance records and news coverage. The analysis is independent and not affiliated with PPS.

**Based on:**
- **220 board and committee meetings** (Jul 2023 – Oct 2026): about 444 hours of video, fully transcribed.
- **296 board meeting records** from BoardBook: agendas, minutes and **2,562 attachments**.
- **118 district documents** from pps.net: scenarios, forecasts, budgets, facility plans and policies.
- **173 news articles and posts** from 19 outlets and groups.
- **Campaign finance records** for the six directors who filed (Sullivan raised too little to file), plus one PAC: 8 state committees, about 1,300 transactions.
- **About 380 sources on individual board members** (bios, voters' pamphlets, campaign sites, questionnaires, coverage).
- **About 490 timestamped, verbatim meeting excerpts**, each attributed to a director with a confidence level.
- **145 research studies and reports (112 peer-reviewed)** on closures, consolidation, grade configuration, school size, costs and process.

## Where things stand

PPS is shrinking and running out of money.
- **Enrollment** was 42,622 in fall 2025 and is forecast to fall to about 36,800 by 2035-36. Elementary enrollment is down 28% in ten years.
- **Budget:** after cutting 336 positions to close a $56M gap for 2026-27, the district projects a **$65M+ gap for 2027-28**.
- **Buildings:** district buildings are 68% full overall.

**How the board set this up**
- **April 14, 2026:** launched rightsizing with Resolution 7296 (**5–2**; La Forte and Engelsman no).
- **May 26:** adopted guiding principles (Resolution 7307, **7–0**).
- **Size targets:** at least 360 students per elementary school, 500 per K-8 or middle school, 1,100 per high school. These come from the state's Quality Education Model, a *funding prototype* that also assumes about 20 students per class. No research sets a minimum school size.

**The two scenarios released October 5, 2026**

| | Scenario A | Scenario B |
|---|---|---|
| Closures | 14 (13 elementary schools + Sellwood MS) | 11 (spares Stephenson, Rose City Park and Lewis) |
| Boundary changes | 38 | 34 |
| K-8 students who change schools | 27% | 24% |

Both scenarios also:
- turn seven K-8s into K-5s;
- close MLC's high school grades;
- move Odyssey;
- relocate several immersion programs;
- end Sunnyside's environmental focus option.

A status-quo option also exists.

## What the research says

145 studies and reports (112 peer-reviewed) from the US and abroad. There is no formal meta-analysis of school closures; the findings below come from reviews and quasi-experimental studies.

- **Displaced students:** closures usually do little for test scores on average, with a short-term dip that starts when closures are announced. Students gain only if they move to a clearly better school, which is least likely when, as in PPS, adequate schools are closing.
- **Receiving schools:** effects range from none to harm that grows with the number of newcomers and with travel distance.
- **K-8 vs. middle school:** moving to a middle school in grade 6 lowered achievement in NYC and Florida, with lasting losses, more dropout and no high-school payoff. This runs against the plan's seven K-8 → K-5 conversions.
- **Savings:** real but modest, and usually below projections (DC's 2008 closures cost $39.5M against $9.7M first reported). Most savings come from staff cuts, not buildings.
- **Equity:** closures fall disproportionately on Black and low-income communities.
- **Boundary changes alone** cause little disruption.
- **Closed buildings:** often sit vacant for years. Over 40% of reused ones went to charter schools.
- **Board statements vs. research:** some directors' claims hold up and some don't. The literature summary checks 20 of them against the studies. The biggest gap is Wang's assertion, based on unpublished 2017-era district data, that K-8s prepare students worse for high school.

## Sunnyside Environmental School

Both scenarios keep Sunnyside open but **end its environmental focus option**. It would become a neighborhood-only K-5, and its grades 6–8 would move to Hosford Middle School. The school is:
- **89% full**, against a 68% district average. Only three neighborhood schools below high school are fuller.
- **Up 8.5% in two years**, against −2.9% district-wide.
- **Forecast flat through 2035**, against −13.6% overall.
- **Cheaper per student than most** PPS schools.
- **A working K-8,** where the strongest peer-reviewed studies favor keeping students through grade 8.

Asked why Sunnyside was chosen, staff called it "arbitrary."

## How this was built

- **Meetings:**
  - Captions from the board's YouTube channel (@ppsboardofeducation) were downloaded and cleaned into readable transcripts.
  - One meeting had no captions (the Jan 30, 2026 retreat), so it was transcribed from audio with whisper.cpp.
- **Board materials:** scraped from BoardBook (PPS org 915), with text extracted from every PDF.
- **District documents:** from pps.net, with retired pages recovered from the Wayback Machine.
- **Campaign finance:** exported from Oregon's ORESTAR system through a browser.
- **News:** collected from about 20 local and national outlets.
- **Research literature:** found through scholarly databases (OpenAlex, Crossref, ERIC, Semantic Scholar, Unpaywall). Each item has its own summary and is labeled peer-reviewed or not. Open-access full text was saved where available.
- **Analysis:** done by reading the transcripts against the votes and documents. All 1,326 quoted fragments in the per-director excerpt files were machine-checked word-for-word against the transcripts, and quotes in the top-level documents were spot-checked against sources.

## Caveats

- **Transcripts are auto-generated** and have no speaker labels. Attributions are marked **confirmed**, **probable** or **possible**. Check important quotes against the linked video timestamp.
- **Leanings and vote predictions are inferences** from public-role evidence: endorsements, donors, votes and statements. No voter files, home addresses, family details or personal social media were used.
- **Personnel matters are left out on purpose.** That includes the superintendent's evaluation and contract.
- **Private individuals:** members of the public are described by role in the analysis files. Raw transcripts reflect the public record as recorded.
- **Research effect sizes** for a few paywalled studies come from the authors' own summaries; each summary file flags this.

## Refreshing the data

Each scraper skips work it has already done, so reruns are incremental.

```sh
python3 scripts/boardbook_scrape.py                 # new BoardBook meetings + attachments
yt-dlp --skip-download --write-auto-subs --sub-langs en --sub-format vtt \
  -o "transcripts/raw_vtt/%(upload_date)s__%(id)s__%(title).120B.%(ext)s" \
  "https://www.youtube.com/@ppsboardofeducation/videos"
python3 scripts/vtt_to_text.py                      # rebuild transcripts/text
python3 scripts/sunnyside_rankings.py               # recompute school rankings
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install openpyxl
.venv/bin/python scripts/summarize_finance.py       # after a new ORESTAR export
```

ORESTAR blocks scripted access, so exports come from a real browser.
