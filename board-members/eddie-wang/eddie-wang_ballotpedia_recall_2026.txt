An effort to recall Eddie Wang from his position representing Zone 7 on the Portland Public Schools school board is underway in Oregon. The prospective petition was filed on September 18, 2026, after a draft audit of a property tax bond became public.[1][2]
Recall supporters
Former Portland Public Schools school board member Mike Rosen is organizing the recall effort. The reasons given for the recall effort are:[1]
| “ | Eddie Wang has violated the public trust and his financial responsibilities and is no longer fit to serve as Zone 7, school board director. As the Chair of the Portland Public Schools Board, he oversaw the award of a $62 million contract to Texas consulting firm, Procedeo. The contract is for the management of $1.5 billion. This money is for the rebuild of Cleveland, Ida B. Wells, and Jefferson High Schools, as well as the Center for Black Excellence. Wang oversaw the discussion of the contract, refused to allow dissenting voices to speak and has shown no interest in monitoring the contract. Recently an outside audit found: 1. The district has overpaid the consultant more than $700,000. 2. There was favoritism and bias in the way the board selected Procedeo. 3. The contract disproportionately favors the contractor. The contract gives Procedeo a $6 million bonus just for doing their job. 4. The school board did not adequately scrutinize the contract. Why you should Care: 35% of your property tax dollars go to PPS and 25% of that goes to the bond. Wang is wasting your tax dollars![3] | ” | 
Recall opponents
Regarding the recall effort, Wang has said, "Unfortunately, the bulk of these allegations are unsubstantiated and purely conjecture at best" and "I stand by the board systems, oversight, and leadership I have developed, all of which have been based on nationally studied best practices for school board governance."[2]
Path to the ballot
  - See also: Laws governing recall in Oregon
No specific grounds are required for recall in Oregon. To get the recall on the ballot, supporters must collect signatures equal to 15% of the votes cast in the last regular gubernatorial election in the relevant jurisdiction. Signatures must be collected within 90 days.[4]
Organizers would need to submit 34,950 valid signatures by December 17, 2026, to trigger a recall election.[5]
