Title: Eddie Wang for PPS Board
Author: None
Published: 2011-02-02
Fetched: 2026-10-07

The voice we need.
With 20 years as a classroom teacher, and now a business owner and parent of a student in PPS, Eddie has the experience needed to push for change that works.
Prioritizing Effective Strategies
With 20 years of teaching experience, and 10 of those years working in PPS, I have seen countless numbers of administrative decisions and policies, and I have seen firsthand how they actually turn out on the ground. I have seen some that were very effective in improving students’ lives, and I have seen many that became eventual wastes of time and money. In these times of dwindling resources, we need to prioritize methods that have proven track records of actually working, focusing on strategies that actually make a positive difference in our students’ lives and the working conditions in our schools.
Eddie’s Top Priorities
Improve education for all students, especially among students that have been historically disadvantaged, as well as improving the working conditions for all teachers, staff, and administrators in PPS.
Working towards racial equity
I support the Portland Public Schools’ stated goals for racial equity and social justice. However, this is not enough. In my experience with PPS, I have seen them take two steps forward, then two steps back. We need to do better with our students of color, especially with our historically underserved populations. I will work towards focusing resources where they are most effective. There are many school districts across the country that have had more success than us - we need to learn from them, use evidence-based methods and strategies, and not try to re-invent the wheel when it’s not needed. We need to not accept the status quo, and change the system so that every student has the support and opportunities to excel.
Improving our schools’ working conditions
The high turnover rate at PPS isn’t just a personnel issue, but also a fiscal issue - millions of dollars are wasted each year as a result of the lost institutional knowledge when those with experience leave. This culture needs to change.
One of the most common complaints among those that work in PPS is that the leadership in the central office seems out of touch, that everything seems very top-down. This needs to flip. Who knows our communities the best? It is our teachers, our school administrators, our school staff, and our students. Their voices should be the dominant voice when determining the top priorities of our district.
We also need to make it a top priority to support and adequately staff teachers and paraeducators in special education. This is not only the right thing to do, but it’s the law!
