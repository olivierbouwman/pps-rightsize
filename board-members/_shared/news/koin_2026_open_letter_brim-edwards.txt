Title: Current, former PPS board members lambast Brim-Edwards’ run for Multnomah County chair
Author: Jashayla Pettigrew
Published: 2026-07-30
Fetched: 2026-10-07

PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) — Current and former board members for Oregon’s largest public school district have lambasted a local official’s run for leader of Multnomah County.
In an open letter released on Wednesday, seven incumbent and ex-members of the Portland Public Schools Board of Education expressed concern that Multnomah County Commissioner Julia Brim-Edwards is not fit for the role of county chair. She announced her campaign for the role in February, after nearly three years of serving as commissioner — and after previously serving on the PPS board.
Oversight, refusal to make decisions and a lack of collaboration and leadership were issues during Brim Edwards’ tenure, according to the letter. It was signed by the school board’s current Chair Eddie Wang and Vice Chair Michelle DePass signed onto the letter, in addition to former members Scott Bailey, Amy Kohnstamm, Eilidh Lowery, Rita Moore and Andrew Scott.
“Governing bodies function best when their members share trust, communicate openly, and work through disagreements constructively,” the letter reads, in part. “Instead of engaging in honest and candid public debate on critical issues, we learned that on multiple occasions, Ms. Brim-Edwards privately lobbied against Board and District positions with public officials and community members, jeopardizing the integrity of District budgets and the effective pursuit of long-term improvement strategies.”
The letter went on to question whether Brim-Edwards acted ethically when she took a temporary role with the Oregon Secretary of State’s Office while it was auditing PPS. Her former colleagues additionally accused her of “counter-productive micro-managing, creating a toxic environment that undermined the district’s functioning.”
Brim-Edwards also “deferred difficult decisions” during her time on the board, therefore disrupting the efforts of fellow members, according to the letter.
But in a statement to KOIN 6, she negated the claims from her former colleagues.
“My record on the PPS Board for 12 years speaks for itself: better learning outcomes, additional funding for teachers and classroom time, extra assistance for struggling students and equal access to sports facilities,” Brim-Edwards said. “I worked hand-in-hand with principals, teachers, nutrition workers, bus drivers, coaches, students, parents, and board members to make these meaningful improvements for our students — that will stand the test of time.”
Her campaign also shared statements of support from former PPS board member Mike Rosen and current member Virginia La Forte, with La Forte saying the chair candidate “has a laser-like focus on delivering results, and she doesn’t waste time on political distractions like this one.”
Brim-Edwards and fellow Multnomah County Commissioner Shannon Singleton — alongside former commissioner Sharon Meieran — are now running to replace Chair Jessica Vega Pederson.
Brim-Edwards has listed the local drug deflection program, homelessness and federal funding cuts among her top concerns if she were to be elected. Her run has been endorsed by every mayor in the county, including Portland’s Keith Wilson.
