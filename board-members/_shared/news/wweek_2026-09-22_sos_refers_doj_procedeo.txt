Title: Secretary of State Refers Portland Public Schools to Department of Justice for Criminal Investigation Over Procedeo Contract
Author: Joanna Hou
Published: 2026-09-22
Fetched: 2026-10-07

Oregon Secretary of State Tobias Read on Monday referred an investigation his office began into Portland Public Schools to the Oregon Department of Justice and the Oregon Government Ethics Commission. In a referral letter to OGEC that WW obtained through a public records request, Read’s deputy, Michael Kaplan, cited potential civil violations of two state laws and noted he’d referred the matter to ODOJ, which handles criminal investigations.
Kaplan wrote that Read’s office had launched an investigation into PPS’s dealings with Procedeo, a Texas-based construction firm overseeing the district’s bond services. In December, the Portland School Board voted 5–2 to approve a $61.5 million contract with Procedeo for five years of overseeing program management services, which include the timely delivery of three high school modernizations and the Grice Adair Center, formerly known as the Center for Black Student Excellence.
The Secretary of State’s Office launched its investigation in response to a report made to the state’s government waste hotline, Kaplan wrote. The office has given records associated with its preliminary investigation to the Oregon Department of Justice in accordance with state law, the letter notes.
A section of the state law cited states: “If the secretary determines during the investigation that fraud or other criminal activity may be occurring or may have occurred, the secretary shall notify the appropriate law enforcement agency of the potential fraud or other criminal activity.”
The ethics commission is being alerted, Kaplan writes in the letter, because the secretary of state suspects potential civil violations of state law around how public officials might stand to benefit from public contracts. The state laws prohibit the use of one’s official public position for financial gain.
“Our initial investigation found documentation to support potential violations of ORS 244.040 and ORS 244.047, which makes clear that public officials may not use or attempt to use their official position to obtain financial gain for themselves or any business with which the public official is associated,” Kaplan’s letter reads.
The letter is vague in identifying any public officials involved, or what the particular offenses within the flagged statutes might be.
A spokesperson with Portland Public Schools tells WW the district “takes seriously our responsibility to be good stewards of public resources and to maintain the trust of the communities we serve.”
“We are committed to transparency, accountability and the highest standards of ethical conduct,” the spokesperson says. “We will cooperate fully with the appropriate state agencies as they review these matters.”
Last week, WW first reported that PPS’s external bond auditor had, in a draft audit, warned that the district’s Procedeo contract had heightened the district’s risk for fraud. Among many findings, the bond auditor found risks around favoritism and bias as PPS selected the firm.
Auditors cataloged several instances in which PPS, ahead of contract solicitation and awarding, communicated with Procedeo about its intentions and plans around the proposed services and how the firm could help PPS. “Although PPS asserted that it talked with other consultants on similar topics, PPS did not provide documentary evidence to support this assertion,” the audit noted.
It also found that the district’s request for proposals process was also potentially subject to bias, pointing to scoring practices by certain evaluators as one example.
