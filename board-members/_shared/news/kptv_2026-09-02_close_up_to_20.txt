Title: Portland Public Schools board weighs plan to close up to 20 schools
Author: Mikhala Armstrong
Published: 2026-09-02
Fetched: 2026-10-07

Portland Public Schools board weighs plan to close up to 20 schools
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - As Portland Public Schools faces a major budget deficit, the board weighed closing up to 20 schools in the district on Tuesday. The move has been met with a lot of concerns and questions from the community.
Up to 20 Portland Public Schools could close under a controversial district plan weighed by board members on Tuesday.
“I recognize that our current path is not sustainable but I’m far from convinced that rightsizing is the best or only step we can take,” said Matt Coolidge.
“It looks like we only remember we’re in a financial crisis when it’s time to cut classrooms, close schools, lay off educators, or bargain with the people doing the work, and that’s not the leadership I want to see,” said Portland Association of Teachers President Alisha Chavez.
The district is calling the plan its “rightsizing initiative” driven by declining enrollment and rising costs.
“That was kind of a shock,” said Chavez.
Chavez said the plan will have a devastating impact.
“When you’re closing 20 schools, your potentially losing 20 admins, and potentially losing all the staff that work within those schools, students will potentially be absorbed within those schools that have smaller class sizes, so it will cause this ripple effect for students,” said Chavez.
PPS is facing a $50 million shortfall, but some argue the district has other options.
“This goes far beyond schools. This goes to the sustainability and livability of Portland, without a bedrock of good Portland schools it’s going to be a huge problem in the long term and I don’t think that’s being weighed enough right now,” said Coolidge.
Adding fuel to the fire, the board also weighed a raise for Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong.
Bumping her salary to nearly $345,000 and extending her contract through 2029.
“It is truly one of the most tone deaf discussions I have seen in a long time,” said Coolidge.
Which is why some said in a time where every dollar matters, the board’s priorities are off.
“There’s a lack of trust within PPS, with educators, with staff, with families, and this is not creating more trust,” said Chavez.
