Portland Public Schools approves major contract after fiery exchange among board members
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — A fiery debate over Portland Public Schools awarding a Texas-based company with a contract to manage more than a billion dollars of construction projects erupted Tuesday among board members.
The board officially hired the firm Procedeo to manage the renovations of three schools and the long-promised Center for Black Student Excellence.
The contract comes with a $6 million bonus if the company finishes the project on time.
"Since when do we give anyone a $6 million bonus for completing work on time?" Keisha Locklear, senior project manager, said. "It just seems highly irregular at minimum."
"The bond program management under one contract will improve cost control and reduce escalation,” PPS Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said. "Procedeo will be held accountable for schedule, cost, scope and quality. Tonight is not about blame, it's about responsibility. It's about restoring trust."
The three high schools -- Cleveland, Jefferson and Ida B. Wells -- are in some of Portland’s most diverse neighborhoods and many feel the renovations have been long overdue.
The crowd urged the board to vote, and after more than an hour of debate, the board passed the resolution 5-2.
Shortly after, Brian Johnson, President of Procedeo, spoke to reporters and promised transparency and accountability.
“We have a lot of transparency as part of our culture, trust, and we're going to earn the trust of the community. We're going to work really hard in it, and we're going to deliver,” Johnson said.
