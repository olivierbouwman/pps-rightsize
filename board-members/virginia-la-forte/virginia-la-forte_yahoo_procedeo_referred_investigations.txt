Title: Portland Public Schools contract referred for potential criminal, ethics investigations
Author: Jamie Goldberg

Portland Public Schools contract referred for potential criminal, ethics investigations
Portland Public Schools' woes over the five-year, $61.5 million contract it signed with a Texas-based firm to manage three massive high school modernization projects keep piling up.
The latest twist: Secretary of State Tobias Read's office has referred concerns about the contract to the Oregon Government Ethics Commission and the Oregon Department of Justice for potential ethics and criminal investigations, respectively. Willamette Week first reported the news earlier this week.
In his referral letter to the ethics commission, Michael Kaplan, deputy secretary of state, wrote that the office's initial investigation into a complaint made to its government waste hotline found documentation to support potential violations of Oregon law which "make clear that public officials may not use or attempt to use their official position to obtain financial gain for themselves or any business with which the public official is associated."
The referral comes after the release this month of a damning external audit that excoriated the school district for its dealings with Procedeo, the construction program management company hired to oversee $1.4 billion in modernization work at Cleveland, Jefferson and Wells high schools, plus upgrades to a building purchased as the new home for the Center for Black Student Excellence, now known as the Grice-Adair Center.
In that sprawling, 103-page document, which is still in draft form and has yet to be publicly released, auditors with Sjoberg Evashenk Consulting found that the district was at a heightened risk of fraud due to imprecise financial reporting, the use of misleading information to justify hiring Procedeo and concerning patterns in its contracting and procurement practices.
In response to the Secretary of State's referral, Valerie Feder, a spokesperson for the district, said "Portland Public Schools takes seriously our responsibility to be good stewards of public resources and to maintain the trust of the communities we serve. We are committed to transparency, accountability and the highest standards of ethical conduct. We will cooperate fully with the appropriate state agencies as they review these matters."
The referral comes nine months after a former employee within a district department, then known as the Office of School Modernization, first filed a complaint with the Secretary of State's government waste hotline. (Almost all of the Office of School Modernization's employees have since been fired or quit; it is now known as the Real Estate, Infrastructure and School Enhancement Department, or RISE, and is staffed mainly by Procedeo employees.)
The Oregon Government Ethics Commission has 60 days from when it receives a complaint to decide whether to open an investigation. A spokesperson for the Department of Justice said there was no timeline for when a decision would be made about whether it would open a formal investigation or dismiss the matter.
"We've assigned an attorney and an investigator to review it," said spokesperson Jenny Hansson, via email. "Once they've done that initial review, they will make recommendations to our Criminal Justice team about next steps."
In his referral letter to the ethics commission, Kaplan did not specify what documentation the Secretary of State's Office found to support potential violations. A spokesperson for the Secretary of State's Office said it couldn't provide additional details about its review at this time.
More Oregon education stories
Portland Public Schools board members voted last December during a tense meeting to award the $61.5 million contract to Procedeo. The vote was 5-2, with board members Virginia La Forte and Stephanie Engelsman casting the "no" votes.
Former student representative Ian Ritorto also cast a "no" vote. Student representatives serve one-year terms on the board and their votes are non-binding.
The Oregonian/OregonLive has previously reported that Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong's adult daughter was hired to work as an office manager at Procedeo. Armstrong told the news outlet that her daughter got the position on her own and that Armstrong was not informed until after the hiring process was complete.
Susan Myers, executive director of the Oregon Government Ethics Commission, previously told the news outlet that there would only be a potential conflict of interest if Armstrong's daughter had been hired before the vote on the contract because of state laws that prohibit public employees from making decisions or recommendations that might result in the financial gain of themselves, their relatives or anyone from their household.
The Oregonian/OregonLive also previously reported that two former school board members who helped connect the firm to the school district, but did not vote on the contract, are now under a monthly contract with Procedeo as consultants. The two former board members have previously said there is nothing untoward in their agreement with Procedeo and that their intent was to help ensure transparency for the three high schools projects.
This week also brought more churn among high-level employees at Portland Public Schools with the departure of Tom Odgers, the district's chief of integrated operations, who oversaw maintenance issues outside the scope of the construction bonds passed by voters.
