Title: Portland Public Schools board votes to pass $2B budget with 200+ job cuts
Author: Aimee Plante; Joelle Jones
Published: 2025-06-11
Fetched: 2026-10-07

PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) – After months of deliberation, the Portland Public Schools board voted to pass its budget proposal for the 2025-2026 school year.
The $2 billion budget is the largest in Oregon’s history. It includes more than 200 job cuts to staff — including more than 60 positions at the central office — as part of an effort to close a $40 million budget gap.
Under the new budget, the district says core academic programs, arts and athletics would be protected, but board members expressed concerns over maintaining outdoor school days and the development of the Center for Black Student Excellence amid the shortfall.
The budget passed on a 5-2 decision Tuesday evening. School board members say the state is once again underfunding public education, and forcing them to do more with less.
“This budget reflects not the values that I think we as a district have — the values that we as a board have — but a reality that we’re stuck with,” PPS board member Christy Split said. “That we’re making cuts to schools that we know need more resources, that we’re losing valuable team members.”
This is the third straight year the district has faced a deficit, in part due to a decline in enrollment. PPS receives funding on a per-student basis. District officials previously told KOIN 6 News that its schools have also been impacted by rising costs.
Back in May, voters passed a $1.8 billion bond measure that teachers said would help reduce proposed cuts. The bond would also go toward modernizing Cleveland, Jefferson and Ida B. Wells high schools.
