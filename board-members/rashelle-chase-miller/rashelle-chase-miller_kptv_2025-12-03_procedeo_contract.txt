Title: PPS board approves contract for long-awaited school modernization projects
Author: Mikhala Armstrong
Published: 2025-12-03
Fetched: 2026-10-07

PPS board approves contract for long-awaited school modernization projects
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - The Portland Public Schools board voted to approve a contract that would manage multiple long-awaited projects within the district.
Those include the modernization of three schools, including Cleveland, Jefferson, and Ida B. Wells high schools, and the district’s planned Center for Black Student Excellence.
However, it wasn’t easy getting the vote done.
After years of waiting for the modernization of Cleveland, Jefferson, and Ida B. Wells high schools, tensions couldn’t help but boil over at Tuesday night’s board meeting, from the crowd yelling directly at the board, to the board sparring with each other, causing one board member to leave the room in tears.
Yet, even with the tension, the board ultimately made a decision.
The board is taking a step forward toward making a long-awaited promise a reality by voting to approve a contract with Texas-based Procedeo, which will serve as the management firm responsible for overseeing the modernization of each school and the district’s Center for Black Student Excellence, which has been delayed for five years.
“We’re seeing a big system try to learn from its mistakes, pivot, but you know you can’t turn a huge ship, in just one wheel turn, it’s going to take some time, so I’m hoping that this new approach is going to show us different results,” said Portland Association of Teachers President Angela Bonilla.
The contract with Procedeo will cost more than $61 million, and the agreement between PPS and Procedeo would be from December 10, 2025, to December 9, 2030, with an option to renew for up to two additional two-year terms through December 2034.
Procedeo President Brian Johnson said with this contract, he plans to give PPS students the schools they deserve.
“We appreciate their passion, and we welcome them holding us accountable, we take pride in that, we have a lot of transparency, it’s a part of our culture, trust, and we’re going to earn the trust of this community. We’re going to work really hard at it, and we’re going to deliver,” said Johnson.
Though some have some hesitancy after years of waiting, Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong stands firm that this contract is what is needed to end this waiting game.
“Our community voted for these bonds, our students walk into these buildings, and our job, my job, is to make sure every dollar is managed responsibly, and every project moves forward with clarity, accountability, and urgency. Our students cannot wait, and they shouldn’t have to,” said Armstrong.
The board assured the crowd that PPS can cancel the contract at any time with 30 days’ notice, but Johnson said he plans to deliver.
