Portland Public Schools board to discuss 15 potential elementary school closures
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — The Portland Public Schools Board is set to discuss the potential closure of 15 elementary schools Tuesday night as Oregon’s largest school district considers major cuts amid declining enrollment.
The discussion comes as PPS weighs how to reduce its footprint and better align the number of schools it operates with the number of students it serves.
A Portland State University enrollment forecast projects PPS enrollment will fall roughly 15% over the next decade or so.

Some individual schools have already experienced much steeper declines. Lent Elementary has lost about 51% of its enrollment over the past decade, while Arleta Elementary has lost about 50%.
At the same time, district board materials show PPS has added two school buildings since the 2018-19 school year.
Those trends raise questions about whether maintaining the district’s current footprint is sustainable and how PPS should determine which schools could ultimately close.
PPS Board Member Rashelle Chase-Miller says the enrollment decline has put the district in an increasingly difficult position as it tries to provide resources and services for students.
“We’re already a school district stretched thin when it comes to resources, and that will continue and actually be exacerbated as time goes on if we don’t make any changes to the way that we operate,” Chase-Miller said.
The district is considering school closures and consolidations. Chase-Miller says those changes could create an opportunity to place students in school buildings that are in better condition.
She says PPS has worked to avoid teacher layoffs, but that has meant other needs have gone unfunded. Many district school buildings, she says, are more than 100 years old.
Chase-Miller pointed to last fall, when schools had to close because of heat, as an example of the challenges facing aging buildings.
Before making a decision, Chase-Miller says she will be looking for a plan that protects student services.
“I want all of our schools to have a librarian that’s full time. I want all of our schools to have art teachers, counselors, social workers,” she said. “What I’ll be looking for is a plan that guarantees those things for as many students as possible.”
She also says any plan must be equitable — providing a high standard of access, opportunity and experience for all students while ensuring historically marginalized students are not left behind.
The Portland Public Schools Board is scheduled to discuss the issue Tuesday evening at PPS district headquarters.
