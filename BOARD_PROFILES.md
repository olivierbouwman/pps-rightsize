# Portland Public Schools Board of Education: Director Profiles for the Rightsizing Decision

*Compiled 2026-10-07; revised the same day to incorporate the full meeting-transcript review for January 2025 – October 2026 (see `BOARD_STATEMENTS_BY_PERIOD.md` and §7). Every file path is relative to the repository root. Most sources are saved twice: the raw file (`.html` or `.pdf`) and a cleaned `.txt` copy. Political-orientation calls are **inferences**. Each one is labeled with a confidence level and the evidence behind it, and rests only on public-role material: endorsements, donors, stated positions, affiliations the director lists themselves, and votes. I did not check voter registration, home addresses, family members or personal social media. I also left out superintendent evaluations and other personnel matters.*

### 2.1 Eddie Wang — Zone 7, Board Chair
Folder: `board-members/eddie-wang/`

**Background.**
- Classroom teacher from 2001 to 2019: about 6 years in North Clackamas, then about 10 at PPS, most recently teaching biology and physics at Benson Polytechnic.
- University supervisor at PSU's College of Education, 2018–21. Has run a musical-instrument business since 2019.
- BA from Carleton College; MEd from Lewis & Clark.
- Parent representative on the Southeast Guiding Coalition before joining the board.
- Sources: `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_pps_bio.txt`, `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_voters_pamphlet_may2023.txt`.

**How elected and coalition.**
- Won Zone 7 on May 16, 2023, effectively unopposed: 98.3%, 70,885 votes (`board-members/_shared/ballotpedia_pps_elections.txt`).
- 2023 endorsements:
  - Teachers union: **PAT PAC**.
  - Education and party groups: **Stand for Children**, **Willamette Women Democrats**.
  - Community and identity PACs: APANO, Unite Oregon Action, Oregon Futures Lab/Color PAC.
  - Building trades: Columbia Pacific Building Trades Council, Laborers Local 737.
  - Current and former directors: Kohnstamm, Andrew Scott, Lowery, Hollands, Brim-Edwards, Knowles, Rosen, Rita Moore.
  - Officials: City Commissioner Dan Ryan; Reps. Neron and Gamba.
  - Sources: `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_voters_pamphlet_may2023.txt`, `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_pat_pac_endorsements_2023.txt`, `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_uniteoregon_2023_endorsements.txt`, `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_wweek_2023-04-26_may2023_endorsements.txt`.
- 2023 platform:
  - Better outcomes for historically disadvantaged students.
  - Evidence-based racial-equity strategies ("not try to reinvent the wheel").
  - Reduce the turnover-driven loss of institutional knowledge.
  - PAT's endorsement praised him for being "ready to center teacher voices" and "holding district leadership accountable."
- Campaign site (Wayback): `board-members/eddie-wang/eddie-wang_campaign_site_wayback_20230517003055_home.txt`.
- In 2025 he endorsed all four winners (Splitt, Chase-Miller, La Forte and Engelsman).

**Donors (ORESTAR committee 22625).** $8,202 total, almost all in 2023. About $2.6k was self/family funding. Political committees gave the rest: Eilidh Lowery for Oregon $913, Communities of Color for a Just Oregon (Color PAC) $600, Renee for the Future $500, Friends of Matt Gaitan $300, PAT PAC only $250, and Friends of Amy Kohnstamm $200. ## 3. Board blocs and dynamics

Synthesis and inference, based on the votes and statements cited above, now covering the full transcript record from January 2025 to October 2026.

**A. Three different alignments, depending on the issue.**

*0. Direction (whether to consolidate at all).* **No current director has opposed school consolidation in principle at any point in the record.** All four directors then serving voted for the 2025-26 cuts (6/10/2025). All seven voted for ending Jefferson dual assignment (Res. 7228) and for the rightsizing guiding principles (Res. 7307, 7-0). The only no votes on launching the process (Res. 7296), La Forte's and Engelsman's, were explained on process grounds: no framework, and no need for a resolution. The splits that matter for December are about **process, magnitude and conditions**, not direction.

*1. Governance and leadership* (Procedeo, board role, audit response, process amendments):

| Bloc | Members | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership bloc | **Wang, DePass, Chase-Miller** | "Unified board" that backs the superintendent. Yes on Procedeo. Opposed expanding audit capacity (9/29). Against La Forte's 7296 framework (4/14). Chase-Miller seconded 7296, moved 7307, and is the hand-picked vice chair. |
| With leadership on money; independent on process | **Sullivan, Splitt** | Yes on Procedeo and both budgets; against the reserve draw and the 2026 staff-restoration amendment. **But** Sullivan joined La Forte and Engelsman on **both** failed 7296 amendments (3-4 and 2-5), and both Sullivan and Splitt joined them on 9/29/2026 to add an internal bond auditor (Rec. 34, 4-3) and to strike the "budget availability" clause (4-3). Splitt co-designed the rightsizing process with Wang, wrote 7296's final Recital E, and on 7/7 (probable) questioned a third chair term. |
| Oversight and process dissenters | **La Forte, Engelsman** | No on Procedeo, no on the rightsizing launch (4/14), no on raising the contract threshold; frequent public friction with the chair. Engelsman filed to challenge Wang for chair (6/9), then withdrew. But both voted for Res. 7307, and La Forte seconded it. |

Two contested votes show the swing positions. On the 4/14/2026 framework amendment, Sullivan joined La Forte and Engelsman, and the amendment failed 3-4 because Splitt stayed with leadership. On bond oversight (9/29/2026), Splitt also joined them, producing a **4-3 majority of Splitt, Sullivan, La Forte and Engelsman against Wang, DePass and Chase-Miller**, the first documented case of the leadership bloc losing a contested vote. **Splitt is the pivotal fourth vote on process questions.** On money, the board is far less divided: Chase-Miller's 4% reserve draw drew only Engelsman (2-4-1), and Engelsman's restoration amendment failed 1-6.

*2. Closure magnitude* (from the 9/1 and 10/6 meetings, read against the earlier record):

| Position | Members |
|---|---|
| "Go big" | **Wang, DePass** (consistent since 2025). **Sullivan** ("going further"; open to closures since May 2025). **La Forte** ("big swings", with cost and neighborhood-desert conditions; argued for "consolidating at scale" in March 2026, probable). |
| Proceed, with conditions | **Chase-Miller** (a conditional yes that keeps the option to "hit the brakes"; she signalled closures to the MLC community in February 2026) |
| Skeptical of scale | **Splitt** ("scalpel"; a co-architect of the process whose skepticism is about how many schools, not whether), **Engelsman** ("mayhem"; boundaries first; not opposed in principle) |

*3. Cross-cutting issue: K-8s.* Both scenarios convert seven K-8s to K-5s. Wang cites data favoring middle schools, and Splitt relayed K-8 families "wishing that they were not K8s" (2025, probable). But La Forte reversed toward K-8s on the research (3/31/2026), Sullivan reported hearing mostly K-8 support at the sessions (probable), and Engelsman opposes split feeders. Expect amendments here even among likely yes votes.

**B. Likely December vote math (inference).** A majority for *some* closure package looks probable, and somewhat firmer than earlier evidence suggested. **Wang and DePass** are near-certain yes votes. **Sullivan** is a likely yes (her openness dates to May 2025). **Chase-Miller** is a conditional yes whose conditions (staffing guarantees, North and Northeast protection, building reuse) are negotiable within the 7307 framework. **La Forte** is more likely a yes than her April vote implied: that no was about process, she seconded 7307, and her remaining tests are fiscal-impact data and avoiding neighborhood "deserts". **Splitt** likely supports only a reduced list, and has said (probable) she will not support adding schools beyond those listed, which points to Scenario B or smaller. **Engelsman** remains the most likely no, though on the merits of this package rather than on principle. Overall, Scenario B (11 closures) or an amended list looks more achievable than Scenario A (14): a 5–6-vote majority for a reduced package is plausible, while Scenario A probably depends on holding La Forte and Splitt together. *Confidence: moderate.* Votes could shift with the 11/17 recommendation, the cost data, and PAT pressure.

**C. Electoral coalitions vs. governing behavior.**

## 7. Method and caveats

- **Full transcript review:** the profiles now incorporate the full review of board and committee meeting transcripts from **January 2025 to October 2026**. The four periods from January 2025 to August 2026 are summarized in `BOARD_STATEMENTS_BY_PERIOD.md`, and September–October 2026 is covered directly here. Verbatim, timestamped excerpts for each director are in `board-members/<director>/<director>_transcript_excerpts_<period>.txt` (periods `2025-01_06`, `2025-07_2026-02`, `2026-03_05`, `2026-06_08`, `2026-09_10`). Every corrections and nuances item listed in that file has been applied above, and every quote added in this revision was checked against the excerpt file cited. Chase-Miller, La Forte and Engelsman joined in July 2025, so their record starts there.
- **Meeting captions:** quotes from meeting videos come from YouTube auto-captions without speaker labels. The transcript excerpt files mark each attribution CONFIRMED, PROBABLE or POSSIBLE, and this document flags "probable" attributions where it relies on them. Those should be checked against the video at the given timestamp. The 1/30/2026 retreat is a local whisper transcription, and two 2025 TLE caption files read as machine re-translations.
- **Vote counts:** some come from BoardBook draft minutes, the rest from video. All 2026 votes were voice votes. Known record problems:
  - The 4/14/2026 draft minutes' summary line says La Forte's amendment "passed (4-yes, 3-no)"; their own roll call and the video show it failed 3-4. The minutes also record the "may" vote as having one abstention without naming anyone, and record La Forte voting no on "may" although she had proposed it.
  - The 1/13/2025 appointment minutes' per-round lists are internally inconsistent; the captions resolve them.
  - There are no minutes for 5/26/2026 or for June–August 2026; those votes come from caption roll calls. Individual votes on the 6/23 postponement motion and the 8/4 Creston resolution are not identifiable.
- **MLC high school:** the February 2026 discontinuation was a staff program change, not a board vote. It was reversed or deferred by early March (3/8 retreat remarks; WW 10/5/2026). The exact mechanism of the reversal is not in the archive and needs checking against March 2026 agendas and news.
- **Personal contact data:** the 2023 voters'-pamphlet filing PDFs were trimmed to the statement pages because page 1 carried personal contact details. Splitt's Oregon School Boards Association (OSBA) questionnaire was kept only as a redacted text copy.
- **Out of scope:**
  - **Excluded:** superintendent evaluation and contract-extension discussions (personnel matters), the 6/10/2025 evaluation resolution, the 3/31/2026 board self-evaluation (mined only for rightsizing remarks), personnel-specific parts of the 2026 auditor-office debate, the 9/16/2026 retreat (evaluation-focused), directors' remarks about their own children's schools or teachers, and the names of private members of the public.
  - **Not used:** voter files, addresses, family details beyond "PPS parent", and personal social media.
- **Stale official data:** pps.net still lists DePass as Vice-Chair. BoardBook notices from September 2026 onward and the meeting videos show Chase-Miller as vice chair since 7/7/2026.
