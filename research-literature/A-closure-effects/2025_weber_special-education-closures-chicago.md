# Disposable Spaces: How Special Education Enrollment Affects School Closures

**Citation:** Weber, R. N., Waitoller, F. R., & Drucker, J. M. (2025). Disposable spaces: How special education enrollment affects school closures. *Urban Education, 60*(1), 154-184. (Online first 2022.)

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00420859221126700

**Type:** Descriptive / quantitative (simulation of closure decisions)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Chicago Public Schools, 2013 mass closure of 44 of 402 elementary schools (underutilization criterion).

**Sample:** 402 CPS elementary schools.

**Method:** Logistic regression simulating the district's administrative decision parameters, using demographic, student and school data.

## Key findings

- CPS was more likely to close buildings with higher shares of students with IEPs.
- Mechanism: building "utilization" formulas do not account for the extra instructional space that special education programs need, so schools serving many students with disabilities can look under-used.
- Students with disabilities, particularly in low-income neighborhoods of color, were left with fewer educational options.

## Limitations

- Examines which schools were selected for closure, not student outcomes after closure.
- Single district.

## Relevance to PPS

Highly relevant to PPS: if PPS ranks schools by capacity utilization, the formula should count special-education classrooms and related-service space correctly, or schools with large IEP populations may be over-selected for closure.
