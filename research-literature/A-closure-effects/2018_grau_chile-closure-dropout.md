# School Closure and Educational Attainment: Evidence from a Market-Based System

**Citation:** Grau, N., Hojman, D., & Mizala, A. (2018). School closure and educational attainment: Evidence from a market-based system. *Economics of Education Review, 65*, 1-17.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2018.05.003

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Chile, 2002-2011; 1,282 schools closed (about one-tenth of the stock) in a voucher-based market system.

**Sample:** Large administrative student panel.

**Method:** Panel identification of causal effects of closure on dropout and grade retention.

## Key findings

- School closure raised the probability of high-school dropout by 49-68 percent (1.8 to 2.5 percentage points).
- Closure raised the probability of grade retention in fifth grade by 3.9 to 4.4 percentage points.

## Limitations

- Market-driven closures (many private-voucher schools); context differs from a US public district.

## Relevance to PPS

Shows closure can affect attainment-related outcomes (dropout, retention), not only test scores. Reinforces the need to track PPS displaced students' persistence.
