# The Effect of School Closings on Student Achievement

**Citation:** Brummet, Q. (2014). The effect of school closings on student achievement. *Journal of Public Economics, 119*, 108-124.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.06.010

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Michigan, statewide; more than 200 school closings (2000s).

**Sample:** Displaced students and students in receiving schools across many districts.

**Method:** Student-level panel comparisons (event-study / difference-in-differences style) with heterogeneity by performance of the closed school.

## Key findings

- On average, closings did no persistent harm to the achievement of displaced students.
- Students displaced from relatively low-performing schools experienced achievement gains.
- The arrival of displaced students and teachers produced modest negative spillover effects on students in receiving schools.
- Because receiving schools hold many more students than closed schools, small per-student spillovers can add up to a meaningful aggregate cost.

## Limitations

- Effect sizes are not reproduced here because the full text is paywalled.
- Michigan closures mix enrollment-driven and performance-driven reasons; statewide data limit detail on implementation.

## Relevance to PPS

Key evidence on receiving-school spillovers, central for PPS where most affected students may be in schools absorbing others. Also shows average effects depend on the quality of the closed school relative to the receiving school.
