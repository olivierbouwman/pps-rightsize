# Extreme Measures: A National Descriptive Analysis of Closure and Restructuring of Traditional Public, Charter, and Private Schools

**Citation:** Harris, D. N., & Martinez Pabon, V. (2024). Extreme measures: A national descriptive analysis of closure and restructuring of traditional public, charter, and private schools. *Education Finance and Policy, 19*(1), 32-60. (Online first 2022.)

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00386

**Type:** Descriptive (national)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** United States, 1991-2019 (best data 2014-18).

**Sample:** Near-census of K-12 schools in all sectors.

**Method:** Descriptive statistics and regressions predicting closure and restructuring.

## Key findings

- Annual closure rates in 2014-18: 5.1 percent for charter, 2.9 percent for private, 0.9 percent for traditional public schools.
- The strongest predictor of closure is low enrollment (especially for private schools); in charter and traditional public schools, achievement predicts closure nearly as strongly as enrollment.
- Racial and income composition are weaker predictors, but they still predict closure after many controls, raising equity concerns.

## Limitations

- Descriptive; no student-outcome effects.

## Relevance to PPS

Places PPS in national context: enrollment-driven closure of traditional public schools is the most common kind of closure, yet most causal outcome studies are about performance-driven closures.
