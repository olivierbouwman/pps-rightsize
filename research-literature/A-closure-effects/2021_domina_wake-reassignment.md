# The Kids on the Bus: The Academic Consequences of Diversity-Driven School Reassignments

**Citation:** Domina, T., Carlson, D., Carter, J., Lenard, M., McEachin, A., & Perera, R. (2021). The kids on the bus: The academic consequences of diversity-driven school reassignments. *Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, 40*(4), 1197-1229.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.22326 (OA author manuscript: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9286063/)

**Type:** Quasi-experimental (event study)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Wake County Public School System, North Carolina, 2000-2010 socioeconomic diversity reassignments.

**Sample:** About 25 percent of students were reassigned over the decade.

**Method:** Event-study design comparing reassigned students before and after reassignment with not-yet-reassigned students.

## Key findings

- Most reassigned students attended their new schools despite opt-out options.
- Reassignment modestly boosted math achievement and reduced suspension rates, with no offsetting negative effects on other outcomes (including attendance).
- Effects did not vary meaningfully by student characteristics.

## Limitations

- Reassignment for diversity in a growing district, not closure; schools of origin remained open.

## Relevance to PPS

Evidence that boundary changes, if carefully designed, need not impose academic or disciplinary costs. Relevant to PPS boundary redraws accompanying closures.
