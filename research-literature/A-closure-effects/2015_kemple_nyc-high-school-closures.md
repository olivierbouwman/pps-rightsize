# High School Closures in New York City: Impacts on Students' Academic Outcomes, Attendance, and Mobility

**Citation:** Kemple, J. J. (2015). *High school closures in New York City: Impacts on students' academic outcomes, attendance, and mobility*. New York: Research Alliance for New York City Schools, NYU. (Summary: Kemple, J. J., 2016, School closures in New York City, *Education Next*.)

**DOI / URL:** https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/research-alliance (ERIC ED573426)

**Type:** Report-not-peer-reviewed (comparative interrupted time series)

**Peer-reviewed:** N (the Education Next summary is editorially reviewed)

**Setting:** New York City; 29 low-performing high schools designated for closure 2002-2008 and phased out gradually.

**Sample:** About 20,600 students: those enrolled during phase-out and "post-closure" cohorts who would likely have enrolled.

**Method:** Comparative interrupted time series against other low-performing schools that were not closed.

## Key findings

- Phase-out had little systematic impact, positive or negative, on academic outcomes of students enrolled at the time (graduation net difference about +5 points, not significant).
- Phase-out increased student mobility by almost 10 percentage points, mostly through transfers to other NYC high schools rather than dropout.
- Post-closure cohorts who had to choose other schools enrolled in higher-performing schools, often new small schools, and their graduation rates rose by about 15.5 percentage points.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; performance-driven closures with gradual phase-out alongside opening of new small schools.

## Relevance to PPS

Shows that a gradual phase-out (no forced mid-course transfer) can protect current students, and that gains depend on better alternatives existing. PPS is using immediate consolidation, not phase-out.
