# Does Grade Configuration Matter? Effects of School Reorganisation on Pupils' Educational Experience

**Citation:** Holmlund, H., & Bohlmark, A. (2019). Does grade configuration matter? Effects of school reorganisation on pupils' educational experience. *Journal of Urban Economics, 109*, 14-26.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jue.2018.11.004

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Sweden; a policy change reorganising middle-school education so pupils stayed in small local schools for grades 1-9 instead of moving to large middle schools at grade 7.

**Sample:** Swedish pupils in affected and unaffected areas.

**Method:** Exploits the reorganisation as a natural experiment.

## Key findings

- Keeping pupils in small local 1-9 schools shortened travel distances and reduced school cohort size.
- Peer composition became more homogeneous, and teacher qualifications and experience fell.
- No evidence that remaining in a small local school (avoiding the grade 6-7 transition) affected educational outcomes.
- A survey suggested pupils perceive little difference in the psychosocial environment across grade configurations.

## Limitations

- Swedish context; outcome is mainly grades and attainment rather than wellbeing.

## Relevance to PPS

Directly relevant to the PPS conversion of K-8s to K-5s plus middle schools: in this Swedish setting, the extra transition and larger middle schools had no measurable effect on outcomes. US studies of K-8 versus middle-school configurations (e.g., Rockoff & Lockwood 2010; Schwerdt & West 2013; covered in another topic area) find negative effects of moving to middle school, so treat as one data point.
