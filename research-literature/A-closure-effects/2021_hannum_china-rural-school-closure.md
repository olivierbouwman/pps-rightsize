# Estimating the Effects of Educational System Consolidation: The Case of China's Rural School Closure Initiative

**Citation:** Hannum, E., Liu, X., & Wang, F. (2021). Estimating the effects of educational system consolidation: The case of China's rural school closure initiative. *Economic Development and Cultural Change, 70*(1), 485-528.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1086/711654 (OA preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17101)

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Rural China, early-2000s primary school closure policy (driven by fertility decline and out-migration).

**Sample:** Household survey covering 728 villages in seven provinces.

**Method:** Exploits variation in villages' year of closure and children's ages at closure.

## Key findings

- Girls exposed to closure during primary-school ages lost on average 0.60 years of schooling by 2011 (mean age 17); effects grew with time since closure.
- No significant effect for boys.
- Gender gap likely reflects girls' enrollment being more sensitive to travel distance and boys' enrollment being more responsive to school quality.

## Limitations

- Developing-country context with very long travel distances and boarding; limited transferability to a US city.

## Relevance to PPS

Clearest evidence that added travel distance can reduce attainment for groups sensitive to distance. For PPS, a reminder to monitor attendance among students whose commute grows, especially those without transportation.
