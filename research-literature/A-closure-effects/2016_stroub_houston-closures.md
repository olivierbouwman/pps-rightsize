# Evaluating the Impact of School Closures in Houston ISD, Part I: The Effect of School Closures on Patterns of Student Attendance and Achievement

**Citation:** Stroub, K. J., & Richards, M. P. (2016). *Evaluating the impact of school closures in Houston ISD, Part I* (Policy brief). Texas Education Research Center, University of Texas at Austin.

**DOI / URL:** https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED615464.pdf (ERIC ED615464)

**Type:** Report-not-peer-reviewed (propensity-matched growth models)

**Peer-reviewed:** N

**Setting:** Houston ISD, Texas; 55 schools closed 2003-2010 for enrollment decline, budget and performance reasons.

**Sample:** 4,168 displaced students matched to comparable non-displaced students.

**Method:** Propensity score matching and discontinuous growth models.

## Key findings

- Closed schools were disproportionately poor (91 percent economically disadvantaged vs. 80 percent districtwide) and Black (43 percent of affected students vs. 27 percent districtwide).
- Displaced students typically moved to slightly higher-performing schools, but 52 percent still went to bottom-third schools in math.
- White displaced students were much more likely to move to top-third schools (51 percent) than Black (28 percent) or Hispanic (20 percent) students; higher-achieving students were 1.6 times as likely as low-achieving students to reach high-performing schools.
- A short-lived math bump was followed by flatter growth, leaving displaced students slightly behind after four years; reading growth was also flatter.
- Moving to a high-performing receiving school moderated the negative effects.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; matching on observables.

## Relevance to PPS

Shows unequal placement by race and prior achievement even under a designated-receiving-school policy, which PPS can monitor.
