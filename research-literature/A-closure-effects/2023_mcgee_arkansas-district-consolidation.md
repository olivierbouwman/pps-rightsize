# The Effect of School District Consolidation on Student Achievement: Evidence from Arkansas

**Citation:** McGee, J. B., Mills, J. N., & Goldstein, J. S. (2023). The effect of school district consolidation on student achievement: Evidence from Arkansas. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 45*(3), 482-495. (EdWorkingPaper 21-347.)

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/01623737221133394 (OA versions: https://scholarworks.uark.edu/edrepub/119; ERIC ED671463)

**Type:** Quasi-experimental (regression discontinuity)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Arkansas; Act 60 (2004) required consolidation of districts with fewer than 350 students for two consecutive years. 105 school closures followed consolidations between 2005 and 2011.

**Sample:** Students in small Arkansas districts near the 350-student threshold.

**Method:** Regression discontinuity on district enrollment relative to the 350-student cutoff.

## Key findings

- Consolidation had null or small positive effects on achievement: about 0.04 SD in math (marginally significant) and about 0.05-0.08 SD in ELA in the preferred bandwidth.
- Some negative math estimates appear in the narrowest bandwidth.
- No evidence of economies of scale: consolidation did not reduce overall costs or shift resources toward the classroom.

## Limitations

- Rural, very small districts; consolidation is of administrative units, and only some involved school closures.
- Summary based on the open-access working-paper version.

## Relevance to PPS

Suggests consolidation is not harmful on average but that promised savings may not materialize. Relevant to PPS budget assumptions.
