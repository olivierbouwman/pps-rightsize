# Beyond the Headlines: Trends and Future Directions in the School Closure Literature

**Citation:** Ewing, E. L., & Green, T. L. (2022). Beyond the headlines: Trends and future directions in the school closure literature. *Educational Researcher, 51*(1), 58-65.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X211050944

**Type:** Narrative / systematic-style review of the empirical literature (nearly two decades)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** United States.

**Sample:** Empirical studies of school closure published over roughly 2000-2021.

**Method:** Maps trends in topics, settings and methods across the closure literature and proposes a research agenda.

## Key findings

- The field is limited in scope and method and focuses overwhelmingly on the mid-2010s mass urban closures (Chicago, Philadelphia, etc.).
- Anticipates more closures driven by post-COVID budgets, school choice growth and population shifts across urban, suburban and rural districts.
- Calls for research beyond test scores and beyond a few big cities, including closures driven by enrollment decline and fiscal stress.

## Limitations

- Short agenda-setting piece; does not estimate effects.

## Relevance to PPS

Directly anticipates the PPS situation (enrollment decline plus budget pressure after ESSER) and flags that most causal evidence comes from performance-driven urban closures, so transfer to an enrollment-driven case should be made cautiously.
