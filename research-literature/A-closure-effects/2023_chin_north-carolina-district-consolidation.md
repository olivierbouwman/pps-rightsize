# School District Consolidation in North Carolina: Impacts on School Composition and Finance, Crime Outcomes, and Educational Attainment

**Citation:** Chin, M. J. (2023). School district consolidation in North Carolina: Impacts on school composition and finance, crime outcomes, and educational attainment. *Economics of Education Review, 95*, 102432.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2023.102432

**Type:** Quasi-experimental (difference-in-differences / event study)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** North Carolina; mergers of multiple K-12 districts into single units.

**Sample:** Longitudinal district, school and youth data.

**Method:** Difference-in-differences and event-study models.

## Key findings

- Where districts consolidated, per-pupil spending on instruction and district support services fell, and schools became more racially integrated.
- Youth exposed to mergers were no more likely to be convicted of a crime in early adulthood; null effects hold by race and crime type.
- No effect on educational attainment.
- Interpreted as evidence that consolidation can reduce operating costs without harming students and can support racial integration.

## Limitations

- District (administrative) consolidation rather than building closure.
- Effect sizes not reproduced (paywalled).

## Relevance to PPS

Shows that consolidation can be neutral for long-run outcomes and can be used to reduce segregation. PPS boundary redraws could be evaluated for integration effects.
