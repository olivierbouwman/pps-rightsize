# The Long Shadow of School Closures: Impacts on Students' Educational and Labor Market Outcomes

**Citation:** Kim, J. (2023). *The long shadow of school closures: Impacts on students' educational and labor market outcomes* (SSRN Working Paper 4607309).

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4607309

**Type:** Quasi-experimental (working paper)

**Peer-reviewed:** N

**Setting:** Texas, statewide administrative data linked to postsecondary and labor-market records.

**Sample:** Students experiencing public school closures (closures due to declining enrollment and chronic low performance).

**Method:** Within-student across-time and within-school across-cohort comparisons.

## Key findings

- Closure causes significant disruption to both test scores and behavior.
- The drop in test scores recovers within about three years, but behavioral problems persist.
- Impacts are particularly pronounced for economically disadvantaged students.
- Long-run effects include lower high school graduation, lower college completion, and lower employment and earnings.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; estimates may change.
- Specific effect sizes not reproduced (only the abstract was retrievable).

## Relevance to PPS

One of the few studies with labor-market outcomes, and it includes enrollment-driven closures. Suggests behavioral and attainment effects can outlast test-score recovery; relevant for PPS monitoring of discipline and graduation after closure.
