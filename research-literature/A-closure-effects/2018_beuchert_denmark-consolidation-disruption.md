# The Short-Term Effects of School Consolidation on Student Achievement: Evidence of Disruption?

**Citation:** Beuchert, L. V., Humlum, M. K., Nielsen, H. S., & Smith, N. (2018). The short-term effects of school consolidation on student achievement: Evidence of disruption? *Economics of Education Review, 65*, 31-47.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2018.05.004 (OA accepted version: https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/3ab53d2d-33aa-49c2-b015-5ae244af6f10; earlier CESifo WP 6082)

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Denmark; wave of school consolidations in 2010-2011.

**Sample:** Danish students observed before consolidation and up to four years after, with national test scores.

**Method:** Panel comparisons of students affected and unaffected by consolidation, before and after (see full text for the exact specification).

## Key findings

- Achievement of students from closing schools was adversely affected in the short run.
- Students initially enrolled in small schools experienced the most harmful effects.
- Effects weaken over time, suggesting part of the effect is disruption.

## Limitations

- Specific effect sizes not reproduced (summary from abstract).
- Short horizon (up to four years).

## Relevance to PPS

A clear non-US analogue to enrollment- and budget-driven consolidation of small schools into larger ones, very similar to PPS's situation. It suggests a temporary dip, largest for students from small schools, that fades.
