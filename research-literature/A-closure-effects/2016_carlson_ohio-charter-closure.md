# Charter School Closure and Student Achievement: Evidence from Ohio

**Citation:** Carlson, D., & Lavertu, S. (2016). Charter school closure and student achievement: Evidence from Ohio. *Journal of Urban Economics, 95*, 31-48.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jue.2016.07.001

**Type:** Quasi-experimental (regression discontinuity)

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Ohio charter schools subject to a mandatory closure law based on performance.

**Sample:** Students attending charter schools identified for closure.

**Method:** Regression discontinuity around the state performance threshold that triggers mandatory closure; closure used as an instrument for leaving low-quality charters.

## Key findings

- Closing low-performing charter schools eventually yields achievement gains of about 0.2-0.3 SD in reading and math for students attending those schools when they were identified for closure.
- Results imply plausible lower-bound estimates of the harm of attending very low-performing charter schools.

## Limitations

- Charter-sector, performance-triggered closure of very low-performing schools; little relevance to closing adequate neighborhood schools for enrollment reasons.

## Relevance to PPS

Shows the largest positive effects arise when the closed school is far below its alternatives. PPS closures are enrollment-driven, so this upside should not be assumed.
