# Rethinking the School Closure Research: School Closure as Spatial Injustice

**Citation:** Tieken, M. C., & Auldridge-Reveles, T. R. (2019). Rethinking the school closure research: School closure as spatial injustice. *Review of Educational Research, 89*(6), 917-953.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654319877151

**Type:** Narrative / critical literature review

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** United States, both urban and rural closures; draws on US and some international literature.

**Sample:** Reviews the body of empirical research on urban and rural school closure (economic, academic, community and policy perspectives).

**Method:** Integrative review using a spatial-justice lens; looks across urban and rural contexts rather than treating them separately.

## Key findings

- Closures are unevenly distributed: they fall disproportionately on places where low-income communities and communities of color live.
- Advocates claim closures improve academic opportunity, efficiency and equality; the reviewed evidence shows closures can instead harm students and adults and reduce access to an important educational and community institution.
- Treats urban "mass closures" and rural consolidation as one phenomenon, so rural evidence (longer travel, loss of community anchor) and urban evidence (disruption, receiving-school quality) are read together.
- Offers recommendations for research and practice, including attention to who bears the costs and community voice in decisions.

## Limitations

- Critical/interpretive review, not a meta-analysis; no pooled effect sizes.
- Framing (spatial injustice) is explicit and normative; quantitative causal studies are weighed alongside qualitative work.

## Relevance to PPS

Most-cited recent review in a top review journal. Useful for the equity frame: which PPS neighborhoods and student groups bear the closures, and the community-institution loss that achievement studies do not measure. Covers both enrollment-driven and performance-driven closures.
