# School Hopscotch: A Comprehensive Review of K-12 Student Mobility in the United States

**Citation:** Welsh, R. O. (2017). School hopscotch: A comprehensive review of K-12 student mobility in the United States. *Review of Educational Research, 87*(3), 475-511.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316672068

**Type:** Integrative literature review

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** United States.

**Sample:** Studies of K-12 student mobility (school changes), not closures specifically.

**Method:** Integrative review contrasting older cross-sectional work with newer longitudinal, within-student designs.

## Key findings

- Changing schools is most common among minority and low-income students in urban districts.
- Overall, switching schools is associated with negative effects on educational outcomes.
- Transferring to a higher-quality school may offset or outweigh the transition cost of moving.
- Strong causal claims are hard because the reasons for moving are usually unobserved; closure-induced moves are one of the cleaner sources of variation.

## Limitations

- Not about closures per se; most mobility is voluntary or residential, unlike closure-forced moves.

## Relevance to PPS

Provides the mechanism behind closure findings: a forced move carries a transition cost, which is recovered only if the receiving school is meaningfully better. Relevant to PPS boundary changes and K-8 to K-5 conversions that add an extra school transition.
