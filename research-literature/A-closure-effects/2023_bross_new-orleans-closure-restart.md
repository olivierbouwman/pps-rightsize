# The Effects of Performance-Based School Closure and Restart on Student Performance

**Citation:** Bross, W., Harris, D. N., & Liu, L. (2023). The effects of performance-based school closure and restart on student performance. *Economics of Education Review, 94*, 102368. (Earlier version: Bross, Harris & Liu, 2016, *Extreme measures: When and how school closures and charter takeovers benefit students*, Education Research Alliance for New Orleans policy brief.)

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2023.102368 (open access, CC BY-NC-ND)

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y (2023 article); the 2016 ERA brief was not peer-reviewed

**Setting:** New Orleans and Baton Rouge, Louisiana; low-performing schools closed or restarted as charter schools after Hurricane Katrina.

**Sample:** Students attending treated schools at the time of closure or restart, compared with matched Louisiana students.

**Method:** Matched difference-in-differences.

## Key findings

- Positive and precise effects of closure/restart on elementary and middle school test scores in New Orleans.
- No clear effects on high school graduation or college entry in New Orleans; in Baton Rouge high schools, the interventions reduced graduation by 11 to 15 percentage points.
- Variation in test-score effects is positively related to the gain in school value-added that students experienced, and negatively related to student age/grade and the amount of disruption.
- Conclusion: effects depend predictably on policy design and implementation.

## Limitations

- Unusual post-Katrina context with near-universal charter conversion; closure here is performance-driven.

## Relevance to PPS

Clarifies the conditions for benefit: younger students, a large step up in school quality, and limited disruption. Helps PPS judge whether consolidated K-5s will actually be higher-quality for displaced students.
