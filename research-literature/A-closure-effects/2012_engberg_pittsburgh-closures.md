# Closing Schools in a Shrinking District: Do Student Outcomes Depend on Which Schools Are Closed?

**Citation:** Engberg, J., Gill, B., Zamarro, G., & Zimmer, R. (2012). Closing schools in a shrinking district: Do student outcomes depend on which schools are closed? *Journal of Urban Economics, 71*(2), 189-203.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jue.2011.10.001

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** An anonymous mid-sized urban district (widely identified as Pittsburgh Public Schools) facing declining enrollment, mid-2000s.

**Sample:** Students displaced by a closure wave and students in receiving schools, compared with students in other schools.

**Method:** Growth comparisons with instrumental variables (residential assignment to school as the instrument for the school actually attended), controlling for concurrent district reforms.

## Key findings

- Facing declining enrollment, the district deliberately used achievement as a closure criterion and tried to move students to higher-performing schools.
- Displaced students can experience adverse effects on test scores and attendance, but these are minimized when students move to schools that are higher-performing in value-added terms.
- The negative effect on attendance disappears after the first year in the new school.
- No adverse effects were found for students already in the receiving schools.

## Limitations

- One district; anonymous so contextual details are limited.
- Effect sizes are not reproduced here because the full text is paywalled.

## Relevance to PPS

The closest analogue among the anchor studies to PPS: an enrollment-driven ("shrinking district") closure plan. Its key lesson is that which schools close, and the value-added of the receiving school, determine whether displaced students are harmed.
