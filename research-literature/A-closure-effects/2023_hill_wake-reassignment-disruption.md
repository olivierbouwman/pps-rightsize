# New Schools and New Classmates: The Disruption and Peer Group Effects of School Reassignment

**Citation:** Hill, D. V., Hughes, R. P., Lenard, M. A., Liebowitz, D. D., & Page, L. C. (2023). New schools and new classmates: The disruption and peer group effects of school reassignment. *Economics of Education Review, 92*, 102316. (NBER Working Paper 30085.)

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2022.102316

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** Wake County Public School System, North Carolina; annual boundary/assignment changes.

**Sample:** Students quasi-randomly reassigned to different schools.

**Method:** Exploits quasi-random reassignment to separate the effect of changing schools from the effect of new peers.

## Key findings

- Rules out all but substantively small effects of changing schools due to reassignment on test scores, course grades and chronic absenteeism.
- Higher-achieving peers raise math and ELA test scores but lower ELA course grades.
- Test-score gains from better peers accrue mainly to higher-income students, though lower-income and lower-achieving students also benefit.
- Assignment policies that avoid concentrating lower-achieving or lower-income students can meet equity goals, with caveats.

## Limitations

- Reassignment without school closure; receiving and sending schools stay open, so the loss-of-school element is absent.

## Relevance to PPS

Directly relevant to PPS boundary redraws: a well-run reassignment by itself causes little measurable disruption; the peer composition of the new school matters more.
