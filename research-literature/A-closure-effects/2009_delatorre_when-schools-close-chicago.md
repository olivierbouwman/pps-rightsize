# When Schools Close: Effects on Displaced Students in Chicago Public Schools

**Citation:** de la Torre, M., & Gwynne, J. (2009). *When schools close: Effects on displaced students in Chicago Public Schools*. Chicago: Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago.

**DOI / URL:** https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED510792.pdf (ERIC ED510792); https://consortium.uchicago.edu

**Type:** Report-not-peer-reviewed (quasi-experimental comparison-group design)

**Peer-reviewed:** N (reviewed by the What Works Clearinghouse "with reservations")

**Setting:** Chicago Public Schools; 18 regular elementary schools closed 2001-2006 for underutilization or low performance.

**Sample:** About 3,800 displaced students aged 8+ and about 4,700 students in 18 comparison schools.

**Method:** Growth-curve models comparing displaced students with students in similar schools that did not close; outcomes measured in months of learning.

## Key findings

- Most displaced students re-enrolled in academically weak schools: 40 percent went to schools on probation, 42 percent to schools in the bottom quartile; only 6 percent attended top-quartile schools.
- The largest negative effect came in the announcement year, before closure: about 1.5 months of learning lost in reading and a little over half a month in math.
- One year after leaving, achievement was not significantly different from expected and stayed there two and three years later.
- Closure did not affect retention or special-education referrals. Displaced students were less likely to attend summer school and more likely to change schools again.
- Eighth graders entered high school behind expected levels, but on-track rates in 9th grade were no different.
- Displaced students who went to higher-achieving schools, or schools with high student-teacher trust and personal attention, made larger gains. Those who did attend strong schools traveled an average of 3.5 miles.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; comparison groups may differ in unmeasured ways (WWC reservations).
- Small number of closed schools.

## Relevance to PPS

Includes underutilization-driven closures, like PPS. Shows the announcement year is the riskiest period and that results depend on getting students into clearly stronger schools; also reports no change in special-education referrals.
