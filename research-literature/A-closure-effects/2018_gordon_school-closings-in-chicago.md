# School Closings in Chicago: Staff and Student Experiences and Academic Outcomes

**Citation:** Gordon, M. F., de la Torre, M., Cowhy, J. R., Moore, P. T., Sartain, L. S., & Knight, D. (2018). *School closings in Chicago: Staff and student experiences and academic outcomes*. Chicago: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research.

**DOI / URL:** https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED589712.pdf (ERIC ED589712); https://consortium.uchicago.edu

**Type:** Report-not-peer-reviewed (mixed methods: quasi-experimental impact analysis plus qualitative case studies of six welcoming schools)

**Peer-reviewed:** N

**Setting:** Chicago Public Schools, 2013 mass closure of 47 elementary schools, driven by a reported $1 billion deficit and underutilization (enrollment below 80 percent of capacity).

**Sample:** About 12,000 displaced students; about 17,000 students in 48 designated "welcoming" schools; comparison students in similar schools.

**Method:** Comparison-group impact models for mobility, attendance, suspensions, test scores and core GPA, with interviews and focus groups.

## Key findings

- Designated welcoming schools had to be within one mile, higher-rated and have seats; 66 percent of displaced students enrolled in them, and about 6 percent left the district (similar to prior years).
- 21 percent of welcoming-school students did not return in fall 2013, about 5 points more than expected, driven entirely by the 14 welcoming schools that had to move into the closed schools' buildings.
- No change in absences or suspension rates for affected students.
- Students from closed schools were about 1.5 months behind in reading and 2 months behind in math in the announcement year. Reading recovered by year two, but the math gap (about 2 months) persisted for four years.
- Welcoming-school students had lower-than-expected reading scores (about 1.5 months) in the first year, which rebounded the next year.
- Small negative effects on core GPA appeared three and four years after closure for students from closed schools, especially those in grades 3-5 at announcement.
- Qualitative: staff and students arrived grieving; welcoming-school staff felt under-supported; building a merged culture took a long time; one-year extra resources were valued but too short.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; comparison-group design.
- Chicago's closures were concentrated in Black neighborhoods on the south and west sides.

## Relevance to PPS

The single most analogous study for PPS: an enrollment- and budget-driven mass consolidation of elementary schools into designated receiving schools. Main lessons: academic results were "neutral at best, and negative in some instances"; receiving-school students were affected too; and relocating the receiving school into the closed building increased attrition.
