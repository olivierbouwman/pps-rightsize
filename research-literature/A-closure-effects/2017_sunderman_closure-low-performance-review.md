# School Closure as a Strategy to Remedy Low Performance

**Citation:** Sunderman, G. L., Coghlan, E., & Mintrop, R. (2017). *School closure as a strategy to remedy low performance*. Boulder, CO: National Education Policy Center.

**DOI / URL:** https://nepc.colorado.edu/publication/closures (ERIC ED574706: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED574706.pdf)

**Type:** Policy review (narrative synthesis)

**Peer-reviewed:** N (NEPC policy brief; NEPC states briefs are blind-reviewed by its editorial board, but it is not a journal article)

**Setting:** United States.

**Sample:** Peer-reviewed studies and policy reports on closure for low performance (Chicago, Pittsburgh, Michigan, Philadelphia, NYC, Ohio, etc.).

**Method:** Narrative synthesis organized around four questions (frequency and reasons; student impact; system impact; equity and community impact).

## Key findings

- The limited evidence base suggests closure is not a promising strategy for remedying low performance.
- Benefits are at best weak and mixed; harms are likely if districts do not ensure seats in higher-performing schools for transferred students.
- Transferred students face non-academic challenges for at least one or two years.
- The authors judge closure especially inadvisable for middle-school students because the short grade span leaves little time to recover.
- Closing buildings and moving staff and students has costs that reduce resources for remaining schools. Closure for enrollment decline may sometimes be unavoidable, but closure purely for performance has unintended consequences.
- Closures disproportionately affect low-income communities and communities of color.

## Limitations

- Advocacy-adjacent policy center; synthesis is not systematic and has no pooled estimates.
- Predates several key studies (Steinberg & MacDonald 2019; Bifulco & Schwegman 2020; Bross et al. 2023).

## Relevance to PPS

Explicitly distinguishes enrollment-driven from performance-driven closure, and its caution about middle-grade closures is relevant to the PPS middle-school closure and K-8 conversions.
