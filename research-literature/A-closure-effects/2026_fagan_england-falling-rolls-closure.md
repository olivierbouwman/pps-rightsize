# Falling Pupil Numbers and School Closures: Setting a Research Agenda for a New Era of Precarity

**Citation:** Fagan, E., & Bradbury, A. (2026). Falling pupil numbers and school closures: Setting a research agenda for a new era of precarity. *British Educational Research Journal*. Advance online publication.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/berj.70193 (open access, CC BY)

**Type:** Qualitative case study plus research agenda

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** England, especially London, where falling birth rates have led to nearly 100 permanent school closures in five years.

**Sample:** One primary school closure (interviews with children, parents, teachers and community members), plus analysis of closure patterns.

**Method:** Exploratory qualitative study using Bourdieu's capitals and spatial justice; descriptive analysis of which schools close.

## Key findings

- Schools that closed were disproportionately in deprived areas and were smaller schools.
- The closure had significant social impacts on a disadvantaged community; those already marginalised felt powerless to challenge or navigate the process.
- England has little research on falling-roll closures; the authors set out research questions and policy implications.

## Limitations

- Single-case qualitative study; no outcome estimates.

## Relevance to PPS

The most recent item and the closest international parallel to PPS: closures driven purely by falling enrollment (birth-rate decline) in a large city. Highlights process and equity concerns.
