# School Consolidation and Student Achievement

**Citation:** de Haan, M., Leuven, E., & Oosterbeek, H. (2016). School consolidation and student achievement. *Journal of Law, Economics, and Organization, 32*(4), 816-839.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/eww006

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** The Netherlands; mid-1990s primary school consolidation reform that raised the minimum required school size.

**Sample:** Dutch primary school students across municipalities.

**Method:** Exploits variation between municipalities in the increase in minimum required school size.

## Key findings

- A 10 percent increase in the minimum required school size had a small positive effect on achievement of about 0.72 percent of a standard deviation.
- The effect is mainly attributable to the increase in actual school size; reduced competition and choice did not appear to harm achievement.
- No evidence that the effect is driven by reduced segregation or by eliminating underperforming small schools.

## Limitations

- Effects are small; Dutch context combines universal school choice with public funding of religious schools.

## Relevance to PPS

Evidence that consolidating small primary schools into larger ones can be neutral to slightly positive for achievement in the medium run. Supports the PPS rationale for larger elementary schools, while noting the small magnitude.
