# Lights Off: Practice and Impact of Closing Low-Performing Schools

**Citation:** Center for Research on Education Outcomes (CREDO). (2017). *Lights off: Practice and impact of closing low-performing schools*. Stanford, CA: CREDO, Stanford University.

**DOI / URL:** https://credo.stanford.edu/ (executive summary saved locally)

**Type:** Report-not-peer-reviewed (multi-state matched comparison, Virtual Control Records)

**Peer-reviewed:** N (critiqued in an NEPC review by Gaertner & Kirshner, 2017)

**Setting:** 26 states, 2006-07 to 2012-13.

**Sample:** 1,522 closed low-performing schools (1,204 traditional public, 318 charter) and their students.

**Method:** Descriptive analysis plus "virtual twin" matching of closure students to similar students in continuing low-performing schools.

## Key findings

- Closures were concentrated in a few states and mostly urban elementary schools.
- Closing schools showed declining achievement and enrollment in the three years before closure.
- Among similarly low-performing schools, those with more Black and Hispanic students, and higher-poverty traditional public schools, were more likely to be closed.
- Students left early in the year before closure, and early leavers were lower-performing.
- A little less than half of displaced students landed in better schools; placements in later years mostly mirrored the first move.
- Students who moved to better schools made greater gains than matched peers; those in equal or worse schools had weaker growth. The pattern was strongest for Black and Hispanic students in poverty.

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; matching on observables; low-performing closures only.

## Relevance to PPS

Largest multi-state dataset; reinforces that receiving-school quality is the key moderator and that disadvantaged students are both more likely to be affected and more dependent on good placement.
