# Who Benefits from Accountability-Driven School Closure? Evidence from New York City

**Citation:** Bifulco, R., & Schwegman, D. J. (2020). Who benefits from accountability-driven school closure? Evidence from New York City. *Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, 39*(1), 96-130.

**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.22140 (OA manuscript: https://surface.syr.edu/cpr/244)

**Type:** Quasi-experimental

**Peer-reviewed:** Y

**Setting:** New York City middle schools closed for low performance.

**Sample:** Students enrolled in closing middle schools at the time of closure, and later cohorts who would likely have attended the closed schools.

**Method:** Comparison of affected cohorts with matched/comparison students using pre-closure trends; heterogeneity by prior achievement.

## Key findings

- Future cohorts who would have entered the closed schools attended schools with better test scores and higher value-added; for the top fifth of performers, these schools were about 0.08 SD higher in value-added, and they traveled slightly farther.
- No measurable effect for the average future-cohort student, but high-performing students in that group gained (about 0.08 SD in 6th-grade math) and the benefits persisted for several cohorts.
- Closure harmed students enrolled in schools during phase-out: on average a significant 0.059 SD drop in 8th-grade math, a non-significant 0.048 SD drop in ELA, and a 4.4 percentage-point rise in the absentee rate (about 52 percent above the 8.4 percent baseline).
- Harms were largest for the lowest performers: the bottom two prior-achievement quintiles lost 0.072 and 0.081 SD in 8th-grade math, and the absence increase was concentrated among them.
- Key trade-off: future cohorts of relatively high-performing students may benefit, while low-performing students in schools designated for closure are hurt.

## Limitations

- Performance-driven closures in a large choice-based system; transfer to an enrollment-driven elementary context is uncertain.
- Effect sizes summarized from the open-access manuscript and may differ slightly from the published version.

## Relevance to PPS

Directly relevant to the PPS middle-school closure. It distinguishes students who experience the closure from future cohorts, and shows harms concentrated among lower-achieving students.
