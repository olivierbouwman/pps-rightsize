## Setting
U.S. public and private enrollment and Census population, 2016-2024, with projections to 2050.

## Method
Combines NCES Common Core of Data, the Private School Universe Survey, and Census SAIPE and population projections.

## Key findings
- The share of children outside traditional public schools rose from 9.7% before COVID to 12.6% in 2023-24. Much of the post-2020 growth is children 'missing' from federal enrollment files.
- Oregon had the largest 'missing' share nationally, about 12% in 2021-22.
- The non-traditional-school share rose fastest in high-poverty and predominantly Black districts.
- Traditional public enrollment is projected at 34.6-40.8M by 2050, down from 43.06M in 2023-24. Population decline alone removes about 2.2M students.
- Steeper enrollment losses raise the odds of permanent closure, but closures are rarely driven by one factor (Goulas 2025).

## Limitations / viewpoint
Not peer-reviewed. Private enrollment is extrapolated for recent years, and 'missing' students include data gaps.

**Relevance to PPS:** Places PPS's decline (elementary down 28.4% over 10 years) in a national demographic trend that is unlikely to reverse soon. The Oregon-specific 'missing students' finding means part of PPS's loss may be families exiting to homeschool or unrecorded options.
