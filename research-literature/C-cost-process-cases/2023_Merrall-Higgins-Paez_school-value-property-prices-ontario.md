# What's a School Worth to a Neighborhood? A Spatial Hedonic Analysis of Property Prices in the Context of Accommodation Reviews in Ontario

- **Citation:** Merrall, J., Higgins, C. D., & Paez, A. (2024). What's a school worth to a neighborhood? *Geographical Analysis, 56*(2), 217-243.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gean.12377
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (hedonic pricing)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Hamilton, Ontario, during provincially driven 'accommodation reviews' that closed many schools.

## Method
Spatial Durbin hedonic models of house prices and distance to schools.

## Key findings
- Significant negative relationship between distance to a school and house prices: proximity to schools is capitalized into property values.
- Implies that closing a neighborhood school 'may result in potentially significant losses of economic value'.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Estimates the value of proximity cross-sectionally rather than observing prices before and after closures. One Canadian city.

**Relevance to PPS:** The best available evidence found here on housing prices in a closure context. No U.S. before-and-after study of public school closures and house prices was located (see SYNTHESIS gaps).
