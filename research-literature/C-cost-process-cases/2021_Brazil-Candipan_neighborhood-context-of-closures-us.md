# The neighborhood ethnoracial and socioeconomic context of public elementary school closures in U.S. metropolitan areas

- **Citation:** Brazil, N., & Candipan, J. (2022). The neighborhood ethnoracial and socioeconomic context of public elementary school closures in U.S. metropolitan areas. *Social Science Research, 103*, 102655.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssresearch.2021.102655
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (national)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Traditional public elementary closures, 2010-2016, in more than 260 U.S. metropolitan areas, using attendance-boundary data.

## Method
Regression of closure on 2010 neighborhood composition and change over time.

## Key findings
- Closures are associated with higher neighborhood percent Black and socioeconomic disadvantage, and lower percent White and Hispanic.
- In urban areas, increasing percent White (a gentrification signal) is also positively associated with closure.
- Patterns vary by region and urbanicity.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Associational. It ends in 2016 and does not identify mechanisms.

**Relevance to PPS:** A national baseline showing that the inner-Northeast Portland pattern Serbulo describes (gentrification alongside closure) is not unique to Portland.
