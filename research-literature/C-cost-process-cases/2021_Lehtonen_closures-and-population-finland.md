# Primary school closures and population development: is school vitality an investment in the attractiveness of the (rural) communities or not?

- **Citation:** Lehtonen, O. (2021). Primary school closures and population development. *Journal of Rural Studies, 82*, 138-147.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrurstud.2021.01.011
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (quasi-experimental)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Finland, school closures 2010-2018, mostly small rural schools closed to cut costs.

## Method
Difference-in-differences with matching.

## Key findings
- After a closure, the surrounding community's population declined faster than before the closure.
- The author argues closures raise questions about municipal vitality, not just school-network efficiency.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Rural Finland. Effects on in-migration of families may differ in a large city with many nearby schools.

**Relevance to PPS:** Causal-leaning evidence that closures can speed population loss locally. In Portland the analogous risk is fewer families moving into, or staying in, neighborhoods that lose their school.
