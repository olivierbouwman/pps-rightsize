# School Closures and the Spatial Ecology of Education Access in 10 U.S. Cities

- **Citation:** Brazil, N. (2025). School closures and the spatial ecology of education access in 10 U.S. cities. *Geographical Analysis, 57*(2), 205-232.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gean.12414
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (spatial)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Public school closures in 10 U.S. cities, 2010-2019.

## Method
Exploratory spatial data analysis of closure clustering, plus block-group travel times by car and on foot to the next nearest open school.

## Key findings
- Closures cluster geographically.
- Neighborhoods with a larger share of Black residents, whether higher-poverty or not, face longer travel times to the nearest open school after closures.
- Clustering and longer travel suggest that 'school deserts' can form after closures.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Travel-time modeling; does not observe actual enrollment choices.

**Relevance to PPS:** Directly applicable test for PPS scenarios: map walk and transit time to the nearest open K-5 before and after each scenario, by neighborhood demographics.
