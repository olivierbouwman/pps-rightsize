# Sticking Points: How School Districts Experience Implementing the Portfolio Strategy

- **Citation:** Lake, R., Posamentier, J., Denice, P., & Hill, P. (2016). *Sticking points: How school districts experience implementing the portfolio strategy.* Seattle: CRPE, University of Washington Bothell.
- **DOI / URL:** https://crpe.org/sticking-points-how-school-districts-experience-implementing-the-portfolio-strategy/
- **Type:** Research report (not peer-reviewed; CRPE originated and advocates the portfolio strategy)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 3. What happens to closed buildings (reuse, vacancy, charters, portfolio models)
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2016_CRPE_sticking-points.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Portfolio Network districts (more than 50 affiliated; a subset tracked annually).

## Method
Annual implementation ratings on seven components: choice, autonomy, funding, talent, support, accountability, engagement.

## Key findings
- Under the accountability component, schools that do not improve 'should be replaced'. Performance frameworks drive expansion, intervention and closure decisions.
- Districts made progress on accountability, talent and choice, but got 'stuck' on autonomy and funding, which require restructuring the central office or changing union contracts.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Written by the strategy's originators and advocates. It measures implementation, not student or community outcomes.

**Relevance to PPS:** Shows the portfolio viewpoint, in which closure is a tool for quality rather than a response to enrollment. Useful for telling apart PPS's enrollment-driven rationale from performance-based closure regimes that critics associate with closures.
