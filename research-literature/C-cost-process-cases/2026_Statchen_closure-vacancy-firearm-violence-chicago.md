# The 2013 mass public school closure and firearm violence in Chicago: A quasi-experimental difference-in-differences analysis

- **Citation:** Statchen, T., Desjardins, M. R., Wagner, E., Johnson, O., Tung, E. L., & Uzzi, M. (2026). The 2013 mass public school closure and firearm violence in Chicago: A quasi-experimental difference-in-differences analysis. *Social Science & Medicine, 403*, 119428.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2026.119428 (press summary: https://news.uchicago.edu/story/public-school-closings-chicago-linked-more-gun-violence-nearby-neighborhoods)
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (quasi-experimental)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. What happens to closed buildings (reuse, vacancy, charters, portfolio models)

## Setting
Chicago, 2010-2019, around schools closed in 2013. As of 2023, 26 buildings were still vacant.

## Method
Inverse-probability-weighted difference-in-differences with Chicago Police Department data.

## Key findings
- Closure was associated with a 9.88% increase in shootings near the school location (95% CI 3.59-16.99).
- Long-term building vacancy was associated with a 10.20% increase in shootings (95% CI 2.73-18.84).
- Weapons violations and firearm assaults and batteries also increased.
- The authors recommend community-driven productive reuse of closed buildings.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Observational; Chicago-specific, with high baseline violence. Conflicts in part with Brazil 2020's short-run findings, which used different outcomes and time windows.

**Relevance to PPS:** The strongest recent evidence that long-vacant closed schools can harm neighborhood safety. PPS should plan reuse or interim activation for each closed building rather than leaving it vacant.
