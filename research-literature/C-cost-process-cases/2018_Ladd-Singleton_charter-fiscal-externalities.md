# The Fiscal Externalities of Charter Schools: Evidence from North Carolina

- **Citation:** Ladd, H. F., & Singleton, J. D. (2020). The fiscal externalities of charter schools: Evidence from North Carolina. *Education Finance and Policy, 15*(1), 191-208.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp_a_00272
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?

## Setting
North Carolina districts with rapid charter growth after the statewide charter cap was lifted in 2011.

## Method
Analysis of district balance sheets to estimate costs that districts cannot shed when students leave.

## Key findings
- In the one urban district studied, the fiscal cost exceeded $500 per remaining traditional-school pupil, about $3,600 for each student enrolled in charters.
- Comparable or larger per-charter-pupil costs in two non-urban districts.
- Mechanism: districts cannot cut buildings, administration and other fixed costs in proportion to enrollment loss.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Charter-driven enrollment loss in one state. Funding rules differ in Oregon, but the fixed-cost mechanism carries over.

**Relevance to PPS:** Shows that enrollment loss leaves stranded fixed costs, which is the underlying fiscal logic of rightsizing. It also implies that closures pushing families out of the district would worsen PPS's per-pupil finances.
