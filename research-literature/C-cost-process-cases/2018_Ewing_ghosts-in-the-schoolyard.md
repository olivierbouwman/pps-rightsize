# Ghosts in the Schoolyard: Racism and School Closings on Chicago's South Side

- **Citation:** Ewing, E. L. (2018). *Ghosts in the schoolyard: Racism and school closings on Chicago's South Side.* Chicago: University of Chicago Press.
- **DOI / URL:** Book (University of Chicago Press, 2018). Review: https://doi.org/10.17763/1943-5045-89.2.322
- **Type:** Book (scholarly, peer-reviewed press; ethnography)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Bronzeville, Chicago, during the 2013 mass closure of 49 elementary schools and one high school program.

## Method
Ethnography, historical analysis, interviews and public-hearing records.

## Key findings
- Frames community opposition as 'institutional mourning': schools were valued as historical, cultural and civic institutions, not just as seat counts.
- The district's utilization and performance metrics and residents' sense of what a school means were fundamentally mismatched, and this deepened distrust.
- Situates the 2013 closures in a long history of racialized disinvestment in Chicago's Black neighborhoods.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Qualitative case study of one neighborhood and one district; the author is a former CPS teacher. No fiscal or achievement analysis.

**Relevance to PPS:** Explains why technical criteria alone tend to produce backlash, and why the history of each school's neighborhood should be part of PPS engagement, especially in historically Black North and Northeast Portland.
