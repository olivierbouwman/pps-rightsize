# Closing schools: How much money does it save, and is it worth it?

- **Citation:** Roza, M., & Dhammani, A. (2024, March 3). Closing schools: How much money does it save, and is it worth it? *EdSource* (commentary).
- **DOI / URL:** https://edsource.org/2024/closing-schools-how-much-money-does-it-save-and-is-it-worth-it/706903
- **Type:** Commentary by researchers (not peer-reviewed; viewpoint)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?

## Setting
U.S. districts facing post-ESSER enrollment-driven revenue loss; example from Los Angeles Unified.

## Method
Rule of thumb from Edunomics Lab school-level finance data. No formal study is cited.

## Key findings
- Edunomics' 'rule of thumb': where a district has under-enrolled schools, closing 1 of every 15 schools saves about 4% of the budget, mostly in labor costs. Labor is 85-95% of budgets, and facility savings are 'nominal'.
- Savings come mainly through attrition: staff from closed schools fill vacancies elsewhere instead of new hires. Without closures, departing principals, nurses and librarians are replaced.
- In LAUSD, average spending is about $23,000 per elementary student in higher-poverty schools, while a few of the smallest schools draw more than $34,000 per student.
- Cautions that a small school that runs cost-efficiently, or shows strong results, may save little if closed.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Op-ed by a fiscal-efficiency research center that generally favors resizing. The 4% figure is a heuristic, not an audited outcome, and is much larger than the realized savings Pew documented.

**Relevance to PPS:** Gives the efficiency case for closures in its strongest form. Its 1-in-15 heuristic can be tested against PPS's own numbers: 11-14 closures out of roughly 80 schools. The gap between this heuristic and Pew's realized figures is a key question for PPS's fiscal case.
