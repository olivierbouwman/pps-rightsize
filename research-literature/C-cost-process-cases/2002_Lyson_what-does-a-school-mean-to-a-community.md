# What Does a School Mean to a Community? Assessing the Social and Economic Benefits of Schools to Rural Villages in New York

- **Citation:** Lyson, T. A. (2002). What does a school mean to a community? Assessing the social and economic benefits of schools to rural villages in New York. *Journal of Research in Rural Education, 17*(3), 131-137.
- **DOI / URL:** https://hdl.handle.net/1813/56033 (Cornell eCommons)
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Rural New York villages with and without a school.

## Method
Cross-sectional comparison of village social and economic indicators by school presence. The full text could not be downloaded, so this summary uses the paper's well-known conclusions.

## Key findings
- Villages with schools had higher housing values, more professional and managerial residents, and better municipal infrastructure than comparable villages without schools. Differences were largest in the smallest villages.
- Argues that schools are anchors of civic and economic life in small communities.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Cross-sectional, not causal: schools may survive in healthier villages. Rural setting.

**Relevance to PPS:** Indirect for urban Portland, but supports treating schools as neighborhood assets with possible property-value and civic effects. Compare Merrall et al. 2023 for urban hedonic evidence and Egelund and Laustsen 2006 for a contrasting view.
