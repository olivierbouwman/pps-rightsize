# Revisiting economies of size in American education: are we any closer to a consensus?

- **Citation:** Andrews, M., Duncombe, W., & Yinger, J. (2002). Revisiting economies of size in American education: Are we any closer to a consensus? *Economics of Education Review, 21*(3), 245-262.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S0272-7757(01)00006-1
- **Type:** Systematic review (literature review of cost-function and production-function studies)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?

## Setting
U.S. literature on district and school size, 1980s-1990s.

## Method
Review of cost-function and production-function studies. The abstract was not available through the APIs used, so this summary relies on the paper's widely cited conclusions and should be checked against the full text.

## Key findings
- Reports evidence of economies of size for small districts. Cost savings are concentrated in moving from very small enrollments (a few hundred students) toward roughly 2,000 students, with possible diseconomies at very large sizes.
- For schools, the review is commonly summarized as finding cost-efficient ranges of roughly 300-500 students for elementary schools and 600-900 for high schools. Verify this against the full text before quoting it.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Paywalled. Mostly cross-sectional evidence from before 2000. School-level cost ranges are inferred from a thin literature and do not include transition costs.

**Relevance to PPS:** Gives a rough benchmark for how small an elementary school can get before per-pupil costs rise steeply. Useful for judging whether PPS's sustainability thresholds are in line with research. Treat the numeric ranges as provisional.
