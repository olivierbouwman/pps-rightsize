# Rethinking the School Closure Research: School Closure as Spatial Injustice

- **Citation:** Tieken, M. C., & Auldridge-Reveles, T. R. (2019). Rethinking the school closure research: School closure as spatial injustice. *Review of Educational Research, 89*(6), 917-953.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654319877151
- **Type:** Systematic/integrative review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
U.S. urban and rural closure research.

## Method
Critical review of the empirical literature on the motivations, distribution and effects of closures, read through a spatial-justice lens.

## Key findings
- Closures are 'unevenly distributed, disproportionately affecting places where poor communities and communities of color live'.
- Closures 'can bring negative effects, harming students and adults and reducing their access to an important educational and community institution'.
- The claimed benefits of closures (academic opportunity, efficiency, equality) are often not realized. The review treats urban and rural closures as one phenomenon.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Interpretive and critical rather than meta-analytic, with no pooled effect sizes. Paywalled.

**Relevance to PPS:** The anchor review for equity analysis of PPS scenarios A and B: check whether closures cluster in lower-income neighborhoods and communities of color, and what nearby school access remains afterward.
