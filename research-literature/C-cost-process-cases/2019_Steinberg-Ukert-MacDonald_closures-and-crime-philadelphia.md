# Schools as places of crime? Evidence from closing chronically underperforming schools

- **Citation:** Steinberg, M. P., Ukert, B., & MacDonald, J. M. (2019). Schools as places of crime? Evidence from closing chronically underperforming schools. *Regional Science and Urban Economics, 77*, 125-140.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.regsciurbeco.2019.04.001
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (difference-in-differences)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. What happens to closed buildings (reuse, vacancy, charters, portfolio models)

## Setting
Philadelphia closures of chronically underperforming schools (2012-2013 era).

## Method
Block-level difference-in-differences on monthly crime.

## Key findings
- Crime declined significantly on blocks where schools closed, driven by violent crime during school hours and concentrated where high schools closed.
- Crime rose on blocks that absorbed more displaced students, but this displacement was smaller than the total reduction, giving a net decrease.

## Limitations / viewpoint
High schools with high misconduct rates; elementary closures in Portland differ. Short-to-medium run.

**Relevance to PPS:** A counterpoint: crime effects depend on school type and what happens to the building. Effects of closing PPS elementary schools are likely to be smaller and tied to vacancy.
