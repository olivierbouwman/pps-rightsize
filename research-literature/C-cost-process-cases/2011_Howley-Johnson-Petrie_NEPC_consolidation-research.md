# Consolidation of Schools and Districts: What the Research Says and What It Means

- **Citation:** Howley, C., Johnson, J., & Petrie, J. (2011). *Consolidation of schools and districts: What the research says and what it means.* Boulder, CO: National Education Policy Center.
- **DOI / URL:** https://nepc.colorado.edu/publication/consolidation-schools-districts (ERIC: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED515900.pdf)
- **Type:** Research review / policy brief (NEPC expert-reviewed, not a journal article)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2011_Howley_NEPC_consolidation-research.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
U.S. research on school and district consolidation, mostly rural.

## Method
Narrative review of cost and achievement research.

## Key findings
- Concludes that 'financial claims about widespread benefits of consolidation are unsubstantiated by contemporary research'. Remaining efficiencies are confined to the very smallest districts.
- Pre/post comparisons of consolidations find that consolidation 'does not on average reduce educational expenditures'. Some studies report higher costs from transportation, management, security and guidance.
- Notes that 'school closures often result in extra costs due to added expenses of transportation, management, and the like.'
- Recommends questioning claimed savings and considering alternatives such as shared services and cooperative purchasing.

## Limitations / viewpoint
NEPC is skeptical of consolidation and of market-based reform. The review leans on rural evidence and is weaker on urban within-district closures.

**Relevance to PPS:** A counterweight to assuming that fewer buildings automatically means lower costs. It flags transportation and middle-management growth as cost lines PPS should model explicitly.
