# Charterization, Gentrification, and the Geography of Opening and Closing Schools in Washington, DC

- **Citation:** Good, R. M. (2025). Charterization, gentrification, and the geography of opening and closing schools in Washington, DC. *Education and Urban Society, 57*(1), 40-62.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00131245241265098
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. What happens to closed buildings (reuse, vacancy, charters, portfolio models)

## Setting
Washington DC, charter and district school openings and closures, 1997-2017, plus a neighborhood case.

## Method
Citywide spatial description combined with a qualitative neighborhood case.

## Key findings
- Maps how opening and closing of both charter and DCPS schools intersected with gentrification and neighborhood change, during a period when DC's charter sector expanded and district buildings were transferred or leased.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Descriptive. The abstract does not give effect sizes.

**Relevance to PPS:** Context for the DC 2008 and 2013 closures. DC law favoring charter leases of surplus buildings filled many of them (see Pew 2013), illustrating how disposition rules shape the long-run school landscape.
