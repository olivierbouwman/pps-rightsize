# Shuttering Schools in the Gateway City: School District Viability and Black Community Relations After Mass K-12 School Closures in St. Louis, MO

- **Citation:** Duncan-Shippy, E. M. (2023). Shuttering schools in the Gateway City: School district viability and Black community relations after mass K-12 school closures in St. Louis, MO. *Peabody Journal of Education, 98*(2), 223-249.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/0161956X.2023.2191569
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (mixed methods)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. District case studies, 2008-2026

## Setting
St. Louis Public Schools, 1990-2020: more than 40% of schools closed because of steep enrollment decline.

## Method
Federal and district data plus local news coverage.

## Key findings
- Closures concentrated in majority-Black neighborhoods, with 'limited evidence of benefits to youth and communities'.
- Desegregation transfers, charter expansion and administrative restructuring each triggered waves of closures.
- Argues that recurrent closures strain Black community relations and 'threaten district viability', so closures can accelerate the exit they respond to.
- Update (news, STLPR, June 29, 2026): SLPS, with 62 schools and a $41M deficit, released 'Future Ready SLPS' draft options to close 15-22 schools by 2027-28. A vote expected in August was delayed (AOL/STLtoday headline, Sept. 9, 2026).

## Limitations / viewpoint
Single district. Mixed-methods synthesis, partly from news sources.

**Relevance to PPS:** A cautionary long-run case: repeated rounds of closures without stabilized enrollment. PPS should ask whether its plan is a one-time reset with growth strategies or the first of several rounds.
