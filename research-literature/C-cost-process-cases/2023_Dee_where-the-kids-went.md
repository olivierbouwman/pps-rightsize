# Where the Kids Went: Nonpublic Schooling and Demographic Change during the Pandemic Exodus from Public Schools

- **Citation:** Dee, T. S. (2023). Where the kids went: Nonpublic schooling and demographic change during the pandemic exodus from public schools. *Teachers College Record, 125*(6), 119-129.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/01614681231190201
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (research note)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Declining enrollment nationally

## Setting
U.S. states, 2019-20 to 2021-22.

## Method
Public enrollment combined with newly collected state private-school and homeschool data and Census school-age population estimates.

## Key findings
- Public K-12 enrollment fell by more than 1.2 million.
- Homeschool enrollment grew 30% and private enrollment 4%.
- Homeschooling and population loss each explain about 26% of the public decline, and private schools about 14%. More than a third is unexplained.

## Limitations / viewpoint
State-level and descriptive; homeschool data are incomplete.

**Relevance to PPS:** Decomposes enrollment loss into demographic and exit components. PPS's projections should separate the two, because closures can affect exit but not births.
