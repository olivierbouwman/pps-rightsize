- **Citation:** Research for Action. (2013). *School closings policy* [Issue brief]. Philadelphia: Research for Action.
- **DOI / URL:** https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED553148.pdf
- **Type:** Research brief (not peer-reviewed)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 4. District case studies, 2008-2026
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2013_RFA_school-closings-policy-brief.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Philadelphia ahead of its March 2013 vote (29 proposed closures), with comparisons to Pittsburgh, Chicago, DC and NYC.

## Method
Synthesis of research and district experience.

## Key findings
- About 70 large or mid-sized U.S. cities closed schools in the prior decade, averaging 11 buildings per closure round.
- Pittsburgh cut its buildings from 93 to 54 after 1997 but continued to face financial and academic pressure.
- Savings come mostly from non-teaching personnel. Teacher layoffs rarely accompany closures, and Philadelphia's plan did not include them.
- Displaced students do better only when sent to higher-performing schools, but seats in such schools are scarce, and in Chicago reaching them meant traveling about 3.5 miles on average.
- The brief describes the DC Auditor's cost finding as 'exceeding $40 million'; the audit itself says $39.5M.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Secondary synthesis.

**Relevance to PPS:** A compact, Philadelphia-focused summary of the cost and achievement evidence. Its point that limited high-quality seats constrain the benefits of closure applies directly to PPS receiving-school choices.
