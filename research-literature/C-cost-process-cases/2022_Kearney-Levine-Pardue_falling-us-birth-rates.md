# The Puzzle of Falling US Birth Rates since the Great Recession

- **Citation:** Kearney, M. S., Levine, P. B., & Pardue, L. (2022). The puzzle of falling US birth rates since the Great Recession. *Journal of Economic Perspectives, 36*(1), 151-176.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.36.1.151 (working paper: https://doi.org/10.3386/w29286)
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed (review/empirical)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Declining enrollment nationally

## Setting
U.S. births, 2007 onward.

## Method
Decomposition of fertility trends and tests of economic and policy explanations.

## Key findings
- U.S. birth rates fell about 20% after 2007, across nearly all demographic groups.
- Measurable economic and policy factors explain little of the decline. The authors point to shifting priorities of recent cohorts and expect low fertility to persist.

## Limitations / viewpoint
National; does not model local migration or housing costs, which matter for Portland.

**Relevance to PPS:** The demographic driver behind falling kindergarten cohorts. It supports treating part of PPS's decline as structural, not cyclical.
