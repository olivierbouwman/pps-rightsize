# Synthesis: costs, process, equity and peer-district experience

*Topic C research base for the PPS rightsizing analysis (11-14 closures; board vote December 2026). Compiled 2026-10-07. Sources marked [NPR] are not peer-reviewed (reports, briefs, an audit, commentary or news). All file names refer to `research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/`.*

## 1. Do closures save money, and how much compared with projections?

The research points to real but modest savings that usually fall short of projections.

- **Operating economies of size are concentrated in very small units.** For rural New York district mergers, operating savings per pupil were large, but one-time adjustment and capital costs cut net savings to 14-32% (`2007_Duncombe-Yinger_does-consolidation-cut-costs.md`). Reviews find that the cost curve flattens at moderate enrollments (`2002_Andrews-Duncombe-Yinger_economies-of-size-review.md`). An NEPC review [NPR, skeptical viewpoint] finds broad savings claims unsupported and notes that closures add transportation and management costs (`2011_Howley-Johnson-Petrie_NEPC_consolidation-research.md`).
- **Realized urban savings were small and below forecast.** Across six districts, short-run savings averaged "well under $1 million per school" a year. Milwaukee realized $6.6M against a $10M projection, and DC about $16.7M against $23M. Vacant buildings cost $1-3M a year to maintain (`2011_Pew_closing-public-schools-six-districts.md` [NPR]). DC's auditor found the 2008 closures cost $17.7M in cash ($39.5M including asset write-downs), against $9.7M first reported (`2012_DC-Auditor_closure-consolidation-23-schools.md` [NPR]).
- **The largest savings came with staff reductions.** Pittsburgh and Kansas City saved the most by pairing closures with layoffs (279 and about 700 positions) (`2011_Pew...`, `2012_Esselman-Lee-Gwin-Rounds_kansas-city-rightsizing.md` [NPR]). The efficiency view is that closing 1 in 15 under-enrolled schools saves about 4% of a budget through attrition (`2024_Roza-Dhammani_Edunomics_closing-schools-savings.md` [NPR, commentary]). That heuristic is well above most documented outcomes.
- **Recent projections are small relative to deficits.** Oakland projected about 2% of revenue and Jeffco (CO) about 1.4% (`2023_Hahnel-Marchitello_PACE_centering-equity-closure-process.md` [NPR]). Seattle's projected savings fell from $31.5M to about $5M as its list shrank, against a deficit of about $100M (`2024_KUOW_seattle-withdraws-closure-plan.md` [news]). Minneapolis says 16 closures would net only $0.7-2.3M because it would reinvest in the remaining schools (`2026_CBS-Minnesota_minneapolis-closures-reallocate-not-save.md` [news]).
- **Building sales rarely produce windfalls.** Most sales brought $200K-$1M (`2013_Pew_shuttered-public-schools-building-reuse.md` [NPR]). Philadelphia expected more than $60M and received about $24M (`2023_Hahnel-Marchitello...`).

**Implication:** a credible PPS savings estimate should show the realistic final list (not the maximal list), separate staffing from facilities savings, and itemize one-time costs: moving, receiving-school upgrades, transportation, and holding vacant buildings. It should also net out any students who leave the district, since lost enrollment leaves fixed costs behind (`2018_Ladd-Singleton_charter-fiscal-externalities.md`).

## 2. Who bears the costs?

The peer-reviewed evidence is consistent: closures concentrate in lower-income neighborhoods, in neighborhoods of color, and especially in Black neighborhoods (`2019_Tieken-Auldridge-Reveles_closure-as-spatial-injustice.md`; `2021_Brazil-Candipan_neighborhood-context-of-closures-us.md`; `2017_Good_spatialized-inequality-philadelphia-closures.md`; `2026_Tieken-Murray_who-gets-closed-arkansas.md`). In California, closure odds rose about 25% per 10-point increase in Black enrollment after controls (`2023_Pearman-Luong-Greene_PACE...` [NPR working paper]). Chicago closures were predicted by race and proximity to downtown and to charters, not only by the official criteria (`2020_Weber-Farmer-Donoghue_predicting-closures-chicago.md`). Closures lengthen trips to the nearest open school, most for Black neighborhoods (`2024_Brazil_closures-spatial-access-10-cities.md`; `2017_Lee-Lubienski_closures-equity-of-access-chicago.md`).

In inner Northeast Portland, gentrification and school choice drained enrollment, and the resulting under-enrollment then made schools closure targets (`2017_Serbulo_closing-schools-gentrification-inner-ne-portland.md`). Utilization metrics can therefore carry forward past displacement.

Community effects are mixed and depend on context. Proximity to schools is reflected in house prices (`2023_Merrall-Higgins-Paez...`). Population loss accelerated after rural closures in Finland (`2021_Lehtonen...`), though Danish evidence frames closure as a symptom rather than a cause of decline (`2006_Egelund-Laustsen...`). Closures reduced trust in local officials and support for incumbents (`2023_Nyholt...`; `2023_Isaksson...`). No U.S. before-and-after study of public-school closures and house prices was found. That is a gap.

## 3. Which process features predicted better outcomes and less backlash?

The evidence here is mostly descriptive [NPR] and qualitative. Practices associated with greater acceptance were:

- making the case for downsizing early;
- using neutral facilitators;
- publishing clear, quantifiable criteria, with community input on the criteria;
- adjusting the list when shown compelling evidence, such as walkability;
- voting once on the whole plan (`2011_Pew...`).

Recommendations from the equity literature are:

- publish demographic and outcome data for closing and receiving schools;
- conduct a formal equity impact analysis (California requires one for districts in fiscal distress);
- guarantee displaced students seats in higher-quality schools (`2023_Hahnel-Marchitello...`).

Displaced students fare better only when they move to stronger schools (`2012_Engberg-Gill-Zamarro-Zimmer...`; `2013_RFA_school-closings-policy-brief.md` [NPR]). Chicago's compressed timeline produced chaotic moves, under-supported receiving schools, and math losses that persisted four years (`2018_Gordon-UChicago-Consortium...` [NPR]).

Backlash follows when officials present closures as inevitable and data-driven without making the data checkable (`2019_Syeed_counterframes-dc-closures.md`), and when the process itself is read as devaluing Black communities (`2019_McWilliams-Kitzmiller...`; `2018_Ewing_ghosts-in-the-schoolyard.md`).

## 4. What happened to the buildings?

Vacancy is common and long-lasting. In 2012, 301 sites in 12 cities were still unsold, and more than 40% of reused buildings went to charter schools (`2013_Pew_shuttered...`). Charter openings clustered as district schools closed in Detroit (`2019_Green-Sanchez-Castro...`) and tracked gentrification in DC (`2024_Good_charterization-gentrification-dc.md`).

Evidence on crime is mixed. Brazil found short-run crime declines near vacant or repurposed Chicago buildings but increases around merged receiving schools (`2020_Brazil...`). A 2026 study found shootings rose about 10% near closed, long-vacant Chicago schools (`2026_Statchen...`). In Philadelphia, closing troubled high schools reduced crime overall (`2019_Steinberg-Ukert-MacDonald...`).

Portfolio-strategy advocates treat closure as a quality tool (`2016_Lake-CRPE...` [NPR, advocate]), and critics see it as market restructuring (`2015_Bulkley-Henig...`). PPS's rationale is enrollment-driven, but its building-disposition policy will shape how the plan is perceived.

## 5. Lessons from comparable districts

- **Retreats are common.** Oakland's 2022 closures were partly rescinded after the 2022 election changed the board (`2023_EdSource...` [news]). Seattle withdrew its plan in 2024, San Francisco paused, and Chicago barred closures until 2027 (`2025_The74_school-closure-paradox.md` [news]). Nationally, closure rates fell even as enrollment declined.
- **Completed plans faced follow-on pressure.** Denver closed 7 schools, then faced a lawsuit and adopted a four-year gap between closure rounds (`2024_Denverite...` [news]). Pittsburgh's plan passed in 2026 only after the board demanded cost and implementation detail and added a transition year (`2026_PublicSource...` [news]).
- **A good process is not enough on its own.** Kansas City's well-received process did not prevent a loss of accreditation soon afterward. St. Louis's repeated rounds of closures eroded Black community trust and district viability (`2023_Duncan-Shippy...`).
- **Demographic pressure is structural.** Birth rates fell about 20% after 2007 (`2022_Kearney-Levine-Pardue...`). Losses concentrate in entry grades (`2023_Schueler-Miller...`). Oregon had the largest share of "missing" students nationally (`2025_Council-Goulas-Monachou_Brookings...` [NPR]), and Oregon's urban decline was driven by White students (`2025_Narayanan...`).

**Caveats:** most cost evidence is from non-peer-reviewed reports and district self-reports. Many urban studies concern performance-based closures in the 2010s rather than today's enrollment-driven closures (`2025_Anglum...` [NPR]). News sources were used only for 2022-2026 district events.
