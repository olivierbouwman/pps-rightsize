# Rightsizing a School District: A Dramatic Self-Examination Led the Kansas City, Mo., Public Schools to Close Schools and Cut $68 Million from Its Annual Budget

- **Citation:** Esselman, M., Lee-Gwin, R., & Rounds, M. (2012). Rightsizing a school district. *Phi Delta Kappan, 93*(6), 56-61.
- **DOI / URL:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ985091 (abstract record; full text paywalled)
- **Type:** Practitioner article by district officials (not peer-reviewed)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 4. District case studies, 2008-2026

## Setting
Kansas City (MO) Public Schools, 2009-2010. Enrollment fell from about 30,000 (2000) to under 17,000 (2010). The district closed 8 schools in 2009 and 21 in 2010 (29 of 61, about half its schools).

## Method
Account by district leaders (the CFO and others), supplemented here with Pew 2011.

## Key findings
- The district reports cutting $68M from its annual budget. Per Pew 2011, the savings came from closures combined with about 700 layoffs and cuts of $30M in outsourced services, not from closures alone.
- Pew 2011 rated KC's process 'among the best-received': early coalition-building with the business Civic Council, a neutral meeting facilitator, community-generated criteria, school report cards, and a single board vote. One school was spared after parents showed that more than 90% of students walked to school.
- Aftermath (other sources): Pew 2011 reports KC spent nearly $3M a year maintaining vacant buildings. The district lost state accreditation shortly after (effective 2012) and regained full accreditation only in January 2022 (KSHB, 'KCPS regains full accreditation after Missouri state school board vote', Jan. 11, 2022; news headline only, not read in full). KCUR (Mar. 27, 2025) headlined 'How Kansas City lost trust in its public school system'.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Self-report by the officials who led the plan. Accreditation history comes from news headlines and was not verified in full text. The causes of the accreditation loss go well beyond closures.

**Relevance to PPS:** The closest precedent for a large, fast, enrollment-driven rightsizing with a well-regarded process. It shows that a good process does not guarantee lasting recovery: savings depended on layoffs, and vacant buildings remained a cost.
