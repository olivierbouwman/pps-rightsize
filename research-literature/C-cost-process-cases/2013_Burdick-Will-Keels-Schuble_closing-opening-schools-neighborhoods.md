# Closing and Opening Schools: The Association between Neighborhood Characteristics and the Location of New Educational Opportunities in a Large Urban District

- **Citation:** Burdick-Will, J., Keels, M., & Schuble, T. (2013). Closing and opening schools: The association between neighborhood characteristics and the location of new educational opportunities in a large urban district. *Journal of Urban Affairs, 35*(1), 59-80.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/juaf.12004
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Chicago elementary school closings and openings, late 1990s-2000s.

## Method
Models of where schools closed and where new (mostly charter) schools opened, using CPS, Common Core of Data and Census data.

## Key findings
- Schools in disadvantaged neighborhoods were more likely to close, but this was explained by low performance and under-enrollment.
- After controlling for educational demand, new schools were more likely to open in neighborhoods showing signs of socioeconomic revitalization and declining shares of white residents.
- Note on the task anchor 'Burdick-Will 2018': Burdick-Will's 2018 papers (Sociology of Education, on neighborhood violence and achievement; City & Community, on school location and neighborhood boundaries) are not closure studies. This 2013 paper is her main closure study.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Chicago-specific and from the pre-2013 era. Location models are associational.

**Relevance to PPS:** Closures can be 'justified' by enrollment and performance metrics that are themselves tied to neighborhood disadvantage. PPS should also watch where new or relocated programs open.
