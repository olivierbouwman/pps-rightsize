# School Closings in Chicago: Staff and Student Experiences and Academic Outcomes

- **Citation:** Gordon, M. F., de la Torre, M., Cowhy, J. R., Moore, P. T., Sartain, L. S., & Knight, D. (2018). *School closings in Chicago: Staff and student experiences and academic outcomes.* Chicago: UChicago Consortium on School Research.
- **DOI / URL:** https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED589712.pdf
- **Type:** Research report (externally reviewed, not journal peer-reviewed)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 4. District case studies, 2008-2026
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2018_UChicago-Consortium_school-closings-chicago.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Chicago's May 2013 vote to close 47 elementary schools (49 announced, plus one high school program), justified by a reported $1B deficit and underutilization. About 12,000 students were displaced to 48 'welcoming' schools.

## Method
Mixed methods: case studies of six welcoming schools, plus comparison-group analysis of 10,708 closed-school and 13,218 welcoming-school students.

## Key findings
- Process: the list went from 330 'underutilized' schools (Dec. 2012) to 129 (Feb. 2013) to 54 (Mar. 2013), with the vote in late May. That left about two months to move roughly 95 buildings. Staff described the move as chaotic, and promised upgrades were often late or fell short.
- 66% of displaced students went to designated welcoming schools; the rest spread across 311 other schools. About 6% left the district, similar to prior years.
- 21% of welcoming-school students did not return in fall 2013, about 5 points above expected, driven by the 14 welcoming schools that had to relocate into closed buildings.
- Academic: closed-school students lost about 1.5 months in reading and 2 months in math in the announcement year. The math gap persisted for four years. Welcoming-school students had a short-term reading dip. 'Academic outcomes were neutral at best, and negative in some instances.'
- Absences and suspensions were not affected. Safe Passage and one-time extra funds were appreciated, but the extra funds were not sustained.
- Grief, 'us versus them' dynamics, and a lack of integration support were central themes.

## Limitations / viewpoint
One district and a mass-closure design; test-based outcomes. Cost savings were not assessed.

**Relevance to PPS:** The most detailed evidence on receiving-school transitions. For PPS: give more lead time between vote and move, avoid relocating receiving schools into closed buildings where possible, fund multi-year integration supports, and choose higher-performing receiving schools.
