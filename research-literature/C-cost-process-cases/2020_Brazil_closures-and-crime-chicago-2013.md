# Effects of Public School Closures on Crime: The Case of the 2013 Chicago Mass School Closure

- **Citation:** Brazil, N. (2020). Effects of public school closures on crime: The case of the 2013 Chicago mass school closure. *Sociological Science, 7*, 128-151.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.15195/v7.a6
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (quasi-experimental)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. What happens to closed buildings (reuse, vacancy, charters, portfolio models)
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2020_Brazil_closures-crime-chicago.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Chicago, 2013 closures. Closed buildings were classified by what happened next: vacant, repurposed, or merged into an existing school.

## Method
Spatiotemporal comparison of crime around closed and comparison sites.

## Key findings
- Vacancy and repurposing into a non-school use were associated with decreased crime, localized within about 75 meters and fading after a year.
- Merging a closed school into an existing school was associated with increased crime around the receiving school, persisting over time and across larger areas.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Short-run, all-crime measures. A 2026 study using a longer window and shootings (Statchen et al.) finds increases near vacant buildings, so the evidence is mixed.

**Relevance to PPS:** The effects depend on what happens to the building and on concentrating students in receiving schools. Planning for receiving schools, including safe routes and supervision, matters.
