## Setting
12 districts: Philadelphia, Atlanta, Chicago, Cincinnati, Cleveland, Detroit, Kansas City (MO), Milwaukee, Pittsburgh, St. Louis, Tulsa and Washington DC.

## Method
District property records and interviews, 2005-2012.

## Key findings
- The 12 districts sold, leased or reused 267 properties after 2005, but 301 sites were still on the market at the end of 2012, some vacant for more than a decade. Detroit alone had 124.
- Most sale prices were between $200,000 and $1 million, 'frequently well below initial projections'. Cincinnati auctioned 11 buildings and one parcel for $3.5M in total, far below official valuation.
- More than 40% of sold, leased or reused properties went to charter schools, which can further reduce district enrollment. Chicago later moved to bar charter use of its surplus buildings, and Philadelphia gave itself power to cap charter expansion.
- Other reuses included market-rate and subsidized housing, shelters, churches, community centers, offices, daycare and green space.
- Advice: dispose of buildings quickly after vacancy, make information public, enforce buyers' commitments, and coordinate reuse with city planning. Some districts 'mothball' buildings to hold for future enrollment growth.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Descriptive, with district-supplied data. The sample covers Rust Belt and Sun Belt cities with weaker real-estate markets than Portland.

**Relevance to PPS:** Sets realistic expectations: sale revenue is usually modest, carrying costs are real, and charter occupancy is the most common reuse. PPS should decide its disposition policy (sale, lease, mothball, community use) before or alongside the closure vote.
