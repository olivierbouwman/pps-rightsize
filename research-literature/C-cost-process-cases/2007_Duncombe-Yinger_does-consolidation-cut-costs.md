# Does School District Consolidation Cut Costs?

- **Citation:** Duncombe, W., & Yinger, J. (2007). Does school district consolidation cut costs? *Education Finance and Policy, 2*(4), 341-375.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1162/edfp.2007.2.4.341
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?

## Setting
Rural school districts in New York State that consolidated (district-level mergers, not within-district school closures).

## Method
Cost-function estimation comparing consolidating and non-consolidating districts over time; separates operating economies of size from one-time and capital adjustment costs.

## Key findings
- Doubling enrollment cuts operating cost per pupil by 61.7% for a 300-pupil district and 49.6% for a 1,500-pupil district, all else equal.
- Adjustment costs, especially capital spending (new or upgraded buildings), are large: net savings fall to 31.5% (300-pupil district) and 14.4% (1,500-pupil district).
- Authors conclude consolidation makes fiscal sense mainly for very small districts, and warn states not to subsidize unwarranted capital projects.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Applies to small rural districts merging, not a large urban district closing schools inside one district. Savings shrink quickly with size; a 45,000-student district like PPS sits far beyond the range where these economies of size were estimated.

**Relevance to PPS:** Main peer-reviewed source on the gap between operating savings and one-time and capital costs. Its warning about capital spending applies directly to PPS receiving-school upgrades: those costs can absorb much of the projected savings.
