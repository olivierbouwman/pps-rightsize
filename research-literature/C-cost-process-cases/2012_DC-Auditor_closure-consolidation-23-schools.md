# Audit of the Closure and Consolidation of 23 D.C. Public Schools

- **Citation:** Office of the District of Columbia Auditor. (2012, September 6). *Audit of the closure and consolidation of 23 D.C. public schools.* Washington, DC.
- **DOI / URL:** http://dcauditor.org/sites/default/files/DCA192012.pdf (retrieved via Internet Archive)
- **Type:** Government audit (not peer-reviewed)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2012_DC-Auditor_closure-consolidation-23-schools.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Washington DC's 2008 closure and consolidation of 23 DCPS schools under Mayor Fenty and Chancellor Rhee.

## Method
Financial audit of closure-related costs, fiscal years 2007-2010.

## Key findings
- The facilities agency (OPEFM) reported $9.7M in capital costs to the DC Council. The Auditor found $8.0M more in unreported costs: $3.1M for moving and relocation, $3.3M to demolish two buildings, $1.5M to bus displaced students to receiving schools, and security.
- Closures also triggered $21.8M in capital-asset impairment losses on eight buildings.
- Total cost was $39.5M including the non-cash write-downs, about four times the $9.7M first reported. Cash outlays alone were $17.7M, about 1.8 times the reported figure. Separately, Pew 2011 reports DC claimed about $16.7M a year in savings against a $23M projection.
- $5.8M was paid to contractors without supporting documentation, and inventories of furniture and equipment were incomplete.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Audits cost, not savings. Impairment losses are accounting write-downs, not cash outlays.

**Relevance to PPS:** Concrete evidence that one-time costs such as moving, busing, demolition, security and asset write-downs are routinely under-reported. PPS's fiscal case should list these lines explicitly.
