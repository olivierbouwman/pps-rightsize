# Closing schools is like 'taking away part of my body': the impact of gentrification on neighborhood public schools in inner Northeast Portland

- **Citation:** Serbulo, L. C. (2017). Closing schools is like 'taking away part of my body': The impact of gentrification on neighborhood public schools in inner Northeast Portland. *Belgeo, 2-3.*
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.4000/belgeo.19835
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (ethnography)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community

## Setting
Inner Northeast Portland (Jefferson cluster), a historically African American area that has been gentrifying since the mid-1990s, and PPS decisions to close, merge or redesign schools.

## Method
'Politically engaged educational ethnography' with enrollment and transfer data.

## Key findings
- As investment flowed into the neighborhood, its schools lost students and resources. Longtime Black families were displaced, and newer white, middle-class residents used PPS transfer and choice options to opt out of neighborhood schools.
- Declining enrollment then made the schools targets for closure or redesign. The author reports that nearly every inner-NE school was proposed for closure at some point.
- The article reports that Jefferson High School drew a small fraction of its attendance-area students, far below the district average capture rate (about 25% versus 68% as summarized; verify against the full text).
- Community resistance prevented the closure of Jefferson High, which was rebuilt with partner investments (for example, Self Enhancement Inc.).

## Limitations / viewpoint
Advocacy-engaged ethnography; numbers should be checked against the article. The account ends in the mid-2010s.

**Relevance to PPS:** Directly about PPS. Shows how choice and transfer policy and gentrification can turn under-enrollment into a closure criterion that falls on historically Black neighborhoods. Essential context for any scenario affecting North and Northeast Portland.
