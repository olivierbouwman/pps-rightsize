# 'It just doesn't add up': Disrupting official arguments for urban school closures with counterframes

- **Citation:** Syeed, E. (2019). 'It just doesn't add up': Disrupting official arguments for urban school closures with counterframes. *Education Policy Analysis Archives, 27*(110).
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.14507/epaa.27.4240
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed empirical (qualitative frame analysis)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. Equity, race, place and community
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2019_Syeed_counterframes-dc.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Washington DC's 2013 closure round (15 schools proposed after the 2008 round).

## Method
Critical frame analysis of qualitative data.

## Key findings
- Officials framed closures as inevitable, data-driven and politically neutral.
- Communities built counterframes that disputed inevitability, questioned the data, and alleged hidden agendas (for example, charter expansion and real estate).

## Limitations / viewpoint
Qualitative; one city.

**Relevance to PPS:** Predicts the counterarguments PPS will face ('it doesn't add up'). Transparent, checkable data on savings, capacity and enrollment projections are the best defense.
