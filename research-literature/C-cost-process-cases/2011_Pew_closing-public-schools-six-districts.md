# Closing Public Schools in Philadelphia: Lessons from Six Urban Districts

- **Citation:** Dowdall, E. (2011, October 19). *Closing public schools in Philadelphia: Lessons from six urban districts.* Philadelphia: The Pew Charitable Trusts.
- **DOI / URL:** https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/reports/2011/10/19/closing-public-schools-in-philadelphia-lessons-from-six-urban-districts
- **Type:** Research report (not peer-reviewed)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 1. Do closures and consolidations save money?
- **Local full text (open access / publicly posted):** research-literature/C-cost-process-cases/pdf/2011_Pew_closing-public-schools-six-districts.pdf (and matching `.txt` from `pdftotext -layout`)

## Setting
Chicago, Detroit, Kansas City (MO), Milwaukee, Pittsburgh and Washington DC, each of which closed at least 20 buildings in the 2000s.

## Method
Document review and interviews with district officials and consultants. District-reported savings were not independently audited.

## Key findings
- Savings were 'relatively small in the context of big-city school-district budgets'. Average annual savings in the short run were 'well under $1 million per school'.
- Savings fell short of projections: Milwaukee projected $10M a year from 20 closures but realized $6.6M; Washington DC projected $23M a year from 23 closures but reported about $16.7M.
- The largest savings came when closures were combined with layoffs. Pittsburgh saved about $14.7M a year from 22 closures plus 279 layoffs, and Kansas City combined 29 closures with about 700 layoffs and outsourcing cuts. Detroit reported $35M a year from 59 buildings.
- New costs offset savings: Milwaukee spent more than $1M a year maintaining vacant buildings, Pittsburgh $2M and Kansas City nearly $3M. At least 200 school buildings stood vacant across the six cities, 92 of them in Detroit.
- Process: public acceptance rose when officials made the case for downsizing early, used outside experts or facilitators, set clear quantifiable criteria, adjusted the list in response to compelling evidence, and voted on the whole plan at once. Kansas City's process was 'among the best-received'.
- Political fallout: in DC, backlash over closures contributed to Mayor Fenty's 2010 defeat and Chancellor Rhee's resignation. In Chicago it led to a 2011 state law requiring earlier notice, more public input and transparent criteria.

## Limitations / viewpoint
Descriptive, based on district self-reports and interviews, with no counterfactual. Written for Philadelphia ahead of its 2013 closures. Pew favors managed downsizing but is not an advocacy group.

**Relevance to PPS:** The most-cited benchmark on actual versus projected closure savings, and largely consistent with later evidence. Its list of process features is a practical checklist for PPS's December 2026 vote.
