# Revisiting economies of size in American education: Are we any closer to a consensus?

- **Citation:** Andrews, M., Duncombe, W., & Yinger, J. (2002). Revisiting economies of size in American education: Are we any closer to a consensus?. *Economics of Education Review, 21(3), 245-262*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S0272-7757(01)00006-1
- **Type:** Systematic review (of cost-function and production-function studies)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** Three decades of cost and production studies suggest economies of size at the low end of the size range; moderate-sized schools appear to balance cost savings against the drawbacks of large size; little direct evaluation of consolidation exists.

## Setting and sample
U.S. cost-function and production-function literature on district and school size.

## Method
Review of econometric studies.

## Key findings
- Cost studies find economies of size, particularly for very small districts, with savings tapering as size grows.
- At the school level the review is commonly cited as concluding that moderate-sized elementary schools (roughly 300-500 students) and high schools (roughly 600-900) may optimally balance economies of size against potential negative effects of large size. This summary could not be verified against the full text here.
- Very little program-evaluation research exists on school consolidation itself (ERIC abstract).

## Limitations
Underlying cost studies rely on cross-sectional variation. School-level optimal ranges are approximate. Consolidation adjustment costs are often ignored.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the standard cost-side citation. The size ranges it is cited for are compatible with PPS's 360 K-5 floor, but its high-school range (600-900) is well below PPS's 1,100 floor, and it warns that direct evidence on consolidation savings is thin.
