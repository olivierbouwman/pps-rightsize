# The effect of primary school size on academic achievement

- **Citation:** Gershenson, S., & Langbein, L. (2015). The effect of primary school size on academic achievement. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 37(1 Suppl.), 135S-155S*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373715576075
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (value-added with fixed effects)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** North Carolina grades 4-5: no average causal effect of school size, but larger grade cohorts harm socioeconomically disadvantaged students and students with learning disabilities (about -0.015 SD per 10 extra students in the grade).

## Setting and sample
North Carolina, student-level longitudinal administrative data, 4th and 5th graders.

## Method
Value-added models exploiting transitory changes in school size, with school-specific trends and teacher-by-school, student and school-by-year fixed effects.

## Key findings
- On average, no causal relationship between school size and achievement.
- Two subgroups are significantly harmed by larger size: socioeconomically disadvantaged students and students with learning disabilities.
- The largest effects are for students with learning disabilities: a 10-student increase in grade size lowers their math and reading achievement by about 0.015 SD.

## Limitations
Identifies effects of transitory size changes within a range, not large structural changes. Effects per 10 students are small, though consolidation can add far more than 10 students per grade.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the most direct size evidence for students with disabilities. Consolidations that enlarge K-5 grade cohorts carry a measurable cost for students with learning disabilities, which matters for Sunnyside, where 23.5% of students receive special education. Note that the average effect is null.
