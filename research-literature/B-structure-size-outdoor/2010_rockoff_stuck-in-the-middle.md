# Stuck in the middle: Impacts of grade configuration in public schools

- **Citation:** Rockoff, J. E., & Lockwood, B. B. (2010). Stuck in the middle: Impacts of grade configuration in public schools. *Journal of Public Economics, 94(11-12), 1051-1061*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2010.06.017
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Entering a middle school in grade 6 or 7 causes a sharp, persistent drop in math and English achievement relative to K-8 peers (about 0.14-0.18 SD in the entry year), larger for low achievers; absences rise.

## Setting and sample
New York City public schools. Students followed from grade 3 through grade 8 in the 2000s; most NYC students move to a middle school in grade 6 or 7, while a minority attend K-8 schools.

## Method
Student-level panel (event-study style) comparing within-student achievement trajectories around the year of middle-school entry with those of students who stay in K-8 schools; timing of entry is driven by the grade span of the school attended in grade 3. Also examines absences and parent/student survey ratings.

## Key findings
- Entering middle school in grade 6: math falls about 0.18 SD and English about 0.16 SD in the first year. Entering in grade 7: math falls about 0.17 SD and English about 0.14 SD. (Figures as reported in the authors' summary of this study, Education Next, Fall 2010.)
- The gap does not close: after three years in a middle school, grade-6 entrants are about 0.17 SD (math) and 0.14 SD (English) below expected K-8 performance in grade 8.
- First-year drops were about 50% larger, and cumulative deficits more than 200% larger, for students who started below the citywide median in grade 3.
- Absences rose at middle-school entry, adding almost two days of missed school per year by grade 8.
- Parents rated middle schools lower on safety and educational quality than K-8 schools. Per-pupil spending was similar, so resources do not explain the gap.

## Limitations
Only one (very large, complex) district. Students could not be followed past grade 8, so the study cannot show whether middle schools ease the move to high school (Schwerdt & West 2013 address this). The middle-school effect bundles the transition itself, grade-cohort size and school quality. The design assumes families do not pick K-8 schools in anticipation of the drop.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the strongest direct evidence on K-8 versus 6-8 configurations. Converting seven K-8s to K-5 adds a grade-6 transition, which this study associates with lasting achievement losses and more absences, concentrated among lower-achieving students. PPS would need to show its receiving middle schools can offset this.
