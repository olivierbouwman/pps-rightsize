# School size, school climate, and student performance (School Improvement Research Series, Close-Up #20)

- **Citation:** Cotton, K. (1996). School size, school climate, and student performance (School Improvement Research Series, Close-Up #20). *Northwest Regional Educational Laboratory (NWREL), Portland, OR*.
- **DOI / URL:** ERIC ED397476 (contained in School Improvement Research Series X, 1995-96): https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED397476
- **Type:** Report - not peer-reviewed (research synthesis)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** Portland-based NWREL synthesis: small schools are at least equal on achievement and better on attitudes, behavior, attendance, dropout, participation and belonging, especially for low-income and minority students.

## Setting and sample
Research synthesis prepared by the Portland-based regional educational laboratory (now Education Northwest).

## Method
Narrative review of school-size literature available through the mid-1990s.

## Key findings
- Small schools show achievement at least equal to large schools.
- Small schools show better student attitudes, social behavior, attendance and lower dropout, greater extracurricular participation and a stronger sense of belonging.
- Benefits are especially pronounced for low-SES and minority students.
- Widely quoted as suggesting roughly 300-400 students for elementary and 400-800 for secondary schools. This figure is from secondary citations and was not verified against the original text here.

## Limitations
Not peer-reviewed; older, mostly correlational literature; no formal quality appraisal.

## Relevance to PPS
This is a locally produced (Portland) synthesis often cited in Oregon size debates. Its suggested sizes are below or near PPS's new floors.
