# Can interdistrict choice boost student achievement? The case of Connecticut's interdistrict magnet school program

- **Citation:** Bifulco, R., Cobb, C. D., & Bell, C. (2009). Can interdistrict choice boost student achievement? The case of Connecticut's interdistrict magnet school program. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 31(4), 323-345*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373709340917
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (lottery-based plus value-added)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Focus-option, magnet and lottery schools

**Headline:** Lottery and value-added estimates: Connecticut interdistrict magnet high schools raised central-city students' math and reading; magnet middle schools raised reading.

## Setting and sample
Connecticut interdistrict magnet schools serving central-city and suburban students.

## Method
Lottery-based random assignment for a subset of schools; value-added and fixed-effects estimators for a broader set.

## Key findings
- Magnet schools gave central-city students access to less racially and economically isolated schools.
- Interdistrict magnet high schools had positive effects on central-city students' math and reading.
- Interdistrict magnet middle schools had positive effects on reading.

## Limitations
Lottery estimates cover only a few schools; desegregation-focused regional context.

## Relevance to PPS
This is evidence that theme or magnet schools can produce achievement gains, especially for disadvantaged students. It is relevant when weighing the loss of a focus option.
