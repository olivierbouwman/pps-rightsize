# The effect of school choice on participants: Evidence from randomized lotteries

- **Citation:** Cullen, J. B., Jacob, B. A., & Levitt, S. (2006). The effect of school choice on participants: Evidence from randomized lotteries. *Econometrica, 74(5), 1191-1230*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00702.x
- **Type:** Randomized (admission lotteries)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Focus-option, magnet and lottery schools

**Headline:** Chicago high-school choice lotteries (including magnet programs): winners attended schools with higher-achieving peers but saw little academic benefit; self-reported discipline incidents and arrests fell.

## Setting and sample
Chicago Public Schools high-school admission lotteries.

## Method
Comparison of lottery winners and losers.

## Key findings
- Winners attended schools that were better on several dimensions, including peer achievement and attainment.
- Little evidence of systematic benefit on traditional academic measures.
- Improvements on some non-traditional outcomes: fewer self-reported disciplinary incidents and lower arrest rates.

## Limitations
High-school level; choice programs vary; outcomes may depend on fit with the chosen school.

## Relevance to PPS
This cautions against assuming chosen or themed schools raise test scores. The benefits may instead appear in behavior and engagement.
