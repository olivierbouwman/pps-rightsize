# Grade configuration is associated with school-level standardized test pass rates for sixth-, seventh-, and eighth-grade students

- **Citation:** Malone, M., Cornell, D. G., & Shukla, K. (2019). Grade configuration is associated with school-level standardized test pass rates for sixth-, seventh-, and eighth-grade students. *School Effectiveness and School Improvement, 31(2), 289-305 (online 2019)*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/09243453.2019.1654526
- **Type:** Descriptive / correlational (latent growth modeling)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Statewide school-level data: grade-6 and grade-7 pass rates were higher in elementary-configured schools (e.g., K-7) than in middle schools (grade-7 math 83.1% vs 69.2%).

## Setting and sample
Statewide school-level standardized test data (the authors are at the University of Virginia; the state is Virginia per the article), across three years.

## Method
Latent growth modeling of school-level reading and math pass rates by grade configuration, separately for grades 6, 7 and 8.

## Key findings
- Grade 6: elementary-configured schools had higher pass rates than middle schools (reading 78.9% vs 72.0%; math 82.5% vs 76.3%).
- Grade 7: higher in elementary-configured schools (reading 78.5% vs 75.9%; math 83.1% vs 69.2%).
- Grade 8: higher in middle schools than in schools configured with high-school grades (e.g., 8-12).
- The authors conclude students benefit from remaining in elementary school through at least grade 7.

## Limitations
School-level and observational; pass rates rather than scale scores; selection into configurations is not addressed causally.

## Relevance to PPS
This is recent, consistent descriptive evidence that keeping grades 6-7 in elementary-style settings is associated with better outcomes.
