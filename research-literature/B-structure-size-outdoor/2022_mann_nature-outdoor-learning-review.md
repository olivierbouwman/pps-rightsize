# Getting out of the classroom and into nature: A systematic review of nature-specific outdoor learning on school children's learning and development

- **Citation:** Mann, J., Gray, T., Truong, S., Brymer, E., Passy, R., Ho, S., Sahlberg, P., Ward, K., Bentsen, P., Curry, C., & Cowper, R. (2022). Getting out of the classroom and into nature: A systematic review of nature-specific outdoor learning on school children's learning and development. *Frontiers in Public Health, 10, 877058*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2022.877058
- **Type:** Systematic review (narrative synthesis, PROSPERO-registered)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2022_mann_nature-outdoor-learning-review.txt`

**Headline:** 147 studies (2000-2020): consistent socio-emotional, engagement and wellbeing benefits; academic results for curricular outdoor lessons are 'mixed'; research quality moderate, with comparison groups in only 44% of quantitative studies.

## Setting and sample
School-aged children worldwide (most studies from the U.S.), in nature-specific outdoor learning: adventure education, school gardens, field trips, curricular lessons outdoors and residential camps.

## Method
Systematic search of nine databases (17,886 records) yielding 147 studies (54 qualitative, 59 quantitative, 34 mixed methods); risk-of-bias and quality rating; narrative synthesis (no pooled effect sizes).

## Key findings
- Reported benefits include increased engagement and ownership of learning, development of social and collaborative skills, improved self-concept, and some evidence of academic improvement.
- For curricular lessons in the local outdoors, the academic findings are 'mixed'. Some higher-quality studies found gains in math, language or science at some time points but not others.
- Comparison groups were used in 44% of quantitative, 35% of mixed-methods and 2% of qualitative studies. Average quality was moderate (about 5 on a -10 to +10 scale).
- One school-garden study of high-school students with disruptive behavior found improved attendance and pass rates.

## Limitations
No meta-analysis. Quality rated by a single reviewer per study. English-language only, no grey literature. Heterogeneous interventions.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the best current overview for an environmental K-8 like Sunnyside. It supports claims about engagement, social and emotional benefits and wellbeing, but not strong claims about test-score gains.
