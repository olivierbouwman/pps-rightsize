# The effects of class size on student achievement: New evidence from population variation

- **Citation:** Hoxby, C. M. (2000). The effects of class size on student achievement: New evidence from population variation. *Quarterly Journal of Economics, 115(4), 1239-1285*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1162/003355300555060
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (natural population variation, regression discontinuity)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. Class size

**Headline:** Connecticut: natural variation in cohort size and class-size-rule cutoffs showed no significant effect of class size, ruling out even modest effects.

## Setting and sample
649 Connecticut elementary schools.

## Method
Natural variation in grade cohort size and discrete jumps from maximum and minimum class-size rules.

## Key findings
- Class size does not have a statistically significant effect on achievement.
- Effects of 2-4% of an SD for a 10% class-size reduction are ruled out.
- Related: Angrist, Lavy, Leder-Luis & Shany (2019, AER: Insights 1(3):309-324, https://doi.org/10.1257/aeri.20180120) find no class-size effects in newer Israeli data, reversing earlier Maimonides-rule findings.

## Limitations
Identifies effects of moderate, natural variation in a relatively advantaged state; teachers may not change practice for small, temporary changes.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the counterweight to STAR. Modest class-size increases from absorbing students may have small or no test effects on average, though these designs say less about high-poverty classrooms.
