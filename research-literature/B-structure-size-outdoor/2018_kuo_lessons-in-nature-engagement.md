# Do lessons in nature boost subsequent classroom engagement? Refueling students in flight

- **Citation:** Kuo, M., Browning, M. H. E. M., & Penner, M. L. (2018). Do lessons in nature boost subsequent classroom engagement? Refueling students in flight. *Frontiers in Psychology, 8, 2253*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.02253
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (matched within-class comparisons)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2018_kuo_lessons-in-nature-engagement.txt`

**Headline:** Two third-grade classrooms: after outdoor lessons, students were more engaged in the next indoor lesson; teacher 'redirects' were nearly halved.

## Setting and sample
Two third-grade classrooms in one U.S. school; 10 matched pairs of outdoor and indoor lessons in each classroom across a school year.

## Method
Matched comparison of subsequent indoor-lesson engagement after an outdoor versus an indoor lesson (same teacher, class, topic, week and time of day), using teacher ratings, redirect tallies, blind photo ratings and student ratings.

## Key findings
- Engagement was significantly better after nature lessons on 4 of 5 measures (teacher ratings, redirect tallies, blind photo ratings, composite); student self-ratings showed no difference.
- Teacher redirects were cut almost in half after a lesson in nature.
- In 48 of 100 paired comparisons the nature lesson was a full SD better; in 20 of those, over 2 SD better.

## Limitations
Very small sample (two classes, one school); teachers not blind; short-term outcomes only.

## Relevance to PPS
This counters the worry that outdoor time disrupts instruction. Fewer redirects is relevant to classrooms with many students with attention or behavior needs, as at Sunnyside.
