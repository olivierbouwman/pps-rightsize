# Development of reading and mathematics skills in early adolescence: Do K-8 public schools make a difference?

- **Citation:** Kieffer, M. J. (2013). Development of reading and mathematics skills in early adolescence: Do K-8 public schools make a difference?. *Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness, 6(4), 361-379*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2013.822954
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (propensity score; selection on observables)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Nationally representative ECLS-K: continuously attending a K-8 school raised grade-8 reading by about 0.15 SD (about 6-8 months of schooling); math effect not significant.

## Setting and sample
U.S. nationally representative Early Childhood Longitudinal Study, Kindergarten Class of 1998-99 (N = 8,237).

## Method
Propensity score stratification comparing students who continuously attended K-8 schools with those who moved from elementary to middle school; robustness checks for nesting and alternative matching.

## Key findings
- K-8 attendance yielded a small, significant positive effect on grade-8 reading (effect size 0.15, about 6-8 months of schooling).
- No significant effect on mathematics.
- Results were robust to alternative specifications. The author calls for cost-effectiveness research and work on mechanisms.

## Limitations
Selection is addressed on observables only. Few K-8 students in a national sample. Mechanisms are not identified.

## Relevance to PPS
This national evidence is consistent with the NYC and Florida findings in direction (a K-8 benefit, at least in reading), which supports generalizing beyond large urban systems.
