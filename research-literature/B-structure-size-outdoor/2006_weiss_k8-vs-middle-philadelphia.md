# Reexamining middle school effects: A comparison of middle grades students in middle schools and K-8 schools

- **Citation:** Weiss, C. C., & Kipnes, L. (2006). Reexamining middle school effects: A comparison of middle grades students in middle schools and K-8 schools. *American Journal of Education, 112(2), 239-272*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1086/498996
- **Type:** Observational (longitudinal survey, covariate-adjusted)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** One urban district (Philadelphia): few differences in 8th-grade outcomes between middle and K-8 schools; differences appeared only in self-esteem and perceived threat.

## Setting and sample
An urban district with both middle schools and K-8 schools (Philadelphia Education Longitudinal Study data); eighth-grade outcomes.

## Method
Regression comparisons of eighth-grade academic and social-emotional outcomes by school type, with student background controls.

## Key findings
- Few differences in eighth-grade outcomes by school type.
- Only self-esteem and perceived threat differed by type of school. The paper reports these favoring K-8 settings; check the direction against the full text before quoting.
- Self-esteem in turn predicted academic outcomes.

## Limitations
Observational, single district, eighth-grade snapshot. Summarized from the abstract only.

## Relevance to PPS
This study suggests the K-8 advantage may show up more in safety and self-concept than in grade-8 test scores. That is relevant to belonging and safety concerns for students moved out of PPS K-8s, including students with disabilities.
