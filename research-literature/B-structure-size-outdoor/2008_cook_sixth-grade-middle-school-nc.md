# The negative impacts of starting middle school in sixth grade

- **Citation:** Cook, P. J., MacCoun, R., Muschkin, C., & Vigdor, J. (2008). The negative impacts of starting middle school in sixth grade. *Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, 27(1), 104-121*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.20309
- **Type:** Observational (statewide administrative data, covariate-adjusted)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** North Carolina: sixth graders in middle schools are much more likely to be cited for discipline problems than sixth graders in elementary schools; higher infraction rates persist through grade 9, and test scores show complementary harm.

## Setting and sample
North Carolina public schools, statewide administrative data, comparing sixth graders housed in middle schools with those housed in elementary schools.

## Method
Regression comparisons adjusting for student and school socioeconomic and demographic characteristics; follows infractions into later grades; complementary analysis of end-of-grade tests.

## Key findings
- Sixth graders in middle schools have substantially higher discipline-infraction rates than sixth graders in elementary schools, after adjustment.
- The higher infraction rates persist at least through grade 9.
- End-of-grade test-score analysis gives complementary results (worse outcomes for middle-school sixth graders).
- Proposed mechanism: exposure to older peers and less supervision at an impressionable age. The authors argue for separating sixth graders from older adolescents.

## Limitations
Selection into configurations is handled by covariate adjustment, not random or natural-experiment variation, so residual confounding is possible. Discipline-recording practices may differ by school type.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the key evidence on discipline and behavior. Moving PPS sixth graders from K-8s into 6-8 middle schools is the exact contrast studied. Discipline exposure also matters for students with disabilities, who are over-represented in exclusionary discipline nationally.
