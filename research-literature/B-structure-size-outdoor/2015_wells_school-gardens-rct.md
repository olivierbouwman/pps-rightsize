# The effects of school gardens on children's science knowledge: A randomized controlled trial of low-income elementary schools

- **Citation:** Wells, N. M., Myers, B. M., Todd, L. E., Barale, K., Gaolach, B., Ferenz, G., et al. (2015). The effects of school gardens on children's science knowledge: A randomized controlled trial of low-income elementary schools. *International Journal of Science Education, 37(17), 2858-2878*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2015.1112048
- **Type:** Randomized controlled trial (cluster)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education

**Headline:** 49 low-income schools randomized: garden intervention raised science knowledge more than waitlist control, but gains were 'very modest', with a dose-response by implementation fidelity.

## Setting and sample
49 low-income elementary schools (50%+ free/reduced lunch) in Arkansas, Iowa, Washington and New York; 3,061 students in grades 2, 4 and 5; 2011-2013.

## Method
Cluster RCT (25 garden schools vs 24 waitlist controls); garden kits plus 19 lessons; 7-item science-knowledge questionnaire at four time points; mixed models.

## Key findings
- Science knowledge rose more among garden-intervention children than controls.
- Scores were uniformly poor and gains very modest.
- Dose-response: higher implementation fidelity was associated with stronger effects.

## Limitations
Narrow, researcher-made outcome measure (7 items); garden component only, not whole-school environmental education.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the rare true experiment in the field. It shows real but small knowledge gains, and supports framing Sunnyside's model as likely beneficial but not proven to raise test scores.
