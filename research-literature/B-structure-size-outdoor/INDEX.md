# Research Index: Topic B (Structure, Size, Outdoor Education)

Peer-reviewed and grey literature on the structural choices in the PPS rightsizing plan: K-8 to K-5 conversions, minimum school sizes, class size in receiving schools, ending the Sunnyside Environmental focus option, and focus-option schools generally. Compiled 2026-10-07.

Within each subtopic, entries are ordered meta-analyses and systematic reviews first, then randomized, then quasi-experimental, then descriptive, then reports that are not peer-reviewed. 'Headline' is a one-line summary; see each file for effect sizes, limitations and relevance to PPS. Open-access full texts (where legally available) are in `oa-fulltext/`.

See [`SYNTHESIS.md`](SYNTHESIS.md) for the cross-study reading.

## 1. Grade configuration: K-8 vs separate middle schools, and school transitions

| Year | Authors | Title | Venue | Type | Peer-reviewed | Headline finding | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | Evans et al. | A review of the academic and psychological impact of the transition to secondary education | Frontiers in Psychology | Narrative review | Y | Narrative review: the move to secondary school is a major stressor associated with declines in academic performance, self-perceptions and well-being, buffered by school belonging and teacher and peer support. | [2018_evans_transition-secondary-review.md](2018_evans_transition-secondary-review.md) |
| 1993 | Eccles et al. | Development during adolescence: The impact of stage-environment fit on young adolescents' experiences in schools and in families | American Psychologist | Theoretical review (drawing on longitudinal studies) | Y | Foundational 'stage-environment fit' account: declines in motivation and self-perception after the move to junior high reflect a mismatch between young adolescents' developmental needs and the more controlling, less personal secondary-school environment. | [1993_eccles_stage-environment-fit.md](1993_eccles_stage-environment-fit.md) |
| 2013 | Schwerdt & West | The impact of alternative grade configurations on student outcomes through middle and high school | Journal of Public Economics | Quasi-experimental | Y | Florida statewide: middle-school entry in grade 6 lowers math by 0.12 SD and reading by 0.09 SD, losses grow through grade 8 and do not recover in high school, and early dropout rises by 1.4 points (about 18%). | [2013_schwerdt_grade-configurations-florida.md](2013_schwerdt_grade-configurations-florida.md) |
| 2013 | Kieffer | Development of reading and mathematics skills in early adolescence: Do K-8 public schools make a difference? | Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness | Quasi-experimental (propensity score; selection on observables) | Y | Nationally representative ECLS-K: continuously attending a K-8 school raised grade-8 reading by about 0.15 SD (about 6-8 months of schooling); math effect not significant. | [2013_kieffer_k8-ecls.md](2013_kieffer_k8-ecls.md) |
| 2013 | Dhuey | Middle school or junior high? How grade-level configurations affect academic achievement | Canadian Journal of Economics | Quasi-experimental (OLS and 2SLS) | Y | British Columbia: attending a middle/junior high school instead of a K-8 reduced grade 4-7 gains by 0.125-0.187 SD in math and 0.055-0.108 SD in reading, with negative effects on grade 10 and 12 English exams. | [2013_dhuey_middle-school-bc.md](2013_dhuey_middle-school-bc.md) |
| 2010 | Rockoff & Lockwood | Stuck in the middle: Impacts of grade configuration in public schools | Journal of Public Economics | Quasi-experimental | Y | Entering a middle school in grade 6 or 7 causes a sharp, persistent drop in math and English achievement relative to K-8 peers (about 0.14-0.18 SD in the entry year), larger for low achievers; absences rise. | [2010_rockoff_stuck-in-the-middle.md](2010_rockoff_stuck-in-the-middle.md) |
| 2005 | Bedard & Do | Are middle schools more effective? The impact of school structure on student outcomes | Journal of Human Resources | Quasi-experimental (district policy changes) | Y | U.S. districts that moved from junior highs (7-9) to middle schools (6-8) saw on-time high-school completion fall by about 1-3%. | [2005_bedard_middle-schools-hs-completion.md](2005_bedard_middle-schools-hs-completion.md) |
| 2019 | Malone et al. | Grade configuration is associated with school-level standardized test pass rates for sixth-, seventh-, and eighth-grade students | School Effectiveness and School Improvement | Descriptive / correlational (latent growth modeling) | Y | Statewide school-level data: grade-6 and grade-7 pass rates were higher in elementary-configured schools (e.g., K-7) than in middle schools (grade-7 math 83.1% vs 69.2%). | [2019_malone_grade-configuration-virginia.md](2019_malone_grade-configuration-virginia.md) |
| 2012 | Holas & Huston | Are middle schools harmful? The role of transition timing, classroom quality and school characteristics | Journal of Youth and Adolescence | Observational (longitudinal, covariate-adjusted) | Y | National sample: grade-6 middle-school entrants had lower classroom quality (and slightly lower achievement) and lower engagement explained by larger school size; the authors argue classroom quality and school size matter more than grade span. | [2012_holas_are-middle-schools-harmful.md](2012_holas_are-middle-schools-harmful.md) |
| 2010 | Dove et al. | Relationship between grade span configuration and academic achievement | Journal of Advanced Academics | Descriptive / correlational | Y | Arkansas: no relationship between grade-span configuration and sixth-grade benchmark achievement. | [2010_dove_grade-span-arkansas.md](2010_dove_grade-span-arkansas.md) |
| 2010 | Weiss & Baker-Smith | Eighth-grade school form and resilience in the transition to high school: A comparison of middle schools and K-8 schools | Journal of Research on Adolescence | Observational (covariate-adjusted) | Y | Former middle-school students had worse 9th-grade outcomes (e.g., more course failures) than former K-8 students, but much of the gap reflected K-8 students' higher rates of attending magnet high schools. | [2010_weiss_k8-high-school-transition.md](2010_weiss_k8-high-school-transition.md) |
| 2008 | Cook et al. | The negative impacts of starting middle school in sixth grade | Journal of Policy Analysis and Management | Observational (statewide administrative data, covariate-adjusted) | Y | North Carolina: sixth graders in middle schools are much more likely to be cited for discipline problems than sixth graders in elementary schools; higher infraction rates persist through grade 9, and test scores show complementary harm. | [2008_cook_sixth-grade-middle-school-nc.md](2008_cook_sixth-grade-middle-school-nc.md) |
| 2007 | Byrnes & Ruby | Comparing achievement between K-8 and middle schools: A large-scale empirical study | American Journal of Education | Observational (multilevel, covariate-adjusted) | Y | Philadelphia: established K-8s outperform middle schools in grade 8, but the advantage is explained by student and teacher populations, smaller grade size and fewer transitions; newly converted K-8s showed no advantage. | [2007_byrnes_k8-vs-middle-philadelphia.md](2007_byrnes_k8-vs-middle-philadelphia.md) |
| 2006 | Weiss & Kipnes | Reexamining middle school effects: A comparison of middle grades students in middle schools and K-8 schools | American Journal of Education | Observational (longitudinal survey, covariate-adjusted) | Y | One urban district (Philadelphia): few differences in 8th-grade outcomes between middle and K-8 schools; differences appeared only in self-esteem and perceived threat. | [2006_weiss_k8-vs-middle-philadelphia.md](2006_weiss_k8-vs-middle-philadelphia.md) |
| 1998 | Alspaugh | Achievement loss associated with the transition to middle school and high school | The Journal of Educational Research | Descriptive (ex post facto, district-level) | Y | Districts with a grade-6 middle-school transition showed achievement loss relative to K-8 districts; loss was larger when several elementary schools fed one middle school; middle-school districts had higher dropout rates. | [1998_alspaugh_transition-achievement-loss.md](1998_alspaugh_transition-achievement-loss.md) |

## 2. School size and economies of scale

| Year | Authors | Title | Venue | Type | Peer-reviewed | Headline finding | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2009 | Leithwood & Jantzi | A review of empirical evidence about school size effects: A policy perspective | Review of Educational Research | Systematic review (narrative synthesis) | Y | 57 post-1990 studies favor smaller schools, especially for disadvantaged students; suggested upper limits are about 300 (high-poverty elementary), 500 (other elementary), 600 (high-poverty secondary) and 1,000 (other secondary). | [2009_leithwood_school-size-review.md](2009_leithwood_school-size-review.md) |
| 2002 | Andrews et al. | Revisiting economies of size in American education: Are we any closer to a consensus? | Economics of Education Review | Systematic review (of cost-function and production-function studies) | Y | Three decades of cost and production studies suggest economies of size at the low end of the size range; moderate-sized schools appear to balance cost savings against the drawbacks of large size; little direct evaluation of consolidation exists. | [2002_andrews_economies-of-size.md](2002_andrews_economies-of-size.md) |
| 2014 | Bloom & Unterman | Can small high schools of choice improve educational prospects for disadvantaged students? | Journal of Policy Analysis and Management | Randomized (lottery-based natural experiment) | Y | NYC small high schools of choice raised graduation rates by 9.5 percentage points for 12,130 mostly disadvantaged lottery participants, without higher operating costs. | [2014_bloom_small-high-schools-nyc.md](2014_bloom_small-high-schools-nyc.md) |
| 2018 | Beuchert et al. | The short-term effects of school consolidation on student achievement: Evidence of disruption? | Economics of Education Review | Quasi-experimental (event study) | Y | Denmark 2010-11 consolidations: students from closing schools lost achievement in the short run, especially those from small schools; effects faded over time, consistent with disruption. | [2018_beuchert_school-consolidation-denmark.md](2018_beuchert_school-consolidation-denmark.md) |
| 2015 | Gershenson & Langbein | The effect of primary school size on academic achievement | Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis | Quasi-experimental (value-added with fixed effects) | Y | North Carolina grades 4-5: no average causal effect of school size, but larger grade cohorts harm socioeconomically disadvantaged students and students with learning disabilities (about -0.015 SD per 10 extra students in the grade). | [2015_gershenson_primary-school-size.md](2015_gershenson_primary-school-size.md) |
| 2015 | Humlum & Smith | Long-term effects of school size on students' outcomes | Economics of Education Review | Quasi-experimental (FE and IV) | Y | Denmark: larger lower-secondary schools had a small positive effect on high-school completion and age-30 earnings (e.g., about +0.8 pp completion per 100 students in OLS), driven by boys and low-education families. | [2015_humlum_long-term-school-size.md](2015_humlum_long-term-school-size.md) |
| 2006 | Kuziemko | Using shocks to school enrollment to estimate the effect of school size on student achievement | Economics of Education Review | Quasi-experimental (panel first differences and IV) | Y | Using enrollment shocks from openings, closings and mergers as instruments, smaller schools raised math scores and attendance, with benefits outweighing costs. | [2006_kuziemko_school-size-shocks.md](2006_kuziemko_school-size-shocks.md) |
| 1997 | Lee & Smith | High school size: Which works best and for whom? | Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis | Observational (HLM on national longitudinal data) | Y | NELS:88: learning was highest in high schools of 600-900 students; much lower above 2,100; size mattered more in low-SES and high-minority schools. | [1997_lee_high-school-size.md](1997_lee_high-school-size.md) |
| 1996 | Cotton | School size, school climate, and student performance (School Improvement Research Series, Close-Up #20) | Northwest Regional Educational Laboratory (NWREL) | Report - not peer-reviewed (research synthesis) | N | Portland-based NWREL synthesis: small schools are at least equal on achievement and better on attitudes, behavior, attendance, dropout, participation and belonging, especially for low-income and minority students. | [1996_cotton_school-size-climate.md](1996_cotton_school-size-climate.md) |

## 3. Class size

| Year | Authors | Title | Venue | Type | Peer-reviewed | Headline finding | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | Filges et al. | Small class sizes for improving student achievement in primary and secondary schools: A systematic review | Campbell Systematic Reviews | Systematic review and meta-analysis | Y | 127 studies from 41 countries (10 in the meta-analysis): smaller classes have at best a small positive effect on reading and no significant effect on math; STAR results are more positive but small. | [2018_filges_class-size-campbell.md](2018_filges_class-size-campbell.md) |
| 2011 | Chetty et al. | How does your kindergarten classroom affect your earnings? Evidence from Project STAR | Quarterly Journal of Economics | Randomized experiment (long-term follow-up) | Y | STAR follow-up: small classes raised college attendance; earnings effects at age 27 were imprecise; overall classroom quality had lasting effects even as test-score gains faded. | [2011_chetty_star-long-term.md](2011_chetty_star-long-term.md) |
| 1999 | Krueger | Experimental estimates of education production functions | Quarterly Journal of Economics | Randomized experiment (re-analysis of Tennessee STAR) | Y | Tennessee STAR: small K-3 classes (13-17 vs 22-25) raised test scores about 4 percentile points in the first year, plus about 1 point per additional year, with larger effects for minority and free-lunch students. | [1999_krueger_star.md](1999_krueger_star.md) |
| 2000 | Hoxby | The effects of class size on student achievement: New evidence from population variation | Quarterly Journal of Economics | Quasi-experimental (natural population variation, regression discontinuity) | Y | Connecticut: natural variation in cohort size and class-size-rule cutoffs showed no significant effect of class size, ruling out even modest effects. | [2000_hoxby_class-size-population-variation.md](2000_hoxby_class-size-population-variation.md) |
| 2014 | Schanzenbach | Does class size matter? | National Education Policy Center (NEPC) Policy Brief | Report - not peer-reviewed (policy research brief) | N | Policy brief: class size is an important determinant of outcomes (STAR: about 0.15-0.20 SD); increasing class sizes will harm outcomes, particularly for low-income and minority children. | [2014_schanzenbach_class-size-nepc.md](2014_schanzenbach_class-size-nepc.md) |

## 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education

| Year | Authors | Title | Venue | Type | Peer-reviewed | Headline finding | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | Fan et al. | Nature-based interventions for autistic children: A systematic review and meta-analysis | JAMA Network Open | Systematic review and meta-analysis | Y | 24 studies (717 children): nature-based interventions were associated with moderate short-term improvements in social communication (SMD -0.59), hyperactivity (SMD -0.56) and sensory functioning, but most were equine-assisted therapy, not school-based outdoor learning. | [2023_fan_nature-autism-meta.md](2023_fan_nature-autism-meta.md) |

| 2024 | Hood & Baumann | Could nature contribute to the management of ADHD in children? A systematic review | International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health | Systematic review | Y | 7 studies: exposure to nature is consistently associated with fewer ADHD diagnoses and lower symptom severity in school-aged children, but 5 of 7 studies are observational and most rely on parent reports. | [2024_hood_nature-adhd-review.md](2024_hood_nature-adhd-review.md) |
| 2023 | Diaz-Martinez et al. | Systematic review: Neurodevelopmental benefits of active/passive school exposure to green and/or blue spaces in children and adolescents | International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health | Systematic review | Y | 28 studies: mixed evidence of a protective link between school green/blue space and cognitive/academic performance, attention, behavior and impulsivity; high methodological heterogeneity. | [2023_diaz-martinez_school-green-blue-neurodevelopment.md](2023_diaz-martinez_school-green-blue-neurodevelopment.md) |

| 2019 | Kuo et al. | Do experiences with nature promote learning? Converging evidence of a cause-and-effect relationship | Frontiers in Psychology | Narrative (integrative) review | Y | Integrative review arguing converging evidence that nature experiences boost learning, personal development and stewardship, via attention restoration, stress reduction, self-discipline, motivation and physical activity. | [2019_kuo_nature-promotes-learning.md](2019_kuo_nature-promotes-learning.md) |
| 2019 | Browning & Rigolon | School green space and its impact on academic performance: A systematic literature review | International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health | Systematic review | Y | 13 studies with 122 findings: 64% non-significant, 28% positive, 8% negative; the literature on school greenness and achievement is small, mixed and mostly observational. | [2019_browning_school-green-space-review.md](2019_browning_school-green-space-review.md) |
| 2018 | Ardoin et al. | Environmental education and K-12 student outcomes: A review and analysis of research | Journal of Environmental Education | Systematic review (descriptive analysis) | Y | 119 peer-reviewed articles (1994-2013) on K-12 environmental education reported 121 distinct outcomes, most positive; the authors call for bolder, more diverse research designs. | [2018_ardoin_ee-k12-outcomes.md](2018_ardoin_ee-k12-outcomes.md) |
| 2017 | Becker et al. | Effects of regular classes in outdoor education settings: A systematic review on students' learning, social and health dimensions | International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health | Systematic review | Y | Only 13 studies of regular, curriculum-based outdoor education qualified; 11 reported positive effects, but quantitative study quality was low; the authors describe 'tendencies' of benefit. | [2017_becker_outdoor-regular-classes.md](2017_becker_outdoor-regular-classes.md) |
| 2013 | Williams & Dixon | Impact of garden-based learning on academic outcomes in schools: Synthesis of research between 1990 and 2010 | Review of Educational Research | Systematic review (research synthesis) | Y | 48 studies: a preponderance of positive academic impacts (strongest in science, then math and language arts), but widespread lack of research rigor. | [2013_williams_garden-based-learning.md](2013_williams_garden-based-learning.md) |

| 2009 | Faber Taylor & Kuo | Children with attention deficits concentrate better after walk in the park | Journal of Attention Disorders | Randomized crossover experiment (within-subjects) | Y | 17 children with ADHD concentrated better after a 20-minute park walk than after urban walks (Cohen's d = 0.52 and 0.77), effect sizes comparable to some medication formulations. | [2009_taylor_adhd-walk-in-park.md](2009_taylor_adhd-walk-in-park.md) |
| 2019 | Otte et al. | Education outside the classroom increases children's reading performance: Results from a one-year quasi-experimental study | International Journal of Educational Research | Quasi-experimental | Y | Denmark (TEACHOUT): one school year of regular (more than 2 hours/week) education outside the classroom had a small positive effect on grade 3-6 reading scores. | [2019_otte_eotc-reading.md](2019_otte_eotc-reading.md) |
| 2018 | Kuo et al. | Do lessons in nature boost subsequent classroom engagement? ## 5. Focus-option, magnet and lottery schools

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Searched but not included

- **Luyten, Hendriks & Scheerens (2014), *School Size Effects Revisited* (SpringerBriefs).** A relevant review and meta-analysis, but the full text could not be retrieved (the repository blocked automated access), so it is not summarized.
- **Bolling et al. (2018), education outside the classroom and school motivation (Int. J. Educ. Res. 89:22-35).** The abstract was not retrievable to verify findings.

- **Anchor note:** all named anchors in the brief were verified and included (Rockoff & Lockwood 2010; Schwerdt & West 2013; Byrnes & Ruby 2007; Holas & Huston 2012; Cook et al. 2008; Dove, Pearson & Hooper 2010; Leithwood & Jantzi 2009; Andrews, Duncombe & Yinger 2002; Cotton 1996; Filges et al. 2018; Schanzenbach 2014; Becker et al. 2017; Kuo, Barnes & Jordan 2019; Mann et al. 2022; Ardoin et al. 2018; SEER/Lieberman & Hoody 1998).
