# Could nature contribute to the management of ADHD in children? A systematic review

- **Citation:** Hood, M., & Baumann, O. (2024). Could nature contribute to the management of ADHD in children? A systematic review. *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 21(6), 736*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph21060736
- **Type:** Systematic review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2024_hood_nature-adhd-review.txt`

**Headline:** 7 studies: exposure to nature is consistently associated with fewer ADHD diagnoses and lower symptom severity in school-aged children, but 5 of 7 studies are observational and most rely on parent reports.

## Setting and sample
Empirical studies of nature exposure and ADHD prevalence or symptoms in school-aged children (458 screened, 7 included).

## Method
Systematic search of PubMed, PsycINFO, Embase and Web of Science; narrative synthesis.

## Key findings
- Included studies consistently reported that nature exposure is associated with reduced ADHD diagnoses and symptom severity, with associations surviving adjustment for income, parental education and prenatal factors.
- Five of seven studies were observational; two were experimental or quasi-experimental (e.g., Faber Taylor & Kuo 2009).
- Most studies relied on parent reports of diagnosis, symptoms and nature exposure.

## Limitations
Very small evidence base; self-report measures; observational dominance. The authors' description of 'strong support' exceeds the design strength.

## Relevance to PPS
This is relevant to the share of Sunnyside students with attention-related disabilities. It is plausible but low-certainty evidence that regular nature contact helps ADHD symptoms.
