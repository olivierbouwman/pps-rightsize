# Small class sizes for improving student achievement in primary and secondary schools: A systematic review

- **Citation:** Filges, T., Sonne-Schmidt, C. S., & Nielsen, B. C. V. (2018). Small class sizes for improving student achievement in primary and secondary schools: A systematic review. *Campbell Systematic Reviews, 14(1), 1-107*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.4073/csr.2018.10
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. Class size

**Headline:** 127 studies from 41 countries (10 in the meta-analysis): smaller classes have at best a small positive effect on reading and no significant effect on math; STAR results are more positive but small.

## Setting and sample
K-12 general education, 127 studies (148 reports, 55 populations, 41 countries); 45 studies used Tennessee STAR data.

## Method
Campbell systematic review; meta-analysis of the 10 studies meeting design criteria (4 from STAR).

## Key findings
- Overall, at best a small effect on reading achievement.
- A negative but statistically insignificant effect on mathematics.
- Non-STAR reading effects were near zero individually, with a small positive, significant weighted average.
- All STAR results were positive for reading and math, but average effects were small.

## Limitations
Few studies met inclusion for pooling. Heterogeneous class-size ranges and contexts. Mostly test-score outcomes.

## Relevance to PPS
If receiving PPS schools absorb students by raising class sizes modestly, the best pooled evidence predicts small average test-score losses at most. Equity effects (below) and non-test outcomes may matter more.
