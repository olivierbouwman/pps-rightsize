# Children with attention deficits concentrate better after walk in the park

- **Citation:** Faber Taylor, A., & Kuo, F. E. (2009). Children with attention deficits concentrate better after walk in the park. *Journal of Attention Disorders, 12(5), 402-409*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054708323000
- **Type:** Randomized crossover experiment (within-subjects)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education

**Headline:** 17 children with ADHD concentrated better after a 20-minute park walk than after urban walks (Cohen's d = 0.52 and 0.77), effect sizes comparable to some medication formulations.

## Setting and sample
17 children aged 7-12 with professionally diagnosed ADHD (U.S.).

## Method
Single-blind, within-subjects design; each child completed guided 20-minute walks in a park, downtown and a neighborhood, a week apart in randomized order; Digit Span Backwards afterwards.

## Key findings
- Concentration was better after the park walk than after the downtown walk (p = .023, d = 0.52) or the neighborhood walk (p = .007, d = 0.77).
- Effect sizes were comparable to those reported for recent formulations of methylphenidate.

## Limitations
Very small sample; single, short-term outcome; not a school-based intervention. (Print issue 2009; online 2008.)

## Relevance to PPS
This is experimental support for short-term attention benefits of nature for children with ADHD. It provides a mechanism for why regular outdoor instruction may help some students with disabilities.
