# Are middle schools more effective? The impact of school structure on student outcomes

- **Citation:** Bedard, K., & Do, C. (2005). Are middle schools more effective? The impact of school structure on student outcomes. *Journal of Human Resources, 40(3), 660-682*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.XL.3.660
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (district policy changes)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** U.S. districts that moved from junior highs (7-9) to middle schools (6-8) saw on-time high-school completion fall by about 1-3%.

## Setting and sample
U.S. school districts that switched from a junior-high to a middle-school system.

## Method
District-level comparisons exploiting the timing of adoption of middle schools.

## Key findings
- Moving to a middle-school system decreased on-time high-school completion by roughly 1-3%.
- This runs counter to the stated aim of middle-school reform, which was to help lower-achieving students.

## Limitations
The comparison is middle school versus junior high, not K-8. District-level aggregates.

## Relevance to PPS
This is indirect but consistent evidence that moving the transition earlier (to grade 6) carries long-run costs. PPS's conversions move the transition from grade 9 to grade 6 for affected students.
