# Experimental estimates of education production functions

- **Citation:** Krueger, A. B. (1999). Experimental estimates of education production functions. *Quarterly Journal of Economics, 114(2), 497-532*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1162/003355399556052
- **Type:** Randomized experiment (re-analysis of Tennessee STAR)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. Class size

**Headline:** Tennessee STAR: small K-3 classes (13-17 vs 22-25) raised test scores about 4 percentile points in the first year, plus about 1 point per additional year, with larger effects for minority and free-lunch students.

## Setting and sample
Tennessee Project STAR, about 11,600 students and their teachers randomly assigned to small or regular classes in grades K-3 (late 1980s).

## Method
Experimental analysis with adjustments for attrition and class switching.

## Key findings
- On average, test performance rose about 4 percentile points in the first year in a small class.
- The advantage grew by about 1 percentile point per year in subsequent years.
- Teacher aides and measured teacher characteristics had little effect.
- Effects were larger for minority students and those on free lunch. Hawthorne effects were unlikely.

## Limitations
Large reductions (about 7-8 students) in the early grades. Effects may not scale to small changes or to older grades.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the benchmark for what class size can do. Absorbing students into receiving schools should avoid raising K-3 class sizes, especially in high-poverty schools, where STAR effects were largest.
