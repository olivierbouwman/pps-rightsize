# A review of empirical evidence about school size effects: A policy perspective

- **Citation:** Leithwood, K., & Jantzi, D. (2009). A review of empirical evidence about school size effects: A policy perspective. *Review of Educational Research, 79(1), 464-490*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308326158
- **Type:** Systematic review (narrative synthesis)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** 57 post-1990 studies favor smaller schools, especially for disadvantaged students; suggested upper limits are about 300 (high-poverty elementary), 500 (other elementary), 600 (high-poverty secondary) and 1,000 (other secondary).

## Setting and sample
57 empirical studies published after 1990, mostly U.S.

## Method
Review of studies on school-size effects on student and organizational outcomes.

## Key findings
- The weight of evidence favors smaller schools.
- Students who traditionally struggle and students from disadvantaged backgrounds benefit most.
- Suggested maximum sizes: elementary schools with many disadvantaged students about 300 or fewer; other elementary schools about 500; secondary schools serving mainly disadvantaged students about 600 or fewer; other secondary schools about 1,000.

## Limitations
Most underlying studies are correlational. Thresholds are the authors' policy judgments rather than estimated break points. Little attention to costs.

## Relevance to PPS
PPS's minimums (360 for K-5, 500 for K-8 or middle, 1,100 for high school) are set at or above the maximums this review suggests for schools serving disadvantaged students. The evidence base on size was built to argue against large schools, not to justify enrollment floors.
