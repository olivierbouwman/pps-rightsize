# High school size: Which works best and for whom?

- **Citation:** Lee, V. E., & Smith, J. B. (1997). High school size: Which works best and for whom?. *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 19(3), 205-227*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/01623737019003205
- **Type:** Observational (HLM on national longitudinal data)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** NELS:88: learning was highest in high schools of 600-900 students; much lower above 2,100; size mattered more in low-SES and high-minority schools.

## Setting and sample
U.S. National Education Longitudinal Study of 1988 (three waves); high-school reading and math growth.

## Method
Hierarchical linear models of achievement growth by enrollment size.

## Key findings
- The 'ideal' high school for learning enrolled 600-900 students. Students in smaller schools learned less, and those in large schools (especially over 2,100) learned considerably less.
- Learning was more equitably distributed by SES in very small schools.
- Size effects were stronger in schools enrolling lower-SES students and higher concentrations of minority students.

## Limitations
Observational; size is correlated with curriculum and community characteristics; data from the early 1990s.

## Relevance to PPS
PPS's 1,100-student high-school minimum exceeds the 600-900 optimum identified here. The paper also suggests size matters most for lower-SES students.
