# Comparing achievement between K-8 and middle schools: A large-scale empirical study

- **Citation:** Byrnes, V., & Ruby, A. (2007). Comparing achievement between K-8 and middle schools: A large-scale empirical study. *American Journal of Education, 114(1), 101-135*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1086/520693
- **Type:** Observational (multilevel, covariate-adjusted)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Philadelphia: established K-8s outperform middle schools in grade 8, but the advantage is explained by student and teacher populations, smaller grade size and fewer transitions; newly converted K-8s showed no advantage.

## Setting and sample
Philadelphia. 40,883 eighth graders in 95 schools across five cohorts, comparing middle schools, long-standing K-8 schools and newly formed K-8s created under a K-8 conversion policy.

## Method
Multilevel models with student, cohort and school levels, controlling for demographics and school characteristics.

## Key findings
- Older K-8 schools performed significantly better than middle schools.
- The advantage was explained by differing student and teacher populations, smaller average grade size and fewer school transitions.
- Newer K-8 schools, despite smaller grades and fewer transitions, did not show the same advantage because they served more disadvantaged populations.

## Limitations
Observational; school type is confounded with population. Single district. Outcome is 8th-grade achievement only.

## Relevance to PPS
The study suggests the K-8 benefit runs through mechanisms such as small grade cohorts and avoiding a transition, not the label. PPS's K-8 to K-5 conversions remove both mechanisms for affected students: they add a transition and move students into larger middle-school grade cohorts.
