# Closing the achievement gap: Using the environment as an integrating context for learning (Results of a nationwide study)

- **Citation:** Lieberman, G. A., & Hoody, L. L. (1998). Closing the achievement gap: Using the environment as an integrating context for learning (Results of a nationwide study). *State Education and Environment Roundtable (SEER), San Diego, CA*.
- **DOI / URL:** ERIC ED428943: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED428943.pdf
- **Type:** Report - not peer-reviewed (descriptive / case study)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/1998_lieberman_seer-eic.txt`

**Headline:** 40 'environment as integrating context' (EIC) schools in 12 states; 14 schools ran their own comparisons and 36 of 39 achievement comparisons favored EIC students, but the authors state it is not a quantitative assessment.

## Setting and sample
40 U.S. K-12 schools with established EIC programs (average program age over 7 years); interviews with 400+ students and 250+ teachers and principals.

## Method
Site visits, interviews and surveys; compilation of school-conducted comparisons (standardized tests, GPA, attendance, discipline) from 14 schools.

## Key findings
- Reported benefits: better performance in reading, writing, math, science and social studies; fewer discipline and classroom-management problems; more engagement and enthusiasm; more pride and ownership.
- 36 of 39 academic comparisons (92%) from 14 schools favored EIC students.
- The report itself states it 'is not a quantitative assessment of the effects of EIC programs' and that evidence from 40 schools 'can not be considered conclusive'.

## Limitations
Not peer-reviewed; schools selected as successful programs; comparisons run by the schools themselves with varied, uncontrolled methods; strong risk of selection and reporting bias.

## Relevance to PPS
This is widely cited by environmental-school advocates, including likely in Sunnyside advocacy. It should be labeled as a promising, non-peer-reviewed descriptive report, not causal evidence.
