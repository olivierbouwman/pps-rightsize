# Do experiences with nature promote learning? Converging evidence of a cause-and-effect relationship

- **Citation:** Kuo, M., Barnes, M., & Jordan, C. (2019). Do experiences with nature promote learning? Converging evidence of a cause-and-effect relationship. *Frontiers in Psychology, 10, 305*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00305
- **Type:** Narrative (integrative) review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2019_kuo_nature-promotes-learning.txt`

**Headline:** Integrative review arguing converging evidence that nature experiences boost learning, personal development and stewardship, via attention restoration, stress reduction, self-discipline, motivation and physical activity.

## Setting and sample
Broad literature on nature and learning, from preschool to wilderness programs.

## Method
Brief integrative review (not systematic; no quality appraisal or pooling). Uses causal language only where experimental evidence exists.

## Key findings
- The authors state that nature-based instruction outperforms traditional instruction, citing a school-garden RCT (Wells et al. 2015), over 200 tests of nature-based instruction 'the vast majority' positive (via Williams & Dixon 2013; Becker et al. 2017), and greener schools scoring higher after poverty controls.
- Proposed mechanisms include attention restoration, lower stress, self-discipline, interest and enjoyment, physical activity and fitness, and calmer, more cooperative settings.
- The authors argue nature is especially valuable 'for students not effectively reached by traditional instruction'.

## Limitations
The review's confidence exceeds that of the systematic reviews. Becker et al. rate quantitative quality as low, Browning & Rigolon find 64% null associations for school greenness, and the Wells RCT gains were 'very modest'. The authors are advocates in the field. Pedagogy (hands-on, student-centered) and setting are confounded.

## Relevance to PPS
This is frequently cited by environmental schools. It is useful for mechanisms (attention, behavior, engagement) relevant to Sunnyside's high special-education share, but should not be presented as settled proof of achievement gains.
