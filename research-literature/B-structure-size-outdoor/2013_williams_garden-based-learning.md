# Impact of garden-based learning on academic outcomes in schools: Synthesis of research between 1990 and 2010

- **Citation:** Williams, D. R., & Dixon, P. S. (2013). Impact of garden-based learning on academic outcomes in schools: Synthesis of research between 1990 and 2010. *Review of Educational Research, 83(2), 211-235*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654313475824
- **Type:** Systematic review (research synthesis)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education

**Headline:** 48 studies: a preponderance of positive academic impacts (strongest in science, then math and language arts), but widespread lack of research rigor.

## Setting and sample
School garden-based learning programs; 48 of 152 articles met inclusion criteria.

## Method
Coded synthesis with a review template (no meta-analytic pooling).

## Key findings
- Positive impacts on direct academic outcomes, highest for science, then math and language arts.
- Indirect outcomes (especially social development) were also frequently positive.
- Results were consistent across programs, samples and school types.
- A common issue was lack of rigor: incomplete descriptions of methods, sampling and validity.

## Limitations
Vote-counting; weak underlying designs; positive publication bias. Note the first author is a Portland State University professor, which is local context only.

## Relevance to PPS
Garden and place-based components are common in environmental schools like Sunnyside. The academic signal is positive but low-certainty.
