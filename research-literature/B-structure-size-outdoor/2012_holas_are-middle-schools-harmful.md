# Are middle schools harmful? The role of transition timing, classroom quality and school characteristics

- **Citation:** Holas, I., & Huston, A. C. (2012). Are middle schools harmful? The role of transition timing, classroom quality and school characteristics. *Journal of Youth and Adolescence, 41(3), 333-345*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-011-9732-9
- **Type:** Observational (longitudinal, covariate-adjusted)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** National sample: grade-6 middle-school entrants had lower classroom quality (and slightly lower achievement) and lower engagement explained by larger school size; the authors argue classroom quality and school size matter more than grade span.

## Setting and sample
U.S. national longitudinal sample, NICHD Study of Early Child Care and Youth Development (n = 855; 52% female; 82% White). Compares students entering middle school in grade 5 or 6 with same-grade peers in elementary schools.

## Method
Longitudinal comparisons with observed and teacher-reported classroom quality and school characteristics (composition, size) as mediators.

## Key findings
- Grade-5 middle-school entrants did not differ from elementary peers.
- Grade-6 entrants experienced lower classroom quality, which predicted slightly lower achievement.
- Grade-6 entrants also had lower school engagement, explained by larger school size.
- Classroom quality and school characteristics predicted functioning regardless of school type.

## Limitations
Sample is mostly White and not representative of urban districts. Small number of middle-school entrants. Observational.

## Relevance to PPS
The study offers a mechanism-level reading. Harm from middle schools may come mainly through larger schools and weaker classrooms. If PPS's receiving middle schools are larger (the plan sets a 500-student minimum), the engagement penalty this study links to school size is a risk for students from converted K-8s.
