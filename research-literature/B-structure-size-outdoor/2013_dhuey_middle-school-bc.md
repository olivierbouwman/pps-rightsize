# Middle school or junior high? How grade-level configurations affect academic achievement

- **Citation:** Dhuey, E. (2013). Middle school or junior high? How grade-level configurations affect academic achievement. *Canadian Journal of Economics, 46(2), 469-496*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/caje.12020
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (OLS and 2SLS)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** British Columbia: attending a middle/junior high school instead of a K-8 reduced grade 4-7 gains by 0.125-0.187 SD in math and 0.055-0.108 SD in reading, with negative effects on grade 10 and 12 English exams.

## Setting and sample
British Columbia, Canada; province-wide student data.

## Method
OLS and two-stage least squares (instrumental-variable) estimates of attending a middle/junior high school relative to a K-8.

## Key findings
- Middle/junior high attendance reduced grade 4-7 achievement gains by 0.125-0.187 SD in math and 0.055-0.108 SD in reading.
- 2SLS estimates were similar for math.
- Large negative effects were also found on grade-10 and grade-12 English exams.

## Limitations
Canadian context. Instrument validity is a standard concern.

## Relevance to PPS
This is a non-U.S. replication of the middle-school penalty, with longer-run effects into secondary exams. It strengthens the case that the K-8 to K-5 conversion carries achievement risk.
