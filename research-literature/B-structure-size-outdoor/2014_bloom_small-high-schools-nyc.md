# Can small high schools of choice improve educational prospects for disadvantaged students?

- **Citation:** Bloom, H. S., & Unterman, R. (2014). Can small high schools of choice improve educational prospects for disadvantaged students?. *Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, 33(2), 290-319*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.21748
- **Type:** Randomized (lottery-based natural experiment)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** NYC small high schools of choice raised graduation rates by 9.5 percentage points for 12,130 mostly disadvantaged lottery participants, without higher operating costs.

## Setting and sample
New York City, 100+ small high schools of choice (about 100 students per grade) created 2002-2008; 12,130 students in naturally occurring admission lotteries.

## Method
Lottery-based comparisons of winners and losers.

## Key findings
- Graduation rates rose by 9.5 percentage points overall and for many subgroups of educationally and economically disadvantaged students of color.
- No increase in annual school operating costs.
- Related evidence: Abdulkadiroglu, Hu & Pathak (2013, NBER Working Paper 19576, not peer-reviewed) find similar lottery-based gains in test scores, graduation and college enrollment. Schwartz, Stiefel & Wiswall (2013, Journal of Urban Economics 77:27-40) find positive effects for newly created small schools but not older ones. Stiefel, Schwartz & Wiswall (2015, Educational Researcher) find the reform lifted outcomes district-wide.

## Limitations
Small size is bundled with new leadership, autonomy, themes and personalization, so the effect cannot be attributed to size alone. High-school level only.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the strongest causal evidence that small schools (about 400 students across four grades) can outperform. It cuts against the premise that PPS's 1,100-student high-school floor is needed for quality, though it does not address costs in a shrinking district.
