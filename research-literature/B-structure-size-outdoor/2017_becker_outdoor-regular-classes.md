# Effects of regular classes in outdoor education settings: A systematic review on students' learning, social and health dimensions

- **Citation:** Becker, C., Lauterbach, G., Spengler, S., Dettweiler, U., & Mess, F. (2017). Effects of regular classes in outdoor education settings: A systematic review on students' learning, social and health dimensions. *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 14(5), 485*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph14050485
- **Type:** Systematic review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2017_becker_outdoor-regular-classes.txt`

**Headline:** Only 13 studies of regular, curriculum-based outdoor education qualified; 11 reported positive effects, but quantitative study quality was low; the authors describe 'tendencies' of benefit.

## Setting and sample
Regular, compulsory, curriculum-based outdoor education programs (e.g., Danish udeskole, forest school, place-based curricula) in Denmark, the U.S., Germany, New Zealand, Sweden, the U.K. and Norway.

## Method
Systematic search of English- and German-language peer-reviewed articles; dual screening; methodological quality appraisal.

## Key findings
- 13 studies included; mean sample about 62 students. Most were case studies.
- Quality: moderate for qualitative studies (M = 0.52) and low for quantitative studies (M = 0.18).
- 11 studies reported positive effects, 1 mixed and 1 negative, across social (8), learning (7) and physical activity and health (4) dimensions.
- Example: a one-year out-of-school science program for 5th graders (Ernst et al.) showed higher reading and writing scores than a control group.
- Conclusion: 'tendencies' that regular outdoor education can promote social, academic, physical and psychological outcomes; more rigorous, larger, longitudinal studies needed.

## Limitations
Very small evidence base; low quantitative quality; positive-results bias possible. (OpenAlex lists the first author incorrectly; PubMed confirms Christoph Becker.)

## Relevance to PPS
This review is closest to Sunnyside's model (regular, curriculum-embedded outdoor learning). It shows plausible benefits but an evidence base too thin to quantify achievement effects.
