# The short-term effects of school consolidation on student achievement: Evidence of disruption?

- **Citation:** Beuchert, L. V., Humlum, M. K., Nielsen, H. S., & Smith, N. (2018). The short-term effects of school consolidation on student achievement: Evidence of disruption?. *Economics of Education Review, 65, 31-47*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2018.05.004
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (event study)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** Denmark 2010-11 consolidations: students from closing schools lost achievement in the short run, especially those from small schools; effects faded over time, consistent with disruption.

## Setting and sample
Danish school consolidations, 2010-2011, with test scores before and up to four years after.

## Method
Difference-in-differences / event-study comparisons of students affected by consolidation with unaffected students.

## Key findings
- Achievement of students in closing schools is adversely affected in the short run.
- Students initially enrolled in small schools experience the most detrimental effects.
- Effects weaken over time, suggesting part of the effect is disruption.

## Limitations
Danish context; short- to medium-run test outcomes only.

## Relevance to PPS
Expect short-run achievement costs for students displaced by PPS closures and mergers, largest for students coming from small schools.
