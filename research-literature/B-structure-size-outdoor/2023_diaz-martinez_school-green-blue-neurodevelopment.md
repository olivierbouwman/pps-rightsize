# Systematic review: Neurodevelopmental benefits of active/passive school exposure to green and/or blue spaces in children and adolescents

- **Citation:** Diaz-Martinez, F., Sanchez-Sauco, M. F., Cabrera-Rivera, L. T., Sanchez, C. O., Hidalgo-Albadalejo, M. D., Claudio, L., & Ortega-Garcia, J. A. (2023). Systematic review: Neurodevelopmental benefits of active/passive school exposure to green and/or blue spaces in children and adolescents. *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 20(5), 3958*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20053958
- **Type:** Systematic review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2023_diaz-martinez_school-green-blue-neurodevelopment.txt`

**Headline:** 28 studies: mixed evidence of a protective link between school green/blue space and cognitive/academic performance, attention, behavior and impulsivity; high methodological heterogeneity.

## Setting and sample
Studies of active (e.g., outdoor lessons) or passive (e.g., greenness around school) exposure to green or blue space at school.

## Method
Systematic search of five databases (August 2022); 28 studies included.

## Key findings
- Cognitive and academic performance was the most studied outcome (15 of 28 studies).
- Most studies assessed passive exposure (19) rather than active exposure (9).
- Results point toward mixed evidence of benefit for cognitive and academic performance, attention restoration, behavior and impulsivity.

## Limitations
Great heterogeneity in methods and confounder adjustment; no pooling.

## Relevance to PPS
This corroborates that the school-nature evidence is promising but mixed, especially for academic outcomes.
