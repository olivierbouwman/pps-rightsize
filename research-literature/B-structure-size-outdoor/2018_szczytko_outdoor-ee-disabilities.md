# Impacts of outdoor environmental education on teacher reports of attention, behavior, and learning outcomes for students with emotional, cognitive, and behavioral disabilities

- **Citation:** Szczytko, R., Carrier, S. J., & Stevenson, K. T. (2018). Impacts of outdoor environmental education on teacher reports of attention, behavior, and learning outcomes for students with emotional, cognitive, and behavioral disabilities. *Frontiers in Education, 3, 46*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2018.00046
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (matched control schools)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2018_szczytko_outdoor-ee-disabilities.txt`

**Headline:** North Carolina 5th graders with emotional, cognitive or behavioral disabilities: teachers reported better attention and behavior during outdoor environmental-education days, and science outcomes kept pace with controls; classroom behavior did not change differentially.

## Setting and sample
North Carolina outdoor environmental-education program for 5th graders (4-10 full school days per year in schoolyards and natural areas). Students with emotional, cognitive or behavioral disabilities: treatment n = 99, matched-control n = 62.

## Method
Pre/post surveys of students and teachers in treatment and matched control schools, with teacher interviews.

## Key findings
- Teachers reported significantly improved attention spans and fewer disruptive behaviors when these students learned outdoors; by program end, outdoor ratings exceeded classroom ratings.
- No difference between treatment and control in changes in classroom (indoor) attention and behavior (attention F = 0.20, p = 0.65; behavior F = 0.04, p = 0.85).
- Science efficacy, nature-of-science understanding and science grades were maintained, in line with controls.
- Interviews supported the survey findings.

## Limitations
Teacher-reported, non-blind outcomes; treatment teachers rated students higher at baseline; self-selected program; low dose.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the most directly relevant study for students with disabilities in environmental education. It supports the claim that outdoor settings help these students' attention and behavior while outdoors, without academic cost. It does not show spillover to indoor classrooms.
