# Eighth-grade school form and resilience in the transition to high school: A comparison of middle schools and K-8 schools

- **Citation:** Weiss, C. C., & Baker-Smith, E. C. (2010). Eighth-grade school form and resilience in the transition to high school: A comparison of middle schools and K-8 schools. *Journal of Research on Adolescence, 20(4), 825-839*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-7795.2010.00664.x
- **Type:** Observational (covariate-adjusted)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Former middle-school students had worse 9th-grade outcomes (e.g., more course failures) than former K-8 students, but much of the gap reflected K-8 students' higher rates of attending magnet high schools.

## Setting and sample
Same urban district data as Weiss & Kipnes (2006); outcomes in the first year of high school.

## Method
Regression comparisons of 9th-grade academic and social outcomes by type of 8th-grade school, with mediation by high-school type.

## Key findings
- Attending a middle school (rather than a K-8) was associated with worse 9th-grade outcomes in several domains, such as course failure.
- A large share of the difference was accounted for by K-8 students' greater likelihood of attending the district's magnet high schools.

## Limitations
Observational; mediation by high-school choice complicates interpretation.

## Relevance to PPS
This bears on the grade-9 transition. The study does not support the claim that a middle school better prepares students for high school, and shows that downstream school-choice pathways can differ by middle-grades setting.
