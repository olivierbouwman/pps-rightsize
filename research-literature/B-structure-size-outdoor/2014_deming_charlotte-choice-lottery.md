# School choice, school quality, and postsecondary attainment

- **Citation:** Deming, D. J., Hastings, J. S., Kane, T. J., & Staiger, D. O. (2014). School choice, school quality, and postsecondary attainment. *American Economic Review, 104(3), 991-1013*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.3.991
- **Type:** Randomized (choice lottery)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Focus-option, magnet and lottery schools

**Headline:** Charlotte-Mecklenburg choice lottery: winning a seat at a first-choice school raised college attainment, with impacts predicted by school-quality gains and concentrated among girls.

## Setting and sample
Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools public school choice lottery (2002), linked to college enrollment and completion records.

## Method
Lottery-based comparisons of winners and losers.

## Key findings
- Significant overall increase in college attainment among lottery winners who attended their first-choice school.
- Impacts were strongly predicted by gains in measured school quality (peers, teachers, course offerings).
- Gains were concentrated among girls, who responded with higher grades and more college-prep course-taking.

## Limitations
Choice of any school, not specifically theme schools; one district.

## Relevance to PPS
Access to a preferred school can have long-run payoffs that test scores miss. This is relevant when a focus-option seat is removed.
