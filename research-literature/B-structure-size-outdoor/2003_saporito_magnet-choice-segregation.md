# Private choices, public consequences: Magnet school choice and segregation by race and poverty

- **Citation:** Saporito, S. (2003). Private choices, public consequences: Magnet school choice and segregation by race and poverty. *Social Problems, 50(2), 181-203*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1525/sp.2003.50.2.181
- **Type:** Descriptive / quantitative (application data)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Focus-option, magnet and lottery schools

**Headline:** Magnet applications in a large city: white and wealthier families avoided schools with more non-white and poor students, and their exits increased segregation in the neighborhood schools they left.

## Setting and sample
Magnet-school application data from a large U.S. city school district.

## Method
Analysis of families' magnet applications in relation to school racial and economic composition, test scores, safety and poverty.

## Key findings
- White families avoided schools with higher percentages of non-white students, which other school characteristics (test scores, safety, poverty) did not explain.
- Wealthier families avoided higher-poverty schools.
- These choices increased racial and economic segregation in the neighborhood schools the students left.

## Limitations
One district; application behavior rather than outcomes; data from the 1990s.

## Relevance to PPS
This is evidence that focus-option schools can draw students away from neighborhood schools along race and class lines. It bears on the equity rationale for ending focus options, but should be weighed against Sunnyside's own demographics, including its high special-education share.
