# Nature-based interventions for autistic children: A systematic review and meta-analysis

- **Citation:** Fan, M. S. N., Li, W. H. C., Ho, L. L. K., Phiri, L., & Choi, K. C. (2023). Nature-based interventions for autistic children: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *JAMA Network Open, 6(12), e2346715*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.46715
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2023_fan_nature-autism-meta.txt`

**Headline:** 24 studies (717 children): nature-based interventions were associated with moderate short-term improvements in social communication (SMD -0.59), hyperactivity (SMD -0.56) and sensory functioning, but most were equine-assisted therapy, not school-based outdoor learning.

## Setting and sample
Children with autism spectrum disorder (ages about 5-18). 24 studies: 7 RCTs, 6 controlled quasi-experiments, 11 single-group pre/post.

## Method
PRISMA systematic review of 10 databases; random-effects meta-analysis; ROB 2 and ROBINS-I appraisal; SWiM synthesis.

## Key findings
- Social communication: SMD -0.59 (95% CI -0.85 to -0.34; lower = better).
- Hyperactivity: SMD -0.56 (-0.86 to -0.26). Irritability: SMD -0.49 (-0.79 to -0.19).
- Sensory functioning: improved inattention and distractibility (SMD 1.13), sensory seeking (0.77) and sensory sensitivity (0.56).
- 83% of interventions were recreational therapy, mostly equine-assisted. All 7 RCTs had 'some concerns' for risk of bias. The authors note the overall quality of evidence is limited.

## Limitations
Interventions are mostly equine therapy and recreation, not classroom or outdoor schooling; short-term outcomes; small samples.

## Relevance to PPS
This should not be cited as evidence that outdoor schools help autistic students academically. It does indicate that structured nature-based activity is associated with better behavioral and sensory functioning in autistic children. Use with caution.
