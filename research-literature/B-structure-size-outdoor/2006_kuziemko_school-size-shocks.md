# Using shocks to school enrollment to estimate the effect of school size on student achievement

- **Citation:** Kuziemko, I. (2006). Using shocks to school enrollment to estimate the effect of school size on student achievement. *Economics of Education Review, 25(1), 63-75*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2004.10.003
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (panel first differences and IV)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale

**Headline:** Using enrollment shocks from openings, closings and mergers as instruments, smaller schools raised math scores and attendance, with benefits outweighing costs.

## Setting and sample
School-level panel data on U.S. (Indiana) schools.

## Method
First-difference panel estimates; two-stage least squares using enrollment shocks from school openings, closings and mergers.

## Key findings
- Smaller schools increase math scores and attendance rates.
- The author estimates that the benefits of smaller schools outweigh their costs.

## Limitations
School-level aggregate outcomes. The instrument (openings, closings, mergers) could affect outcomes through disruption as well as size.

## Relevance to PPS
Closures and mergers like PPS's enlarge receiving schools. This study's causal estimates point to lower attendance and math achievement as school size rises.
