# Does class size matter?

- **Citation:** Schanzenbach, D. W. (2014). Does class size matter?. *National Education Policy Center (NEPC) Policy Brief, University of Colorado Boulder*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://nepc.colorado.edu/publication/does-class-size-matter
- **Type:** Report - not peer-reviewed (policy research brief)
- **Peer-reviewed:** No
- **Subtopic:** 3. Class size
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2014_schanzenbach_class-size-nepc.txt`

**Headline:** Policy brief: class size is an important determinant of outcomes (STAR: about 0.15-0.20 SD); increasing class sizes will harm outcomes, particularly for low-income and minority children.

## Setting and sample
Review of U.S. and international class-size research, centered on STAR and quasi-experimental replications.

## Method
Narrative research synthesis by a STAR researcher.

## Key findings
- STAR improved math and reading scores by about 0.15-0.20 SD (about 5 percentile points), with larger gains for low-SES and Black students.
- Most quasi-experimental evidence is consistent with STAR. Some large statewide class-size reduction policies (e.g., California) had smaller effects, partly due to hiring inexperienced teachers.
- Recommendations: 'All else being equal, increasing class sizes will harm student outcomes'; class-size reduction may be cost-effective overall.

## Limitations
Not peer-reviewed; written from a pro-class-size-reduction perspective. Downplays null findings such as Hoxby 2000 and the Filges et al. meta-analysis.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the most-cited summary for the argument that consolidation should not raise class sizes in receiving schools, especially for disadvantaged students.
