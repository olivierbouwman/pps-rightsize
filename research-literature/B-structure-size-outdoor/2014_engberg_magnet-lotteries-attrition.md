# Evaluating education programs that have lotteried admission and selective attrition

- **Citation:** Engberg, J., Epple, D., Imbrogno, J., Sieg, H., & Zimmer, R. (2014). Evaluating education programs that have lotteried admission and selective attrition. *Journal of Labor Economics, 32(1), 27-63*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1086/671797
- **Type:** Randomized (lotteries) with bounds for attrition
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 5. Focus-option, magnet and lottery schools

**Headline:** Urban-district magnet programs with admission lotteries improved behavioral outcomes but had no significant effect on achievement.

## Setting and sample
An anonymous U.S. urban district rationing magnet programs by lottery; lottery losers often left the district.

## Method
Lottery-based estimates with partial-identification bounds to address differential attrition.

## Key findings
- Magnet programs improve behavioral outcomes.
- No significant effect on achievement.
- Methodological point: lottery losers are more likely to leave the district, which biases naive estimates.

## Limitations
One district; bounds rather than point estimates.

## Relevance to PPS
This mirrors Cullen et al.: focus or magnet programs show behavioral rather than test-score benefits. Losing an option may also push families out of the district, which matters for an enrollment-driven plan.
