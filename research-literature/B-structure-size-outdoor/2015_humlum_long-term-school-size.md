# Long-term effects of school size on students' outcomes

- **Citation:** Humlum, M. K., & Smith, N. (2015). Long-term effects of school size on students' outcomes. *Economics of Education Review, 45, 28-43*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2015.01.003
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental (FE and IV)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 2. School size and economies of scale
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2015_humlum_long-term-school-size.txt`

**Headline:** Denmark: larger lower-secondary schools had a small positive effect on high-school completion and age-30 earnings (e.g., about +0.8 pp completion per 100 students in OLS), driven by boys and low-education families.

## Setting and sample
Danish register data on all public-school grade-9 students, 1986-2004. Average school size about 460, much smaller than typical U.S. schools.

## Method
Fixed-effects and instrumental-variable estimators, using natural population variation in catchment areas and school openings and closures as instruments.

## Key findings
- A robust positive but numerically small relationship between school size and long-term educational and labor-market outcomes.
- For example, the OLS estimate implies 100 more students raises the probability of completing high school by about 0.8 percentage points; fixed-effects estimates are smaller.
- Effects are driven mainly by boys and students from families with low education.
- The authors stress that Danish schools are small (average about 460), so results may not carry over to larger U.S. or U.K. schools.

## Limitations
Danish context with small schools; IV estimates less precise; long-run outcomes reflect old cohorts.

## Relevance to PPS
This is a credible counterpoint. Moving up from very small schools (well under about 460) may help. It supports some consolidation of very small schools, but says little about pushing schools well above that size.
