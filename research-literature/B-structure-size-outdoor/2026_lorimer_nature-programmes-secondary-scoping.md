# A scoping review of nature-based programmes in schools for mental health and wellbeing in young people

- **Citation:** Lorimer, J., Menon, S., Polgreen, K., Wilhelm, K., & Singh, I. (2026). A scoping review of nature-based programmes in schools for mental health and wellbeing in young people. *Health & Place, 100, 103704*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healthplace.2026.103704
- **Type:** Scoping review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 4. Outdoor, environmental, place-based and nature-based education

**Headline:** 22 studies of school nature-based programs for ages 10-18: reduced anxiety and stress, better self-esteem, prosocial behavior and school connectedness; academic evidence mixed with little sign of broad academic gains.

## Setting and sample
School-based nature programs (gardening, outdoor education, conservation, forest pedagogy) for students aged 10-18; international evidence, U.K. policy framing.

## Method
PRISMA-ScR scoping review (17,723 records screened); MMAT appraisal; co-produced with educators and young people.

## Key findings
- Reported mental-health and wellbeing outcomes: reduced anxiety and stress, improved self-esteem and resilience, enhanced prosocial behavior, school connectedness and nature connectedness.
- Academic outcomes were mixed: modest gains in science engagement and arithmetic, but little evidence of broader academic benefit.
- Study quality was moderate: small samples, limited control groups, heterogeneous outcomes.

## Limitations
Scoping (not effectiveness) review; small, heterogeneous studies.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the most recent evidence for the middle-grade age band (Sunnyside's grades 6-8). Wellbeing and connectedness benefits are the best-supported outcomes, which is relevant when those students are moved to a conventional middle school.
