# Development during adolescence: The impact of stage-environment fit on young adolescents' experiences in schools and in families

- **Citation:** Eccles, J. S., Midgley, C., Wigfield, A., Buchanan, C. M., Reuman, D., Flanagan, C., & Mac Iver, D. (1993). Development during adolescence: The impact of stage-environment fit on young adolescents' experiences in schools and in families. *American Psychologist, 48(2), 90-101*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.48.2.90
- **Type:** Theoretical review (drawing on longitudinal studies)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Foundational 'stage-environment fit' account: declines in motivation and self-perception after the move to junior high reflect a mismatch between young adolescents' developmental needs and the more controlling, less personal secondary-school environment.

## Setting and sample
U.S. longitudinal research on young adolescents, including the Michigan Study of Adolescent Life Transitions.

## Method
Integrative theoretical review.

## Key findings
- After the transition, students commonly encounter more teacher control and less autonomy, less personal teacher-student relationships, more whole-class instruction and ability grouping, and stricter grading.
- This mismatch with adolescents' growing needs for autonomy and supportive adult relationships is linked to declines in motivation, interest and self-perceptions.
- The authors argue the problem is the environment, not adolescence itself, so schools can be designed to fit better.

## Limitations
Theoretical and synthesis piece; specific effect sizes come from underlying studies. Pre-dates modern causal designs.

## Relevance to PPS
This is the main theoretical lens for why K-8 settings may do better: continuity of relationships and smaller, more personal environments. It is useful for judging whether PPS middle schools can replicate those features.
