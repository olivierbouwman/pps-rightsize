# How does your kindergarten classroom affect your earnings? Evidence from Project STAR

- **Citation:** Chetty, R., Friedman, J. N., Hilger, N., Saez, E., Schanzenbach, D. W., & Yagan, D. (2011). How does your kindergarten classroom affect your earnings? Evidence from Project STAR. *Quarterly Journal of Economics, 126(4), 1593-1660*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjr041
- **Type:** Randomized experiment (long-term follow-up)
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 3. Class size

**Headline:** STAR follow-up: small classes raised college attendance; earnings effects at age 27 were imprecise; overall classroom quality had lasting effects even as test-score gains faded.

## Setting and sample
11,571 Tennessee STAR students linked to tax records into adulthood.

## Method
Experimental comparisons using random assignment to classes in grades K-3.

## Key findings
- Students in small classes were significantly more likely to attend college and showed improvements on other adult outcomes.
- The class-size effect on earnings at age 27 was not significant, but imprecisely estimated.
- More experienced kindergarten teachers raised earnings. Classroom quality (measured by peers' end-of-year scores) affected earnings and college attendance.
- Test-score effects faded, but non-cognitive gains persisted.

## Limitations
One state and cohort; long-run class-size effects imprecise.

## Relevance to PPS
Class conditions in the early grades have long-run effects. Disruption to classroom quality and teacher stability during consolidation may matter beyond measured test scores.
