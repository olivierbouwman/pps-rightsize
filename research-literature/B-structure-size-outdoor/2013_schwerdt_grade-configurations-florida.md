# The impact of alternative grade configurations on student outcomes through middle and high school

- **Citation:** Schwerdt, G., & West, M. R. (2013). The impact of alternative grade configurations on student outcomes through middle and high school. *Journal of Public Economics, 97, 308-326*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2012.10.002
- **Type:** Quasi-experimental
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions

**Headline:** Florida statewide: middle-school entry in grade 6 lowers math by 0.12 SD and reading by 0.09 SD, losses grow through grade 8 and do not recover in high school, and early dropout rises by 1.4 points (about 18%).

## Setting and sample
All Florida public school students in grades 3-10, 2000-01 through 2008-09 (urban, suburban and rural). Compares students who enter a middle school in grade 6 or 7 with those in K-8 schools, and high-school entry in grade 9 with students in 6-12 or K-12 schools.

## Method
Within-student fixed-effects models of test-score trajectories, with transition timing determined by the grade configuration of the school attended in grade 3 (or grade 6 for the high-school analysis). Controls for retention and charter attendance. Separate Miami-Dade and urbanicity analyses.

## Key findings
- Grade-6 middle-school entry: math -0.12 SD and reading -0.09 SD in the entry year. Grade-7 entry: -0.22 SD and -0.15 SD. These equal roughly 3.5-7 months of typical learning.
- Relative losses keep growing. After three years, grade-6 entrants are 0.23 SD (math) and 0.14 SD (reading) below K-8 peers.
- No catch-up in grades 9-10. Students who went through middle school do not gain more at the high-school transition.
- Grade-6 middle-school entry raises the probability of not being enrolled in grade 10 (a dropout proxy) by 1.4 percentage points, about 18%.
- The grade-9 transition itself costs only 0.03-0.04 SD, which does not persist.
- Effects are largest in cities but sizable in suburbs and rural areas. Math losses are larger for students below the median in grade 3 and for Black students.

## Limitations
Test-score and enrollment outcomes only; no direct measures of belonging or discipline. The study cannot fully separate a transition effect from a middle-school quality effect. Some families may select K-8 schools. Results for students with disabilities are not reported in the summaries reviewed.

## Relevance to PPS
This study directly tests the common argument that middle schools prepare students for high school, and finds no high-school payoff. For PPS's seven K-8 to K-5 conversions it predicts measurable achievement losses and a higher dropout risk for affected cohorts unless mitigated. Lower-achieving students are the most affected.
