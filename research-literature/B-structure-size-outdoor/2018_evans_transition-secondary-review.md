# A review of the academic and psychological impact of the transition to secondary education

- **Citation:** Evans, D., Borriello, G. A., & Field, A. P. (2018). A review of the academic and psychological impact of the transition to secondary education. *Frontiers in Psychology, 9, 1482*.
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01482
- **Type:** Narrative review
- **Peer-reviewed:** Yes
- **Subtopic:** 1. Grade configuration (K-8 vs middle school) and school transitions
- **Open-access full text (local):** `oa-fulltext/2018_evans_transition-secondary-review.txt`

**Headline:** Narrative review: the move to secondary school is a major stressor associated with declines in academic performance, self-perceptions and well-being, buffered by school belonging and teacher and peer support.

## Setting and sample
International literature (mostly U.K., U.S., Australia) on the primary-to-secondary transition.

## Method
Narrative (non-systematic) review.

## Key findings
- The transition coincides with early adolescence and is associated with declines in grades, academic self-perceptions and motivation, and with increased anxiety for some students.
- Risk and protective factors include school belonging, teacher and peer relationships, and prior mental health.
- Psychological health and achievement are intertwined across the transition.

## Limitations
Not systematic; no pooled effect sizes; much of the cited work is correlational. Mostly concerns systems where secondary entry is unavoidable, so it does not compare K-8 with middle schools directly.

## Relevance to PPS
This review supplies the mechanism (stress, belonging, relationships) behind the achievement dips in the grade-configuration studies. It implies PPS's conversions should come with deliberate transition supports, especially for vulnerable students.
