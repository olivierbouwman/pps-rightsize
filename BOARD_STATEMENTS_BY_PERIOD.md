### 2. Director-by-director

#### Eddie Wang (chair)
- **He sized facilities to projected enrollment early.** On 2/4 he backed ~300,000-sq-ft high schools. He cited a PSU projection that even equal enrollment would fill the high schools to only "a little less than 1,200 each", making the choice "30 empty classrooms" versus nicer rooms (CONFIRMED). On 2/11 he framed Res. 7051 as making "public dollars go as far as possible" while still delivering equivalent schools (PROBABLE).
- **He favored need over legacy promises in the SEGC area.** On 3/18 (PROBABLE; "this is my zone") he said he was "not pushing for" the 10 FTE promised to Southeast Guiding Coalition schools, because neighboring higher-need schools were left out. On Jefferson (6/10, PROBABLE) he argued "it's better to just do one change … lessons learned during the SEGC process". This is the same one-comprehensive-change logic he used in September 2026.
- **His budget framing:** a yes vote approves "the behavior of our district with what we are given", not the state's (6/10, CONFIRMED). He warned that some cuts "we're going to pay for down the line" in operations and deferred maintenance (4/22, PROBABLE). On Oregon's boom-and-bust funding, he said districts end up "rebuilding instead of progressing" (2/19).
- **Facilities:** he drafted Res. 7110 on seismic oversight ("limited funds … go as far as possible") and declared seismic "an urgent district priority" (5/6). He opposed adding $40M to the bond for a new Native student center and preferred an existing building (1/7, CONFIRMED).
- POSSIBLE: on 5/19 he flagged K-8 versus middle-school "equitable experiences" as a future board topic.
- Votes: yes on all budget items (7116, 7120, 7121) and on 7051, 7099, 7109 and 7110. No on the $40M amendment. In the Zone 1 appointment he voted Wirz, then switched to Splitt "to move us forward".

#### Michelle DePass (vice chair)
- **In 2025 she floated spending reserves down.** On 4/29 (CONFIRMED) she cited the 5–10% reserve policy and asked "if we would revisit spending that down a little bit", calling the situation "an emergency … dire". This contrasts with June 2026 (see §5).
- **She tied facilities to enrollment.** On 2/4 (CONFIRMED) she argued against sizing the high-school rebuilds on current enrollment because "Jefferson suffers" and asked for population projections instead. On 5/6 (PROBABLE) she asked whether seismic prioritization would use "criteria such as enrollment".
- **She preferred reusing existing buildings.** On 1/7 (PROBABLE) she opposed a new standalone center and said "the most sustainable option is to use the buildings that we have".
- **Jefferson:** she called for a recruitment partnership with realtors, banks and businesses, citing the neighborhood's gentrification history (6/10, PROBABLE).
- Votes: moved the budget approval (5/19) and the budget adoption (6/10). Yes on 7051, 7099, 7109 and 7110. No on the $40M amendment. She voted Wirz until the runoff, then Splitt.

#### Patte Sullivan
- **She raised school consolidation in May 2025.** On 5/19 (CONFIRMED; the draft minutes summarize this turn as hers) she said: "Do we need to consolidate or close some schools to get the optimal number of students? These are ideas that have come from the community. Let's consider them." This is the earliest openness to closures by any current director in the record.
- In the same speech: the budget "attempts to … protect classroom staffing as much as possible". She said that without ending the kicker or passing a sales tax, the legislature may "never" fund the Quality Education Model (QEM), and urged the district to grow enrollment by promoting its schools.
- **Enrollment and staffing tactics:** she warned against solving the "enrollment problem by putting kids in school too early" (5/12, PROBABLE). She asked what supports teachers moving into blended (combined-grade) classrooms would get (3/18, CONFIRMED).
- **Bond and seismic:** "this bond does have to pass if we're going to do anything we want to do" (4/29). She moved Res. 7110.
- Votes: Splitt in every appointment round. Seconded the budget adoption (7121). Yes on all budget items, 7051, 7099, 7109 and 7110. No on the $40M amendment. Absent 3/4.

#### Christy Splitt (appointed 1/13)
- **She shrank the high schools, including her own zone's.** She co-drafted Res. 7051 and told her Wells constituents that "looking at the size is important … $24 million", saying the board should build the high schools "roughly the same size" as Lincoln and Franklin (2/4, 2/11, CONFIRMED). She asked that the savings serve "middle school and elementary schools".
- **Enrollment balancing ideas:** declining enrollment needs a state "off-ramp" because losses are spread thinly across grades. She also suggested volunteers could "start kindergarten at the neighboring school" to "move kids around strategically … by choice" (4/22, PROBABLE).
- **K-8 versus middle school:** she relayed "whispers or downright shouts" from K-8s "wishing that they were not K8s" and asked about "transitioning those remaining six K8s". She requested data comparing K-8s with comprehensive middle schools (5/19, PROBABLE; she self-identified as "longtime PTA treasurer").
- **She was open early to consolidating under-enrolled schools.** In the Jefferson discussion (6/10, CONFIRMED) she urged "not letting affluent parents stop us". She relayed the Irvington PTA's question, "should we have two underenrolled elementary schools right next door to each other … maybe not", and asked the district to "be open-minded about that dynamic".
- **Budget:** cuts may be needed, but state underfunding is "unacceptable" (2/4). "It is really hard to vote yes on something … inadequate" (5/19). "It's because we need to have a budget" (6/10). She flagged that the seismic priority pushes other needs such as HVAC "further down the road" (5/6).
- POSSIBLE (4/22): praised the central-office restructure (Res. 7099) for cutting "middle manager positions" as "a cost savings measure".

#### Rashelle Chase-Miller, Virginia La Forte, Stephanie Engelsman
None was on the board in this window, and none spoke at a board meeting in it. The only mention: on 5/6/2025, the PAT president said she canvassed alongside Splitt and "pat endorsed schoolboard candidates like [Rashelle] Chase Miller, Jorge Sanchez Bautista, and Stephanie Englesman". ### 4. Notable staff framing

- **1/22 (press conference):** Armstrong said enrollment balancing "is a conversation that we will look into in the spring" and that the district will be "finding ways to optimize all of our learning spaces". Adams said enrollment is "predicted to decline for the next 14 years".
- **2/4:** Armstrong said PPS is "declining at a faster rate than our Oregon partner schools". The CFO showed school-based cuts (~$30M) overtaking central cuts (~$12M) for 2025-26.
- **4/22 (Res. 7099):** Armstrong: "we will be having conversations and continuing to reduce in size for years to come."
- **4/29:** the chief of staff warned against treating the 5% reserve as a rainy-day fund because of bond ratings. The seismic consultant's outputs include "schools that are not likely to be consolidated should that be a thing" (staff paraphrase on record).
- **5/6 bond update:** the third phase of seismic prioritization applies a "PPS lens". Staff asked, "if we're looking at changes to school models … or even consolidations of schools", whether "we want to put investments into schools that could potentially be consolidated".
- **6/6 facilities committee:** a school "that has other facility deficiencies or low enrollment" would feed into the district's "optimization work" when seismic projects are prioritized.
- **6/10 TSCC hearing:** Chief of Schools Franco said closures and consolidation save on "utilities, custodial maintenance, transportation" and deferred maintenance, and bigger schools mean "more elective offerings". He said the district would "start talking about it" in June and refine it in the fall. Armstrong said closure decisions will be "grounded in community" and cited DLI (dual-language immersion) schools with seven students in the neighborhood strand.

### 5. Contradictions with and nuances to BOARD_PROFILES.md

1. **Sullivan's openness to closures came earlier than PROFILES suggests.** PROFILES (§2.3) says she moved "from 'keep schools open' (2023) to conditional support … (2026)". But on **5/19/2025** she already asked "Do we need to consolidate or close some schools to get the optimal number of students? … Let's consider them" (`board-members/patte-sullivan/patte-sullivan_transcript_excerpts_2025-01_06.txt`, 01:09:56). Her shift predates the 2026 process by about a year.
2. **DePass on reserves reversed.** PROFILES (§2.2, via the 6/23/2026 quotes) presents her as firmly against drawing reserves, and on 6/23/2026 she called it "really irresponsible". But on **4/29/2025** she asked whether the board should "revisit spending that down a little bit" (`board-members/michelle-depass/michelle-depass_transcript_excerpts_2025-01_06.txt`, 02:02:39). This is an evolution, not an inconsistency within 2026.
3. **Splitt's record is more consolidation-friendly than the 2026 "scalpel" framing alone suggests.** PROFILES (§2.4) shows her as a skeptical swing vote on magnitude. In 2025 she (a) supported shrinking the Wells rebuild in her own zone, (b) raised converting the "remaining six K8s" (PROBABLE attribution), and (c) urged openness to merging adjacent under-enrolled elementary schools in the Jefferson area and "not letting affluent parents stop us" (6/10/2025). Her later skepticism is about scale, not direction, which fits PROFILES' "accepts some closures". PROFILES' 2025-02 timeline entry on the HS-footprint resolution is accurate; it **passed 7-0 on 2/11/2025** (Res. 7051), which PROFILES does not record.
4. **The first public closure signal came earlier than PROFILES dates it.** PROFILES (§0) dates it to **2025-02-05** (WW). The captions show Armstrong at the **1/22/2025** budget press conference already saying enrollment balancing would be taken up "in the spring" and promising ways "to optimize all of our learning spaces". By **6/10/2025** staff were describing a "school optimization plan" and closure trade-offs to the TSCC. The seismic program was also being designed in spring 2025 to avoid investing in schools "that could potentially be consolidated".
5. **The appointment vote is more nuanced than "4-2".** PROFILES (§2.4) lists DePass, Sullivan, Brim-Edwards and Wang as Splitt's supporters. That is correct for the final round, but **Wang and DePass first backed Jackie Wirz**. Wang switched in round 2, explicitly to "move us forward", and DePass switched only in the runoff. The draft minutes' per-round lists are internally inconsistent; the captions resolve them.
6. **The 2025-26 budget vote is missing from PROFILES.** It was adopted **5-2 on 6/10/2025** (Res. 7121), with Wang, DePass, Splitt and Sullivan yes and Brim-Edwards and Hollands no. All four current directors then serving voted for the 2025-26 cuts, including the ~157 school-based teacher reductions cited in the 4/29 work session.
7. **Wang's "one change, not two" stance predates 2026.** It appears on **6/10/2025** in the Jefferson discussion (PROBABLE), consistent with PROFILES' 9/1/2026 entry.

### 1. Shape of the period

In this period "rightsizing" was not yet a formal process. The process launched later, with Res. 7296 on 2026-04-14. The term came up in three settings:

1. **Seismic prioritization (Aug 2025 – Feb 2026).** The recurring question was which buildings should get roughly $100M of seismic money if some of them will close.
2. **The Jefferson HS dual-assignment and boundary decision (Sept 2025 – Jan 2026).** Staff and several directors framed it as groundwork for rightsizing.
3. **The Metropolitan Learning Center (MLC) high school.** Grades 9–12 were discontinued (announced about 2/10/2026, presented 2/24/2026). Directors treated it as the first closure, before the rightsizing process existed.

Behind all three sat a recurring budget gap: about $50M for 2026-27, after reserves were exhausted.

Key dates:
- **8/12/2025:** Office of School Modernization (OSM) staff already describe facilities and seismic data as inputs to "right sizing schools".
- **9/9/2025:** La Forte is the first director in this period to say "right sizing our schools" publicly, in the seismic study discussion.
- **9/30/2025:** Wang's draft work plan includes "board town halls", which he says are "eventually right sizing". He expects a boundary vote around January 2026.
- **10/28/2025:** The CFO projects a $50M shortfall for 2026-27. Her budget scenario assumes "no consolidations or school closures for 2627".
- **11/10/2025 and 11/13/2025:** Staff call the seismic list "preemptive before the right sizing conversation". Calvert says the Jefferson work will "dovetail into… right sizing", starting with a review of the Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC) boundary process and an advisory committee.
- **12/16/2025:** Armstrong says boundaries and consolidations will be discussed "for the next 12 to 18 months". Staff say the elementary and middle-school conversations start "as early as February".
- **1/13/2026:** Res. 7228 (7-0) and Res. 7229 (6-1) end Jefferson dual assignment and redraw the boundaries.
- **1/27/2026:** Four directors object that seismic contracts are effectively deciding which schools stay open. They still vote yes, 7-0.
- **2/24/2026:** The MLC work session. Engelsman says she "learned today" that the initial rightsizing list would come "sometime in the fall", with a December vote.

### 2. Director-by-director

#### Eddie Wang (chair)
- **He held the line on the budget, citing history.** "I've lived through the 2010 budget cuts … cake compared to this" (CONFIRMED). He said his first two years' cuts ($30M, then $48M) were steered mostly to central office, "kicking the can down the road" in hope of Salem, "and so now we're at the end of the road". He voted **no on 7342** because it "commits us to something that we may not be able to do", and **yes on the budget** (6/23).
- **Reserves and one-time money:** drawing reserves risks the credit rating and capital work ("might not be able to retrofit another elementary school with HVAC", PROBABLE). On 7/7 (CONFIRMED) he said one-time funds were deliberately used to "preserve the number of teachers, even though enrollment declined", and that this "props up" staffing until "picking up the can that we've kicked down the road". "Gratification today for … increased pain later … doesn't sound like a good deal to me."
- **Central office:** he resisted protecting a central-office position while teaching jobs were cut (OIPA amendment, CONFIRMED; personnel specifics omitted). On 7/7 he framed the district as having "turned over many stones … sacrifices in the … central office".
- **Rightsizing as the fix for split feeders:** on 8/4 (PROBABLE) he argued the board should set system policy, not one-off exceptions, which "can often be because of people who … have more access", and that "the right sizing process is where we make those … fixes … minimize or eliminate a lot of these split [feeders]". He explained that SEGC left out inner Southeast for "lack of capacity" (CONFIRMED).
- **Leadership:** he stayed on as chair "out of a need" for "stability" (7/7). He faced a filed challenge from Engelsman, which she withdrew.

#### Michelle DePass (vice chair until 7/7; Policy Committee chair)
- **"Courage" and no more yeses.** On 6/23 (CONFIRMED; WW/OPB): "this is a devastating budget … when they come and advocate, we … say yes … sometimes we need to display courage and do hard things … We can't say yes to every single thing." She called it "the worst in my 7 years here".
- **Against drawing reserves:** "if something were to happen … We would have to shut the district down". Having "voted three times now … to keep digging away at that reserves", she called going below the floor "really irresponsible" and said "There's no hope in Salem right now" (CONFIRMED). She suggested "let's re-look at MLC" for savings.
- **Process objections:** she criticized last-minute amendments made "on the dais", which "leave out important voices" (6/23, CONFIRMED). On AI she said good policy needs "careful, thoughtful, and intentional process, not a rushed and not a reactive one".
- **Equity and "vocal minorities":** she opposed the Creston sports exception as "listening to a very small but perhaps vocal minority … which is what … our board tends to do", calling it "a management issue" (8/4, CONFIRMED).
- **Contracts:** she defended multi-year construction contracts as price guarantees and backed a contracts work session that looks at "opportunity cost" (7/7). PROBABLE (6/15): from her construction background, she said amendments over 125% were "a red flag".

#### Patte Sullivan
- **Against the reserve draw and the restoration amendment.** On 6/23 (CONFIRMED): "when I first got on the board … 7.5% reserve … Didn't get built back up … to think we're going to build that back up is just a daydream … irresponsible. … Maybe Salem's looking at this board meeting … I can't fiscally vote yes on this." Earlier (PROBABLE) she said: "even if we did take the reserve, we're 7 million short … it's absolutely impossible to get that 13 million", noting the board "voted not to have foundations".
- **PAT's savings list:** she forwarded PAT's ideas to staff and learned "we've done a lot about it already" (7/7, CONFIRMED). She warned that early-retirement incentives are "costing more money than they ever thought … Immediate rewards don't always work" (7/7).
- POSSIBLE (6/23): the travel freeze is "such a tiny bit … Maybe a half of a paraprofessional", and "doing this at the last minute … inappropriate".
- She attended the 6/11 TLE and 6/15 Policy meetings as a non-member. No remarks of hers were captured on 8/4 (apparently absent).

#### Christy Splitt
- **Against $13M, but "first do the schools".** On 6/23 (CONFIRMED) she said she had "testified … about protecting student-facing positions at all costs" and recalled her predecessor's "fiscal cliff" warning: "we are falling off the fiscal cliff". She said "I just like don't know where we could cut another $13 million", while agreeing "first do the schools and then do the district office" for "a really big, maybe too big school district". Her idea was to house central special-education administrators in schools. She voted **no on 7342** and **yes on the budget**.
- **Wanted only a small reserve cushion:** she ran the numbers (4% leaves $8.2M; 4.5% leaves $4.1M) and would support "a small dip … that isn't guaranteed … adding back staff on … an as need basis", but not 3.4%. She pointed to Measures 5 and 50: "let's spend the next year doing all the things we're talking about … so that it can fix next year's budget" (CONFIRMED).
- **Transparency on contracts and pay:** she worried "we may be continuing to invest in contracts for services that are valuable but maybe not as valuable as educators" (7/7, CONFIRMED). She asked whether the highest earners took "the biggest hit" (7/7, PROBABLE). She asked the board to learn the layoff and seniority process ahead of bargaining (6/9, CONFIRMED).
- **Split feeders and focus options:** "I'm more interested in solving the root cause problem which is a very small number of kids being assigned to a different middle school … not set up a system where we have 10 kids … going to a different middle school" (8/4, CONFIRMED). She proposed an athletics appeal process as an alternative to the resolution. PROBABLE (6/11): suspected focus-option schools "might have lower class sizes" and asked for data. On 8/4 she asked how whole-school Spanish DLI admissions work (CONFIRMED).
- **Governance:** PROBABLE (7/7): a third year as chair "feels like not quite in line" with her preference for "more frequent turnover of leadership and less … centralized power". She nonetheless moved Chase-Miller's nomination.

#### Rashelle Chase-Miller (vice chair from 7/7)
- **She proposed the reserve draw and then voted against restoration.** On 6/23 (CONFIRMED): "show me the path to that 13-plus million dollars … what happens next year when we have another 65 million dollars in cuts". Her sub-amendment would direct the superintendent "to dip into our reserves", coupled with "aggressive revenue-generating, cost-saving measures": "property we can sell off" and reductions in "that top tier of leadership". She said "I never thought that I would propose dipping into the reserves for any reason". It was narrowed to a 4% floor (~$8.2M, about half the positions, PROBABLE) and **failed 2-4-1**. She then voted **no on 7342** and **yes on the budget**.
- "We're compelled to look at every option. So, I say we look at every option" (CONFIRMED).
- On the OIPA amendment she said "We don't need this amendment" because the position was already funded (CONFIRMED by DePass's reply).
- **Equity framing of cross-school middle-school sports** (8/4, CONFIRMED): the cluster model "is different and intentional … really important from an equity perspective", helping to dispel prejudice between neighborhoods. She wanted "the big picture" before voting. Staff confirmed on 7/7 that a single **$2.5M real-property sale** was already built into the budget, after she had raised asset sales.
- She was absent from the 6/11 TLE meeting she chairs.

#### Virginia La Forte
- **Cut-the-top amendments.** On 6/23 (CONFIRMED) she authored a one-year freeze on discretionary travel for the board and leadership, with savings "to classroom-based FTE positions" ("if it's $5, it's worth passing", OPB). It passed 7-0 after Splitt narrowed it to out-of-state travel. She also wrote the OIPA-funding amendment (failed 3-4) and the generative-AI contract pause (passed 7-0). She said the travel freeze "sends a message that … we're in this with everybody" as classrooms take "heavy hits".
- She voted **no on 7342** (the tally was 1-6) and **yes on the budget**. POSSIBLE (6/23): she floated renegotiating "personal services and outside contracts" and pushing the vote to June 30.
- **Contract transparency:** multi-year not-to-exceed service contracts "don't come back to this board", which she called a "fair criticism" from the public. She proposed a public, searchable check register like the City of Portland's (7/7, CONFIRMED).
- **Focus options and Jefferson:** she chaired the 6/11 TLE session on focus and alternative schools ("my favorite topic ever") and flagged the "Jefferson is rising" update and the instructional-time exemptions.
- **Split feeders:** she moved the Creston resolution, asked staff for "the root cause and … the solution", and asked that "the cluster model athletics structure [be] part of what we are considering when we go through right sizing … restructuring of our K8s and middle schools". She also rejected the "small vocal minority" framing (8/4, PROBABLE).

#### Stephanie Engelsman
- **Restore $13.12M; "not my job to find the money".** On 6/23 (CONFIRMED): "it's not my job or our job to come up with finding the money. It's our job to protect the students." Fully staff every school to 2025-26 levels first, "then we ask, where do we get the money for everything else". Her enrollment argument: $13.12M ÷ $7,200 per student = 1,822 students, so "if we lose 1,822 students … we will have saved nothing". She suggested cutting outside contracts by 7–10%, AI contracts, and a week's delay. The amendment **failed 1-6**.
- **Delay and the reserve vote:** she voted yes on Chase-Miller's reserve sub-amendment (caption roll-check). She moved to postpone the budget a week (PROBABLE; failed 3-4) and argued Salem could be pressed in an election year to open the Education Stability Fund (PROBABLE). She **abstained on the budget**.
- **Leadership bid:** she filed to run for chair against Wang (6/9), withdrew on the day of the vote, and was absent 7/7.
- **Closures and boundaries:** she asked Dr. Green "how many families did PPS lose as a result of school closure boundary change?" (8/4, CONFIRMED). She asked why neighborhood children can be shut out of lottery-only DLI schools across the street (CONFIRMED). POSSIBLE: she demanded the district follow the QEM's class-size prototypes, not just its school-size thresholds ("do we end up saving anything …?").
- **Split feeder (Creston):** her resolution (8/4) described the SEGC K-8-to-K-5 conversion that splits ~10 students a year away from their cohort. She proposed a fix for 2026-27 only, pending the rightsizing boundary change. It failed 1-4-1.
- Smaller items: she objected to giving ACCESS students priority in neighborhood after-school programs (6/9, CONFIRMED). She raised contract-amendment oversight gaps (6/15, CONFIRMED).

#### Student representatives
- **Ian Ritorto** (6/23): "repeal 5 and 50". He was skeptical that $13M could be found and called the reserve draw "maybe a bit fiscally irresponsible … show Salem that the road is ended". His unofficial votes were no on 7342, the reserve sub-amendment and postponement, and yes on the budget.
- **Rose Sandell** (8/4): opposed the Creston exception because cross-school teams "allow us to make connections throughout the district". Her unofficial vote was no.

### 4. Notable staff framing

- **Armstrong, 6/9:** "going into fall of 2026 when we get into the conversations on closures and boundaries and program moves … we're not going to be able to have a conversation with every impacted human."
- **Armstrong, 6/23:** the 2027-28 reduction of ~$65M is meant to be "the stabilizing reduction". "The amount of central office admin, like all of the admin, is less than $5 million." Restoring positions now adds to next year's gap ($65M → $78M, per the CFO).
- **CFO, 6/23:** taking the full $13.12M from reserves would leave them at 3.4%, hurting the credit rating and cash flow. Bonds for capital projects are next expected in 2028.
- **Franco, 6/23 TSCC:** rightsizing savings are "approximately 15 to 20% in operational costs" per fully closed site, "but we made it very clear that we are not doing this for the purpose of saving costs". Savings "cannot initially be used to balance the budget" because transitions must be funded. Sale proceeds are directed to facilities under current board policy.
- **CFO, 7/7:** one real-property sale of **$2.5M** is already counted in the 2026-27 budget as one-time money. More sales are possible "as we continue to decline in enrollment".
- **Armstrong, 7/7:** "as we have fewer students, um that means we're going to have fewer humans, uh, fewer services". Any $2–4M found would go to the 2027-28 deficit, not to restoring positions.
- **Equity audit, 8/4 (Dr. Green):** SEGC balanced middle-school enrollment and outcomes were "pretty comparable". However, 12 of 20 schools under-enrolled versus projections. Staff turnover was 32% versus 19% in the first year. About 60% of the neighborhood was displaced from one whole-school DLI conversion, and Black enrollment there fell from ~10% to 2%. Excluding inner-SE schools "severed lingering trust". Program relocations disrupted schools more than boundary changes. Receiving schools need ~3 years of support. Recommendations: clear decision criteria, early engagement, receiving-school readiness, durable transition plans, and tracking cumulative burden.
- **Staff, 8/4:** roughly one in three or fewer elementary schools is at a QEM-sustaining enrollment today (the caption is garbled: "one and lesson one and three"), projected to fall below "one in five" within five years. About one in four K-8s meets it. 86% of the decline to date is in K-5, and "the leading edge … is going to hit middle schools in the next two years". High schools are "on a different path" after the Jefferson decision.

### 5. Contradictions with and nuances to BOARD_PROFILES.md

1. **Engelsman ran for chair, which PROFILES omits.** PROFILES (§0, §1) records the 7/7 officer votes as 6-0 but not that **Engelsman had filed to run for chair against Wang** (announced 6/9) and withdrew on 7/7. She was also absent that night, which is why both votes were 6-0. The challenge is relevant to the Wang-versus-dissenters dynamic (`board-members/stephanie-engelsman/stephanie-engelsman_transcript_excerpts_2026-06_08.txt`, RECORDED VOTES).
2. **Chase-Miller's reserve proposal was narrower and less successful than PROFILES implies.** PROFILES (§2.5) says she "proposed tapping reserves instead of cutting staff (failed)". Captions show her motion went to a 4% floor (~$8.2M), coupled with property sales and leadership reductions, and **failed 2-4-1**. Only she and Engelsman voted yes. Her stated reason was wanting "a path" to the full $13M, and she said she "never thought" she would propose it. She then voted against Engelsman's restoration. The current wording is accurate but understates how isolated the proposal was.
3. **DePass's 2026 reserve stance reverses her 2025 position.** PROFILES quotes her 6/23/2026 opposition, but on 4/29/2025 she had asked about spending reserves down (see the 2025-01_06 summary). Her 2026 framing ("voted three times … to keep digging away at that reserves") explains the change.
4. **Splitt was not against any reserve use.** PROFILES (§2.4) records her no on the $13.2M amendment and yes on the budget. Captions add that she was **open to a small, contingent reserve cushion** for as-needed staffing. She also said PPS is "maybe too big" a district to run on what remains after school staffing. She moved the narrowing of La Forte's travel freeze and on 7/7 publicly criticized a third term for the chair (PROBABLE). That is a governance nuance alongside her "bridge" coding.
5. **Wang's description of the board as pro-teacher relies on contested history.** PROFILES (§2.1, via WW) notes he called it "the most pro-teacher board I've ever seen". On the record (6/23) he claims his first-year $30M cut came "all from the central office" with "zero cuts from teacher teaching positions", and that most of the $48M second-year cut was central. That fits the CBRC (Community Budget Review Committee) report he quotes, but in the 2025-01_06 captions staff show 2025-26 school-based cuts (~$30M) exceeding central cuts (~$12M). His "second year" claim is therefore debatable.
6. **La Forte: §2.6 is accurate but incomplete.** Captions add that she also authored the **OIPA-restoration amendment (failed 3-4)** and the **generative-AI pause (passed 7-0)**, and **moved Engelsman's Creston resolution** on 8/4. In the split-feeder debate she pressed for rightsizing to restructure "our K8s and middle schools" together with the athletics cluster model (PROBABLE). That is evidence for PROFILES' "conditional yes on an amended package" reading. She explicitly disputed DePass's "vocal minority" framing.
7. **Engelsman's closure position is earlier and more data-driven than PROFILES records.** PROFILES places her closure questions at 9/1/2026. On **8/4/2026** she already asked Dr. Green how many families PPS lost "as a result of school closure boundary change" (CONFIRMED). On 6/23 she made the enrollment-loss argument against cuts (1,822 students). This is consistent with PROFILES' "most likely no" coding.
8. **Sullivan was probably absent from the 8/4 equity-audit meeting.** No caption names her, and the Creston vote total is six. PROFILES does not rely on 8/4 for her, so there is no contradiction, but she did not hear the SEGC lessons-learned presentation in the room.
9. **There are small timing and wording nuances on the budget vote.** The 6/23 budget vote happened after midnight, so captions note "It's Wednesday now". PROFILES' "2026-06-23/24" covers this. Captions confirm Engelsman's abstention ("six with one abstention") and the 1-6 amendment vote. The amendment amount was **$13,120,000** ("reinstate funding … for staffing in schools in student-facing positions"). The "87 educators" figure comes from public comment (the PAT vice president); staff counted 82 layoffs on 6/9.
