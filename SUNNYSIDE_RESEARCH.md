# Sunnyside Environmental School and PPS Rightsizing

Advocacy versions: [`SUNNYSIDE_CASE.md`](SUNNYSIDE_CASE.md) (the short case) and [`SUNNYSIDE_BOARD_GUIDE.md`](SUNNYSIDE_BOARD_GUIDE.md) (per-director approach).

As of 2026-10-07. Compiled from the local research folder (board meeting transcripts, BoardBook materials, program documents, news). File paths are relative to this folder.

## 1. Summary

Under both rightsizing scenarios, Sunnyside stays open but ends its environmental focus option. It would become a neighborhood-only K-5 school, its grades 6–8 would move to Hosford Middle School, and it would take in about half of Buckman's attendance area when Buckman closes. The scenarios were released 2026-10-05; the superintendent recommends one in mid-November and the board votes in December, with changes starting fall 2027.

- **This is the only focus-option discontinuation in the plan.** The rest of Sunnyside's changes are shared with other schools: seven K-8s become K-5s, and Buckman, an arts focus option, closes outright.
- **Sunnyside is not a struggling school by the district's own numbers.** It had 485 students in 2025-26 (89% of 546 seats). PSU forecasts it flat at about 475–485 through 2035-36 while the district declines.
- **Staff called the choice of Sunnyside "arbitrary."** On 2026-10-06, Splitt asked why Sunnyside was picked. Staff answered that "Sunnyside just happened to be one of the ones just arbitrarily because some of them needed to go and some needed to stay."
- **Three directors have raised Sunnyside so far: Engelsman, Splitt and La Forte.** Engelsman objects most directly; Sunnyside is in her zone. Splitt and La Forte asked for the rationale but have broader doubts about focus options. Wang, DePass, Sullivan and Chase-Miller have not spoken about Sunnyside specifically in any recorded meeting.

**History**
- The program started as a middle school in 1995, according to a long-time community member's public comment on 2026-10-06. It now runs as a hybrid: a neighborhood school with a lottery focus program, a model PPS also uses at Buckman (arts) and Bridger (creative science).
- **Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC), 2022:** excluded Sunnyside from its boundary work although the coalition called it "another overcrowded school adjacent to both Mt. Tabor and Hosford." The coalition recommended that the board review K-8s, including Sunnyside, because they "create challenges in properly planning for stable enrollment in surrounding middle schools" (`program-docs/2022-04-22_SEGC-phase-2-final-report.txt`).
- **The 2021 Long-Range Facility Plan** listed Sunnyside at 546 capacity with projected utilization falling from 96% to 83% (`program-docs/2021-12_PPS-long-range-facility-plan-vol1.txt`). That decline did not happen; enrollment rose instead.
- **Split feeder:** the 2026-09-15 rightsizing deck lists Sunnyside as a "split feeder," because its focus-option students return to their own neighborhood high schools. Reducing split feeders is one of the district's rightsizing performance indicators (`boardbook/2026-09-15_Regular_Meeting_766050/09.15.26_BOE_Meeting_Rightsizing_2_14430171.txt`).

## 3. What the scenarios propose

Scenarios A and B treat Sunnyside identically. The only difference near Sunnyside is that Lewis stays open in B.

| Change | Scenario A | Scenario B | Status quo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Environmental focus option | Discontinued | Discontinued | Kept |
| New lottery transfers | None; existing lottery students may stay through grade 5 | Same | Lottery continues |
| Grades | K-5 "Neighborhood ONLY" | Same | K-8 |
| Grades 6–8 | Move to Hosford MS | Same | Stay |
| Buckman closure | 50% of Buckman's area to Sunnyside, 50% to Abernethy | Same | — |
| Attendance boundary | Changed | Changed | — |

Sources: `boardbook/2026-10-06_Regular_Meeting_769955/Rightsizing_Update_Scenario_Release_Memo_14509719.txt`, `.../10.06.26_PowerPoint_14511742.txt`, `.../District_wide_scenarios_10.04.26_14505788.txt`, `.../Regional_summary_-_Cleveland-Franklin_14505790.txt`, and the boundary map PDFs in the same folder.

**What the district says about it**
- **The memo's own words:** "Sunnyside's environmental focus option would be discontinued; it would become a neighborhood school with no new lottery transfers, while existing lottery students could remain through fifth grade." It also says "No alternative or focus option school closure is proposed."
- **The stated reason (Oct 6 presentation):** grades 6–8 move "to Hosford to maintain enrollment," and the K-5 change lets Sunnyside "offer sustainable enrollment at the nearby K5 schools." Buckman's closure was driven by enrollment plus "severe facility concerns." Buckman is projected at 239 students and Abernethy at 241, both severely under-enrolled by 2031-32.
- **The ripple effects:**
  - Hosford (528 students, 711 seats, 74% in 2025-26) would take Sunnyside's 6–8 students plus the Llewellyn-area students from closing Sellwood MS.
  - Abernethy (269 students, 61%) and Buckman (283 students, 43%, Title I) are Sunnyside's neighbors in the Cleveland-Franklin region.

**Two numbers that don't add up yet**
- **About 55% of Sunnyside's students come by lottery.** The scenarios assume Buckman's half-area replaces them, but no Sunnyside-specific projection under A or B is published in the files collected.
- **Capacity:** taking half of Buckman's area into a building whose functional capacity staff put at about 490 needs a seat count. That count is not in the materials yet.

**How each director reads on this question (inference)**
- **Engelsman:** the most likely to fight for Sunnyside's 6–8 and focus program, given her zone, her specific questions and her overall opposition to the scenarios.
- **Splitt and La Forte:** cross-pressured. Both questioned the selection, but both have said focus options pull students from neighborhood schools. La Forte openly expected all focus options to be on the table.
- **Sullivan:** the spring transcripts show her praising Sunnyside's hybrid model by name. Likely the fourth vote.
- **Wang and Chase-Miller:** both said in April they'd rather fix focus-option impacts through lottery or policy, or through focus programs inside neighborhood schools, than by closing programs. They are reachable with that framing.
- **DePass:** favors a larger package. No Sunnyside-specific record.

## 6. Community, stakeholders and news

- **A parent of a neurodivergent sixth grader** said Sunnyside "attracts neurodivergent students because they take care of them" and asked: "Do not close Sunnyside Middle."

- **Portland City Councilor Tiffany Koyama-Lane** is a former Sunnyside K-8 teacher (Oregonian, 2026-06-24). She spoke against educator cuts at the June budget vote.

## 7. Open questions and next decision points

**Questions on the record but not yet answered**
- **Why Sunnyside rather than another focus option?** Engelsman, Splitt and La Forte all asked. The only answer so far is "arbitrarily," plus a general rationale about balancing focus and neighborhood schools.
- **Could Sunnyside keep its environmental programming as a neighborhood-only school?** Splitt asked this; no answer yet.
- **Hosford's capacity** with Sunnyside 6–8 and Sellwood students combined (Engelsman).
- **A Sunnyside-specific enrollment projection** under A/B, and the seat count against the ~490 functional capacity.
- **The net savings** from this change. A fiscal impact statement is promised with the November recommendation.

**Next decision points**
- **Community engagement sessions:** through late October.
- **Teaching, Learning & Enrollment committee on immersion and middle grades:** 2026-10-13. This is the most direct venue for the K-8 → K-5 question.
- **Board receives the engagement summary:** 2026-11-04.
- **Superintendent's recommendation, with fiscal impact:** 2026-11-17.
- **Board vote:** 2026-12-15. Changes start fall 2027.
