Budget crunch forces Portland Public Schools to weigh furloughs, school closures
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Portland Public Schools has a money problem.
With about three months left in the school year, the district must close an unexpected $14 million budget deficit.
That figure exceeds the $10 million deficit the district originally reported. The total shortfall is closer to $22 million, but Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said cost-cutting measures have reduced part of the gap.

The district has begun cutting positions in the central office. Armstrong said leaders are considering temporary furloughs for some administrators and staff.
District officials said the move aims to reduce expenses without cutting programs that directly support students and schools.
“We’ve communicated that central office leaders will take five furlough days. I’ll take six furlough days. Four of those days will be unpaid. We’ve also announced a plan to reduce about a third of our licensed administrators,” Armstrong said.

“We can’t afford to employ the same number of people we have in the past. We’re in an uncomfortable situation and must respond to our budget. That means we need to do things differently. We need to be tighter in how we fund programs and staff both the central office and our schools,” she added.
These cuts mark the first of three phases to address this year’s shortfall. KATU is working to learn what additional reductions PPS plans to make.
The district also faces a $50 million shortfall for the 2027 school year. Armstrong said she plans to discuss closing five to 10 schools at the March 10 school board meeting as part of a long-term strategy.
“I anticipate presenting the board with a list of schools for closure in November, with board action in December and community notification in January. There will be no school closures next academic year, but we anticipate some closures after that,” Armstrong said.
Armstrong acknowledged that closing schools would not generate significant savings but said it would help optimize school communities.
The district did not name any schools Tuesday as part of the potential closure plan. Officials plan to seek community feedback before Armstrong makes her recommendations.
Armstrong said she will present a proposed budget to the school board in April. The board is expected to take a final vote in June.
