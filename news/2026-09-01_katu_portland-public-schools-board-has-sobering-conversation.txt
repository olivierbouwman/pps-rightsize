Portland Public Schools board has 'sobering' conversation about school closure priorities
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong opened up Tuesday night's conversation about Rightsizing, the district's plan to close or consolidate schools to adjust for drops in enrollment that are projected to continue for several more years, by saying there is no plan yet.
"Tonight is about establishing the runway for the work. Not determining its outcome," Armstrong told board members at their Tuesday night meeting at Grant High School.

District staff wrote in Tuesday's presentation that 15 elementary schools and three middle schools would need to close for each remaining building to be at a "sustainable" enrollment level.
Board member Stephanie Engelsman posed several questions out of the gate: How much would the district save with each closure? How many layoffs would be involved? Where would employees go after a closure or consolidation?
Armstrong said she and district staff will work to answer those questions.
While each member said they want to learn more about the potential ramifications of closing schools, some expressed surprise at the projection that 15 to 20 buildings may need to shutter.
"This is not easy," said board member Patte Sullivan. She said she's noticed inequalities between schools after visiting multiple buildings recently for the start of the year.
"It's important to me that they all look good," Sullivan said. "If that means we have to do some Rightsizing, then I'm really wanting to look at it. Because I've visited schools where things look really bad. I don't think there should be that difference."
Christy Splitt said that she is concerned about bigger schools that already fit into the enrollment threshold of 360+ students for elementary and 500+ for middle and K-8 schools being spared from the cuts. Those schools tend to be in more affluent areas, she said.
"So the Duniways and Alamadas get protected just because they're big? That doesn't seem very fair."
Armstrong and her staff will develop recommendations, which will include which schools could be on the chopping block. Board members will make the final decision by the end of 2026.
