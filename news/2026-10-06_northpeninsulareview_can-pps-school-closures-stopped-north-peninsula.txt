Title: Can PPS School closures be stopped? - North Peninsula Review
Outlet: North Peninsula Review
Author: Mark Kirchmeier
Date: 2026-10-06
URL: https://northpeninsulareview.com/can-pps-school-closures-be-stopped/

By Mark Kirchmeier
“Portland school district administrators are walking off a cliff if they close 10 to 20 schools,” predicts Paul Anthony, who formerly represented part of North Portland on the school board.
Anthony and fellow former board member Steve Buel, who previously served as a N/NE Portland board member, believe that a coalition of parents and the Portland Alliance of Teachers union might still stop the district’s scheduled plan to close some elementary and K-8 schools next year.
Portland Public School (PPS) leaders maintain some closures are needed to save operating costs and enable the district potentially to sell some school properties. Anthony and Buel believe that school administrators may have flunked their homework in failing to see the legal obstacles to the latter.
“The district will have a difficult time, impossible in some cases, trying to sell some school buildings because of deed restrictions on those properties, keeping them in use as schools, or general public use, in perpetuity,” Anthony said.
Buel has asked the PPS Public Records Office to provide him with information on deed restrictions that could affect any future sale of some 20 elementary and K-8 schools, including James John, Peninsula, Rosa Parks, Sitton and Woodlawn in North and inner Northeast Portland. PPS currently faces a $50 million deficit in its $2.77 billion budget.
Anthony believes that shuttering some schools will only exacerbate the deficit. “School consolidations inevitably require costly investment in building renovations. The last such consolidation, closing Humboldt School and merging it into Boise-Eliot, required $10 million in renovations — even though the district had just put millions into improvements — at Boise-Eliot the previous year,” Anthony said.
“Many of the buildings that the district is now contemplating to house consolidated schools aren’t even in as good condition as Boise-Eliot before renovations. PPS leaders seem unaware of that,” Anthony said. He estimated that the costs of consolidating 20 schools could exceed $300 million.
Impact on communities
And there’s the effect on neighborhoods. “You can’t close 15 schools — without denigrating 15 neighborhoods,” Buel explained. Terrance Moses agrees, and specifically with Peninsula Elementary School, which might be among the closures.
“If Peninsula should close, that would be a hard pill to swallow for our North Portland community,” said Moses, a longtime community organizer.
“This would cause our families unimaginable hardship in trying to find a new school for their kids. Earlier rise times to get kids to the next nearest school on time, more stress on the tough schedule some parents have trying to get kids to school and get to work etc. Families are already feeling the pinch, and to have one more disruption in their lives could send many families into overload.
“PPS needs to do better and serve the marginalized communities, and go back to the table, and look at the worth of the schools in the community [that] they are about to affect,” he said
Closures would impact the entire city, said Portland City Councilor Dan Ryan, one of the three councilors representing N/NE Portland. “The city’s in charge of the infrastructure surrounding these schools and we have a big responsibility for families and safety.”
Ryan served on the school board from 2004 to 2008. He found that some PPS administrators at that time didn’t have on-the-ground knowledge of neighborhoods, knowledge that can affect closure decisions. “If you’re looking at white boards and spread sheets, you’re not talking to neighbors and don’t know the neighborhood,” Ryan said.
Valerie Feder, PPS communication director, and school board member Raschelle Chase Miller did not respond for comment before press deadline
