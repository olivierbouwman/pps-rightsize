Portland Association of Teachers president fears more cuts could strain classrooms
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Alisha Chavez, president of the Portland Association of Teachers (PAT), said she learned on the first day of school that Portland Public Schools may need to make further cuts.
“It was a shock, especially on the first day of school,” Chavez said.
The district said it expects to make an additional $65 million in reductions. On its website, PPS said ongoing enrollment changes, rising costs and the loss of one-time funding have made the cuts part of a long-term effort to stabilize the district and protect student learning.

Chavez said students are already feeling the effects of previous cuts. She said PPS laid off 87 educators last year, most of them from elementary schools. As a result, some children are beginning kindergarten with fewer educators than they otherwise would have had, Chavez said.
Chavez believes the district should first examine management spending and contracts rather than make cuts that further affect classrooms.
“I always think that we have a large management budget and we could start cutting some of that, as well as really looking at our contracts,” she said.
PPS has cut central office spending in recent years, but Chavez said educators inside schools are being asked to do more with less.
“I know the experience of what’s happening in our schools,” Chavez said. “Our students are receiving way less and our educators are doing more with less, so our schools are bare bones.”
She is especially concerned about potential classroom consolidations. Chavez pointed to a fifth-grade teacher she spoke with last year who had a class of 20 students — a relatively small class size — but said about half the students had individualized education programs, or IEPs.
Chavez said the teacher was already struggling to meet students’ needs and would be unable to adequately educate them if another 15 students with additional needs were added to the class.
PPS said it is working to limit the impact on students. On its website, the district said it aims to avoid unsafe or unmanageable class sizes while working within budget constraints. The district also said it is reviewing potential impacts to avoid disproportionate harm and protect students who face the greatest barriers.
In September, the district plans to review data to determine which schools could be consolidated. KATU asked the district what data it would review but did not receive a response.
