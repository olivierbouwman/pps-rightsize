Parents and teachers cram into PPS board meeting, calling for delay on closures
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Parents and teachers crowded a Portland Public Schools board meeting Tuesday night to urge the district to pause its school closure proposal.
The proposed plan could shut down as many as 13 elementary schools and Sellwood Middle School by next fall. KATU has been following this story since the list was released Monday.
At Tuesday's meeting, board members publicly discussed the closure list for the first time.
"To me, this plan is mayhem," said board member Stephanie Engelsman.

She listed several concerns, including how far some children will have to travel if their local elementary school closes.
"It's gonna take these kids a very long time to get home, which is frankly widening an equity gap," Engelsman said. "Some kids will sit in a van with a snack and other kids are spending two hours a day on a bus."
Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong and board members said they are unlikely to add any schools to the closure list that have not already been named.
People filled both the outside and inside of the meeting building, urging the board to pause any decisions about closing schools.
Discussion grew tense as Engelsman pressed district staff about enrollment numbers for each school. When a district staffer asked about consolidating Clark, Engelsman replied, "I don't know. I'm recommending you do the work." Which drew cheers and applause from the crowd.
Armstrong, the superintendent, responded by explaining the district's approach.
"Our job isn't to come here and persuade here or come here and give you a list of demands," Armstrong said. "It's to share information about what it will take to stabilize our system," she said.
The district has several events scheduled in October to hear from community members about the proposed closure plan.
Armstrong is set to release revised recommendations for closures in mid-November. The board expects to make a final decision in December.
