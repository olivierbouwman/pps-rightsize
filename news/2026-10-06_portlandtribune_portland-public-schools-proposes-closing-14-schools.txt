Portland Public Schools proposes closing up to 14 schools in ‘rightsizing’ plan
Published 4:30 pm Tuesday, October 6, 2026

Portland Public Schools is considering closing as many as 14 schools as the district responds to declining enrollment and uneven student populations across the city.
PPS released two rightsizing scenarios Monday, Oct. 5. Scenario A would close 14 schools, while Scenario B would close 11. Both plans also include changes to school boundaries, grade levels and specialized programs.
A third option would postpone the changes and maintain the district’s current school configuration.
The proposals are not final.
PPS plans to collect community feedback through November before Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong makes a recommendation to the Board of Education. The Board is expected to vote in December, with approved changes taking effect in fall 2027.
“We know how deeply connected students, families, educators and communities are to their schools,” Armstrong said in a district announcement. “But we also have a responsibility to look at Portland Public Schools as a whole.”
What PPS is proposing
Scenario A would close 13 elementary schools — Maplewood, Stephenson, Irvington, Rose City Park, Buckman, Creston, Marysville, Woodmere, Lewis, Beach, James John, Peninsula and Sabin — and Sellwood Middle School.
Scenario B would close the same schools except Stephenson, Rose City Park and Lewis.
Both plans would redraw attendance boundaries, move programs and change the grade configuration at several schools. Seven K-8 schools would become K-5 schools, with their middle-school students moving to other campuses.
The district also proposes consolidating or relocating several dual-language immersion programs. PPS said no dual-language immersion program would be eliminated.
Under both scenarios, the environmental focus program at Sunnyside Environmental School would be discontinued. Sunnyside would become a neighborhood K-5 school and stop accepting new lottery transfers, although currently enrolled lottery students could remain through fifth grade.
PPS also proposes moving the Odyssey program from East Sylvan to Metropolitan Learning Center. MLC would remain a K-8 school, but its grades 9-12 program would close. East Sylvan would become vacant and could be considered for another use through a separate district process.
Teachers propose alternatives
The Portland Association of Teachers is opposing the closure approach and has called for the district to pursue other ways of addressing its financial and enrollment problems.
In a petition to Armstrong and the school board, PAT calls for redrawing boundaries to better distribute students, arguing that some schools have large classes while nearby schools have unused capacity.
The union also calls for reducing district-office staffing and redirecting money toward student-facing positions. PAT claims that 79 additional central-office administrators have been hired since 2019.
Other PAT proposals include selling or repurposing the Portland Education Center, evaluating vacant district properties for possible sale or lease, and moving some district employees into underused school buildings.
The union also argues that PPS should focus on increasing enrollment through measures such as investing in kindergarten, supporting housing projects and promoting public schools to prospective families.
PAT said closing schools could lead to staff layoffs, larger class sizes and fewer resources for students. The union also cited research it said showed school closures can disproportionately affect marginalized communities.
The next steps
PPS said its rightsizing effort is intended to better align schools, programs and resources with current enrollment.
The district said enrollment has declined while the number of schools has remained relatively stable, leaving some campuses significantly underenrolled while others remain large.
The Board will ultimately decide whether to close schools or pursue another option. Until that vote, the specific schools and other changes in the two scenarios could change as PPS gathers public input.
The final decision is expected in December, with changes taking effect beginning in the 2027-28 school year.
