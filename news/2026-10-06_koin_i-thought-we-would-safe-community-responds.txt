PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) – Portland Public Schools announced Monday evening the 14 schools they are considering closing.
After releasing a trio of proposals, Scenario A includes closing these 14 schools.
- Maplewood Elementary (Receiving schools: Hayhurst and Rieke).
- Stephenson Elementary (Receiving schools: Markham and Capitol Hill).
- Irvington Elementary (Receiving schools: Beverly Cleary for K–5 students and Beaumont for the area’s middle school pathway).
- Rose City Park Elementary (Receiving schools: Scott for neighborhood K–5 students and Vestal for Vietnamese immersion).
- Buckman Elementary (Receiving schools: Abernethy and Sunnyside).
- Creston Elementary (Receiving schools: Atkinson for neighborhood K–5 students and Glencoe for Deaf and Hard of Hearing classrooms).
- Marysville Elementary (Receiving school: Arleta).
- Woodmere Elementary (Receiving school: Whitman).
- Lewis Elementary (Receiving school: Duniway).
- Beach Elementary (Receiving schools: Chief Joseph for neighborhood K–5 students and César Chávez for Spanish immersion).
- James John Elementary (Receiving schools: Sitton for neighborhood K–5 students and César Chávez for Spanish immersion).
- Peninsula Elementary (Receiving school: Rosa Parks).
- Sabin Elementary (Receiving school: Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.).
- Sellwood Middle School (Receiving schools: Hosford for students from the Llewellyn area and Brentwood for students from the Duniway and Lewis areas).
This scenario also involves multiple K-8 schools becoming K-5 schools, with 6th to 8th-grade students moving to comprehensive middle schools.
Scenario B, features 11 school closures as opposed to 14 — Rose City Park Elementary, Lewis Elementary and Stephenson Elementary would stay open in this proposal.
PPS also said that a third option would “defer the decision and maintain the current school configuration for now, without addressing the district’s declining and uneven enrollment.”
The list of possible closures and consolidations may go into effect at the beginning of next school year after declining enrollments led to budgetary constraints.
PPS Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong told KOIN 6 News that even with less money coming in from the state, the bigger issue is making sure all students have the same access to resources, like the arts, science and electives.
“We know how deeply connected students, families, educators and communities are to their schools, and considering the closure of any school is extraordinarily difficult,” she said. “But we also have a responsibility to look at Portland Public Schools as a whole and consider changes that give every student access to strong programs, stable staffing and the opportunities and support they deserve.”
While the list is only preliminary at this stage, it does lay the groundwork for potential elementary and middle schools being shut down or consolidated, with no high schools on the chopping block yet.
Parents and teachers fight to keep schools open
Parents are already lining up to make the case to keep their local school open.
James John PTA Co-President Sam Ellingson said his community had hoped the school would avoid the closure list.
“I thought we would be safe and we’re not,” Ellingson said. “We already live in an underserved community. These kids, these families, they deserve the same resources at every other school in the Portland Public School system.”
“We already live in an underserved community. These kids, these families, they deserve the same resources at every other school in the Portland Public School system,” Ellingson said.
For families at Sellwood Middle, PTA representative Tara Stein said the potential closures reach beyond the schools themselves.
“The children are gonna be the ones to lose in these scenarios, and then the neighborhoods are gonna be the next ones to lose in this scenario,” Stein said.
The Portland Association of Teachers is also pushing back on the timeline, saying PPS should slow down and exhaust other options before closing, consolidating or changing so many schools and programs, in part calling for “a more measured and community centered approach.” PAT is planning a rally before the PPS board meeting on Tuesday.
Next steps
While some of the closures and consolidations may go into effect as soon as a year from now, there are still many steps along the way until they’re finalized.
On Tuesday night, Armstrong will present her list to PPS board members during their regular meeting.
There will also be opportunities for public input and meetings at schools, with teachers, staff, families, unions and administrative staff expected to make their voices heard before any options are narrowed down. The board is tentatively slated to vote on the matter by the end of the year.
Below is a statement from the Portland Association of Teachers in reaction to PPS’ announcement:
“Today PPS released scenarios for school closures and it is clear a more measured and
community centered approach is needed. After all, we are talking about closing,
consolidating, and profoundly altering over two dozen schools and programs. Decisions
of that magnitude take time and care, and PPS leadership has proven inept at making
decisions of that scale in a rushed timeline. Their timeline is less than a year, and the
current PPS proposal is to decide by December when we should all be focused on
supporting student outcomes this fall.
“Closing, consolidating, and transforming programs at over 25 schools is a dramatic
change. It’s critical that PPS get the process right, which requires slowing down and
involving more stakeholders. Students and families deserve a plan to ensure every
student has access to a well-resourced high-quality school.
“Closing schools is not a plan, it’s a reaction. Our students deserve better. Families
deserve better. Educators deserve better.
“We ask that the district exhaust every available option it has before considering closing
schools. And at PAT, we will fight hard to secure quality education funding because
students and families deserve high quality, well-resourced neighborhood schools.”
