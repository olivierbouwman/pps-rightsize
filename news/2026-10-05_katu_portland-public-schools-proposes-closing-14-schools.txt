Portland Public Schools proposes closing up to 14 schools amid declining enrollment
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Portland Public Schools released two scenarios on Monday night that it says will address declining enrollment in the district. One of the scenarios could mean the closure of up to 14 schools. The other, the closure of 11.
(The two scenarios are listed in full farther down in this article.)
In a news release, the district said Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong will gather feedback about the proposals from the community and offer a final recommendation to the school board. The board will vote on a course of action in December.
“We know how deeply connected students, families, educators and communities are to their schools, and considering the closure of any school is extraordinarily difficult,” said Armstrong in a statement. “But we also have a responsibility to look at Portland Public Schools as a whole and consider changes that give every student access to strong programs, stable staffing and the opportunities and support they deserve.”
Approved changes will begin in fall 2027.

Sam Ellingson, a parent of a 2nd grader at James John Elementary School and co-president of its PTA, said his school being on the short list for closure is hard to comprehend.
"There's a lot of shock," Ellingson told KATU. "This is where families and kids come to play and hang out and connect and just be a community."
Under both of the district's proposed scenarios, James John students would be absorbed by Sitton Elementary School. That's where Dylan Day's daughter attends kindergarten.
"It's obviously very concerning," Day said, adding that he's worried about his daughter's classrooms becoming overcrowded. "I would hope that it's maybe a short-term solution, and then, eventually, we get back to a place where population numbers make sense to reopen more schools."
Both scenarios proposed by the district include over 30 boundary changes across PPS schools, converting several K-8 schools into K-5, and shifting certain programs across buildings.
The PPS Board of Education will meet on Tuesday night to hold its first discussion on the proposed closures and changes. Just before, the Portland Association of Teachers is planning to hold a rally to push for a "pause" of any closure.
"Decisions of that magnitude take time and care, and PPS leadership has proven inept at making decisions of that scale in a rushed timeline," the PAT said in a statement Monday night. "Closing schools is not a plan, it’s a reaction. Our students deserve better. Families deserve better. Educators deserve better."
Scenario A, as released by the district.
Scenario A proposes closing 14 schools—13 elementary schools and one middle school. Students and programs from those schools would be consolidated into the receiving schools identified below.
Maplewood Elementary
Receiving schools: Hayhurst and Rieke.
Stephenson Elementary
Receiving schools: Markham and Capitol Hill.
Irvington Elementary
Receiving schools: Beverly Cleary for K–5 students and Beaumont for the area’s middle school pathway.
Rose City Park Elementary
Receiving schools: Scott for neighborhood K–5 students and Vestal for Vietnamese immersion.
Buckman Elementary
Receiving schools: Abernethy and Sunnyside.
Creston Elementary
Receiving schools: Atkinson for neighborhood K–5 students and Glencoe for Deaf and Hard of Hearing classrooms.
Marysville Elementary
Receiving school: Arleta.
Woodmere Elementary
Receiving school: Whitman.
Lewis Elementary
Receiving school: Duniway.
Beach Elementary
Receiving schools: Chief Joseph for neighborhood K–5 students and César Chávez for Spanish immersion.
James John Elementary
Receiving schools: Sitton for neighborhood K–5 students and César Chávez for Spanish immersion.
Peninsula Elementary
Receiving school: Rosa Parks.
Sabin Elementary
Receiving school: Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
Sellwood Middle School
Receiving schools: Hosford for students from the Llewellyn area and Brentwood for students from the Duniway and Lewis areas.
Scenario A also includes 38 boundary changes affecting elementary, middle and high school attendance areas; eight grade-level reconfigurations; 13 program moves; and the discontinuation of one focus-option program.
Several K–8 schools would become K–5 schools, with their middle-grade students moving to comprehensive middle schools.
Dual Language Immersion programs would be consolidated to provide more consistent pathways across grade levels, but no DLI program would be eliminated. Spanish, Mandarin and Vietnamese immersion programs would be relocated or combined at designated schools.
