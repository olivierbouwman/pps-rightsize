PORTLAND, Ore. — Portland Public Schools floated the idea of cutting nearly 300 full-time equivalent positions in an early draft on how to close a projected $50 million budget shortfall for the 2026-27 school year.
That $50 million represents about 4.58% of the district's total $1.09 billion current year operating budget. It comes as the district continues to struggles with rising costs, shrinking available funds and declining enrollment.
The proposed job cuts released on the district's website Tuesday includes cuts to 288 positions, with 180 staff reductions at schools in all levels — elementary, middle and high school — and 108 employees from the district's Central Office. It would be a 6% reduction in overall district services.
Most of the cuts would be the result of the district changing the ratio of teachers and staff to students and also blending classrooms. For example, if more first- and second-grade classes combined, as well as fourth- and fifth-grade classes, it could result in $6.8 million projected cost savings. It would also result in 43 fewer full-time employees.
Per the document, adjusting the high school classroom sizes to be roughly 33 students per class on average could save the district $4.5 million, with the elimination of 27 positions.
These are among the first publicly released cost-saving ideas for the 2026-27 school year and may change after community feedback. There's four budget sessions scheduled for this week where people can provide input: at Harriet Tubman Middle School and McDaniel High School on Wednesday, Jan. 21, 6-7:30 p.m., and at Cleveland High School and Ida B. Wells High School on Thursday, Jan. 22, 6-7:30 p.m.
The fresh round of cuts come after PPS cut from the budget $40 million for the current school year. That came with reducing language access support, sunsetting the International Baccalaureate Program at the elementary level, and blending fourth- and fifth-grade students within classrooms.
It was the largest budget cut in what's been a multi-year effort for the district since 2022 to reduce costs, due primarily to a decline in enrollment, rise in costs and the expiration of one-time funding.
However, Angela Bonilla, who is president of the Portland Association of Teachers, said that these cuts could hurt students.
“We’re going to lose mental health supports, counselors. We’re looking at increased class sizes from elementary, middle and high schools. We’re looking at less educators to support our multilingual learners, who are the same communities being targeted by ICE and the federal government," she said.
Bonilla stated this proposal makes her furious.
“This is a failure on the part of the legislature, in funding us appropriately. We have been underfunded for decades at this point, we are digging ourselves out of a hole," she said. “We’re seeing the product of decades of under investment coming to a head."
She said more adults are needed in schools, and this will only hurt learning, and children as a whole.
“The impact on students is they become anonymous," she said. “One thing that is so special about being in school is that every kid has an opportunity to find that one grown up that they can connect with," she continued.
“When kids don't feel connection to their school community, to their peers, to the adults in their lives — that's how we lose students. That’s how we lose students not just enrollment wise, but in our society.”
She also said blending grades will only create more of a deficit for student who are already struggling.
“One of the proposals is in addition to having fourth and fifth grade classes smooshed together in what we call blends, which we did as cuts last year, they’re also going to try to do that with first and second graders," she explained.
“Let’s say you come in as a student who gets identified as having special needs in kindergarten. For first grade, you're going to be in a class with first- and second-grade students, and a teacher trying to figure out how to teach every student at their level," she said.
Other unions also were not thrilled about the proposed changes.
In a statement from SEIU 503 Local 140, they wrote that union leadership of PPS Local 140 were made aware of the large budget cuts earlier this month.
"These cuts amount to $50 to $55 million dollars and will reduce specialized programs, central office programs including core academics and the work our union members do in custodial and nutrition services," they wrote in part.
They continued: "We are calling on the administration and local government to pursue solutions that keep the needs of Portland students front and center. We must facilitate a learning environment that is healthy and meets professional standards. The only way to do so is to make sure that all PPS workers have the resources they need to keep public schools running."
