PPS Superintendent discusses ‘tough decisions’ with 2025-2026 budget proposal
PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) — Portland Public Schools Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong laid out her proposal for the 2025-2026 school year at a press conference Wednesday morning, a proposal which includes laying off hundreds of positions in the district.
At the event, Armstrong spoke on the current deficit the district is facing, $40 million, and the fact that this is the district's third straight year facing a deficit.
She said the current budget reflects "tough decisions" being made.
"Our expenses to run schools continue to exceed the revenue we receive, and the gap is only widening," said Armstrong. "The budget reflects tough decisions made with students at the center."

Under the proposed budget, PPS would be cutting 156 school-based jobs and around 85 jobs in the district's central office. In total, they're looking to reduce 242.2 full time equivalents or FTEs. That means two part-time positions that make up the equivalent hours of one full-time position could be cut, for example. Armstrong said the district is looking at a few different ways to hit that mark.
"Those positions do include layoffs. It includes positions that were vacant that will remain unfilled. And it also includes positions that folks are vacating either through retire and/or resignation at the end of this year. So the total amount of FTE's is that 240 number," Armstrong said.
The district is also considering other cost-cutting measures like blending classes, Armstrong said. For example, some 4th and 5th grade classes could be combined, with upper grade levels seeing a class size minimum.
"Schools will no longer be able to run classes with 15 students at the high school level. Dual language immersion staffing at middle schools is also something that we reduced. And of course, we took a look at our contracts, our partner contracts, and we made some reductions," Armstrong said.
At the same time, the superintendent emphasized the district will still work to make sure classrooms have all the essential services needed to be successful, continue producing high-quality instruction and education materials, adhere to high standards and provide wrap-around supports for students who need it most.
"We still believe that Portland Public Schools is the best option and choice for families in our community, and we are going to continue to uplift those experiences," Armstrong said.

When asked about some of the key reasons for the budget deficit, Armstrong said that the district has been seeing declining enrollment and that schools receive funding on a per-student basis. She also said that the district has been affected by rising costs.
PPS Chief Financial Officer Michelle Morrison emphasized that this current budget proposal is only preliminary and that it will be revised.
Next week at Grant High School, PPS is hosting a Board Budget Public Comment Session meant to allow the public to voice their input to the board on next year's budget.
