PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) — It was a big night for many Portland Public School families, as the district officially outlined why they have certain schools on the potential chopping block during a packed public comment hearing on Tuesday.
A large rally was held by the Portland teachers’ union before the meeting, with their petition pleading with PPS to redraw boundary lines to avoid school closures receiving nearly 3,600 signatures.
The full presentation can be found here.
One person at the meeting, Nadia Estrada, spoke about recently enrolling her son at Sellwood Middle School after years of homeschooling, only to learn that her child might not be able to attend next year.

“My reaction was just devastation. And then wanting to fight to say, ‘we can’t let this happen,” she said. “Coming out of homeschool and going into brick-and-mortar school as a seventh grader, it was pretty scary for him. But the staff was so compassionate, so kind. They gave him everything he needed to make him feel safe.
Estrada was one of many PPS parents who spent their afternoon rallying around their schools.
“We’re all in a stage of grief right now, hearing one scenario for 14 schools and one for 11. But it’s going to affect the entire (district) as a whole with different boundary changes, different program changes that are going to be happening,” said Alicia Chavez, President of the Portland Association of Teachers.
District leaders faced tough testimony from parents, teachers and students.
“Evidence shows that school closures are adversarial, affecting our most disadvantaged learners,” said PPS parent Jackie Wirz. “Imagine what we could do collaboratively if we had more time.”
“It gave me much-needed relief to have a local community public school to put my children into it. It changes lives. Kids having a school to go to and having a community to belong to,” added Estrada.
A final decision will be made on potential closures in December.
