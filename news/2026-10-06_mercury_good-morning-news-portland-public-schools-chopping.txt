IN LOCAL NEWS:
• Parents… at least some of you… get ready to potentially lose your got-damned minds, because last night Portland Public Schools (PPS) released the names of up to 14 schools that could be on the chopping block thanks to budget concerns, as well as several program and boundary changes. PPS came up with two scenarios to pitch to board members tonight, and Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong will give her recommendation for which plan she prefers in November, with a final board vote by the end of the year. The elementary schools that could be closed in the first scenario include Buckman, Creston, Marysville, Woodmere and Lewis in Southeast Portland (along with Sellwood Middle School); Maplewood and Stephenson in the Southwest section of the city; Irvington, Rose City Park and Sabin in Northeast; and Beach, James John, and Peninsula in the northern quadrant. This plan would also include 38 boundary changes. Under the second plan only 11 schools would be closed, which would include all the above institutions except for Stephenson, Lewis, and Rose City Park. This scenario would include 34 boundary changes. [Correction: An earlier version of this item incorrectly stated that all of the currently threatened schools, except for Buckman, have a majority of students of color. That is incorrect. Six of the 14 proposed schools on the chopping block are Title 1 schools, and of those all enroll a majority of POC students, with the exception of Buckman. We regret the error.] The reason behind the closures, PPS says, is due to falling enrollment, skyrocketing operational costs, and of course, that $65 million budget hole that’s expected in the next school year. The Portland Association of Teachers (PAT) had their statement locked and loaded following the PPS announcement, saying that “Closing, consolidating, and transforming programs at over 25 schools is a dramatic change,” and that there needs to be a lot more input from the community before taking such drastic measures. Parents, you have been informed—and now, let the shit storm commence.
• A protest broke out at venerable Central Eastside restaurant, Clarklewis, on Sunday after it was discovered that the eatery was hosting a large group of alleged Christian white supremacists during brunch. (It’s hard to imagine white supremacists drinking mimosas… but on the other hand, maybe it’s not weird at all and should be expected?) According to one of Clarklewis’ owners, Joe Rogers, protesters got wind of the gathering of roughly 40 people, and after demonstrating for a bit, broke a window and some glassware. Rogers denies they knew that the group were alleged white supremacists when they accepted the reservation. The gathering, who Rogers identified as the “Oregon Group”—though there’s no obvious information about such an organization online—reportedly were handing out pamphlets to other diners, and the protesters scattered when police arrived on the scene. Rogers also said Clarklewis would be donating the $1,700 they received from the alleged supremacists to an unspecified charity.
• Blazers ownership are sticking by their decision not to rehire announcer Braiden Bell, after he and his lawyer asked for him to be reinstated. Bell was hired and summarily fired last week after KGW hurriedly published racial and offensive tweets that the announcer wrote when he was 15 years old, in order to get a scoop. “Braiden is not asking anyone to excuse what he wrote,” Bell’s lawyer, Arnie Herz, said. “He has owned it, without excuses. What he is asking is whether a boy’s words from fourteen years ago should outweigh everything the man has done since. If that is the standard, there is hardly a person in any organization in America who could meet it.” However, the Blazers owners are not buying it, and are standing by their decision to fire him, telling The Athletic, “We wish Mr. Bell the best.”
