Superintendent: 5-10 Portland schools may be closed next year
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - The superintendent of Portland Public Schools says the district is addressing a budget deficit with temporary furloughs for central office leadership and staff. Plus, there is a possibility of closing five to ten schools because of next year’s shortfall.
That deficit is projected at $50 million.
“I anticipate presenting the board with a list of schools for closure in November, for board action in December, and notification out to community by January of this upcoming year,” said Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong. “So no school closures this coming academic year but we are anticipating that there will be some closures.”
Armstrong said, like many districts across the country, PPS is facing financial challenges because of declining student enrollment and rising operational costs.
Armstrong said the PPS cuts will come in three phases. Phase one includes furloughing 13 district leaders for 5 days, including superintendent Amstrong who will be furloughed for 6 days, working 4 without pay. PPS administration has also stopped hiring for open office positions.
Phase two would expand those furloughs to other levels of the district. the Portland Association of Teachers said they were asked if teachers were willing to be furloughed for 4 days, the alternative the district offered was laying off 200 educators. PAT and the other 5 labor unions representing PPS employees are currently in contract negotiations with the district.
The Portland Association of Teachers sent a statement, saying in part, “it is clear that much greater clarity is needed on PPS’ financial situation before any agreements can be made. Per our contract, there is a 60-day notice required before any layoffs of teachers can occur. We plan to use the next 60 days to do an expedited forensic analysis of PPS financials, and to actively engage our members and PPS parents in a process the district has shut them out of.”
