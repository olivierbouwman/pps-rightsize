Title: Portland Public Schools will cut administrator jobs amid budget shortfall
Outlet: The Oregonian/OregonLive
Author: Julia Silverman
Date: 2026-03-10

Multiple senior-level employees who work in administrative roles for Portland Public Schools were told Monday that they will have to reapply for their jobs for the upcoming school year — and that there will not be enough positions to go around.
To help close a budget hole of at least $50 million at the state’s largest school district, Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong unveiled an overhaul of the much beleaguered central office’s organizational chart, a move she said would save up to $2.75 million by eliminating duplicative positions.
“We are reducing 13 licensed administrative roles and consolidating responsibilities across the [academic] division, resulting in nearly a one-third reduction of central office academic administrators,” Armstrong wrote in a memo to central office staff.
Multiple positions from the new organizational chart, copies of which were shared with The Oregonian/OregonLive, had no names attached, including six roles for senior directors of instructional leadership, who supervise building principals districtwide.
Via her memo, Armstrong told employees who were currently in such jobs, along with those in positions that supervise math, literacy, social studies and sciences, that they could reapply for one of the new positions, with interviews scheduled over the next few weeks.
Special education was the sole area to go untouched.
Senior Chief of Academics Kristina Howard said there would be reductions in a range of areas, including supports for new teachers and administrators, instructional coaches and multilingual education.
Armstrong also announced that to help offset a midyear budget shortfall that’s now pegged at about $14 million, she would take six furlough days, including four working days and two holidays and that senior-level managers would take five furlough days. That should save about $1.2 million, she wrote.
How the school district will close the remainder of its midyear budget gap remains the subject of intense negotiations with the Portland Association of Teachers and other labor unions. Over the weekend, union president Angela Bonilla told her membership that they’d been asked to take four furlough days or risk losing up to 200 jobs before the end of the school year.
“It is clear that much greater clarity is needed on PPS’ financial situation before any agreements can be made,” Rachel Gumpert, a spokesperson for the Portland Association of Teachers, said in a press release. “Per our contract, there is a 60-day notice required before any layoffs of teachers can occur. We plan to use the next 60 days to do an expedited forensic analysis of PPS financials.”
Angela Freeman, who is the human resources director at Portland Public Schools, said Tuesday that the district was still negotiating with labor unions about whether any furlough days would include upcoming planning and grading time set aside for teachers, during which students are already scheduled to be out of school. The alternative would be to cut four instructional days, most likely from the end of the school calendar in June.
“What I have said to the Portland Association of Teachers is that we will always try to put our students in front of teachers as much as we possibly can,” Freeman said.
Armstrong said that needed to be balanced against a “significant ask of our staff members, to give back a portion of what they receive every month to the school district, and that is essentially what we are asking.”
