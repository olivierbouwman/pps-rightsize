PORTLAND, Ore. — Oregon's next round of county special elections is coming up on May 20, and the biggest races on the ballot in Portland are all about Portland Public Schools (PPS). Four of the seven seats on the school district's board of directors will be on the ballot, along with a key bond renewal ballot measure.
PPS board elections are held in May of odd-numbered years, electing members to four-year terms that begin in July of that year. Candidates are elected to represent specific geographic districts and they have to be residents of those districts, but the elections themselves are all citywide.
The four seats on the ballot this year are Zone 1, which includes South and Southwest Portland; Zone 4, which is primarily in North Portland; Zone 5, which is primarily in Northeast Portland; and Zone 6, which is primarily in Southeast Portland. The remaining board members were elected in 2023 and are still midway through their terms.
The information below is drawn from the candidates' websites and information submitted for the Multnomah County Voters' Pamphlet.
Zone 1
- Christy Splitt (incumbent)
- Ken Cavagnolo
The winner of this race will only hold the seat for two years, because they’ll be serving out the remainder of the term of former board member Andrew Scott, who moved out of Zone 1 last year and resigned in January. Splitt was appointed to fill the seat through June, with the election winner taking over in July.
Splitt is a PPS parent who began her career as a high school social studies teacher and later worked in the Oregon Legislature focused on environmental policy. She now works at the Oregon Department of Energy. She has drawn endorsements from PPS board members Eddie Wang and Julia Brim-Edwards, as well as the Portland Association of Teachers.
Cavagnolo began his career as an astrophysicist but later switched to a business focus, according to his website, and currently serves as an AI executive at a Portland business services firm.
Zone 4
- Herman Greene (incumbent)
- Rashelle Chase-Miller
Greene is a pastor at the Abundant Life PDX church and community advocate who has served on the PPS board since 2021. Chase-Miller is a PPS parent and the program director of SMART Reading, and has a background in early childhood education.
Both have drawn prominent endorsements; Greene has the backing of fellow PPS board members Gary Hollands, Julia Brim-Edwards and Patte Sullivan, along with Gov. Tina Kotek and Portland City Councilor Dan Ryan.
Chase-Miller has drawn support from board members Eddie Wang and Michelle DePass, plus the Portland Association of Teachers and Portland City Councilors Tiffany Koyama Lane, Candace Avalos and Angelita Morillo.

Zone 6
- Rob Galanakis
- Stephanie Engelsman
- Simone Crowe
Galanakis is a software engineer and business owner and PPS parent who has drawn endorsements from PPS board member Eddie Wang and Portland City Councilor Mitch Green.
Engelsman is a public defender and PPS parent who is endorsed by the Portland Association of Teachers, as well as PPS board members Michelle DePass, Julia Brim-Edwards and Eddie Wang.
Simone Crowe is an environmental policy advocate with a background in nonprofits, fundraising and communications.
Incumbent Julia Brim-Edwards was elected to the Multnomah County Commission in 2023 and is not seeking reelection at PPS.
Measure 26-259
The measure is the latest in a series of PPS school bonds aimed at modernizing district schools. Voters passed previous bonds in 2012, 2017 and 2020. The new measure is timed to coincide with a scheduled decline in the tax rate from the previous bonds, with the expectation that the tax rate won't change from the existing $2.50-per-$1,000 rate, according to PPS.
The renewed measure is expected to raise up to $1.83 billion to be spent on capital projects, most notably the renovation or replacement of Cleveland High School and Ida B. Wells High School. It will also fund repairs at multiple elementary and middle schools, seismic resiliency and some upgrades to curriculum materials and technology.
Supporters of the measure point to the advanced age and associated safety problems at many of the school buildings in the district's portfolio, while critics argue that the district has failed to keep construction costs in check under prior bonds and is building more capacity than needed to keep up with student enrollment trends.
The bond has essentially universal support from Portland-area elected officials, with endorsements from Mayor Keith Wilson and all 12 Portland city councilors, plus all five members of the Multnomah County board of commissioners and five members of the Metro Council, along with a dozen state and federal lawmakers.
The Portland Association of Teachers has also urged a Yes vote, along with nearly three dozen PTAs from across the school district.
