Title: Readers respond: False justification for closing schools
Outlet: The Oregonian/OregonLive
Author: Letters
Date: 2026-09-14
URL: https://www.oregonlive.com/opinion/2026/09/readers-respond-false-justification-for-closing-schools.html
Note: Fetched via browser (curl blocked with 403).

Portland Public Schools proposes closing 15-20 schools to meet newly announced minimum school enrollment goals: 360 students for elementary schools and 500 students for middle schools. For elementary schools, Margaret Calvert — the PPS executive director of school planning and modernization — said this should allow two classes per grade, which could mean 30 students per class, (“As Portland Public Schools weighs sweeping closures, parents wonder which schools will be left standing,” Aug. 28).
The Oregonian/OregonLive article says these 360- and 500-student thresholds were set by Oregon’s Quality Education Model, but these are merely the QEM’s enrollment numbers for hypothetical average schools. Notably, these prototype schools have class sizes of 20 students for elementary school and 17.5 for middle school.
The QEM quotes a research review, “School Size, School Climate, and Student Performance” by Kathleen Cotton, which suggests 300-400 students is an “effective” size for an elementary school and 400-800 for a middle school.
Cotton’s review specifically criticizes school consolidations and concludes that smaller schools are superior or equal to larger schools in every metric of student success – graduation rates, test scores, students’ sense of well-being and belonging, interpersonal relationships between students and staff, college acceptance rates and more. The benefits were most pronounced for “poor students and those of racial and ethnic minorities.”
This subtle misrepresentation of enrollment numbers is alarming because, at the recent school board meeting, it was the only measurable objective they provided. This objective in and of itself has no particular merit and may even be harmful. Portland deserves better.
Frances Chewning, Portland
Chewning is a parent of a PPS student
