PORTLAND, Ore. — Across the Pacific Northwest school districts are dealing with budget cuts and falling enrollment. Oregon’s biggest school district, Portland Public Schools is no exception.
The school district is looking into the possibility of closing schools in what leaders call a "rightsizing" process. That prospect has left some parents nervous, as to whether or not their students will be in the same building in a couple years.
“It would be very hard emotionally, academically,” Rebecca Rascoe, a parent of a student at Jason Lee Elementary said of the prospect of the school closing.
“It's a really good community here," she added.
Jason Lee is just five blocks from Rascoe’s home. Like many students at the elementary school, Rascoe’s son walks to class. Over the past few years, she said he has fallen in love with the school.
"They have wonderful staff, principal, counselors like everything there," Rascoe said.
Jason Lee is a unique school. It is a Title I school, meaning at least 40% of its students come from low-income households. A third of the students have learning differences that require special support. Rascoe’s son receives help learning to read.
"They truly treat every child there as an individual," she said.
Rascoe, along with parents at other Title I schools, is growing concerned their schools may close as Portland Public Schools looks to address a $50 million shortfall.
On Tuesday, PPS began what is expected to be a monthlong process to determine whether any schools and programs should be closed or consolidated. The decision could be based on declining or uneven enrollment, whether school buildings are underutilized, and available resources.
"This isn't just a decision about closing schools,” David Jones, whose daughter attends Vestal Elementary School said. “This is a process about what happens to those schools when they close."
Parents told KGW that families at a handful of Title I schools are creating a coalition to advocate for the schools and keep them open.
"Title I schools sometimes don't have the loudest voices," Jones said.
PPS has not made a final decision on closing schools, though it is under consideration. On Saturday, administrators met with parents to discuss the idea at a forum at Kellogg Middle School.
"We're no different than any other district that's experiencing declining enrollment," said Jon Franco, PPS senior chief of operations.
As conversations continue, Title I parents are pushing to keep their schools open.
"We got to fight for our kids, we got to fight for our community," Rascoe said.
