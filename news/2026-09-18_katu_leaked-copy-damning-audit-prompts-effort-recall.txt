Leaked copy of damning audit prompts effort to recall PPS Board Chair
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — A leaked draft of a damning independent audit into a Texas contractor hired to oversee over a billion dollars’ worth of construction for Portland Public Schools has prompted an effort to recall Board Chair Eddie Wang.
"Mr. Wang has neglected his fiduciary responsibility. He, as board chair, has contributed to construction contract overpayments in excess of $700,000," the petition to recall Wang reads in part.
The petition, filed by former board member Michael Rosen, cites the draft audit obtained exclusively and first published first by Willamette Week and then The Oregonian. PPS’s records request department has not yet responded to a expedited records request to obtain a copy. Sjoberg Evashenk Consulting, who conducted the audit, also has not yet provided KATU with a copy.
The reports about the audit note that it alleges the district placed itself at an increased risk of fraud by offloading construction projects funded through a $1.83 billion bond to the Texas based consulting firm, PROCEDEO.

It criticizes the district’s decision to follow the contractor's advice and gut dozens of staff members from its own Office of Modernization, thus placing more control and money into the hands of the contractor. The reports note auditors claim this removed the district's ability to properly oversee the contractor.
As noted in Rosen's petition, the news reports also cite auditors claiming PROCEDEO was overpaid by more than $700,000.
Rosen will need to collect 38,000 valid signatures over the next three months to allow for a recall vote. Prior to any potential vote Wang would have the opportunity to step down.
Rosen said other than having a grandchild in the district and paying property taxes for the bond, he has no skin in the game and does not plan to run for office again. He said he became concerned with how quickly the board moved forward with a vote on the $61.5 million contract with PROCEDEO in 2025 and blames Wang for expediting that process.
He said he decided to file the recall petition after reading the news reports about the audit draft. Rosen said he will not be hiring professional signature gatherers, instead the effort would be grassroots.
"I'm starting to amass volunteers that will spread out around the city to go to PTA meetings, get the word out. We have three months to do it. I think it's possible, I'm not saying it'll be easy. But it's absolutely imperative that we do this and send a strong message to the school district and the school board that this behavior and lack of fiduciary responsibility cannot stand," he said during an interview with KATU.
He said if the effort fails, he will stand behind candidates to replace both Wang and the board members who voted in favor of the contract.
One of those board members, Rashelle Chase-Miller, said in an interview with KATU that she stands behind that vote and behind the work Wang is doing.
"My initial thoughts are that the audit process is incomplete. I think it's really unfortunate that the audit was leaked before the process was complete. I don't feel comfortable saying much more about the audit specifically until that process has concluded and we have a complete document to discuss. As far as the recall goes, I think that Chair Wang has done a great job in extremely difficult circumstances," she said. "I think the recall effort is unfortunate because it's really hard to say what the audit is about and the work we're trying to do for our kids and is kind of really just a distraction and misguided."
When asked what portions of the audit draft she felt were incomplete, Chase-Miller said the full spectrum of communication between auditors and PPS as well as the board have not yet occurred. Generally, prior to an audit going public the agency or entity being audited is given the opportunity to respond and aspects of the final publicized audit may be edited as a result. Chase-Miller said until that process is complete, she would not further comment on details that the two news outlets have reported on.
For example, Chase-Miller would not discuss whether she disputes the claims of overpayments but said she feels the contractor has saved the district money.
"In a bond update, it was shared that PROCEDEO has saved us $19 million already, which is amazing and was really the hope was that they were going to be able to find us savings and efficiencies," she said.
When asked whether she supports the decision to gut the Office of Modernization and whether the audit reports had raised any red flags about that action, she noted that was not a board level decision.
"Our job is to supervise and evaluate the superintendent. And the superintendent makes decisions about staffing and about the teams and structure and size that she needs to get the work done. So, and, you know, I was new on the board last year, so I wasn't here for the previous builds," she said.
Chase-Miller urged Portlanders not to buy into speculation over the still unpublished audit.
"I can understand why the public would be concerned. I think these building projects and the contracts around them and all of the pieces to it are really complicated and really nuanced. And I think often what rises to the top in the narrative is kind of like the things that are easy to headline, kind of the broad strokes," she said. "There's a lot of subtext that the public isn't aware of. So I would just ask, you know, the public to be patient to withhold judgment and, you know, see how these projects progress."
Rosen believes there was reason for public scrutiny even before the audit reports were published and feels this should be cause for alarm for all taxpayers who are funding the district.
"This current administration, the superintendent, in the school board are talking about closing 20 public schools. People move into neighborhoods because they want a good public school. It's gonna affect property values. People are not going to move into neighborhoods. It's going to affect businesses. People that don't have kids in public schools spend a lot of property taxes, Portland Public Schools, I think, it’s the second or third largest amount of your property taxes. Everyone should care especially when they find out it's not being spent legally," he said.

Wang declined an interview but sent the following written statement:
Unfortunately, the bulk of these allegations are unsubstantiated and purely conjecture at best. I stand by the board systems, oversight, and leadership I have developed, all of which have been based on nationally studied best practices for school board governance.
But I would like to get to the root of this petition - fear of change. For far too long, change in Portland has come too slow, and our students, especially those from our underserved and historically marginalized groups, can not and should not wait any longer. We are finally seeing the needle move on student scores in reading and math after decades of stagnation. Jefferson High School has finally broken ground after over half a decade of delays. Cleveland and Wells will see a brand new high school sooner than if we had done nothing.
PROCEDEO also sent a written statement:
Our job is to let Portland Public Schools do what it does best, educating students and supporting families, while we lead the complex, specialized work of managing a multi-hundred-million-dollar construction program. In less than seven months, PROCEDEO has provided significant value to the district, including delivering over $19 million in realized savings and cost avoidance, with millions more in savings being identified.
We take our responsibility to serve the students, families, and taxpayers of Portland very seriously, and we are committed to continued clear communications, reporting, and accountability at every step of the way.
