PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) — Portland Public Schools is set to release its first draft for possible school closures and other changes next Monday.
It comes amid declining enrollment, and families are worried about what comes next.
The PPS Board of Education held a community meeting at Ida B. Wells High School in Southwest Portland. Around the room, district officials faced families who could soon learn their neighborhood school may close.
In a school cafeteria Wednesday, parents got a crash course on a plan that could soon change where their kids learn.
A PPS parent, Whitney Marshall, said, “It makes me feel sad that we’re here, but I think it’s a bigger issue. It’s state funding”
Parents like Marshall say they want more details about how PPS will decide which schools could close.
The district calls the process “Rightsizing.”
“I don’t know if people really understand exactly what’s going on,” said Marshall.
PPS says it’s looking at how to reconfigure schools and resources as enrollment declines.
Michael Reunert said, “Well, the hope is they don’t close our school.”
It’s not just parents.
Reunert lives in Hillsdale, and says schools help shape the neighborhoods around them, “They train our future generation. They impact our property values. They impact whether or not people choose to live in Portland or maybe Beaverton or maybe somewhere else. So, it’s all tied together.”
Closing schools is just one option.
PPS is also considering changes to boundaries, programs and building use, with enrollment, costs, building conditions and equity all part of the review.
Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong met with parents and community members ahead of her draft scenario, expected Monday.
Whitney hopes her kids’ schools aren’t on it, “You know, neighborhood schools are the heart and the hub of a community. And so, if any of these schools closed people are going to feel that. I would feel that my kids would feel that I can’t even begin to say what our next steps would be as a family.”
Once the Superintendent releases her draft Monday, the school board will have the final say.
The board is expected to vote in December, but not before more community input. KOIN 6 News expects a large turnout at Tuesday’s board meeting.
