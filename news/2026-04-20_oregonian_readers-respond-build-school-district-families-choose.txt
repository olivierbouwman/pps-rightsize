Portland Public Schools faces a crisis. But the question should not only be which schools to close, but how to build a district that families choose. Right now, only one of those questions is being asked, (“PPS families question plan to close 5-10 schools,” April 14).
PPS enrollment has fallen 12% since 2018. The causes are well-documented — housing costs that push families out of the city, a pandemic-era loss of trust and demographic shifts. A Brookings Institution report found that 12% of Oregon’s school-age children were unaccounted for — not in public school, not in private school. They are simply outside the system.
Yet at a recent community meeting, PPS families were not asked whether closing schools is the right approach or how to reverse declining enrollment.
Some argue that focus options drain resources from neighborhood schools – focus options are programs that specialize in areas such as science, history or dual-language immersion. Yet families choosing those programs navigated a lottery and made an active choice to stay in PPS. They are among the least likely to transfer to a neighborhood school if the option disappears — and among the most likely to leave the district. The district’s K-8 schools are also popular. Eliminating programs families choose is not redistribution. It is contraction.
The district’s data already tells us which programs families want. Rather than closing them, an effective strategy would bring the best elements of these programs — experiential learning, project-based curriculum and strong community — into more neighborhood schools. That is harder and slower than closures. It is also the only approach that addresses the real problem.
Elissa Scott, Portland
