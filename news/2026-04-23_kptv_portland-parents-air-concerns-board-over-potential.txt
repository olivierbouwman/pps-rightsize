Portland parents air concerns to board over potential school closures
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - At Ida B. Wells High School Wednesday night, the Portland Public Schools board heard concerns from parents about the district’s “Rightsizing” initiative, an effort that could lead to consolidations and closures at five to ten schools.
The initiative is aimed at addressing an approximate $50 million budget shortfall for the 2026–27 fiscal year. According to the district, the deficit is driven by declining enrollment, rising costs, and the expiration of one-time funding.
“I know they’re trying to make lemons out of lemonade, but it’s not right,” said George Alvarez, a parent of a first grader at Chief Joseph Elementary School in North Portland. “It’s not right that we should have to be ‘right-sizing.’ There’s a little bit of irony in that word.”
Alvarez was one of dozens of parents who attended the district’s third community engagement session on Wednesday. Many advocated for programs and resources at K-8, K-5, and alternative schools to remain in place, while also asking which schools could be impacted.
Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong confirmed the scope of the review is broad.
“Really, we’re looking across the board,” Dr. Armstrong said. “It isn’t about one size, one style, or one category. All of our Portland Public Schools are being considered, with the goal of determining the best fit for our students.”
The superintendent said no final decisions have been made, but she plans to present a list of schools recommended for closure in November.
For parents like Alvarez, the uncertainty is personal.
“Chief Joe is at 240 students. I think the max capacity is 350, and I know when you fall below 300, you could be on the chopping block,” he said.
Alvarez says his daughter has autism and a speech delay, and he credits the school’s reading intervention program for her progress. If the school appears on the superintendent’s list, he worries about losing that support.
He added that some Chief Joseph families are planning to advocate at the district, city, state, and federal levels in hopes of securing more funding.
“Some people are going to be left out in the cold, and it’s not due to a lack of planning or creative input,” Alvarez said. “It’s because we’re getting 60 cents when we should be getting a dollar. For 20 years, this district seems to have received less funding than it should at the state and federal level.”
Superintendent Armstrong is scheduled to release her proposed budget for the 2026–27 school year on April 28.
The district says school “rightsizing” decisions will not be announced during the budget engagement process.
Additional community engagement sessions are scheduled for April 25 from 2:00 to 3:30 p.m. at Benson High School and April 27 from 6:00 to 7:30 p.m. at Roosevelt High School. Both sessions will offer optional virtual participation.
