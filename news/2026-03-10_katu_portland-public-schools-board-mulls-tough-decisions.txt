Portland Public Schools board mulls tough decisions amid budget woes
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Hundreds of Portland families may soon have their school routine upended.
Portland Public Schools could close up to 10 schools, Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong announced on Tuesday.
"We're in a situation where we need to save Portland Public Schools from some of the financial impacts that are happening across our city,” Armstrong said.

The district shared the revelation as it faces a $50 million deficit going into the next school year.
It's also working to find immediate steps to close a $14 million gap from this year.
"At what point in this parallel right-sizing, seismic conversation, do we then say this school is staying open?" PPS Board Member Stephanie Engelsman asked.
Board members were presented with a list of schools scheduled for extensive seismic upgrades.
The district is weighing what schools would be upgraded and which could be on the chopping block.
The district calls the closure of schools "right-sizing."
It was revealed during Tuesday night's meeting that, before any final decision is made, board members would have to consider what school that students would temporarily vacate while seismic upgrades are being done and what schools students would still occupy during construction.
One board member expressed concern about pushing construction too far ahead, which could lead to rising construction costs.
Armstrong says school population could be considered among other factors.
“We also know that we have a list of schools that have serious seismic concerns should we experience an earthquake. And so all of those details will be factored in when we look at this framework and list out the school. So I don't know the list could be five to 10 schools maybe,” Armstrong said.
She added, "I anticipate presenting the board with a list of schools for closure in November for board action in December and then notification out to community by January of this upcoming year.”
Without giving much detail, Armstrong said closing schools wouldn't provide much savings for the district but would optimize their operations.
The district plans to reach out to the community for feedback before Armstrong makes her recommendations.
