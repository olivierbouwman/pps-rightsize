PORTLAND, Oregon — Amid all the excitement of back to school this week, a sort of "budgetary black cloud" hangs over Portland Public Schools.
Outside Rosa Parks Elementary School Wednesday morning, Portland Public Schools Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong shared that the district faces upwards of $65 million in reductions and perhaps more layoffs this school year.
"When we're right sizing or laying off, it's to make sure that we're staffing our classrooms to our budget, and we do keep in mind class sizes," Portland Public Schools superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said.
The district laid off hundreds of staff members last school year as PPS grappled with declining enrollment.
Pre-COVID, the district predicted having more than 50,000 students by 2030. Now, that number sits around 40,000, with the latest PPS projection having enrollment down to 36,000 by 2040.
"To my eye, the declines and expectations are quite steep and stark, and it's going to have real impacts on all sorts of things that affect Portland and Portlanders. We fund schools in Oregon not exclusively — but a lot of it — based on a per-pupil basis, money from the state, so PPS can expect less funding to come from the state," John Horvick with DHM Research said.
Horvick said this problem isn't unique to Portland but seems exacerbated here.
"People are having fewer children," Horvick said. "Portland is attracting people who are choosing to have fewer children, and we're getting older as a community."

Facing these currents and looming budget shortfalls, Portland may be forced to close or consolidate schools.
"Ultimately, we will be presenting a draft list of schools in November to our board to consider for consolidations, closures and even some program balancing or moves," Armstrong said.
Last spring, the district estimated they may close 5 to 12 schools, but on Wednesday, Armstrong said that number could be much higher.
"That number could be as great as 20 or as few as 10. I think it just really depends on the decisions we make and the input from the board as we make our way through September," Armstrong said.
Armstrong said the latest data will be presented to the school board at their Sept. 1 meeting.
