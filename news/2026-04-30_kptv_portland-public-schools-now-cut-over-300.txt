Portland Public Schools now to cut over 300 jobs next year and may close schools
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - The Portland Public Schools superintendent said on Wednesday that the district is considering cutting more than 300 jobs next year as major budget deficits continue.
This year PPS was facing a $50 million budget gap. Earlier this year they announced multiple furlough days for both teachers and administrative staff, to try and close that gap.
However, in a Wednesday morning news conference, Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong said that, looking ahead to next year’s projected budget, they will face an even larger deficit of $65 million. So now the school district is cutting 322 positions, mainly out of the central office, but there will be impacts in classrooms as well.
“If we don’t take those big swings and make those reductions, then those reductions will continue on to year four, five, six. There is an old saying about kicking the can down the road. Well, we have. So, we are actively addressing it,” Dr. Armstrong said.
Dr. Armstrong said they are still considering closing some schools next year, but she could not share what campuses are being considered at this time. Those options will be presented to the school board by the end of the year.
The Portland Association of Teachers, the teachers’ union, issued a statement on Wednesday, calling on Governor Kotek to convene a special legislative session on education and encourage the legislature to release the Education Stability Funds.
“...Districts across the state will experience hundreds of layoffs which leaves us further from the goal of a fully funded and successful school year. These ‘Kotek Cuts’ don’t build faith in our state leadership but do exactly the opposite.
“Tina Kotek is inflicting generational trauma on PPS students and school communities across Oregon with her cold indifference and petty refusal to allow local districts access to $1 billion in existing state relief funds that her predecessors did allow to be tapped in times of emergency,” PAT said in their statement.
“...Long term, we need revenue reform in Oregon so our schools are funded off mega-corporations like Nike and Intel paying their fair share, instead of off the backs of working people like it is now. In the interim, one-time emergency access to the $1 billion existing state reserves is necessary...”
