Portland Public Schools Proposes Up to 14 School Closures in Major Restructuring Plan
PORTLAND, Ore. — Portland Public Schools officials presented sweeping “rightsizing” proposals to the Board of Education, outlining plans to close up to 14 schools, adjust dozens of attendance boundaries, and overhaul specialized programs to address decades of declining and uneven enrollment.
Under the proposals, the district is considering three main paths forward: Scenario A, which would close 14 schools (13 elementary and one middle school); Scenario B, which would close 11 schools (10 elementary and one middle school); or a third option to defer the decision entirely, maintaining current operations despite resource strain.
If approved, the changes would take effect in the fall of 2027.
Addressing Enrollment Realities
District leadership highlighted that overall student enrollment has steadily dropped while PPS has continued to maintain roughly the same number of buildings. The imbalance has left smaller schools struggling with stretched staffing and limited program offerings compared to larger counterparts.
“We know how deeply connected students, families, educators and communities are to their schools, and considering the closure of any school is extraordinarily difficult,” said Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong. “But we also have a responsibility to look at Portland Public Schools as a whole and consider changes that give every student access to strong programs, stable staffing and the opportunities and support they deserve.”
Key Differences Between Scenarios
Both active proposals would impact attendance boundaries and program locations across Portland, though they differ in scope:
- Scenario A (14 Closures): Proposes closing Maplewood, Stephenson, Irvington, Rose City Park, Buckman, Creston, Marysville, Woodmere, Lewis, Beach, James John, Peninsula, and Sabin elementary schools, along with Sellwood Middle School. It includes 38 boundary changes and moves Rose City Park’s Vietnamese immersion program to Vestal.
- Scenario B (11 Closures): Retains Stephenson, Lewis, and Rose City Park elementary schools while proposing the closure of the other 11 facilities listed in Scenario A. It incorporates 34 boundary changes and keeps the Vietnamese immersion program at Rose City Park.
- Deferring the Decision: Postpones closures and structural changes, leaving current configurations intact. PPS officials warn this option would leave staffing and financial resources increasingly stretched across under-enrolled facilities.
Broad Program Shifts Across the District
In addition to individual school closures, both Scenario A and Scenario B outline major operational shifts across the district:
- Transitioning K–8 Schools: Seven K–8 schools—Beverly Cleary, Laurelhurst, Sunnyside, Bridger Creative Science, César Chávez, Faubion, and Vernon—would transition into K–5 neighborhood elementary schools. Grades 6–8 students from these campuses would move to comprehensive middle schools.
- High School Changes at MLC: Metropolitan Learning Center (MLC) would retain its K–8 program and absorb the Odyssey program from the East Sylvan facility. However, MLC’s grades 9–12 high school program would close, with high schoolers transitioned to other district options.
- Dual Language Immersion (DLI): DLI programs would be consolidated to build consistent pathways, though no immersion language would be eliminated. Spanish and Mandarin programs would shift to designated hubs including César Chávez, George, Rigler, Roseway Heights, Lent, Woodstock, and Boise-Eliot/Humboldt.
- Focus Options: Sunnyside Environmental School would lose its environmental focus option, transitioning into a traditional neighborhood K–5 school.
Next Steps and Community Input
District officials will host community engagement sessions through November to gather feedback from parents, students, and educators.
Superintendent Armstrong will review community input before submitting a final recommendation to the Board of Education. The Board is scheduled to vote on the final plan in December.
Detailed, school-by-school impacts and boundary maps are available on the district’s website at pps.net.
