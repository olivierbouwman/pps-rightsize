Title: Oregon’s Shrinking Enrollment Puts Neighborhood Schools on the Closure List
Outlet: Grants Pass Tribune
Author: John Oliver
Date: 2026-10-07
URL: https://www.grantspasstribune.com/oregons-shrinking-enrollment-puts-neighborhood-schools-on-the-closure-list/

Portland and Salem-Keizer are moving toward decisions that could reshape their public school systems in 2027, with proposed closures, attendance boundary changes and program relocations affecting families well beyond the campuses under consideration. Both districts are evaluating how to operate fewer schools as enrollment declines, while keeping enough staff, classroom space and educational services available for the students who remain.
Portland Public Schools released alternatives October 5 that would close either 14 or 11 schools. Salem-Keizer Public Schools plans to identify up to eight elementary schools recommended for closure Friday, October 9, along with proposed attendance boundary adjustments. Neither district has approved the closures, and families will have opportunities to review the recommendations before school boards make decisions in December.
The proposals come as the districts confront a widening gap between the number of students they serve and the buildings they operate. Smaller incoming classes, changing enrollment patterns and the cost of maintaining facilities are driving evaluations that extend beyond annual budget reductions. District administrators are examining whether consolidating students into fewer locations would provide more stable staffing and consistent access to educational programs.
In Portland, the more extensive alternative, Scenario A, would close 13 elementary schools and Sellwood Middle School. The elementary schools named are Maplewood, Stephenson, Irvington, Rose City Park, Buckman, Creston, Marysville, Woodmere, Lewis, Beach, James John, Peninsula and Sabin. Students attending those schools would be reassigned to receiving campuses, and the proposal includes 38 attendance boundary changes.
Scenario B would retain Stephenson, Rose City Park and Lewis, reducing the closure list to 10 elementary schools and Sellwood Middle School. That alternative includes 34 boundary changes. A third option would preserve the district’s existing school arrangements for now.
Portland’s proposed changes also reach schools that would remain open. Seven campuses serving kindergarten through eighth grade would shift to kindergarten through fifth grade: Beverly Cleary, Laurelhurst, Sunnyside, Bridger Creative Science, César Chávez, Faubion and Vernon. Those changes would require new arrangements for students entering the middle grades.
Other recommendations include ending the high school component at Metropolitan Learning Center and relocating the Odyssey program into that building. Sunnyside would remain open but lose its designation as an environmental focus option school. Portland officials say dual language immersion programs would continue, although some would move or combine at designated campuses.
Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong is expected to submit a formal recommendation in November, followed by a school board vote in December. The alternatives could change during public review. Parents and teachers have already organized opposition, including petitions asking the district to consider boundary adjustments, central office staffing reductions and efforts to attract additional students before closing schools.
Salem-Keizer’s process is approaching its first school-specific recommendations. The district operates 42 elementary schools, meaning a closure list of eight would encompass nearly one-fifth of those campuses. The October 9 release is preliminary and will begin a period of public discussion rather than settle which schools will close.
Salem-Keizer serves approximately 36,000 students, compared with more than 40,000 several years ago. District officials identify lower local birth rates and smaller kindergarten classes as principal contributors to that decline. As larger classes graduate and smaller classes enter, enrollment decreases, along with the state funding associated with those students.
The Salem-Keizer School Board approved evaluation criteria September 8 covering enrollment, building use and condition, educational needs, operating considerations and community effects. The review also examines whether a closure could disproportionately affect historically marginalized or underserved students and neighborhoods. No individual school was approved for closure through that vote.
For families, the eventual decisions could change school assignments, transportation arrangements and the locations where children receive specialized services. Boundary adjustments could also affect students whose current schools remain open. Salem-Keizer officials say class-size effects will depend on the final configurations and staffing decisions.
The Portland and Salem-Keizer proposals do not include Southern Oregon schools. Future closure considerations in Southern Oregon would depend on enrollment, finances and facility needs within individual districts, with any proposals requiring their own local review and decisions.
Salem-Keizer has scheduled five community listening sessions October 14 through October 17 and a final closure decision for December 8. A boundary review task force will work from November through January. In both districts, approved changes would begin taking effect with the 2027-28 school year, leaving the coming months for decisions about receiving schools, attendance areas, staffing and student transitions.
