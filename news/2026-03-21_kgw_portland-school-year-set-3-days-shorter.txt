PORTLAND, Ore. — This school year for Portland Public Schools will likely be three days shorter as the district attempts to plug a $14 million budget shortfall.
Pending ratification by PPS board members, the last day of school will be Friday, June 5. Previously, school was set to end on Tuesday, June 9.
On Friday, a majority of the Portland Association of Teachers union, or 85%, voted in favor of a plan to take four furlough days, according to reporting from the Oregonian. As a result of the furloughs, PPS said any potential layoffs applicable to the 2025-2026 school year will be eliminated.
Those furlough days are designated for May 1, May 25, June 9 and June 10; May 25 was originally a paid holiday but will instead be an unpaid furlough day. June 8 will be a grading day instead of an instructional day.
PPS plans to cancel its early release days on April 13 and May 13, instead becoming full instructional days; high school staff meetings will now only happen once a month.
PPS has already cut roughly a third of its central office academic administrators, the first part of its three-phase plan to address the budget gap, reportedly supposed to save $2.75 million. Portland Public Schools Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong also said she would take six furlough days.
The state's largest school district has experienced declining enrollment for the last few years and is expected to increase over the next decade.
In a statement, PAT called the current outlook "systemically broken," adding that four state school districts — PPS, Reynolds, West Linn Wilsonville and St. Helens — are "all having mid-year crisis furloughs rammed through at the same time."
The union cited administrative fiscal mismanagement and chronic underfunding from lawmakers, continuing, in part:
"With the 4 unpaid furlough days, PPS has given teachers next to no choice but to accept, we are, as always, tasked with doing the heavy lifting of losing pay and instructional days with our students to bail PPS out. The 4 furlough days we have accepted are not just. They were accepted only to avoid what we were told would invariably be the mass termination of 500 FTE jobs for teachers across PPS — 200 this school year, and 200-300 next school year — if we did not acquiesce."
