Title: Portland Public Schools discloses unexpected $10 million gap in current year’s budget

Author: Julia Silverman

Portland Public Schools is facing an unexpected $10 million gap in its operating budget for the current school year, Chief Fiscal Officer Michelle Morrison told the district’s nearly 3,900 employees in an email this week.
That’s separate from the $50 million that the school district is seeking to cut from its operating budget for the 2026-2027 school year, Morrison wrote, and means that belt-tightening will begin even sooner than expected.
That $50 million hole is driven by external and internal factors, including rising labor costs negotiated by the district and its employee unions and declining enrollment levels.
The midyear gap is different and exists because of unexpected costs that have cropped up or ballooned during the school year, Morrison wrote. Those include:
- Less-than-expected savings from jobs left vacant after resignations and retirements that have unfolded during the course of the school year.
- Costs of responding to multiple federal lawsuits, including a case filed by a high school math teacher alleging that the district allowed his colleagues to promote and distribute “anti-Israel material,” a Washington, D.C. think tank’s suit alleging that the formula the district uses to allocate staff is biased in favor of schools with higher proportions of non-white students, and an investigation by the Trump administration into the district’s pending Center for Black Student Excellence. The district is also facing an investigation from the U.S. Department of Education’s Office of Civil Rights over its policies on transgender athletes. “Although no single claim has generated significant cost to date, as a whole, they are outside the norm for PPS and implicate important issues and risk for PPS,” said Sydney Kelly, a spokesperson for the school district, via email.
- Services that were slated for cuts being restored without equivalent trims made elsewhere. Examples include positions added to schools to help meet student needs, Kelly said, as well as increased costs in transportation and maintenance.
- Emergency facility spending, including a steam leak at Cleveland High School, a cooling system and guard rail repairs at Benson High School, a broken pipe at Sabin Elementary, a sewer break at Rigler Elementary and other significant repairs at Sitton and Buckman elementary schools.
- Revenues from the district’s local operating levy are $5 million less than predicted because of lower commercial property tax collections, an issue that is also dogging the city of Portland’s budget.
The $10 million figure has already been whittled down from $22.5 million via “cost controls and adjustments already underway,” Kelly said.
The district will address the remaining hole in its operating budget in part by instituting a spending freeze and by canceling a planned day of professional development in March for teachers at schools that have been identified as needing extra support because of lagging student performance, Morrison wrote.
There will also be increased oversight into substitute and overtime requests, she said.
Typically, it is unusual for school district employees to be laid off while school is still in session. But because staffing costs constitute about 80% of the district’s budget, Kelly said, closing the $10 million gap may “require examining personnel cost.”
“We are working closely with our labor partners on all options that might address this budget gap,” Kelly wrote. “At this time, no final decisions have been made regarding the number or types of positions that may be impacted.”
