Oregon Public Schools are getting smaller and more school closures could follow
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Oregon’s public schools are getting smaller.
Statewide enrollment fell by more than 9,000 students in the 2025-26 school year — the largest one-year decline since the initial shock of the COVID-19 pandemic. And some of Oregon’s largest school districts are shrinking even faster.
That decline is already forcing districts to make difficult decisions about staffing, budgets, and school buildings — with more closures potentially on the way.
Portland, Salem-Keizer, and Beaverton change places
For decades, the order of Oregon’s three largest school districts remained the same:
- Portland was No. 1.
- Salem-Keizer was No. 2.
- Beaverton was No. 3.
That has now changed. Beaverton has moved into the No. 2 spot, behind Portland. But the change isn't because Beaverton is growing. All three districts are losing students, and all three are now at their lowest enrollment levels in at least 17 years.

Salem-Keizer has lost more than 5,000 students since its enrollment peak in 2018. A district forecast shows it could lose another 4,500 students by 2030. That decline is already forcing difficult decisions.
Salem-Keizer is considering closing to eight elementary schools beginning next fall. Superintendent Andrea Castañeda says there is no perfect solution.
“No proposal list is going to be perfect. There are always going to be trade-offs,” Castañeda said. She says the district will weigh the competing factors as carefully as possible.
Seven of Oregon’s largest districts are at 17-year lows
Salem-Keizer isn't an isolated example.
State enrollment data show that seven of Oregon’s 10 largest school districts are now at their lowest enrollment levels in at least 17 years.
Those districts are:
- Portland Public Schools
- Salem-Keizer
- Beaverton
- Hillsboro
- Eugene
- Tigard-Tualatin
- Gresham-Barlow
Portland remains Oregon’s largest school district. But it has lost about 6,570 students since its 2019 peak.
Beaverton has lost nearly 4,650 students since its peak. The enrollment decline is reshaping districts statewide.
Fewer children are entering kindergarten
So, what's driving the decline? Demographics are a major factor. Oregon's birth rate has fallen, meaning fewer children are entering the public school system.
In Salem-Keizer, district officials say they graduate roughly 3,500 seniors each year but bring in only about 2,500 kindergarteners.
That's a gap of roughly 1,000 students between the number of students leaving the system and the number entering.
Districts also say more families are choosing private schools, homeschooling, and other educational options.
Fewer students, but many of the same costs
Declining enrollment creates a difficult financial problem for school districts. Fewer students generally mean less state funding. But districts still have many of the same fixed costs — including school buildings, transportation, utilities, and other infrastructure.
That leaves districts with fewer students to support the same physical system. And for families, one of the most visible consequences can be the closure of a neighborhood school.
As enrollment continues to fall, districts across Oregon are increasingly being forced to ask a difficult question: Which schools can they afford to keep open?
