‘I’m worried’: Parents, teachers voice concerns over possible school closures within PPS
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - Portlanders showed up at a Portland Public Schools committee meeting Thursday to voice their concerns about the tough decisions the district is making to address its budget deficit.
As PPS faces hard decisions amid a budget crisis, community members said they want answers on what that means for their schools.
“I’ve weathered a lot of different storms, but this seems like the biggest storm that the district has faced in all of my time here,” said Buckman Elementary School Music Teacher Eric Schopmeyer.
PPS is grappling with a $50 million budget deficit and addressing it by reducing licensed administrative jobs, implementing temporary furloughs, and possibly closing five to 10 schools by next year.
“I wasn’t surprised, to be honest with you, we’ve seen the writing on the wall for a long time,” said PTA member Arianna Avena.
The district is facing financial challenges due to rising operational costs and declining student enrollment, which the district said has declined 12% since 2018 and is projected to continue to decline at that rate through 2035.
But with change comes fear, growing in concerned parents.
“I’m worried that something will end up happening, not because that is what is best for the students, but because hands are tied and that’s just what needs to happen,” said Rose City Park Elementary School Parent Britt Appleton.
The district hasn’t said how many schools would close or which ones would if it moves forward with that plan, but some are already fighting to make sure they aren’t on the chopping block.
“Us finding out that the arts are now being cut fits very well into my perspective of the district wants to close our school, particularly,” said Avena.
“Next year, because of budget cuts, the arts team is being pretty much decimated and really cut back, so it feels like the end of an era in our community,” said Schopmeyer.
In a statement, the Portland Association of Teachers said, “PPS’ current plan is balancing the budget for this $14 million mid-year deficit, and the upcoming $50 million deficit for next school year, with up to 400 layoffs of teachers.” The statement goes on to say, “the district is once again expecting teachers and students to bear the brunt of their mistakes.”
That’s why some plan on fighting back until something changes.
“If they say it’s going to take through the whole 2026 until they make their decision on whose cut, we’re going to be here through the entire 2026 showing up every single board meeting, and they can expect us because we’re not a school that backs down,” said Avena.
