PORTLAND, Oregon — Portland Public Schools is weighing the closure of 15 elementary schools and three middle schools as district leaders confront declining enrollment and a projected $50 million budget shortfall. The proposal drew sharp concern from parents and teachers during a board meeting Tuesday.
The district has discussed possible school closures and “right-sizing” efforts since March, but many parents said they were surprised by the scope of the latest proposal.
“Closing schools is a drastic step with hugely negative repercussions,” one community member told the board during public testimony.
Others criticized what they described as a lack of clarity from district leaders.
“It clearly feels like we’re trying to build the plane as we’re flying it here, which hardly feels like the recipe for a successful outcome in public education,” said another parent.
District officials have not publicly identified which schools could be affected. However, Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong outlined challenges she said the district faces if no action is taken.
“In too many places, that means we are stretching our people, programs and resources too thinly to consistently provide the educational experience we want for every student,” Armstrong told the board.
According to district data, enrollment has fallen by more than 6,000 students since 2019. Portland Public Schools projects enrollment will decline by another 3,400 students over the next six years.
District leaders said that without consolidating schools, many elementary and middle schools could become vulnerable to enrollment fluctuations, creating difficulties in maintaining stable staffing levels and educational programs.
Parents at the meeting questioned both the district’s financial decisions and its long-term strategy.
“Fifteen to 20 (schools) seems absolutely shocking,” said parent Frances Chewning. “I’m concerned not just for my school but for the whole city.”
Another parent, Ratchanee Vasilev, criticized spending on major school construction projects while neighborhood schools face potential closure.
“Why are we going so over budget on all these high school renovations and other school renovations when we’re suffering so much here?” Vasilev said.
Some community members urged district leaders to focus on attracting students back to the district rather than closing schools.
“I’d love to hear the school board’s view on how we could better recruit more of the many, many unenrolled students into PPS for a more sustainable future,” one speaker said. “Why not build bridges instead of destroy them?”
Community engagement sessions are expected to continue throughout the fall. The president of the district’s teachers union said a formal proposal, including a list of affected schools, is expected next month.
The PPS school board is expected to vote on a recommendation in January. If approved, the closures and consolidations could begin as early as the next school year.
