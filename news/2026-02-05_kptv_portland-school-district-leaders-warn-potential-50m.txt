Portland school district leaders warn potential $50M cuts could eliminate 300 jobs, close school
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - Portland Public Schools leaders are asking state lawmakers not to approve a proposed $50 million budget cut as the legislative session continues in Salem.
The cut would mark the third consecutive year of multi-million dollar budget reductions for the district. Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said the cuts would force the elimination of nearly 300 full-time equivalent positions and could lead to a school closure, but she could not give specifics yet as to what campuses would be impacted.
“I would say 90% of funds are spent on personal so that’s why when we look at a $50 million deficit we are looking at loosing nearly 300 FTE,” Armstrong said.
District warns of academic impact
PPS sent a letter to state lawmakers saying a cut of this size would reverse progress the district has made with academic achievement. District leaders said the continued cuts are making it difficult to retain teachers.
“Here at PPS moral is at an all time low the stress with our teachers and staff is honestly unsustainable,” said Eddie Wang, PPS school board chair.
Leaders propose funding solution
School officials suggested using Oregon’s Education Stability Fund to avoid the cuts. The billion-dollar rainy day fund is designed to maintain school funding if state revenue falls short.
“This is an emergency three years of multi million dollar cuts and the spirit of the law says the purpose of the stability fund is to prevent emergencies like this,” Wang said.
According to the latest revenue forecast, the state is bringing in nearly $100 million more than projected.
“We are hoping that is enough to back off any cuts were going to come,” Armstrong said.
Lawmakers remain cautious
Representative Zach Hudson, chair of the House Committee on Education, said he is encouraging colleagues not to touch the State School Fund but cannot make promises to individual districts.
“Oregon is in a difficult spot at the moment. The budget we passed in 2025 has been ripped apart by federal changes. We have to pass a balanced budget each biennium, and usually the even-year short sessions are the time to make small adjustments. This year is different: we need to make big changes in revenue, spending, and probably both, to balance the budget appropriately.
The good news is that the new revenue forecast released this morning show that we’re bringing in more corporate income tax than we had anticipated. That said, we still have revenue problems, largely caused by the Federal bill HR 1," Hudson said in a statement. “I’m working with my colleagues in both chambers as they develop a revenue package and a balanced budget, but there is no way to make promises to individual school districts. We just aren’t at that point in the process yet. I thank Portland Public Schools for advocating for their teachers and students, and I hope we can put together a budget that doesn’t disrupt teaching and learning anywhere in Oregon.”
The PPS budget will be finalized in June.
