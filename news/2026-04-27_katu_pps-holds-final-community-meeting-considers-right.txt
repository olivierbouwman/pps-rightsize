PPS holds final community meeting as it considers 'right-sizing,' school closures
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Portland Public Schools families can gather to ask questions and voice concerns about the district's pending "right-sizing," which could include closing up to a dozen elementary and middle schools.
The last planned community engagement session was held inside North Portland's Roosevelt High School on Monday.
During a community engagement session last week in Southwest Portland, Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong told dozens of parents and staff that the number of schools that could be closed could be anywhere from one to 12 K-8, elementary, middle, and alternative schools.
The generational changes come as the district plans another round of budget cuts. PPS is planning how to close a $50 million shortfall for the 26-27 fiscal year. Board members will finalize the budget in June.
“School communities have so much pride and so much joy in their sites, and so it’s hard to have a conversation that could lead to a disruption of that,” Armstrong said last week. “It’s a conversation that has been avoided for too long, and it’s time to have it. I’m ready to have it, and I think my board is ready to take action.”

Armstrong is expected to deliver her recommendations for "right-sizing" the district by the end of 2026.
Multiple James John Elementary School parents told the board at Monday's meeting that they are concerned about losing unconventional learning programs, like the arts, or that North Portland may bear the brunt of the cuts.
"For my child specifically, when she comes home, she doesn't really want to talk to me about what's happening at school. But when she has a day when she can really play, or experience art, or experience music, she has all kinds of exciting things to say about her education. Those are the days I want to see more of at PPS," said Sam Ellingson.
"I’m concerned about North Portland having a voice in the conversation," said Kelly Keller.
District officials told the crowd that the "right-sizing" process could result in several changes beyond school closures, including the "movement of programs," changing boundary lines, or consolidating schools.
Multiple parents and board members later pushed the crowd to lobby lawmakers to make fundamental changes to the state's funding formula, which they say is failing to meet the needs of districts across Oregon.
"If you're not pissed off at the state Legislature and our governor right now, you're not paying attention," said PPS Board Member Christy Splitt.
