The Oregonian/OregonLive article profiling newly elected Portland Public Schools board members suggests they might be open to a different emphasis for their future deliberations, (“At Portland Public Schools, a ‘mom takeover’ and a brewing storm,” July 23). A former PPS board adopted a policy of providing an equitable curriculum at the district’s high schools. Certainly, there are lessons that are beneficial to all students, but high school also offers students a choice of electives to further individual student interests.
At Benson Polytechnic High School, these are largely career training courses. Students from anywhere in the district can apply to attend. Typically, there are more 8th graders submitting these applications each year than there is space available, and Benson graduation rates are among the top of the PPS high schools.
These magnet programs that attract high school students from throughout the district have thrived since they were introduced at what was then called the Portland School of Trades in 1908. Benson is not the only Portland high school with specialty courses, but we should consider whether this approach might work at all schools. Doing so would put a core of enthused students in each school and allow the district to increase the range of topics offered, attract teachers eager to teach these specialties, and perhaps create a new way to meet state requirements.
Designating a school for language instruction might ease teaching English as a Second Language. Science, business, mathematics, information technology and geography are also among the countless subjects with a latent demand.
Roger Edwards, Portland
