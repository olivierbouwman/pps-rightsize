Parents push back on PPS plan to close neighborhood schools at packed listening session
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Next week, Portland Public Schools (PPS) will hear from families about its plan to close between five and 10 schools.
PPS has begun researching which schools to close starting in the 2027-28 school year. The district will select schools from K-8, middle and alternative schools.
Rebecca Roscoe has a second grader at Jason Lee Elementary School in Southeast Portland. The school’s enrollment has fallen below 300 students, and that worries her. She said she doesn’t know what would happen to her son’s education, and it is scary to think about Jason Lee closing.

Hundreds of parents packed the Kellogg Middle School cafeteria Saturday, questioning district leaders about the plan to close schools. It marked the first of three community engagement sessions planned this month.
The PPS district faces a financial crisis and is already $50 million in the hole heading into the new school year.
“We are not doing this from a money perspective. We are doing this because it's about comprehensive programming and well-resourced schools, so kids can have access to comprehensive programming," said Dr. Jon Franco, senior chief of operations at Portland Public Schools.
But that doesn’t calm the nerves of families across Portland.
Amelia Dalton worries that if Woodmere Elementary School closed, “we would lose that sense of togetherness.” She said she has been at the school for 10 years and “it feels like we are being punished for having small class sizes, which seems to be detrimental to all learning as a whole."
Franco reiterated the district is “just starting this process, and we are checking it in phases.”
The district said it plans to continue community engagement once it establishes guiding principles.
Parents urged the district to pause the plan and “do some serious research about the equity and how this is affecting our poorest children and our poorest families, and then re-figure out how we fund our schools.”
There are four more engagement sessions where families can make their voices heard. Monday, April 20, there will be a Zoom call at 6 p.m. On Wednesday, April 22, there will be an in-person session at Ida B. Wells High School at 6 p.m. On Saturday, April 25, Benson High School will host an engagement session on Zoom from 2 p.m. to 3:30 p.m. And on Monday, April 27, Roosevelt High School will hold an engagement session on Zoom from 6 p.m. to 7:30 p.m.
