Portland Public School board members will consider giving Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong a raise and three-year contract extension during a Tuesday evening meeting at Grant High School.
The new contract comes as Armstrong and Portland Public Schools head into an extraordinarily complicated year. The district is facing a $65 million budget shortfall, preparing to potentially close up to 20 elementary, K-8 and middle schools and readying for negotiations with the Portland Association of Teachers over a new three-year contract.
When Armstrong was hired in 2024, she made $325,000 per year. That was less than the $342,000 that previous Superintendent Guadalupe Guerrero was making in base salary when he departed Portland after six years, in part because Armstrong had no previous superintendent experience when she was hired, board chair Eddie Wang said.
Her original contract provided for salary increases of 3% per year. But the revised contract specifies that she will receive either a 3% raise annually or the cost of living adjustment agreed to by the Portland Association of Teachers’ union for its members, whichever is lower.
A copy of her proposed contract extension lists her new base salary at $344,792 per year, or $28,733 dollars a month. That’s a 3% raise from what she made last year. The contract was approved too late to match the PAT rate for the year, school board members said Tuesday night.
Armstrong’s base salary is slightly lower than that of her peers who lead similarly sized districts around the country, including the superintendents of Denver Public Schools, Oakland Public Schools and Omaha Public Schools; it is below what the superintendent makes in Seattle, Beaverton and San Francisco.
Armstrong’s proposed new contract includes significant perks and raises in other areas, beyond her base salary. For example:
- An increase in the district’s contribution to her retirement account, from 8.5% to 9.5% of base salary, or about $32,000 annually. The contract provides that it will eventually rise to 10.5%, starting in July of 2027.
- A $750 per month car allowance, up from $400, or from $4,800 a year to $9,000 per year.
- A new retention bonus that would kick in if she stays in Portland for four years since she was hired, adding an additional 3% to her base salary starting in her June 2028 paycheck. That rises to 5% for the 2028-2029 school year, or roughly $17,000 per year. Wang said that is a relatively low bonus compared to the 8% to 10% paid out to superintendents who lead comparable districts around the country.
The contract also provides for the ability to cash out more vacation days, from 12 per year to 17 per year, which could represent another $4,700 if she chooses to be paid out for those extra days instead of taking time off. The new contract would also allow her to take up to five “flex” days per year, to offset work done on holidays or weekends on the district’s behalf.
“This small increase is getting slightly closer to what we consider fair in a time where the superintendent is leading the district through the most challenging time we’ve seen in decades,” Wang said. “We need to show her we support her in her work and acknowledge the difficulty of the decisions she has to make.”
