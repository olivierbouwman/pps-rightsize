Community voices concern over Portland Public Schools 'rightsizing'
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Portland Public Schools is beginning what leaders call a “rightsizing” process that could eventually change how some school buildings are used across the district.
District officials say the effort is meant to respond to declining student enrollment, rising costs, and the challenge of maintaining older school facilities.
This comes as some buildings are under capacity, while others remain crowded.

Right now, the district is in the early stages, reviewing enrollment data and gathering community feedback.
“Enrollment decline is not just about fewer children, it’s about fewer families choosing PPS. If confidence erodes, enrollment will continue to decline, compounding the problem,” one person told the school board on Tuesday.
"Let equity truly be your north star, guiding your process steadily and consistently when the pressures are loud around you,” said Leslie Brown, PTA president at Lee Elementary. “Protect Title 1 school communities whenever possible and ensure that students who rely mostly on their neighborhood schools are the last to bear the burden of closures.”
Over the coming months, PPS could release proposals that include consolidating schools, repurposing buildings, or possible closures before any final recommendations go to the school board.
