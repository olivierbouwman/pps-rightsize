Portland school board approves pay raise for superintendent amid closure talks
PORTLAND Ore. (KPTV) - The Portland Public Schools board has approved a pay increase for Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong as the district discusses the possibility of closing some schools.
The board voted 5-2 this week to approve a contract extension with a 3% pay increase for Armstrong, who is in her third year as superintendent.
The vote means Armstrong will earn a salary of $344,792. Her initial contract with the district started at $325,000 with 3% cost of living increases.
Armstrong has been the face of what she has called the PPS “rightsizing” plan, which could lead to closing as many as 20 schools.

The raise comes as PPS faces a $50 million budget shortfall for the 2026-27 school year. The Portland teachers union received what is essentially a 1% cost of living increase.
Portland City Councilor Tiffany Koyama Lane spoke at the board meeting and said the pay raise is a bad idea given the district’s economic reality.
“We just watched nearly 90 educators get cut, we’re hearing more cuts are on the way, we’re hearing schools are about to be shuttered, but we’re seriously talking about a raise for the highest paid employee in the whole district? I’m not sure how you can look parents and teachers in the eye right now,” Koyama Lane said.
Some board members and parents say the raise is needed to keep in line with what other districts are paying.
Maya Pueo von Geldern, a parent, said the district cannot advocate for equal pay and racial equity while treating Armstrong as though she doesn’t deserve the going rate for her position.
“We can’t say that we fight for equal pay and racial equity or for women to break the class ceiling and then treat Dr. Armstrong like she doesn’t deserve this position for the going rate,” von Geldern said.
Some board members pointed to the fact that even with the raise, Armstrong will make less than the superintendent of the smaller Beaverton School District. Superintendents in several smaller Washington school districts make more than $400,000 a year.
PPS will also need to sign a new contract with the teachers union for the next school year. The union president said the pay raise will set a dangerous precedent for contract negotiations. Portland teachers went on strike in 2023.
