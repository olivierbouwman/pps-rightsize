This fall, Portland Public Schools will decide whether to close up to 20 schools - a decision with impacts far beyond the classroom.
Schools are the hearts of Portland neighborhoods. At our neighborhood school in Southeast Portland – a cornerstone of its community for over 100 years – an elderly neighbor brings his dog to the school because he enjoys watching children pet the dog. Neighbors line the street to cheer on the morning bike bus. After school hours, people gather to play sports and use the playground. Neighbors, students and teachers came together to paint a vibrant 200-foot mural on a wall previously filled with graffiti. Research shows that school closures lead to decreased community cohesion and social connections. These social connections don’t have an easy replacement, and their loss would feed into the epidemic of loneliness with significant health implications.
The effects of school closures will be economic too. After drop-off, parents spill into local coffee shops. After school, families head to nearby ice cream stores. The economic engine of schools extends beyond local businesses, as schools are linked to real estate values and tax revenue, and empty buildings bring disorder to neighborhoods. The effects can be exacerbated or mitigated depending on what is done with buildings after closures. Currently, PPS provides no plans for what would happen to up to 20 closed schools across the city.
School closures aren’t just a parent issue. They are a Portland issue and should be an urgent concern and priority for our city.
Alice Graham, Portland
Meredith Barrett, Portland
Gina Altomare, Portland
