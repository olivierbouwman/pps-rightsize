PPS starts district 'rightsizing' amid budget gap, declining enrollment, aging buildings
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — The Portland Public Schools Board is hearing from families and staff as it looks to reorganize the district amid a multi-million dollar budget deficit and declining enrollment.
The district has begun its process of rightsizing, researching how spaces are being used and seeing whether schools could be consolidated, repurposed, or closed altogether.
“School communities have so much pride and so much joy in their sites, and so it’s hard to have a conversation that could lead to a disruption of that,” Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said. “It’s a conversation that has been avoided for too long and it’s time to have it. I’m ready to have it, and I think my board is ready to take action.”
During a community engagement session Wednesday, Armstrong told dozens of parents and staff that the number of schools that could be closed could be anywhere from one to 12 schools between K-8, middle, and alternative schools.
“We have fewer students, we have many buildings, and they’re not fully utilized,” Armstrong said. “We also have a significant budget gap, and we really need to think about the way we resource our buildings. We also have aging facilities. They’re not heating when they need to be warm, and they’re not cooling when they need to be cool, and so we have some issues to solve.”
The school board heard from community members Wednesday about where their priorities lie and their concerns with a rightsizing approach.
Thia Knowlton is a parent to a fifth grader and eighth grader at PPS. She worries schools with lower enrollment could be a target at the expense of students who are doing well because of it.
“There’s a give and take on that because lower enrollment schools -- you’re getting a better teacher to kid ratio, so you’re feeling better about what your child is seeing than you have other schools,” she said. “Obviously, they have massive classrooms and we’re trying to figure out how to balance all of them together to make sense of that, and I don’t think there’s a winner here. I think, to be honest, no matter what, the kids lose.”
Knowlton said it’s important for the district to be looking ahead and evaluating each school’s potential to bring in more students over the coming years.
“I think we all understand the limitations that they’re dealing with budgetarily and that a lot of this comes from the state and their lack of funding for our schools and our students, but I also hope they understand that there’s a lot of growth happening in different neighborhoods like I pointed out tonight,” Knowlton said. “And so, we don’t wanna be closing down or combining schools without really looking at the population differentiation that’s gonna be occurring in the coming years.”
Armstrong emphasizes that this is the very beginning of the process.
“These initial conversations are around, what are the guiding principles that our school board will consider when beginning the process of the framework, so that I can bring make some schools for consideration for either closure or optimization, consolidation, or even just boundary adjustments,” Armstrong said. “So, the engagement series is all about informing the guiding principles.”
The district said it plans to continue community engagement once it establishes guiding principles.
“We’re going to continue to be in conversation about the guiding principles over the next four to six weeks, and then we’ll move to a framework,” Armstrong said. “Once we get into a framework, that’s when we’re going to do a deep dive on program elements, and demographics, and all those other wonderful things that make up Portland.”
Armstrong will present a draft list of recommendations for boundary changes, consolidations, and closures in November with the board expected to take action in December.
“We’re going to create as many opportunities as we can to listen, to learn, and to inform,” Armstrong said.
There are two more engagement sessions where families can voice their thoughts and opinions: Saturday, April 25 at 2 p.m. at Benson High School, and Monday, April 27 at 6 p.m. at Roosevelt High School. Both meetings have virtual participation options.
