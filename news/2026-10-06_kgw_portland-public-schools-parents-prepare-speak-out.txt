PORTLAND, Ore. — Portland Public Schools parents are preparing to voice their concerns Tuesday as the school board discusses the district's proposed "rightsizing" plan, which includes scenarios that could close up to 14 schools.
PPS released the potential closure list Monday as the district works to address declining enrollment and financial challenges. The district is considering three scenarios: closing all 14 schools on the list, closing 11 of the 14, or making no school closures.
The Portland Association of Teachers released a statement, saying in part, "Closing, consolidating, and transforming programs at over 25 schools is a dramatic change. It’s critical that PPS get the process right, which requires slowing down and involving more stakeholders. Students and families deserve a plan to ensure every student has access to a well-resourced high-quality school.
Some parents, however, say the proposed changes could have a significant impact on students and school communities.
"Taking [my daughter] out of this environment will be a tremendous challenge for her," co-president of the James John PTA Sam Elingson said. "But it's not just about her, it's about this entire community. It's about keeping these families connected and keeping these families together."
He also raised concerns about how the changes could affect Title I schools.
Parents will have an opportunity to ask questions during Tuesday night's school board meeting, scheduled for 6 p.m. at the district headquarters.
The Portland Association of Teachers also planned a protest outside district headquarters at 5 p.m. ahead of the meeting.
The schools under consideration are:
- Maplewood Elementary
- Stephenson Elementary
- Irvington Elementary
- Rose City Park Elementary
- Buckman Elementary
- Creston Elementary
- Marysville Elementary
- Woodmere Elementary
- Lewis Elementary
- Beach Elementary
- James John Elementary
- Peninsula Elementary
- Sabin Elementary
- Sellwood Middle School
