PORTLAND, Ore. — Portland Public Schools on Monday evening released a list of schools that it's considering shutting down as it weighs how to address declining enrollment and rising costs.
The district has proposed three options for the school board to consider: close 14 schools under Scenario A, 11 schools under Scenario B, or maintain the current configuration.
The possible closures are part of the district's "rightsizing" plan. PPS has described it as a long-term, districtwide plan to try to solve declining enrollment and rising costs while ensuring that school facilities are used in a sustainable way. Other options the district is studying include changes to boundaries, programs and how buildings are used.
PPS said under Scenario A, a total of 14 schools would close, made up of 13 elementary and one middle school: Maplewood Elementary, Stephenson Elementary, Irvington Elementary, Rose City Park Elementary, Buckman Elementary, Creston Elementary, Marysville Elementary, Woodmere Elementary, Lewis Elementary, Beach Elementary, James John Elementary, Peninsula Elementary, Sabin Elementary, and Sellwood Middle School.
Scenario A would also include 38 boundary changes that would impact elementary, middle and high school attendance areas; eight grade level reconfigurations, 13 program moves and the discontinuation of one focus-program. Dual Language Immersion programs would be consolidated while Spanish, Mandarin and Vietnamese immersion programs would relocate or be combined at certain schools.
The district has also proposed Scenario B, which would impact 11 schools in addition to boundary, grade-level and program changes: Maplewood Elementary, Irvington Elementary, Buckman Elementary, Creston Elementary, Marysville Elementary, Woodmere Elementary, Beach Elementary, James John Elementary Peninsula Elementary, Sabin Elementary and Sellwood Middle School.
Under Scenario B, there would also be 34 boundary changes that would impact elementary, middle and high school attendance areas. Eight grade levels would also be reconfigured, 12 programs would move and one focus-option program would be discontinued. Additionally, several K-8 schools would become K-5 schools, and middle grade students would move to comprehensive middle schools.
PPS also said DLE programs would be consolidated; Spanish and Mandarin immersion programs would either be relocated or combined at designated schools, but Vietnamese immersion would remain at Rose City Park.
The district has proposed a third option that would defer the decision and maintain the current school configuration for the time being.

"We know how deeply connected students, families, educators and communities are to their schools, and considering the closure of any school is extraordinarily difficult," said Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong. "But we also have a responsibility to look at Portland Public Schools as a whole and consider changes that give every student access to strong programs, stable staffing and the opportunities and support they deserve."
Since 2019, district data shows enrollment has decreased by more than 6,000 students. PPS estimates it will fall by another 3,400 students in the next six years. District leaders have said that if action isn't taken to consolidate schools, many elementary and middle schools could become vulnerable to enrollment fluctuations, causing staffing resources to be spread too thin, leading to fewer educational programs.
The district cites declining birth rates in Portland as a key factor that has led to under-enrollment at many schools.
The Portland Association of Teachers released a statement, saying in part, "Closing, consolidating, and transforming programs at over 25 schools is a dramatic change. It’s critical that PPS get the process right, which requires slowing down and involving more stakeholders. Students and families deserve a plan to ensure every student has access to a well-resourced high-quality school.
"Closing schools is not a plan, it’s a reaction. Our students deserve better. Families deserve better. Educators deserve better. We ask that the district exhaust every available option it has before considering closing schools. And at PAT, we will fight hard to secure quality education funding because students and families deserve high quality, well-resourced neighborhood schools."
PPS' rightsizing plan has received pushback from parents. At a recent PPS Board of Education meeting, some parents questioned the district's long-term strategy and financial decisions, including spending on major school renovation projects.
No final decisions have been made yet. The school board will discuss the rightsizing plan during a meeting scheduled for 6 p.m. on Tuesday.
The district said families, students and staff will have the chance to ask questions and provide feedback before the school board votes on the superintendent's final recommendations in December. If approved, changes would begin to go into effect for the 2027-2028 school year, with full implementation completed within three years.
