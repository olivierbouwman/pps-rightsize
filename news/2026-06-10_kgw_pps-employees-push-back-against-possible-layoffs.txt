PORTLAND, Ore. — Employees and union leaders voiced concerns about layoffs and staffing reductions during a Portland Public Schools board meeting Tuesday night, warning that the cuts could worsen existing challenges across the district.
Several staff members told the school board that departments are already operating with limited resources and that additional reductions could affect day-to-day operations.
The concerns come as the Portland Association of Teachers continues to challenge the district's handling of layoffs. The union recently filed a grievance against Portland Public Schools, alleging that dozens of layoff notices do not comply with procedures outlined in the collective bargaining agreement.
According to the union, at least 82 layoff notices have been issued so far, and approximately 77 of them may violate contractual layoff procedures. Portland Public Schools disputes those claims.
During the meeting, employees spoke about the potential impact of staffing cuts and criticized the district's staffing decisions. One bus driver said the transportation department is routinely short between 20 and 25 drivers each day and that management personnel are sometimes filling positions to keep operations running.
Dylan Serkin, a PPS bus driver, told the board that operational challenges increase when departments lack adequate staffing and communication.
Angela Bonilla, president of the Portland Association of Teachers, also criticized the district's staffing process, saying the handling of "unassignments," layoffs and placements has been an "absolute disaster" for confidence among employees.
Portland Public Schools acknowledged that layoffs are difficult but said the district is following its contractual obligations. District officials maintain that staffing decisions are being made based on factors including seniority and employee qualifications.
The dispute comes as the district faces continued scrutiny over staffing levels and budget-related reductions ahead of the next school year.
