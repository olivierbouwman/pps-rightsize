Title: Portland Public Schools board approves raise, contract extension for its superintendent

Author: Julia Silverman

Portland Public Schools board members voted 5-2 in favor of giving Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong a raise and three-year contract extension during a Tuesday evening meeting at Grant High School.
The two board members who voted against the proposal were Virginia La Forte and Stephanie Engelsman, both of whom said they were fans of Armstrong’s, but felt the timing and terms of the contract extension were misplaced, given ongoing conversations about budget cuts and school closures.
The district is facing a $65 million budget shortfall, preparing to potentially close up to 20 elementary, K-8 and middle schools and readying for negotiations with the Portland Association of Teachers over a new three-year contract.
When Armstrong was hired in 2024, she made $325,000 per year. That was less than the $342,000 that previous Superintendent Guadalupe Guerrero was making in base salary when he departed Portland after six years.
Her original contract provided for salary increases of 3% per year. The revised contract specifies that beginning in July 2027, she will receive either a 3% raise annually or the cost of living adjustment agreed to by the Portland Association of Teachers’ union for its members, whichever is lower.
Armstrong had requested to match the 1% cost of living adjustment agreed to by the teachers union this year, Board Chair Eddie Wang said. But board members did not complete negotiations over her contract extension in time, meaning she was granted the 3% cost of living adjustment for the current school year that her previous contract guaranteed.
A copy of her contract extension lists her new base salary at $344,792 per year, or $28,733 a month.
Armstrong’s base salary is slightly lower than that of her peers who lead similarly sized districts around the country, including the superintendents of Denver Public Schools, Oakland Public Schools and Omaha Public Schools; it is below what the superintendent makes in Seattle, Beaverton and San Francisco.
The contract extension drew pushback from the Portland Association of Teachers and City Councilor Tiffany Koyama Lane, who criticized the timing of the extension and minimal opportunity for public feedback.
“We are hearing that even more cuts are coming and that school buildings will be shuttered in the near future,” Koyama Lane said at Tuesday’s meeting. “Yet right now, this board is seriously talking about prioritizing a raise for our highest paid employee in the whole district.”
Longtime schools activist and PPS parent Maya Pueo von Geldern, though, offered a counterpoint.
“We can’t say that we fight for equal pay for racial equity in the workplace or for women to break the glass ceiling and then treat Dr. Armstrong like she doesn’t deserve this position for the going rate,” Pueo von Geldern said. “If we lose the superintendent now, our district implodes. We go through an extremely costly and lengthy process to hire a new superintendent who will likely command an even higher salary.”
Armstrong’s proposed new contract includes notable perks and raises in other areas, beyond her base salary. For example:
- An increase in the district’s contribution to her retirement account, from 8.5% to 9.5% of base salary, or about $32,000 annually. The contract provides that it will eventually rise to 10.5%, starting in July 2027.
- A $750 per month car allowance, up from $400, or from $4,800 a year to $9,000 per year.
- A new retention bonus that would kick in if she stays in Portland for four years since she was hired, adding an additional 3% to her base salary starting in her June 2028 paycheck. That rises to 5% for the 2028-29 school year, or roughly $17,000 per year. Wang said that is a relatively low bonus compared to the 8% to 10% in retention incentives paid out to superintendents who lead comparable districts around the country.
- The ability to cash out more vacation days, from 12 per year to 17 per year, which could represent another $4,700 if she chooses to be paid out for those days instead of taking time off. The new contract would also allow her to take up to five “flex” days per year, to offset work done on holidays or weekends on the district’s behalf.
