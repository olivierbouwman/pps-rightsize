Portland Public Schools to discuss closing 15 elementary schools Tuesday; equity a focus
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Portland Public Schools officials are set to present a series of charts to the Portland School Board that lay out, in stark terms, why Superintendent Armstrong recently raised her estimate of how many schools the district needs to close.
The data points to a widening gap between the number of school buildings PPS operates and the number of students actually enrolled in them—a mismatch that, left unaddressed, will leave most schools too small to be properly staffed for years to come.
Since the 2018-19 school year, PPS has lost 6,086 students districtwide. Over that same period, rather than shrinking its footprint to match, the district actually added school buildings—moving from 81 to 83 sites, a net increase of two.
The trend isn't reversing. District projections show enrollment will drop by another 3,446 students by the 2031-32 school year, on top of the losses already absorbed. Put simply: PPS has been serving a shrinking number of students across a growing number of buildings, and that mismatch is only expected to deepen.
As consultants Dr. Terrance Green and Dr. Havala Hanson framed it in guiding the district's approach:
PPS should ask whether a proposed change will create stronger and more equitable learning environments—not simply whether it saves money.
A newly formed coalition of Portland Public Schools parents is urging the district to put equity at the center of any decision closure.
The group is launching a campaign called “Right-Sizing the Right Way” as PPS prepares to discuss a plan that could close up to 15 elementary schools.
“Right now, I mean, the district is in a tough spot,” parent Mike Westling said.

District materials prepared for Monday evening’s school board meeting list a goal of ensuring all students have access to sufficient resources, supports, programming and staffing.
The coalition says it agrees that the district faces difficult decisions, but wants leaders to consider how any changes would affect students across the city.
“When we face tough choices, we need to make decisions that are in the best interest of as many students as possible,” Westling said.
Parent Elizabeth Doerr said the group wants the district to preserve as many neighborhood schools as possible.
She pointed to the district’s focus-option schools — campuses with specialized programming, including creative arts or science-focused programs.
“What we found in the data provided by PPS is that they serve more of a privileged, white, economically privileged community of students than the larger district,” Doerr said.
The PPS board is not expected to vote on any school closures until late fall. The district is expected to propose closures in November.
In the meantime, the coalition is asking parents and community members at neighborhood schools to sign a petition calling on PPS to prioritize those schools during the process.
“The one thing that we’re really looking for now is for parents and community members within neighborhood schools to sign a petition,” Doerr said, “to basically show that there is momentum and there’s interest in really prioritizing neighborhood schools in this process.”
PPS says community members will have opportunities to weigh in before any final decision is made.
