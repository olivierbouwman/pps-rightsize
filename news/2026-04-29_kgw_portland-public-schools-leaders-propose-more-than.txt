PORTLAND, Ore. — Portland Public Schools is facing down a looming $56 million budget gap, and officials say that the cuts they have to make will be "devastating" to the district.
PPS Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong shared a new budget proposal during a school board meeting Tuesday night. As part of the proposal, more than 320 full-time positions could be eliminated. Some employees may retire or resign, Armstrong said, so it's unclear how many employees will be laid off. The district plans to reduce its central office staff by roughly 14%.
"We are here because this is an emergency situation. The sky isn't just falling — it has fell," Armstrong said.
Rising costs, limited revenue and falling employment rates have squeezed the district's budget tighter and tighter. Since the 2019-20 school year, Portland Public Schools has seen a 12% drop in enrollment.
Armstrong added that the district likely faces another $65.2 million budget reduction for the 2027 to 2028 school year. If that happens, Portland Public would have slashed around $170 million over a three-year span. That's the most that PPS would have ever slashed over the same time span, Armstrong said Wednesday morning.
Board members acknowledged Wednesday that these budget cuts will have long lasting impacts on schools.
"There's no more fat to cut," PPS school board chair Eddie Wang said. "I mean, we are literally beyond the meat; we are into the bone. And every cut we are doing is going to hurt, whether now or in the long run."
Wednesday, Armstrong and other board members accused Oregon lawmakers of failing to tap into the state's Education Stability Fund to help support the district.
"I call on our elected leaders to continue to have conversations on what it means to adequately fund schools," said Armstrong.
Chief of Staff Deborah Kafoury encouraged parents in the district to call their elected representatives to "make them understand" how students would be impacted by further cuts.
PPS parent Maya Pueo von Geldern agreed. “This is the emergency. We need to access that fund so that our kids are not facing this huge decline next year and the following year.”
The Portland Association of Teachers released a statement regarding the cuts, with PAT President Angela Bonilla calling the planned layoffs of 180 educators "disastrous" and asking Kotek to convene a special session, as well as call a state of emergency in order for Education Stability Funds to be released.
"Long term, we need revenue reform in Oregon so our schools are funded off mega-corporations like Nike and Intel paying their fair share, instead of off the backs of working people like it is now. In the interim, one-time emergency access to the $1 Billion existing state reserves is necessary," said Bonilla, in part.
PPS's school board has until May 26 to approve a final budget, before adopting it in late June.
