I have been a Metropolitan Learning Center parent for 12 years. I served four years as the president of the parent-teacher-student association and chairedMLC’s 50th anniversary in 2018. I have seen firsthand what makes it such a gem.
Our community is devastated by Portland Public Schools’ decision to close MLC’s high school, (“A Portland Public Schools campus is set to lose its high school grades,” Feb. 11). This decision was made without transparency, notice or meaningful community engagement.
The district cites under-enrollment and funding limitations. But describing MLC’s high school as serving 40 students misrepresents our integrated grade 6–12 model. Teachers work across grade levels. Eliminating 8.9 FTE will not simply shrink a small high school — it destabilizes the entire school and puts MLC’s future at risk.
Over the past decade, we have cycled through five principals and endured steady erosion: shortened lunches, reduced electives, loss of Friday experiential learning, fewer field trips and key staff cuts. Yet under fresh leadership, MLC enrolled a full freshman cohort with a waiting list for the upcoming school year.
Enrollment declines are not unique to MLC; many of Portland’s public schools face similar challenges.
Even though MLC has fewer bells and whistles, there is a culture of belonging that money cannot buy. Many students attend because of untenable learning environments at other schools. Instead of closing this beacon of acceptance, PPS should address why students do not feel safe elsewhere.
More than 1,500 community members have signed a petition urging reversal. Decisions of this magnitude deserve more care.
Alisa Welch, Portland
