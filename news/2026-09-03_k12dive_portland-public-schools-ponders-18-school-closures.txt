Title: Portland Public Schools ponders up to 18 school closures
Outlet: K-12 Dive
Author: Kara Arundel
Date: 2026-09-03
URL: https://www.k12dive.com/news/portland-public-schools-ponders-up-to-18-school-closures/829476/

Dive Brief:
- Oregon's Portland Public Schools is considering closing up to 18 schools, including 15 elementary schools, as it tries to "rightsize" due to declining enrollment, according to a presentation at its board of education meeting Tuesday.
- The district's data shows that enrollment has decreased by 6,086 students since the 2018-19 school year, and enrollment is projected to decrease by another 3,446 students in the 2031-32 school year. As of the 2025-26 school year, there were nearly 43,500 students in the district schools, community-based programs, special services programs and public charter schools.
- Enrollment drops and the subsequent loss of per-pupil funding are causing several school districts across the country to close or consolidate schools as factors including declining birthrates, school choice competition and rising housing costs impact the school-age population.
Dive Insight:
Portland Public Schools began its rightsizing exploration last school year, and it will spend this fall reaching out to stakeholders and developing recommendations for changes that the board will consider later this year. Several district administrators emphasized during Tuesday's meeting that no decisions have been made and that any closure recommendations will be based on consistent and sustainable learning spaces and resources for students.
Margaret Calvert, the district's executive director of school planning and modernization, told the board that for at least the last eight years, the district has had decreasing enrollment but bascially the same number of school buildings. That has led to short-term solutions to balance resources to needed areas.
"We are now in a place where these essentially stopgap measures, which are short-term adjustments, are becoming the norm and are far more widespread," Calvert said. "The goal of this rightsizing process is to move from immediate, short-term problem-solving to provide options that will improve student experiences in every school for years to come."
If the district doesn't close any elementary schools by 2031-32, 37 of the district's 45 current elementary schools are expected to have fewer than 360 students each. At the middle school level, 13 of the 15 current schools are estimated to have enrollments under 500 students if no action is taken by 2031-32, according to the board presentation.
Those projected reduced school enrollments could cause fluctuations in staffing, programming and resources, Calvert said.
"These are very sobering conversations, and I just wanted to underscore that we're at a point where we're trying to engage in putting our district on a path to longer-term stability, that we don't return to these conversations again and again," Calvert said.
Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong, also speaking during the Tuesday board meeting, echoed that discussions about school closures are difficult. "This isn't an easy conversation. As a matter of fact, it's a conversation that nobody wants to have. It's not fun. There's not joy in it, and yet it's a necessary conversation," Armstrong said.
