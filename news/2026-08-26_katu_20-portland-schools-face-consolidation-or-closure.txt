Up to 20 Portland schools could face consolidation or closure due to growing budget crunch
PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Portland Public Schools (PPS) leaders kicked off the new school year at Rosa Parks Elementary School on Wednesday while warning that another difficult budget season could bring additional layoffs, school closures and program changes.
Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong said the district expects to face up to $65 million in additional reductions after a year marked by budget cuts and layoffs.
“This year will be even harder,” Armstrong said, adding that PPS will spend the next four to five months working through potential cuts, school consolidations and efforts to “right-size” schools.

Parents have raised concerns that another round of layoffs could increase class sizes and hurt student learning. Armstrong said the district will review classroom enrollment regularly and make staffing decisions with students’ learning environments in mind.
“Students are at the center of all the things that we do,” she said.
PPS projects overall enrollment will decline about 2% this year. However, Armstrong said elementary enrollment is expected to remain stable, while middle school enrollment is projected to exceed earlier estimates.
The district will review consolidation data throughout September and plans to present information to the school board at its Sept. 1 meeting. PPS expects to bring a draft list of possible school consolidations, closures and program moves to the board in November.
Armstrong said the number of schools affected remains unclear. In the spring, she estimated between five and 12 schools could be considered. Now, she said the number could range from about 10 to 20, depending on district data, school board decisions and community input.
Despite the challenges ahead, Armstrong said district leaders remain focused on academic growth and welcoming students back to Portland schools.
Woodmere Elementary
At Woodmere Elementary School in Southeast Portland’s Woodstock neighborhood, families are waiting for answers.
The district has not identified which schools could be affected. But Alyssa Brainard, president of Woodmere’s Parent Teacher Organization, expressed concern about how her community could be impacted.
“A certain percentage of our population is living under the poverty line,” Brainard said. “That’s not everyone, but that is a large demographic of our school and they’re getting the supports that they need here. And we can’t guarantee that that’s just going to easily transfer over to another school.”
Brainard said Woodmere serves as more than a place for children to learn. She said its community network helps connect families with essential support.
The school’s Parent-Teacher Organization runs a community cares fund, supported in part by a local church, that Brainard said helps families stay housed and fed.
“We are literally keeping people housed, fed, like, surviving and thriving through our school,” she said.
For some families, Brainard said, a nearby school is also a matter of access. Not every family has reliable access to a car, making distance and transportation a major concern if students are reassigned.
On Wednesday, PPS Superintendent Dr. Kimberlee Armstrong acknowledged that districtwide enrollment is down 2% this year. She said elementary enrollment is stable compared with projections, while middle-school enrollment is higher than expected.
Armstrong said students will remain at the center of the district’s decision-making as it considers potential closures and consolidations.
