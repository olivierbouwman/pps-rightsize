PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) — Districts across Oregon are looking at closing some schools as enrollments are dropping, including in the Portland area.
On Saturday, Portland Public Schools hosted a conversation with parents about how to make this happen. Oregon’s largest school district is looking to close down some elementary schools as soon as next fall.
“We need to consider the impact on communities of color and low-income kids,” said Molly Franks, a PPS parent.
Dozens of parents voiced their concerns at a community event inside the Faubion K-8 school in Northeast Portland. PPS leaders listened to parents about their views on how to make decisions on what schools to close or consolidate.
“I think it’s going to mean we’re in a really challenging moment as a district because of budget cuts,” said PPS parent Elizabeth Doerr. “So this is going to be a big upheaval, and every single school is affected”
After steep drops in enrollment PPS leaders looking at closing more than a dozens of the district’s 81 schools, some closures could happen as soon as next fall
“I see the data and that we have declining enrollment and so it does make sense to make some changes,” said Franks.
No word of which schools are on the chopping block, yet. Leadership is taking into consideration enrollment, boundaries, and building conditions.
“We also have many multicultural events, very specific to some of our multicultural groups, which our face team is helping organize, which is our family and community engagement team,” said Senior Chief of Operations Jon Franco.
Several parents used their voices to promote equity, arts and special programs for kids at neighborhood schools.
“I want special programming in the arts and math and all that in every neighborhood school so that families don’t feel like they have to leave to get a special experience for their kid because I think every kid deserves all of that and all of the above,” said Doerr.
“Yeah, my main concern is that we not repeat a pattern of privileging middle-class white people and their kids over low-income communities of color,” Franks expressed.
Any staffing changes have to follow PPS collective bargaining agreements.
In November, the superintendent of schools will present a formal recommendation to the board of a short list of schools for closure or consolidation. The next Board of Education Community Connection is Wednesday, Sept. 30 at Ida B. Wells. The school board expects to make a final decision in December.
