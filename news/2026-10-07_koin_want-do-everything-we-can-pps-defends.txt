PORTLAND, Ore. (KOIN) — Portland Public Schools is considering school closures and other changes as the district works to address declining enrollment and a projected $65 million budget deficit.
PPS is losing about 1,000 students each year, Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said.
She presented recommended scenarios Monday that would concentrate students and resources in fewer schools. The full presentation can be found here.
The proposals follow priorities set by the school board, including enrollment, building conditions, walkability, transportation and equity.
Armstrong argued that concentrating students and resources could create more stable staffing and support.
“We have taken from some schools to subsidize others,” Armstrong said.
The superintendent acknowledged that closing schools alone will not solve the district’s budget problems.
Instead, she said the broader goal is to stabilize the entire system and better align staffing and resources with enrollment.
The recommendations drew a large crowd Tuesday as the school board heard directly from families who could see their schools close.
“We expect community to show up and push back and present other data points and other ways to think about things, and we welcome that,” Armstrong told KOIN 6 News.
Armstrong said the scenarios could change as PPS receives public feedback and reviews additional data.
“We want to do everything that we can to protect it, to resource, to stabilize and to lead towards the next decade of learning in our schools,” she said.
Armstrong plans to issue her final recommendation Nov. 17.
The school board expects to vote on the proposal in December.
The school closure debate comes as Armstrong starts a new contract paying her nearly $345,000 annually, with a 3% annual increase.
