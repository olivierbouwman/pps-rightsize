As of 2026-10-07. Companion to [`SUNNYSIDE_CASE.md`](SUNNYSIDE_CASE.md). These reads are inferences from each director's public record (votes, statements, campaigns and donors) in [`BOARD_PROFILES.md`](BOARD_PROFILES.md). Treat them as working hypotheses, not certainties.

**Path to four votes** (updated with the Mar–May 2026 transcripts):
1. Engelsman (lead).
2. Sullivan.
3. Splitt.
4. La Forte.

Chase-Miller and Wang are reachable by proposing to *fix the lottery instead of closing the program*, which matches Wang's own April comment. DePass is the long shot. The best route is still changing the staff recommendation before Nov 17.

## Stephanie Engelsman (Zone 6): the champion

**Why:**
- Sunnyside is in her zone ("we're parents at Sunnyside. It's in my zone," 3/8 retreat).
- On Oct 6 she asked why Sunnyside and Laurelhurst lose 6–8 while Winterhaven keeps theirs. She noted Sunnyside "managed to maintain enrollment," and raised Hosford's size.
- She called the plan "mayhem" and is the most likely no on the overall package.

**What resonates:** special education and class size (her platform), climate (her platform), boundaries-first, and the Bridger merger as a cautionary tale (752 → 446 students). She also wants hard numbers on savings and transportation.

**Best ask:**
- Put the three questions (Hosford projection, savings, "why Sunnyside") on the board question tracker.
- Prepare an amendment to remove Sunnyside's changes, and recruit Splitt or La Forte to co-sponsor it.

**Caution:** her public friction with the chair means an Engelsman-only amendment starts with 1–2 votes. Sunnyside needs a co-lead from the middle.

## Christy Splitt (Zone 1): the key swing

**New from spring transcripts:** she was a co-architect of the process and moved the final 7296 wording. At the Apr 27 session she said she agreed with Sullivan that "having focus options that are neighborhood schools plus focus option is such a cool idea." She also pushed for cohort-cohesion language in the guiding principles. Moving Sunnyside's middle-schoolers to Hosford splits cohorts, so it fails her own test.

**Why:**
- On Oct 6: *"I promised our friends from Sunnyside I would ask why Sunnyside was selected…"*
- She floated letting the neighborhood school "retain the Sunnyside programming."
- She says the plan affects too many students (24–27%) and wants a "scalpel," not a hammer.
- She's not running in 2027, so she's free to vote her judgment.

**What resonates:**
- *Subtraction.* Removing the Sunnyside change shrinks the package without adding anyone else to the list, consistent with her pledge not to add schools.
- *Fairness.* She objected that big schools get protected "just because they're big"; Sunnyside is big *and* working.
- *Teacher voices.*
- *Climate:* she spent years at the Oregon League of Conservation Voters and works in government relations at the state Department of Energy.

**Handle her focus-option doubts head-on.** She has "concerns with" focus options and suggested capping students from certain schools. Offer exactly that: keep Sunnyside, but cap lottery seats from under-enrolled neighborhood schools. It lets her say yes on her own terms.

**Avoid:** a general defense of all focus options or lotteries.

## Virginia La Forte (Zone 5): the numbers person

**New from spring transcripts:** on Mar 10 (probable attribution) she said "we need to pick a lane… I think it's middle schools." By Mar 31 (confirmed) she had shifted: "I've always believed that middle schools were [better], but the more recent research has actually shown the opposite." She asked staff for the survey split by K-8 versus middle school, citing a "statistically significant… 20-point difference." Her April no on launching rightsizing was about process and guardrails, not opposition to consolidation.

**Give her the research she referred to:** Rockoff & Lockwood 2010 (NYC) and Schwerdt & West 2013 (Florida) both found that moving to middle school in grade 6 lowers achievement relative to K-8 peers, with losses that persist. See `research-literature/B-structure-size-outdoor/`.

**Why:**
- On Oct 6 she said she "fully expected to see focus options eliminated." She also said she hadn't gotten a real answer on "why Sunnyside" and "can't make a decision" without cost impacts.
- She earlier focused on whether K-8s retain families better than middle schools.
- She worries about "large swaths of the city" without a school at the center of a community.

**What resonates:**
- **Cost:** no published savings, about $10.5k per student (average), and the building stays open anyway.
- **Retention:** Sunnyside enrollment grew while its neighbors shrank, and a K-8 keeps families through grade 8.
- **Environmental health:** a long-time PPS lead, water and air-quality advocate, she served on the Environmental Technical Advisory Committee.
- **Oversight:** "arbitrary" is a governance red flag for a fiscal hawk.

**Best ask:** have her request the Sunnyside-specific fiscal impact and the Hosford projection in her capacity as chair of the Teaching, Learning & Enrollment committee. Testify at that committee on Oct 13.

**Avoid:** pure emotion, or "pause everything." She does think "big swings" may be needed.

## Patte Sullivan (Zone 3): the persuadable fourth vote

**New from spring transcripts (probable attribution):**
- **Apr 27 engagement session:** "I'm really interested in the um having the neighborhood school and the focus together. That sounds like a you know, a win-win. I know Sunnyside does it as well."
- **Apr 28:** she reported talking with Sunnyside families about the model ("they seem to think that worked really well"), and noted that people at the session favored K-8.
- **Res. 7296:** she voted with Engelsman and La Forte on both failed amendments.

She is now the strongest candidate for the fourth vote.

**Why:**
- She said she's "for going further," but also that she wants honesty that closures "don't save money."
- She has broken with leadership on specific items: local school foundations, and the 9/29 bond-oversight amendments.
- She has no donors or coalition to answer to.

**What resonates:**
- She spent about 20 years as a PPS teacher, including **at Metropolitan Learning Center**, a focus/alternative school.
- She taught kindergarten and has a biology degree, emphasizes social-emotional support and "arts as well as STEM," and is a SURJ member.
- Stories of individual students thriving, especially neurodivergent kids, land with her. So does hands-on outdoor learning, and the argument that this change costs a working program and saves nothing.

**Best ask:** a short note and a school visit (a Thursday field-study day), then a request that she support removing the Sunnyside change as a "keep what works" amendment.

**Avoid:** attacks on Wang or Procedeo. She still defends that vote.

## Rashelle Chase-Miller (Zone 4, vice chair): handle with care

**New from spring transcripts (probable attribution):**
- **Mar 10:** wanted focus options "on the table."
- **Mar 12:** asked whether focus options drain lower-income North Portland schools.
- **Apr 28:** "I also found my um own opinions about uh focus option schools being challenged. I'm really interested in the idea of focus option schools that exist in neighborhood schools… so that there are options within a community, but we're also not pulling kids out of their neighborhood."

That describes Sunnyside. Pitch it as her model, already working.

**Why uncertain:**
- She's in the leadership bloc and frames everything through equity for low-income, Black and brown students. She has said the district "historically… listened to the more affluent schools."
- Sunnyside is not Title I (19% direct certification), so a careless pitch reads as privilege.

**What resonates:**
- **Special education:** 23.5% of Sunnyside students, more than triple Winterhaven's share.
- **Her own campaign's "Soft Neighborhood Model,"** keeping kids in their communities. Sunnyside's neighborhood-plus-lottery hybrid is close to that.
- **Teacher voice,** given her PAT backing.
- **Her conditions for any change:** guaranteed staffing and programming at receiving schools. Hosford's capacity is a fair question under that standard.

**Best ask:** don't ask her to champion it. Ask her not to oppose a narrow amendment, and to insist on the Hosford and special-education analysis before the vote. Pair Sunnyside's ask with support for Title I communities' asks; don't compete with them.

**Avoid:** comparisons that put Sunnyside's needs above Buckman's or other Title I schools'.

## Eddie Wang (Zone 7, chair): change the recommendation, not his mind

**New from spring transcripts (Apr 28, probable attribution):** the engagement sessions "changed my mind" on focus options. He heard from families "that they weren't able to find these services in their neighborhood schools." To address focus options' impact on neighborhood schools, he wants "not a closure solution but potentially maybe a um how the lottery is done or a policy solution." Sunnyside's fallback ask (keep it, cap lottery seats from under-enrolled schools) is exactly that. He also wants directors to act as "trustees" rather than responding to single-school pressure, so frame the ask as a policy fix, not a Sunnyside exception.

**Why unlikely:**
- He's "for going for as much as needed."
- He cited data that K-8s prepared students for high school worse than middle schools.
- He says scenario tweaks "cascade" and must go back through staff.
- He defers to the superintendent.

**What resonates:**
- An engineering framing: this is the *smallest* possible change, it doesn't cascade, and here are the Hosford numbers.
- "Equity must be the outcome": the special-education share.
- **Evidence** (he's a former Benson science teacher): he has cited 2017-era district data (never published in any meeting in this archive) showing K-8s prepare students worse for high school. Ask PPS to publish that analysis. Florida's statewide study (Schwerdt & West 2013, peer-reviewed) tested exactly that and found no high-school payoff from middle schools, plus lasting losses from moving in grade 6. Pair it with a request for Sunnyside-specific 9th-grade outcomes.

**Best path:** win staff over before Nov 17. If the recommendation drops the Sunnyside change, Wang will likely accept it.

**Avoid:** "pause rightsizing," the recall, the Procedeo audit, crowd pressure at meetings. On Oct 6 he warned a protesting crowd that the meeting would be moved.

## Michelle DePass (Zone 2): long shot, but there's a climate opening

**Why unlikely:**
- She said "go big" (9.5/10) and "be courageous."
- She didn't save the MLC high school, her own alma mater.
- She is the board's strongest "don't delay" voice.

**What resonates:**
- Her stated "north star" is students in poverty, **in special education**, and multilingual learners. Lead with special education.
- Her career is in climate and sustainability: a city climate analyst at the Bureau of Planning and Sustainability, Sustainability at Work, and Sierra Club backing. A PPS environmental-justice school is aligned with her values.

**Best ask:** frame keeping Sunnyside as *courage applied precisely*: making the hard closures while protecting the one program that serves special-education students this way.

**Avoid:** any message that sounds like "slow down" or "not in my school."

## Rose Sandell (student representative, advisory vote)

**What resonates:** she asked on Oct 6 how special-education classrooms would be moved, how "belonging" is measured, and how student voice is gathered.

**Best ask:** Sunnyside students, especially middle-schoolers, testifying in their own words about belonging and field studies. Her questions on the record carry weight with the adults.

**Ground rules for every contact:**
- **Board quorums.** Oregon public-meetings law limits directors from deliberating privately as a quorum (4 or more), so contact directors individually. Don't ask them to coordinate.
- **Be accurate.** Use only the figures in `SUNNYSIDE_CASE.md`; each is sourced.
- **Privacy.** Speak for your own family; don't share other children's diagnoses.
