# 2026 - 27 Budget Process

**Engaging Our Community, Prioritizing Our Students.**

## **Welcome to the 2026-27 PPS Budget Process**

Portland Public Schools is committed to transparency, collaboration, and engagement in shaping our district’s budget. With a $50 million shortfall, we are working to make strategic reductions while centering student outcomes.

This webpage is your hub for updates, engagement opportunities, and ways to provide feedback.

---

**PPS Budget Update: What We Heard. What Changed. What Comes Next.*****As of May 7, 2026***

On April 28, 2026, Portland Public Schools released the Superintendent's Proposed Budget. The district continues to engage with PPS communities as the budget is finalized with the Board of Education.

***As of February 10, 2026***

Over the past several weeks, Portland Public Schools invited families, staff, and community members to provide feedback on proposed budget reductions for the 2026–27 school year. Through multiple engagement opportunities, including community listening sessions, staff meetings, principal feedback, and an online feedback form, more than 300 people participated. This input directly informed updates to the district’s budget recommendations. 

While these changes reflect district-level decisions, how they show up will look different from school to school, based on enrollment, grade levels, and staffing needs.

Detailed staffing information is shared first with school leaders so they can plan responsibly and support their communities.

Community members asked PPS to:

* Prioritize classroom instruction
* Protect mental health and counseling services
* Reduce impacts to schools and student-facing staff
* Provide clearer information about decisions and timelines

Based on this feedback, PPS updated its budget approach to:

* Restore funding to schools to reduce impacts
* Ensure every school has at least one counselor
* Strengthen student support services, especially in middle and high schools
* Shift more reductions to central office
* Adjust school staffing models based on feedback
* These changes represent approximately $3.5 million restored to support students and schools.

* Updated school-level information was released
* On April 28, 2026 the Superintendent's Proposed Budget was released
* Engagement will continue as the budget moves through remaining phases with the School Board
* School-level conversations continue
* The School Board will review and vote on the budget later this spring

This update reflects district-level decisions; school-level implementation will continue through established processes and timelines.

PPS remains committed to transparency, ongoing engagement, and centering students in every budget decision.

### **Why is there a budget deficit?**

Portland Public Schools (PPS) is facing a $50 million budget shortfall for the 2026-27 school year. This deficit is driven by three major factors:

* Rising Costs – Inflation has made everything more expensive, from classroom supplies to staff salaries. The district has also increased investments in teacher planning time and benefits.
* Limited Funding – Oregon’s school funding has not kept up with the rising costs of operating our schools, and federal pandemic relief funds have ended. Year-over-year reduced federal funding is squeezing state budgets as well, which impacts our bottom line.
* Declining Enrollment – Fewer students are enrolling in PPS, but the district still operates the same number of school buildings. Because state funding is based on student numbers, fewer students mean less funding.

Despite these financial pressures, PPS remains committed to prioritizing students, educators, and quality education while making strategic budget adjustments.

---

## **A Multi-Year Challenge**

## What have we done so far?

Over the last several years, PPS has taken steps to reduce costs while trying to protect classrooms:

* 2022-23: Cut $30 million from central office budgets to keep schools funded.
* 2023-24: Froze hiring for non-teaching positions and cut $10 million in non-personnel costs.
* 2024-25: Reduced central office services and outside contracts, totaling $15 million in cuts.
* 2025-26: An additional $40 million in reductions were needed, impacting both school and district-level budgets.
* 2026-27: Looking for areas to trim $50 million in expenditures while trying to cut the least amount of people and services as possible.

These reductions mean that some programs, services, and staff positions will be affected. PPS is working to make thoughtful adjustments that minimize harm to students and teachers.

---

## **Where Will Reductions Be Made?**

### Proposed Impacts

To close the $50 million gap, both school-based and district-wide cuts will be necessary:

* Some District Central Budget Cuts – Further reductions in administrative costs, contracts, and operational expenses.
* Some School-Based Cuts – Potential reductions in staffing, support services, and extracurricular programs.
